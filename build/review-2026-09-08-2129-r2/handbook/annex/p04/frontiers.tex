%% Proofs for the separately authored Omega and computable-structure results.

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p4.frontiers.1}{thm:p04-rational-order-uniform-index}{Uniform effective back-and-forth}
\begin{proof}
Given indices \(e,d\), compile a program that treats them as decision
procedures for two binary order relations on \(\N\).  It constructs finite
order isomorphisms in alternating stages.  At an even stage, take the least
number outside the current domain, use the \(e\)-decider to locate its cut
among the finitely many mapped elements, and search with the \(d\)-decider for
the least unused element in the corresponding target interval.  At an odd
stage perform the symmetric range extension.  If the inputs really present
countable dense orders without endpoints, every search terminates and the
union is a total order isomorphism.

On input \(a\), the compiled program simulates this construction until \(a\)
enters the domain and then returns its mate.  The source text and the indices
\(e,d\) are fixed parameters of one effective program template, so the
parameter theorem yields a total computable compiler \(F(e,d)\) for its
program index.  Invalid presentation indices may make the compiled program
partial; the corollary asserts totality only under its displayed validity
hypothesis.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p4.frontiers.2}{thm:p04-omega-halting}{Information carried by \(\Omega\)}
\begin{proof}
Dovetail all computations of the prefix-free machine \(U_K\).  Whenever a
previously unseen program \(p\) halts, add \(2^{-\operatorname{len}(p)}\) to
the current rational sum.  This is a computable nondecreasing sequence, and
its supremum is \(\Omega_{U_K}\); hence the real is left-c.e.

The halting domain of \(U_K\) is undecidable.  Otherwise, on input \(x\),
inspect programs in increasing length, use the alleged decider to discard the
nonhalting ones, run the remaining finite set, and stop at the first length
whose outputs include \(x\).  Universality guarantees eventual success, and
this computes \(K_{U_K}(x)\), contradicting
Theorem~\ref{thm:p04-kolmogorov-noncomputable}.

Suppose \(\Omega_{U_K}\) were computable.  Given \(n\), compute a rational
\(q<\Omega_{U_K}\) with
\(\Omega_{U_K}-q<2^{-(n+1)}\), and continue the dovetailing until its partial
sum exceeds \(q\).  If an as-yet unseen program of length at most \(n\) later
halted, it would add at least \(2^{-n}\), making the final sum larger than
\(\Omega_{U_K}\).  Thus at that stage the observed list decides the halting
of every program of length at most \(n\), contradicting undecidability of the
universal machine's halting domain.  Therefore \(\Omega_{U_K}\) is
noncomputable and, in particular, is not a dyadic rational.

Now suppose the first \(n\) digits of its unique nonterminating binary
expansion are given, and let \(q_n\) be the corresponding lower truncation.
Then
\(q_n<\Omega_{U_K}<q_n+2^{-n}\).  Continue the partial sums until one exceeds
\(q_n\), which eventually happens.  Any unseen halting program of length at
most \(n\) would contribute at least \(2^{-n}\) and push the sum beyond the
upper endpoint.  Hence precisely the programs of length at most \(n\) already
seen to halt will ever halt, proving the finite-bit determination claim.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}
