## SRC-01899-01945

The commentary on the final row of the necessary-and-sufficient-conditions example stated the "unless" rule as "P unless R is neg R -> P" and then said that R "is 'the checksum is invalid,' namely neg Q". Under the convention fixed in the propositional-language definition (handbook/restoration/inherited-01661-01816.tex: P, Q, R, S denote specified members of Prop, while phi, psi, chi are metavariables over formulas), R is a propositional variable and cannot be the compound formula neg Q, so the passage was false as written, and the rule read literally yields neg R -> P rather than the tabulated neg neg Q -> P. The corrected text states the rule with the formula metavariables: if phi and psi translate two clauses, "phi unless psi" is translated by neg psi -> phi; it adds the check, immediate from the truth clauses for neg and ->, that this formula is false exactly when phi and psi are both false (so "unless" is read inclusively); and it instantiates phi to P and psi to the compound formula neg Q, since the key gives "the checksum is invalid" no variable of its own. The tabulated formula neg neg Q -> P, the remark that simplifying to Q -> P is a separate classical equivalence step, the table rows, the "only if" commentary and the exclusive-disjunction example are unchanged.
