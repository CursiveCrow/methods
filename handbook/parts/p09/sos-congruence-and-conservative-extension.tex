\section{SOS congruence formats and conservative extension}
\label{sec:p09-sos-formats}

Existence of a transition relation and preservation of bisimulation by
operators are different claims. This section continues the standard
negative-premise/predicate language of
Definition~\ref{def:p09-np-language}. It treats the actual first-order
conservative-extension criterion of the separate author SOS presentation,
\S4.1--4.2, and a fully proved local-input congruence theorem containing
its nonrecursive GSOS class \parencite{AcetoFokkinkVerhoefSOSAuthorNP}.
The general panth theorem is kept distinct from this selected class.

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p09-sos-panth}{The literal source formats and their distinct scope}
The source's panth format requires: every positive action-premise target
is a single variable; the conclusion source contains at most one function
symbol; and variables in that source and positive-premise targets are
all pairwise distinct. Positive-premise sources may be arbitrary terms:
lookahead is not forbidden by panth. The path subformat has no negative
premises, ntyft/ntyxt has no predicates, and tyft/tyxt has neither.
An ntree rule is panth, has no variables outside the conclusion source
and positive-premise targets, has a well-founded backwards variable
dependency graph, and has single-variable positive-premise sources.

The actual GSOS format of source Definition~5.21 instead tests only
input variables \(x_i\). Its finitely many positive action tests have
distinct targets \(y_{ij}\), fresh from all \(x_i\); negatives
are target-independent action absence on \(x_i\). These are the
only rule variables. The conclusion source is
\(f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\), and its target is any finite term over
these variables, possibly copying them. A finitary GSOS language has
finite signature, action set and rule set. It has no recursion binder
in this definition. Its format is displayed on the registered sheet.
\end{hbdefinition}

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p09-sos-local}{An explicit predicate-bearing local-input subclass}
For the selected congruence proof allow set-sized signatures, actions,
predicates and rule sets, with finite operator arities and finite terms.
Each rule has source \(f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\) with distinct inputs.
Its premises test only these inputs: positive actions
\(x_i\xrightarrow a y\), positive predicates \(x_iP\),
standard negatives \(x_i\mathrel{\not\!\xrightarrow b}\)
or \(x_i\neg P\). All action-target variables are distinct and
fresh from the inputs. No other variables occur. The conclusion is
an action to a term over those variables, or a predicate on the same
source. Premise sets may be arbitrary sets; no target-specific absence
or lookahead is added. Call this the local-input class \(\mathsf{LI}\).
It is an explicitly declared subclass of panth, not another name for
all panth or for the source's finitary, predicate-free GSOS language.
The latter is included as its literal special case.
\end{hbdefinition}

\begin{hblemma}{thm:p09-sos-local-semantics}{proved=annex:p09-sos-local-semantics}{The exact semantic prerequisite for local rules}
Source-node count is a strict ordinal stratification for every
\(\mathsf{LI}\) TSS. Hence its associated relation is the unique
supported and stable model and the total well-founded interpretation.
This includes arbitrary premise sets with the regular bound already
proved for the TSS construction; it does not assume finite branching.
\end{hblemma}

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p09-sos-bisimulation}{Predicate-respecting strong bisimulation}
For the associated relation, \(R\subseteq C\times C\) is a
bisimulation when each related pair agrees on every predicate and
each action move on either side has a same-labelled matching move
whose endpoints are again related. Write \(\sim\) for the union
of all such relations. Predicate agreement is essential for the source
termination and panth examples; it is not discarded by considering
the action relation alone.
\end{hbdefinition}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p09-sos-congruence}{proved=annex:p09-sos-congruence}{Full congruence proof for the selected local-input class}
For every \(\mathsf{LI}\) TSS, \(\sim\) is an equivalence and
a congruence for every operator. In particular this proves the actual
nonrecursive finitary GSOS congruence conclusion, with all its source
variable conditions retained. The proof constructs the compatible
closure, proves substitution transport, and proves that closure itself
a bisimulation, including both directions of every absence test.
It is not a theorem for arbitrary lookahead or recursive rule binders.
\end{hbtheorem}

\subsection{The source conservative-extension criterion}

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p09-sos-conservative}{Source dependency, freshness and proof-level conservativity}
Let \(T_0,T_1\) be standard first-order TSSs with signatures
\(\Sigma_0,\Sigma_1\) agreeing on arities in their intersection.
Their sum has the union signature and union rules. Action/predicate
names are considered in a common ambient universe. A term is fresh
when it contains a function name in \(\Sigma_1\setminus\Sigma_0\);
an action or predicate is fresh when it does not occur in \(T_0\).
Let \(C_i\) be the old and union closed-term sets.

In a rule, variables in its conclusion source are source-dependent.
Whenever every variable of a positive action-premise source is already
source-dependent, all variables of that premise's target become
source-dependent. Take the least such closure. A rule is source-dependent
when every variable occurring anywhere in it belongs to this closure.
Predicate and negative premises do not introduce target variables.

Proof-level operational conservativity means that any ordinary proof
\(T_0\oplus T_1\vdash N/\alpha\), with only negative hypotheses
\(N\) and old source in \(\alpha\), can already be made from
\(T_0\). Unused hypotheses may be retained in the common ambient
language; the proof below shows that every used hypothesis is old.
This is stronger than merely checking a chosen list of old transitions.
\end{hbdefinition}

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p09-sos-extension-conditions}{Both alternatives in source Theorem~4.4}
Assume every old rule is source-dependent. Every new rule must satisfy
at least one of these two conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item its conclusion source is fresh;
\item it has a positive premise \(t\xrightarrow a t'\) or
      \(tP\), with \(t\) a term over \(\Sigma_0\), every
      variable of \(t\) occurring in the rule's conclusion source,
      and at least one of \(t',a,P\), as applicable, fresh.
\end{enumerate}
No GSOS restriction, completeness assumption, finiteness or premise
dependency well-foundedness is silently imposed on this theorem.
Terms are free first-order terms, not substitution harnesses or
binding terms modulo alpha-conversion. The latter belong to the
distinct larger dependency report
\parencite{FokkinkVerhoef1995SOSBindingReport}.
\end{hbdefinition}

\begin{hblemma}{thm:p09-sos-old-proof}{proved=annex:p09-sos-old-proof}{The actual proof-height and source-dependency mechanism}
Under these conditions, every ordinary proof under negative hypotheses
with an old conclusion source uses only old rules. Its action targets,
all terms in its used premises, and all rule-variable values are old;
its conclusion's action/predicate occurs in \(T_0\). The proof handles
both freshness alternatives and arbitrary well-founded proof trees.
\end{hblemma}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p09-sos-conservative}{proved=annex:p09-sos-conservative}{The full first-order operational conservative-extension criterion}
The exact conditions above imply proof-level operational conservativity
of \(T_0\oplus T_1\) over \(T_0\), in both directions of
provability for old-source conclusions. Source dependency and the
negative-only hypothesis requirement cannot simply be dropped.
\end{hbtheorem}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p09-sos-models}{proved=annex:p09-sos-models}{Projection, lifting and the least three-valued relation}
For any operationally conservative sum in the preceding proof-level
sense, every three-valued stable pair of the extension restricts on old
sources to a three-valued stable pair of \(T_0\). Conversely every
old pair has at least one extension pair restricting to it. The
least well-founded pair of the extension restricts to the old least
pair. No new target, action or predicate can become grounded at an
old source. This supplies the constructions behind source
Propositions 4.5--4.6, not just their names.
\end{hbtheorem}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p09-sos-failures}{proved=annex:p09-sos-failures}{Discriminating failures of the omitted conditions}
The sheet's shared-target rule destroys congruence although its
input processes are bisimilar and its TSS has a unique positive
grounded meaning. The source pattern test \(\zeta(0)\) distinguishes
the bisimilar stopped terms \(0\) and \(0+0\)
\parencite[\S6]{BloomIstrailMeyer2003GSOSAuthor}.
The actual source extension \(xP/aP\) plus the fresh axiom \(bP\)
is not conservative without source dependency. Allowing positive
hypotheses also invalidates the claimed proof conservativity, even
under the two freshness alternatives. The annex gives actual rule
instances and distinguishing transitions for all four cases.
\end{hbtheorem}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p09-sos-applications}{proved=annex:p09-sos-applications}{The actual BPA, priority, time and silent-step applications}
All source BPA rules are source-dependent. Its separate priority,
the precise Table~3 discrete-time extension, and the source silent-step
extension satisfy the full conservative-extension criterion. The
local-input congruence theorem applies to BPA, priority and discrete
time, including their successful-termination predicates and negative
guards. These are the exact source rule sets, not every later version
of a similarly named timed or recursive language.
\end{hbtheorem}

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p09-sos-panth-example}{The actual completeness counterexample from source p.44}
The registered panth example has constants \(a,b\), unary \(f\),
predicates \(P,Q_1,Q_2\), the two \(P\)-axioms and the two
source predicate rules. It has no action transitions. There are
infinitely many closed terms, but only the displayed two base
\(P\)-judgements can be grounded.
\end{hbdefinition}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p09-sos-panth-example}{proved=annex:p09-sos-panth-example}{Unique stability cannot replace completeness in the panth theorem}
This actual source TSS is panth and has the unique stable model
\(\{aP,bP,f(a)Q_1,f(b)Q_2\}\). Its least three-valued pair
has the two base \(P\)-judgements certain and all four
\(f(a)Q_i,f(b)Q_i\) unknown, so it is not complete.
In that unique total stable model, \(a\sim b\) but
\(f(a)\not\sim f(b)\). Thus the general source theorem's
\(ws\)-completeness assumption is not replaceable by unique stability.
\end{hbtheorem}

\begin{proofboundary}{def:p09-sos-general-panth}{The remaining general-format and recursion boundary}
Source Theorem~5.3 states congruence for every complete panth TSS.
It permits lookahead and cases outside the local-input subclass.
Its proof uses the source Theorem~5.6 panth-to-ntree transformation
and further general congruence arguments; those constructions are
not established by the local proof above. The source explicitly
removed an older premise-well-foundedness requirement via that
transformation; it must not be reimposed to call the general theorem
proved. De Simone/GSOS recursion, binding and many-sorted report
variants also require their separate semantics and proofs. The
1995 binding report, July 2003 GSOS author file, 133-page author
chapter and unread 2001 publisher chapter remain distinct manifestations.
\end{proofboundary}
