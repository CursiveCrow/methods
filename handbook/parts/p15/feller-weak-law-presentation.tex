\chapter{Feller's Symmetrization Proof of the Weak Law}
\label{sec:p15-feller-weak-law-presentation}

Feller's generalized weak law uses a median to compare the tails of a
distribution with those of its symmetrization. Its principal estimate
comes from the largest summand and the sign symmetry of the remaining
sum. This is a different source route from the first-crossing proof in
Chapter~\ref{sec:p15-weak-laws-and-triangular-arrays}.

The presentation is Feller, Volume II, second edition: the median
definition in V.2, the definitions V.(5.1)--(5.3), the atomic
formulas V.(5.4)--(5.5) with the Bessel function II.(7.1), and
inequalities V.(5.6)--(5.10),
the integration-by-parts argument V.(6.1), VII.7 through Theorem 1 and
the remarks following it, and the converse VII.8, Theorem 4
\cite[pp.~58, 137, 148--150, 234--236, 241]{Feller1971ProbabilityII}.
Every premise used by the median chain is proved below. Endpoint
conventions are explicit for atomic laws.

\section{Medians, reflection and symmetrization}
\label{sec:p15-feller-median-symmetrization}

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p15-feller-law-conventions}{Feller's distribution notation and medians}
Let \(X\) be a real random variable on
\((\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)\). Write
\(F(x)=\mathbb P(X\leq x)\) for its right-continuous CDF,
and \(F\{dx\}\) for integration against its associated Borel
probability measure. In particular
\(F(x-)=\mathbb P(X<x)\).
A median is a finite real \(m\) such that
\[
 F(m)\geq\tfrac12,\qquad F(m-)\leq\tfrac12,
\]
equivalently \(\mathbb P(X\leq m)\geq1/2\) and
\(\mathbb P(X\geq m)\geq1/2\).

Following the source, \(-F\) denotes the law of \(-X\); it is
not the pointwise negative of a CDF. For independent copies \(X,X'\)
of the same law, put
\({}^{\circ}X=X-X'\), and let \({}^{\circ}F\) be its CDF.
The operation is called symmetrization. A law is symmetric about zero
when the laws of \(X\) and \(-X\) agree.
\index{median}\index{symmetrization}
\end{hbdefinition}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p15-feller-symmetrization-structure}{proved=annex:p15.feller-symmetrization-structure}{Medians and the exact reflected and symmetrized laws}
Every real probability law has a median, which need not be unique.
Centering \(X\) by a median makes zero a median.
For every real \(x\),
\[
 (-F)(x)=1-F((-x)-),\qquad
 {}^{\circ}F(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}F(x+y)\,F\{dy\}.
\]
The symmetrized law is \(F\star(-F)\), the convolution of the
two indicated laws, and is symmetric:
\[
 {}^{\circ}F(x)=1-{}^{\circ}F((-x)-).
\]
The source formulas without the left-limit sign apply at the
corresponding continuity points.
Independent copies of an entire sequence can be constructed on the
finite product of its original probability space with itself. If the
original sequence is independent, its copied differences are independent;
if it is also identically distributed, so are the differences.
Symmetrization is unchanged by any deterministic translation.
\end{hbtheorem}

Feller's median paragraph adds that a median need not be unique: when
\(F(x)=\tfrac12\) at every \(x\) of an interval with endpoints \(a<b\),
every such \(x\) is a median
\cite[V.2, p.~137]{Feller1971ProbabilityII}. In Feller's notation an
interval written with a bar over \(a,b\) is the open interval
\(\{a<x<b\}\) \cite[I.2, p.~4]{Feller1971ProbabilityII}. The next result
proves that remark, extends it to the two endpoints, and shows that it
describes every failure of uniqueness.

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p15-w02c-p15-015-median-set}{proved=annex:p15-w02c-p15-015-median-set}{The set of medians}
Let \(F\) be a CDF and put
\[
 m_-=\inf\{x:F(x)\geq\tfrac12\},\qquad
 m_+=\sup\{x:F(x-)\leq\tfrac12\}.
\]
Both are finite, \(m_-\leq m_+\), and the set of medians of \(F\) is
the closed interval \([m_-,m_+]\). If \(m_-<m_+\), then
\(F(x)=\tfrac12\) for every \(x\in[m_-,m_+)\).
Conversely, if \(a<b\) and \(F(x)=\tfrac12\) for every
\(x\in(a,b)\), then every point of the closed interval \([a,b]\) is a
median. Hence the median is unique exactly when \(F\) takes the value
\(\tfrac12\) on no open interval.
\end{hbtheorem}

For the fair law on \(\{-1,1\}\), \(F=\tfrac12\) on \([-1,1)\) and the
median set is \([-1,1]\), as recorded in the annex proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:p15-feller-symmetrization-structure}. For a law with a
density that is strictly positive at every point, \(F\) is strictly
increasing and the median is unique; the annex proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:p15-w02c-p15-015-median-set} includes this case.

Feller defines symmetry by the identity \(-F=F\) of laws and adds, in
brackets, that when a density \(f\) exists this means \(f(-x)=f(x)\)
\cite[V.5, p.~148]{Feller1971ProbabilityII}. A density is determined
only up to a Lebesgue-null set, so the bracketed form holds for a
suitable choice of density, not for every choice.

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p15-w02c-p15-015-density-symmetry}{proved=annex:p15-w02c-p15-015-density-symmetry}{Symmetry of a law with a density}
Let \(X\) have a Lebesgue density \(f\), so that
\(\mathbb P(X\in B)=\int_Bf(x)\,dx\) for every Borel set \(B\).
Then \(-X\) has the density \(x\mapsto f(-x)\). The following are
equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the law of \(X\) is symmetric, that is, \(-F=F\);
\item \(f(-x)=f(x)\) for Lebesgue-almost every real \(x\);
\item \(F\) has an even density \(g\), that is, \(g(-x)=g(x)\) for every
real \(x\) and \(g=f\) almost everywhere.
\end{enumerate}
One such even density is \(g(x)=\tfrac12[f(x)+f(-x)]\). The pointwise
identity \(f(-x)=f(x)\) at every \(x\) need not hold for a given density
of a symmetric law: \(f=\tfrac12\mathbf1_{[-1,1)}\) is a density of the
uniform law on \([-1,1]\), which is symmetric, yet \(f(-1)=\tfrac12\) and
\(f(1)=0\).
\end{hbtheorem}

Feller motivates the next inequalities with a methodological claim:
passing to the symmetrized law spares many untidy arguments, and for
that purpose what matters is that the tails of \(F\) and of
\({}^{\circ}F\) have comparable size. Lemma~1 of V.5, inequalities
(5.6)--(5.8), makes this comparison precise
\cite[V.5, p.~149]{Feller1971ProbabilityII}. The upper bound shows that
the symmetrized tail is at most twice a tail of \(F\) at half the
level; the lower bounds show that it is at least a fixed fraction of a
tail of \(F\) at a shifted level. The weak-law necessity proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:p15-feller-generalized-weak-law} is an instance of
the claim: a statement about \(S_n\) is moved to the symmetrized sum,
proved there, and moved back through these inequalities.

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p15-feller-symmetrization-inequalities}{proved=annex:p15.feller-symmetrization-inequalities}{The complete tail comparisons V.(5.6)--(5.8)}
For independent identically distributed real \(X,X'\) and \(t>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P(|X-X'|>t)\leq2\mathbb P(|X|>t/2).
\]
If \(a\geq0\), \(p\in[0,1]\), and
\(\mathbb P(X\leq a)\geq p\),
\(\mathbb P(X\geq-a)\geq p\), then
\[
 \mathbb P(|X-X'|>t)\geq p\,\mathbb P(|X|>t+a).
\]
In particular, if zero is a median, the right side can be
\(\tfrac12\mathbb P(|X|>t)\).
For an arbitrary median \(m\),
\[
 \mathbb P(|X-X'|>t)
           \geq\tfrac12\mathbb P(|X|>t+|m|).
\]
For every finite real \(b\) the upper bound may also be written
\(\mathbb P(|X-X'|>2t)\leq2\mathbb P(|X-b|>t)\).
None of these statements assumes that an expectation exists.
\end{hbtheorem}

\section{Feller's largest-summand argument}
\label{sec:p15-feller-largest-summand}

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p15-feller-largest-term}{The first largest absolute summand}
For independent symmetric real \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n\), \(n\geq1\),
write \(S=\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\), and define
\[
 J=\min\{j:|Y_j|=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|Y_i|\},\qquad
 M=Y_J,\qquad R=S-M.
\]
The first index resolves every tie, including the case in which all
summands vanish. Both \(M\) and \(R\) are measurable. Their roles
are a selected signed summand and the sum of the others; they are
not assumed independent.
\end{hbdefinition}

Feller introduces Lemma~2 of V.5, inequalities (5.9)--(5.10), by
observing that symmetrization is often used to estimate sums of
independent random variables, and he names this inequality as an
especially useful one for that work
\cite[V.5, p.~149]{Feller1971ProbabilityII}. Its use here is the
second step of the necessity chain in
Theorem~\ref{thm:p15-feller-generalized-weak-law}: after
symmetrization, the probability that the symmetrized sum is large is
bounded below by the probability that a single symmetrized summand is
large.

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p15-feller-largest-term-inequality}{proved=annex:p15.feller-largest-term-inequality}{The source inequalities V.(5.9)--(5.10), including atoms}
Under the independent symmetric-summand hypotheses of
Definition~\ref{def:p15-feller-largest-term},
the four pairs \((M,R),(-M,R),(M,-R),(-M,-R)\) have the same
law. For every \(t>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P(|S|>t)\geq\tfrac12
                    \mathbb P(\max_i|Y_i|>t),\qquad
 \mathbb P(|S|\geq t)\geq\tfrac12
                    \mathbb P(\max_i|Y_i|\geq t).
\]
If the \(Y_i\) have a common CDF \(G\), put
\(q_>(t)=\mathbb P(|Y_1|>t)\) and
\(q_\geq(t)=\mathbb P(|Y_1|\geq t)\). Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb P(|S|>t)&\geq\tfrac12[1-(1-q_>(t))^n]
                         \geq\tfrac12[1-e^{-nq_>(t)}],\\
 \mathbb P(|S|\geq t)&\geq\tfrac12[1-(1-q_\geq(t))^n]
                         \geq\tfrac12[1-e^{-nq_\geq(t)}].
 \end{aligned}
\]
In particular, the exact source CDF form is valid at every \(t>0\):
\[
 \mathbb P(|S|\geq t)
       \geq\tfrac12\left(1-e^{-n[1-G(t)+G(-t)]}\right).
\]
The strict-tail formula in the preceding display is the version used
in the weak-law necessity chain.
\end{hbtheorem}

\section{The truncated moment identity}
\label{sec:p15-feller-truncated-moment}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p15-feller-integration-by-parts}{proved=annex:p15.feller-integration-by-parts}{The source finite-interval Stieltjes formula}
Let \(a<b\) be finite, let \(u\) be continuously differentiable
on \([a,b]\), and let \(F\) be a CDF. Then
\[
 \int_{(a,b]}u(x)\,F\{dx\}
 =u(b)F(b)-u(a)F(a)-\int_a^b u'(x)F(x)\,dx.
\]
The first integral excludes \(a\) and includes \(b\); the last
integral is with respect to Borel Lebesgue measure. All terms are
finite. The annex supplies the source's partition argument, including
the finite summation identity and both approximation errors.
\end{hbtheorem}

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p15-feller-tail-moment-functions}{The source tail quantity and explicit endpoint conventions}
For the common law \(F\), and a real \(t>0\), put
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \tau(t)&=t[1-F(t)+F(-t)]
       =t[\mathbb P(X>t)+\mathbb P(X\leq-t)],\\
 \sigma(t)&=\frac1t\int_{[-t,t]}x^2\,F\{dx\},&
 \sigma_h(t)&=\frac1t\int_{(-t,t]}x^2\,F\{dx\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The closed version \(\sigma\) corresponds to the source's deletion
truncation \(X\mathbf1_{\{|X|\leq t\}}\); \(\sigma_h\) records
the half-open Stieltjes interval. Define the corresponding first
truncated moments by
\[
 m(t)=\int_{[-t,t]}x\,F\{dx\},\qquad
 m_h(t)=\int_{(-t,t]}x\,F\{dx\}.
\]
All four truncated integrals are finite without a moment assumption
on the original variable. Set \(\tau(0)=0\) for the ordinary
Lebesgue integrals beginning at zero.
\end{hbdefinition}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p15-feller-tail-moment-equivalence}{proved=annex:p15.feller-tail-moment-equivalence}{Exact moment identities and equivalent vanishing conditions}
For every \(t>0\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sigma_h(t)&=-\tau(t)+\frac2t\int_0^t\tau(y)\,dy,\\
 \sigma(t)&=\sigma_h(t)+t\,\mathbb P(X=-t),\\
 m(t)&=m_h(t)-t\,\mathbb P(X=-t).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Consequently the following four conditions are equivalent:
\[
 \tau(t)\to0,\qquad
 t\mathbb P(|X|>t)\to0,\qquad
 \sigma(t)\to0,\qquad
 \sigma_h(t)\to0
                         \quad(t\to\infty).
\]
The atom terms vanish in the identities when \(F\) is continuous at
\(-t\). The proof of the converse from the truncated moment uses an
explicit dyadic tail estimate; it is not left as a source referral.
\end{hbtheorem}

\section{The full source weak-law chain}
\label{sec:p15-feller-generalized-weak-law}

Feller introduces the truncation (7.2) at an arbitrary fixed level,
the truncated sum and its mean (7.3), and the comparison (7.4) of the
untruncated deviation with the truncated one. He remarks that (7.4)
remains valid for dependent variables with varying distributions,
although his use of it is for independent identically distributed
summands \cite[VII.7, pp.~234--235]{Feller1971ProbabilityII}. The
general form is stated first; the i.i.d.\ Chebyshev bound is its
specialization.

\begin{hblemma}{thm:p15-w02c-p15-015-general-truncation}{proved=annex:p15-w02c-p15-015-general-truncation}{Feller's comparison (7.4) without independence or common laws}
Let \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\) be real random variables on one probability
space, with arbitrary joint law: they need not be independent, and
their laws may differ. Let \(s>0\) be a fixed level and put
\[
 X_k'=X_k\mathbf1_{\{|X_k|\leq s\}},\quad
 S_n=\sum_{k=1}^nX_k,\quad S_n'=\sum_{k=1}^nX_k',\quad
 m_n'=\mathbb E(S_n').
\]
Then \(m_n'\) is finite, \(|m_n'|\leq ns\), and for every real \(t\),
\[
 \mathbb P(|S_n-m_n'|>t)\leq\mathbb P(|S_n'-m_n'|>t)
                       +\mathbb P(S_n\ne S_n'),
\]
with
\[
 \mathbb P(S_n\ne S_n')\leq\mathbb P\Bigl(\bigcup_{k=1}^n\{|X_k|>s\}\Bigr)
                     \leq\sum_{k=1}^n\mathbb P(|X_k|>s).
\]
For \(t>0\), Chebyshev's inequality turns this into
\[
 \mathbb P(|S_n-m_n'|>t)\leq
 \frac{1}{t^2}\sum_{j=1}^n\sum_{k=1}^n
       \operatorname{Cov}(X_j',X_k')
 +\sum_{k=1}^n\mathbb P(|X_k|>s),
\]
where every covariance is finite because \(|X_k'|\leq s\).
\end{hblemma}

\begin{hbexample}{def:p15-w02c-p15-015-dependent-truncation}{Complete dependence: the second error term cannot be dropped}
Let \(X\) take the value \(2\) with probability \(p\in(0,1)\) and
\(0\) otherwise, and let \(X_1=\cdots=X_n=X\). With \(s=1\), every
\(X_k'=0\), so \(S_n'=0\) and \(m_n'=0\), while \(S_n=nX\). For
\(0\leq t<2n\),
\[
 \mathbb P(|S_n-m_n'|>t)=p,\qquad
 \mathbb P(|S_n'-m_n'|>t)=0,\qquad
 \mathbb P(S_n\ne S_n')=p.
\]
Thus the comparison holds with equality, and the event
\(\{S_n\ne S_n'\}\) carries the whole deviation. The union bound gives
\(\sum_k\mathbb P(|X_k|>1)=np\), which exceeds the exact value \(p\) by
the factor \(n\): under dependence the event
\(\bigcup_k\{|X_k|>s\}\) can be much smaller than the sum of the
individual probabilities. The variance term can likewise lose the
\(n^{-1}\) scale it has for i.i.d.\ summands: with the level \(s=3\)
instead, \(X_k'=X\) for every \(k\), and the double sum of covariances
equals \(n^2\operatorname{Var}(X)=4n^2p(1-p)\), so at \(t=n\varepsilon\)
the Chebyshev term is \(4p(1-p)/\varepsilon^2\) for every \(n\).
\end{hbexample}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p15-feller-truncation-inequality}{proved=annex:p15.feller-truncation-inequality}{The source truncation and Chebyshev estimate}
Let \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\) be independent with common real law \(F\),
let \(s>0\), and put
\[
 X_k'=X_k\mathbf1_{\{|X_k|\leq s\}},\quad
 S_n=\sum_{k=1}^nX_k,\quad S_n'=\sum_{k=1}^nX_k',
 \quad m_n'=n\,m(s).
\]
For every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P(|S_n/n-m(s)|>\varepsilon)
 \leq\frac{\mathbb E[(X_1')^2]}{n\varepsilon^2}
                         +n\mathbb P(|X_1|>s).
\]
The center is the finite truncated mean, not an assumed expectation
of \(X_1\). The variance contribution has the \(n^{-1}\) scale.
It is the specialization of
Lemma~\ref{thm:p15-w02c-p15-015-general-truncation} with
\(t=n\varepsilon\) to independent summands with a common law. Feller's
Lemma (7.5) prints the coefficient \(1/(n^2x^2)\) in front of
\(\mathbb E(X_1'^2)\) \cite[VII.7, p.~235]{Feller1971ProbabilityII};
the Chebyshev step yields \(1/(nx^2)\), and item~5 of
Theorem~\ref{thm:p15-feller-proof-boundaries} shows that the printed
coefficient gives a false bound.
\end{hbtheorem}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p15-feller-generalized-weak-law}{proved=annex:p15.feller-generalized-weak-law}{Feller VII.7, Theorem 1: necessity and sufficiency}
Let \(X_1,X_2,\ldots\) be i.i.d. real random variables with law
\(F\), and write \(S_n=\sum_{k=1}^nX_k\). Real deterministic
centers \(\mu_n\) satisfying
\[
 \mathbb P(|S_n/n-\mu_n|>\varepsilon)\longrightarrow0
                           \quad\text{for every }\varepsilon>0
\]
exist if and only if \(\tau(t)\to0\) as \(t\to\infty\).
When the condition holds, one may choose
\(\mu_n=m(n)=\mathbb E[X_1\mathbf1_{\{|X_1|\leq n\}}]\);
the half-open centers \(m_h(n)\) work as well.

For necessity, let \(a\) be any median of the common law, and let
\({}^{\circ}S_n\) be the sum of its independent copied differences,
with product probability denoted by \(\mathbb Q\). The complete source chain is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 2\mathbb P(|S_n-n\mu_n|>n\varepsilon)
 &\geq\mathbb Q(|{}^{\circ}S_n|>2n\varepsilon)\\
 &\geq\tfrac12\left(1-e^{-n\mathbb Q(|{}^{\circ}X_1|>2n\varepsilon)}\right)\\
 &\geq\tfrac12\left(1-e^{-(n/2)\mathbb P(|X_1|>2n\varepsilon+|a|)}\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Probabilities of copied quantities in this display are taken in their
constructed product space. The annex supplies that construction and
every step connecting the chain to \(\tau(t)\to0\).
\end{hbtheorem}

\begin{hbcorollary}{thm:p15-feller-centering-corollaries}{proved=annex:p15.feller-centering-corollaries}{All centers, finite means and symmetric laws without a mean}
Under \(\tau(t)\to0\), a deterministic sequence \(c_n\) centers
\(S_n/n\) to zero in probability exactly when \(c_n-m(n)\to0\).
If \(\mathbb E|X_1|<\infty\), then
\(\tau(t)\to0\), \(m(n)\to\mathbb EX_1\), and
\(S_n/n\to\mathbb EX_1\) in probability.
If \(F\) is symmetric, every \(m(n)=0\); hence \(\tau(t)\to0\)
gives \(S_n/n\to0\) in probability even when the first absolute
moment is infinite.
\end{hbcorollary}

Feller places these statements in their history
\cite[VII.7, pp.~234--235]{Feller1971ProbabilityII}. A footnote to the
heading of VII.7 explains why the section is there at all: its topics
belong to the oldest part of probability theory and are of no special
consequence for the rest of his book, and he includes them for their
historical and methodological interest and because a large literature
concerns partial converses of the law of large numbers
\cite[VII.7, p.~234, footnote]{Feller1971ProbabilityII}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:p15-feller-generalized-weak-law} is itself such a
converse: its necessity half shows that convergence in probability of
centered averages forces \(\tau(t)\to0\). He describes the
finite-variance statement (7.1), that
\(\mathbb P(|n^{-1}S_n-\mu|>\varepsilon)\to0\) for each fixed
\(\varepsilon>0\) when the i.i.d.\ summands have expectation \(\mu\)
and finite variance, as the oldest version of the law of large
numbers, and notes that it is contained in Chebyshev's inequality
(p.~234). He calls the statement that (7.1) holds for every
\(\varepsilon>0\) as soon as the expectation \(\mu\) is finite
\emph{Khintchine's law of large numbers}; he does not prove it
separately, remarking that its proof would essentially repeat the
discrete-case proof of his first volume, and passes directly to the
generalized law of Theorem~\ref{thm:p15-feller-generalized-weak-law}
(p.~235). In this chapter Khintchine's law is the finite-mean clause of
Corollary~\ref{thm:p15-feller-centering-corollaries}, which contains the
finite-variance version because a finite second moment forces a finite
first absolute moment: \(\mathbb E|X_1|\leq(\mathbb EX_1^2)^{1/2}\) by
the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality.

After the proof of Theorem~1, Feller makes two further claims
\cite[VII.7, p.~236]{Feller1971ProbabilityII}. First, the weak law
reaches beyond variables with an expectation: for a symmetric \(F\) with
\(t[1-F(t)]\to0\) the averages \(n^{-1}S_n\) tend to zero in
probability, whereas an expectation exists only when \(1-F(t)\) is
integrable over \((0,\infty)\), and that is a stronger requirement.
Second, in a parenthetical remark, the strong law holds only for
variables that have an expectation; he refers to Theorem~4 of VII.8,
the converse to the strong law
\cite[VII.8, Theorem~4, p.~241]{Feller1971ProbabilityII}. The next two
results state both claims exactly and prove them.

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p15-w02c-p15-015-mean-versus-tau}{proved=annex:p15-w02c-p15-015-mean-versus-tau}{An expectation requires an integrable tail, which is stronger than \(\tau(t)\to0\)}
Let \(X\) be a real random variable with CDF \(F\). In \([0,\infty]\),
\[
 \mathbb EX^+=\int_0^\infty[1-F(t)]\,dt,\qquad
 \mathbb EX^-=\int_0^\infty F(-t)\,dt,\qquad
 \mathbb E|X|=\int_0^\infty\frac{\tau(t)}{t}\,dt,
\]
with \(\tau\) as in Definition~\ref{def:p15-feller-tail-moment-functions}.
If \(F\) is symmetric, then \(1-F(t)=F((-t)-)\) for every \(t\), and
\(\mathbb E|X|=2\int_0^\infty[1-F(t)]\,dt\); so \(X\) has an
expectation exactly when \(1-F\) is integrable over \((0,\infty)\).
For symmetric \(F\) also \(\mathbb P(|X|>t)=2[1-F(t)]\) for every
\(t>0\), so \(t[1-F(t)]\to0\) holds exactly when \(\tau(t)\to0\);
under either condition, for i.i.d.\ summands with law \(F\),
\(n^{-1}S_n\to0\) in probability. This is Feller's first claim in the
form he states it.
For every \(F\), finiteness of \(\mathbb E|X|\) implies
\(\tau(t)\to0\) and \(t\,\mathbb P(|X|>t)\to0\). The converse
implication fails: the symmetric law of item~6 of
Theorem~\ref{thm:p15-feller-proof-boundaries} has \(\tau(t)\to0\) and
\(\int_0^\infty[1-F(t)]\,dt=\infty\). Hence for symmetric laws
integrability of \(1-F\) is strictly stronger than
\(t[1-F(t)]\to0\), and by
Corollary~\ref{thm:p15-feller-centering-corollaries} the weak law
\(n^{-1}S_n\to0\) in probability holds for that law although it has no
expectation.
\end{hbtheorem}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p15-w02c-p15-015-strong-law-converse}{proved=annex:p15-w02c-p15-015-strong-law-converse}{Feller VII.8, Theorem~4: the converse to the strong law}
Let \(X_1,X_2,\ldots\) be independent with a common real law, and
suppose \(\mathbb E|X_1|=\infty\). Then for every real sequence
\((c_n)\), with probability one,
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|n^{-1}S_n-c_n\right|=\infty.
\]
In particular, no deterministic centering makes \(n^{-1}S_n-c_n\)
converge almost surely to a finite limit, or even stay bounded, on an
event of positive probability; for i.i.d.\ summands an almost-sure law
of large numbers forces \(\mathbb E|X_1|<\infty\). The law of item~6 of
Theorem~\ref{thm:p15-feller-proof-boundaries} therefore obeys the weak
law \(n^{-1}S_n\to0\) in probability but not the strong law. With
\(c_n=0\) the conclusion is also the case \(a_n=n\) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:p15-feller-infinite-mean}.
\end{hbtheorem}

\section{Worked cases and hypothesis boundaries}
\label{sec:p15-feller-worked-cases}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p15-feller-symmetrization-examples}{proved=annex:p15.feller-symmetrization-examples}{The source continuous examples and an atomic calculation}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Symmetrizing an exponential law of rate \(\lambda>0\) gives
the density \((\lambda/2)e^{-\lambda|z|}\).
Symmetrizing the uniform law on \((0,1)\) gives
\((1-|z|)\mathbf1_{\{|z|\leq1\}}\).
\item If \(X\) has probabilities \(1-p,p\) at \(0,1\), where
\(0\leq p\leq1\), then \(X-X'\) has masses
\(p(1-p),p^2+(1-p)^2,p(1-p)\) at \(-1,0,1\), respectively.
In particular its CDF and its left limit at zero differ.
\item The fair law on \(\{-1,1\}\) is symmetric but cannot be
the law of \(X-X'\) for independent identically distributed real
\(X,X'\). Every such copied difference has positive probability
in each neighborhood of zero.
\item For the constant law \(X=-1\),
\[
 \tau(t)=t\mathbf1_{\{0<t\leq1\}},\quad
 \sigma(t)=t^{-1}\mathbf1_{\{t\geq1\}},\quad
 \sigma_h(t)=t^{-1}\mathbf1_{\{t>1\}}.
\]
Thus closed and half-open truncations already differ at one atom.
\end{enumerate}
\end{hbtheorem}

Item~2 is the two-atom case of Feller's Example V.5(c), which treats
every law carried by the nonnegative integers
\cite[V.5, p.~149]{Feller1971ProbabilityII}. His Poisson formula uses
the Bessel function of order \(p>-1\) defined in II.(7.1) by
\[
 I_p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{1}{k!\,\Gamma(k+p+1)}
          \left(\frac x2\right)^{2k+p}
\]
for real \(x\) \cite[II.7, p.~58]{Feller1971ProbabilityII}.

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p15-w02c-p15-015-atomic-symmetrization}{proved=annex:p15-w02c-p15-015-atomic-symmetrization}{Feller's Example V.5(c): formulas (5.4) and (5.5)}
Let \(F\) be atomic with weights \(p_0,p_1,\ldots\) at the points
\(0,1,\ldots\), so \(p_k\geq0\) and \(\sum_{k\geq0}p_k=1\). Then the
symmetrized law \({}^{\circ}F\) is atomic, carried by the integers, and
for every integer \(n\geq0\) each of the points \(\pm n\) carries the
weight
\[
 q_n=\sum_{k=0}^\infty p_kp_{k+n},\qquad q_{-n}=q_n.
\]
At \(n=0\) the two points coincide and the single atom at zero has
weight \(q_0=\sum_kp_k^2\). The weights satisfy
\(q_0+2\sum_{n\geq1}q_n=1\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Poisson law, (5.5).} If
\(p_k=e^{-\alpha}\alpha^k/k!\) with \(\alpha>0\), then for \(n\geq0\)
\[
 q_n=e^{-2\alpha}\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{\alpha^{n+2k}}{k!\,(n+k)!}
    =e^{-2\alpha}I_n(2\alpha).
\]
\item \emph{Geometric law.} If \(p_k=(1-r)r^k\) with \(0<r<1\), then
\(q_n=(1-r)r^n/(1+r)\) for \(n\geq0\).
\item \emph{Two atoms.} If \(p_0=1-p\), \(p_1=p\) and all other
weights vanish, then \(q_0=p^2+(1-p)^2\), \(q_1=p(1-p)\) and
\(q_n=0\) for \(n\geq2\), which is item~2 of
Theorem~\ref{thm:p15-feller-symmetrization-examples}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{hbtheorem}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p15-feller-proof-boundaries}{proved=annex:p15.feller-proof-boundaries}{Sharp constants, atoms and missing assumptions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The constant two in V.(5.6) cannot be decreased uniformly:
for Bernoulli probability \(0<p<1\) and \(0<t<1\), the ratio of the
copied-difference tail to \(\mathbb P(|X|>t/2)\) is \(2(1-p)\).
The factor one half in the zero-median lower bound and in the
largest-term bound is attained by independent fair signs.
\item For independent fair signs \(Y_1\) and the constant \(Y_2=0\),
the remainder \(R\) in the largest-term construction is zero.
Its nonnegative and negative sign events do not have equal probability.
The proof's at-least-half bound still applies.
\item Dependence can invalidate the lower symmetrization bound:
take the same fair Bernoulli variable as both \(X\) and \(X'\).
Dependence can also invalidate the largest-term bound: take
\(Y_2=-Y_1\) for a fair sign. Removing symmetry invalidates that
bound even for independent constants \(Y_1=1,Y_2=-1\).
\item If \(X=1\), zero is not a median and the zero-median lower
bound would be false. For a symmetric fair sign at \(t=1\),
the source CDF form V.(5.10) cannot have its left-hand
non-strict threshold changed to a strict one.
\item For \(n=2\) independent fair signs, cutoff \(s=1\), and
\(\varepsilon=9/10\),
\(\mathbb P(|S_n/n|>\varepsilon)=1/2\).
An \(n^{-2}\) coefficient multiplying the single-summand truncated
second moment would give the false bound \(25/81\).
\item The symmetric law assigning mass
\(1/(2\log2\,j2^j)\) to each of \(\pm2^j\), \(j\geq1\),
has infinite positive and negative part expectations but satisfies
\(\tau(t)\to0\). Its i.i.d. averages tend to zero in probability.
\end{enumerate}
\end{hbtheorem}
