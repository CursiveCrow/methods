\chapter{Definitional Extensions and the Elimination of Defined Symbols}
\label{sec:p02-w02b-p02-018-defined-relation-elimination}

A definitional extension adds a relation symbol \(R\) to a signature
\(\Lang\) together with an axiom \(\delta_R\) saying that \(R\) holds exactly
where a fixed \(\Lang\)-formula \(\varphi\) holds (Example ``Definitions
inside a theory'' in \cref{ch:fol-theories}).
\Cref{thm:definitional-conservativity} shows that every \(\Lang\)-model of
the theory has exactly one expansion satisfying \(\delta_R\), and that the
extended theory proves no new sentence of the old signature.  This chapter
supplies a translation \(\psi\mapsto\psi^{*}\) that removes \(R\) from
\emph{every} formula of the extended signature, with a proof that the
unique expansion satisfies \(\psi\) exactly when the old structure
satisfies \(\psi^{*}\), under every assignment.  It then treats a defined
function or constant symbol \(f\), whose defining formula must take
exactly one value for each argument tuple in every model of the theory:
it states that unique-value premise, proves the existence and uniqueness of
the expansion, a translation \(\psi\mapsto\psi^{\sharp}\) with its
satisfaction theorem, conservativity, and the necessity of the premise, and
extends conservativity to finite sequences of definitions
(\cref{sec:p02-w02b-p02-018-function-symbols}).

\emph{Inputs.}  A theory \(T\) in \(\Lang\), a new \(n\)-ary relation symbol
\(R\), pairwise distinct variables \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\), and an
\(\Lang\)-formula \(\varphi\) whose free variables lie among them.
\emph{Conclusions.}  For every formula \(\psi\) of the extended signature,
an \(\Lang\)-formula \(\psi^{*}\) with no more free variables than \(\psi\)
(\cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-translation-syntax}); agreement of \(\psi\) in
the expansion with \(\psi^{*}\) in the original structure
(\cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-satisfaction-translation}); and the consequence
and derivability forms \(\{\delta_R\}\entails\psi\leftrightarrow\psi^{*}\)
and \(T\cup\{\delta_R\}\entails\psi\) iff \(T\entails\psi^{*}\)
(\cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-consequences}).  For a function symbol \(f\) of
arity \(m\) the inputs are \(T\), pairwise distinct variables
\(x_1,\ldots,x_m,y\), and an \(\Lang\)-formula \(\varphi\) whose free
variables lie among them, such that \(T\) entails the two unique-value
sentences; the conclusions are the corresponding expansion, translation,
conservativity and necessity results
(\cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-fn-expansion,thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-fn-satisfaction,thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-fn-conservativity}).

\section{Standing data and the defined relation}
\label{sec:p02-w02b-p02-018-standing}

\begin{hbconvention}{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-standing}{Standing data for a definitional extension}
Throughout this chapter, except in
\cref{sec:p02-w02b-p02-018-function-symbols}, which fixes its own data:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \(\Lang\) is a signature, \(T\) is a theory in \(\Lang\), and
       \(R\) is a relation symbol not in \(\Lang\) of arity \(n\geq0\);
 \item \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) are pairwise distinct variables, and
       \(\varphi\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang)\) satisfies
       \(\FV(\varphi)\subseteq\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}\);
 \item \(\Lang_R\) is the signature obtained from \(\Lang\) by adjoining
       \(R\) with arity \(n\), and
       \[
        \delta_R\defeq\forall x_1\cdots\forall x_n
        \bigl(R(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\leftrightarrow\varphi\bigr),
       \]
       where \(\leftrightarrow\) is the defined biconditional and, for
       \(n=0\), the atomic formula is the nullary symbol \(R\) and no
       quantifier is written.
\end{enumerate}
Since \(R\) is a relation symbol, \(\Lang\) and \(\Lang_R\) have the same
function symbols, so their terms coincide.  ``Least'' variable always
refers to the fixed enumeration of the variables used for fresh choices in
\cref{sec:fol-syntax-semantics-04}; the names \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) here
denote the defining variables, which need not be the first members of that
enumeration.  A consequence \(\Gamma\entails\chi\) is evaluated over
structures of the signature to which \(\Gamma\cup\{\chi\}\) belongs:
\(\Lang_R\)-structures for \(T\cup\{\delta_R\}\), and \(\Lang\)-structures
for \(T\).  For an assignment \(s\) and a tuple
\(\bar a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\), write
\(s[\bar x\mapsto\bar a]\) for \(s[x_1\mapsto a_1]\cdots[x_n\mapsto a_n]\).
\end{hbconvention}

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-defined-relation}{Relation defined by \(\varphi\), and the defining expansion}
Let \(\mathcal M\) be an \(\Lang\)-structure.  The relation \emph{defined by
\(\varphi\) in \(\mathcal M\)} is
\[
 R^{\mathcal M}_{\varphi}\defeq
 \bigl\{\bar a\in(\dom\mathcal M)^n:
 \mathcal M,s[\bar x\mapsto\bar a]\entails\varphi
 \text{ for every assignment }s\text{ in }\mathcal M\bigr\}.
\]
The \emph{defining expansion} \(\mathcal M_{\varphi}\) is the
\(\Lang_R\)-structure with domain \(\dom\mathcal M\), the interpretations of
\(\mathcal M\) for the symbols of \(\Lang\), and
\(R^{\mathcal M_{\varphi}}\defeq R^{\mathcal M}_{\varphi}\).  Thus
\(\mathcal M_{\varphi}\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\Lang=\mathcal M\).  For
\(n=0\), \((\dom\mathcal M)^0\) has the empty tuple as its only member,
which lies in \(R^{\mathcal M}_{\varphi}\) iff the sentence \(\varphi\) is
true in \(\mathcal M\).
\end{hbdefinition}

By \cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-instance}(3) below, the quantifier ``for
every assignment'' in this definition may be replaced by ``for some
assignment''; in the proof of \cref{thm:definitional-conservativity} the
same relation is written \(R^{*}\).
\Cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-consequences}(1) shows that
\(\mathcal M_{\varphi}\) is the unique expansion of \(\mathcal M\)
satisfying \(\delta_R\), whether or not \(\mathcal M\) is a model of \(T\).

\section{Instances of the defining formula}
\label{sec:p02-w02b-p02-018-instances}

To eliminate an atomic formula \(R(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\), the arguments
\(t_1,\ldots,t_n\) have to be put in place of \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\) in
\(\varphi\) \emph{simultaneously}.  The handbook's substitution
\(\chi[t/x]\) replaces one variable at a time and avoids capture.  The
following procedure builds the simultaneous instance from it.

\begin{hbmethod}{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-instance}{The instance \(\varphi(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\)}
\emph{Input:} terms \(t_1,\ldots,t_n\in\mathsf{Term}(\Lang)\).
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \emph{Fresh names.}  Let \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\) be the \(n\) least
       variables, listed in increasing order, that lie outside
       \[
        V\defeq\operatorname{Var}(\varphi)\cup\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}
        \cup\operatorname{Var}(t_1)\cup\cdots\cup\operatorname{Var}(t_n).
       \]
 \item \emph{Renaming stage.}  Put \(\chi_0\defeq\varphi\) and
       \(\chi_k\defeq\chi_{k-1}[z_k/x_k]\) for \(k=1,\ldots,n\).
 \item \emph{Insertion stage.}  Put \(\theta_0\defeq\chi_n\) and
       \(\theta_k\defeq\theta_{k-1}[t_k/z_k]\) for \(k=1,\ldots,n\).
 \item \emph{Output:} \(\varphi(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\defeq\theta_n\).
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Side conditions.}  The set \(V\) is finite and the variables are
infinitely many, so step~1 always succeeds; the choice is fixed by the
enumeration, so the output is a function of \(\varphi\), \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\)
and \(t_1,\ldots,t_n\).  The variables \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\) are pairwise
distinct, differ from every \(x_i\), and occur in no \(t_j\) and nowhere in
\(\varphi\).  Capture of a variable of some \(t_k\) by a quantifier of
\(\varphi\) is prevented by the renaming clause of capture-avoiding
substitution itself.  For \(n=0\) both stages are empty and
\(\varphi()\) is \(\varphi\).
\end{hbmethod}

\begin{hblemma}{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-instance}{proved=annex:p02-w02b-p02-018-instance}{Instance lemma}
Let \(t_1,\ldots,t_n\in\mathsf{Term}(\Lang)\).
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \(\varphi(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang)\) and
       \(\FV(\varphi(t_1,\ldots,t_n))\subseteq
       \operatorname{Var}(t_1)\cup\cdots\cup\operatorname{Var}(t_n)\).
 \item For every structure \(\mathcal N\) whose signature extends
       \(\Lang\) and every assignment \(s\) in \(\mathcal N\), with
       \(a_i\defeq\sem{t_i}^{\mathcal N}_s\),
       \[
        \mathcal N,s\entails\varphi(t_1,\ldots,t_n)
        \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
        \mathcal N,s[\bar x\mapsto\bar a]\entails\varphi .
       \]
 \item For every \(\Lang\)-structure \(\mathcal M\), every assignment \(s\)
       in \(\mathcal M\) and every \(\bar a\in(\dom\mathcal M)^n\),
       \(\bar a\in R^{\mathcal M}_{\varphi}\) iff
       \(\mathcal M,s[\bar x\mapsto\bar a]\entails\varphi\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{hblemma}

\section{The translation and its satisfaction theorem}
\label{sec:p02-w02b-p02-018-translation}

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-translation}{Elimination of \(R\)}
For \(\psi\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang_R)\), the formula \(\psi^{*}\) is
defined by recursion on the complexity \(c(\psi)\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
 (r=t)^{*}&\defeq r=t,\\
 (S(t_1,\ldots,t_m))^{*}&\defeq S(t_1,\ldots,t_m)
   &&\text{for each relation symbol }S\in\Lang,\\
 (R(t_1,\ldots,t_n))^{*}&\defeq\varphi(t_1,\ldots,t_n),\\
 \bottom^{*}&\defeq\bottom,\qquad
 (\neg\chi)^{*}\defeq\neg\chi^{*},\\
 (\chi\circ\eta)^{*}&\defeq(\chi^{*}\circ\eta^{*})
   &&\text{for }\circ\in\{\land,\lor,\to\},\\
 (Qy\,\chi)^{*}&\defeq Qy\,\chi^{*}
   &&\text{for }Q\in\{\forall,\exists\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The instance \(\varphi(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\) is that of
\cref{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-instance}.  By unique readability
(\cref{thm:fol-unique-readability}) exactly one clause applies to each
formula, and every recursive call is on an immediate subformula of lower
complexity, so \(\psi^{*}\) is well defined.  Abbreviations are expanded
before translating; in particular
\((\chi\leftrightarrow\eta)^{*}=(\chi^{*}\leftrightarrow\eta^{*})\).
\end{hbdefinition}

The translation changes only the atomic formulas whose relation symbol is
\(R\), and it leaves every quantifier and bound variable of \(\psi\) in
place.  The bound variables that capture-avoiding substitution introduces
are confined to the inserted instances of \(\varphi\).

\begin{hblemma}{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-translation-syntax}{proved=annex:p02-w02b-p02-018-translation-syntax}{The translation lands in the old signature}
For every \(\psi\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang_R)\):
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \(\psi^{*}\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang)\) and
       \(\FV(\psi^{*})\subseteq\FV(\psi)\); in particular the translation
       of an \(\Lang_R\)-sentence is an \(\Lang\)-sentence;
 \item if \(\psi\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang)\), then \(\psi^{*}=\psi\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{hblemma}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-satisfaction-translation}{proved=annex:p02-w02b-p02-018-satisfaction-translation}{Satisfaction translation}
For every \(\Lang\)-structure \(\mathcal M\), every assignment \(s\) in
\(\mathcal M\) and every \(\psi\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang_R)\),
\[
 \mathcal M_{\varphi},s\entails\psi
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal M,s\entails\psi^{*}.
\]
In particular, if \(\mathcal M\) is an \(\Lang\)-model of \(T\), then
\(\mathcal M_{\varphi}\) is the unique \(\Lang_R\)-expansion of
\(\mathcal M\) satisfying \(\delta_R\), it is a model of
\(T\cup\{\delta_R\}\), and for every assignment \(s\) and every
\(\psi\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang_R)\) it satisfies \(\psi\) under \(s\)
iff \(\mathcal M\) satisfies \(\psi^{*}\) under \(s\).
\end{hbtheorem}

\begin{hbcorollary}{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-consequences}{proved=annex:p02-w02b-p02-018-consequences}{Consequence and derivability forms}
\begin{enumerate}
 \item An \(\Lang_R\)-structure \(\mathcal N\) satisfies \(\delta_R\) iff
       \(R^{\mathcal N}=R^{\mathcal N\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\Lang}_{\varphi}\),
       that is, iff
       \(\mathcal N=(\mathcal N\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\Lang)_{\varphi}\).
       Hence every \(\Lang\)-structure has exactly one \(\Lang_R\)-expansion
       satisfying \(\delta_R\).
 \item For every \(\psi\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang_R)\),
       \(\{\delta_R\}\entails\psi\leftrightarrow\psi^{*}\), and therefore
       \(T\cup\{\delta_R\}\entails\psi\leftrightarrow\psi^{*}\).
 \item For every \(\Gamma\cup\{\psi\}\subseteq\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang_R)\), with
       \(\Gamma^{*}\defeq\{\gamma^{*}:\gamma\in\Gamma\}\),
       \[
        T\cup\{\delta_R\}\cup\Gamma\entails\psi
        \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
        T\cup\Gamma^{*}\entails\psi^{*}.
       \]
       In particular \(T\cup\{\delta_R\}\entails\psi\) iff
       \(T\entails\psi^{*}\).  For \(\psi\in\mathsf{Sent}(\Lang)\) and
       \(\Gamma=\varnothing\) this is clause~2 of
       \cref{thm:definitional-conservativity}, since then
       \(\psi^{*}=\psi\).
 \item Assume the Axiom of Choice.  For every
       \(\Gamma\cup\{\psi\}\subseteq\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang_R)\),
       \(T\cup\{\delta_R\}\derives_{\mathsf{ND}_{\mathsf{FOL}}}
       \psi\leftrightarrow\psi^{*}\), and
       \[
        T\cup\{\delta_R\}\cup\Gamma\derives_{\mathsf{ND}_{\mathsf{FOL}}}\psi
        \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
        T\cup\Gamma^{*}\derives_{\mathsf{ND}_{\mathsf{FOL}}}\psi^{*},
       \]
       the derivations on the left being in the signature \(\Lang_R\) and
       those on the right in \(\Lang\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{hbcorollary}

Clause~1 is the argument for clause~1 of
\cref{thm:definitional-conservativity}, now stated for every
\(\Lang\)-structure; clause~3 reduces any question about consequence in the
extended theory, from any further premises, to a question in the original
signature.  Clause~4 uses soundness (\cref{thm:fol-soundness}) and the
Axiom-of-Choice form of completeness (\cref{thm:fol-completeness}) in both
directions.

Kleene calls added symbols with their postulates \emph{eliminable} when an
effective process assigns to every formula of the enlarged system a formula
of the original one so that three relations hold: formulas of the original
system are left unchanged, each formula is provably equivalent in the
enlarged system to the formula assigned to it, and deducibility from
premises in the enlarged system passes to deducibility of the assigned
formulas from the assigned premises in the original system
\cite[\S\,74, p.~405]{Kleene1952}.  Taking the enlarged system to have the
premises \(T\cup\{\delta_R\}\) and the original one the premises \(T\),
\cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-translation-syntax}(2) and clauses~2 and~4
above supply the counterparts of these three relations for the
natural-deduction calculus of this book under the Axiom of Choice.  The
translation is computed by a terminating recursion with the
deterministic fresh-variable choice of \cref{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-instance}.

\section{A worked translation}
\label{sec:p02-w02b-p02-018-example}

\begin{hbexample}{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-example}{Betweenness in a dense order}
Let \(\Lang=\{<\}\) with \(<\) binary, let \(T\) be the theory of dense
linear orders without endpoints (\cref{ch:fol-theories}), let \(R\) be
binary, and take distinct variables \(x_1,x_2,y\) with
\[
 \varphi\defeq\exists y\,(x_1<y\land y<x_2).
\]
In a model \(\mathcal M\) of \(T\), the relation
\(R^{\mathcal M}_{\varphi}\) holds of \((a,b)\) iff some element lies
strictly between \(a\) and \(b\); by density it coincides with
\(<^{\mathcal M}\).

\emph{The atom \(R(x_2,x_1)\).}  Here \(t_1=x_2\) and \(t_2=x_1\), so
\(V=\{x_1,x_2,y\}\) and \(z_1,z_2\) are the two least variables outside
\(V\).  The renaming stage gives
\(\chi_1=\exists y\,(z_1<y\land y<x_2)\) and
\(\chi_2=\exists y\,(z_1<y\land y<z_2)\); no binder is renamed because
\(y\) differs from the substituted variables and from the inserted ones.
The insertion stage gives \(\theta_1=\exists y\,(x_2<y\land y<z_2)\) and
\[
 (R(x_2,x_1))^{*}=\theta_2=\exists y\,(x_2<y\land y<x_1).
\]

\emph{The atom \(R(y,x_1)\), whose argument \(y\) is bound in
\(\varphi\).}  The renaming stage is as before.  In the insertion stage the
substituted term \(y\) contains the bound variable \(y\), so the
capture-avoiding clause renames that binder to the least variable \(w\)
outside \(\{z_1,z_2,y\}\) before inserting:
\(\theta_1=\exists w\,(y<w\land w<z_2)\).  The last step inserts \(x_1\)
for \(z_2\); if \(w\) happens to be \(x_1\), the binder is renamed once
more.  In either case the result has the shape
\[
 (R(y,x_1))^{*}=\exists w'\,(y<w'\land w'<x_1)
\]
for a bound variable \(w'\) distinct from \(y\) and \(x_1\) that the fixed
enumeration determines.  Consequently
\[
 \bigl(\forall x_1\,\exists y\,R(y,x_1)\bigr)^{*}
 =\forall x_1\,\exists y\,\exists w'\,(y<w'\land w'<x_1),
\]
and by \cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-satisfaction-translation} the sentence
\(\forall x_1\,\exists y\,R(y,x_1)\) is true in \(\mathcal M_{\varphi}\)
iff every element of \(\mathcal M\) has two elements below it, which holds
in every model of \(T\) because there are no endpoints.  By
\cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-consequences}(3),
\(T\cup\{\delta_R\}\entails\forall x_1\,\exists y\,R(y,x_1)\).
\end{hbexample}

\section{Defined function and constant symbols}
\label{sec:p02-w02b-p02-018-function-symbols}

A function symbol may be introduced by definition only when the defining
formula determines exactly one value for each argument tuple, and that
condition has to hold in every model of the theory.  This section states
the condition as a pair of sentences, shows that it is exactly what makes
the expansion exist, translates every formula of the extended signature
back into the old one, and proves conservativity.  A constant symbol is the
case of arity~0.

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-function-standing}{Definitional extension by a function or constant symbol}
In this section the following data replace those of
\cref{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-standing}.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \(\Lang\) is a signature, \(T\) is a theory in \(\Lang\), and
       \(f\) is a function symbol not in \(\Lang\) of arity \(m\geq0\); for
       \(m=0\), \(f\) is a constant symbol and the term \(f()\) is written
       \(f\).
 \item \(x_1,\ldots,x_m,y\) are pairwise distinct variables, and
       \(\varphi\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang)\) satisfies
       \(\FV(\varphi)\subseteq\{x_1,\ldots,x_m,y\}\).
 \item \(\Lang_f\) is the signature obtained from \(\Lang\) by adjoining
       \(f\) with arity \(m\), and the \emph{defining axiom} is
       \[
        \delta_f\defeq\forall x_1\cdots\forall x_m\,\forall y\,
        \bigl(f(x_1,\ldots,x_m)=y\leftrightarrow\varphi\bigr).
       \]
 \item Let \(y'\) be the least variable outside
       \(\operatorname{Var}(\varphi)\cup\{x_1,\ldots,x_m,y\}\).  The
       \emph{unique-value sentences} of \(\varphi\) are
       \[
        \begin{aligned}
         \operatorname{Tot}_{\varphi}&\defeq
          \forall x_1\cdots\forall x_m\,\exists y\,\varphi,\\
         \operatorname{Uni}_{\varphi}&\defeq
          \forall x_1\cdots\forall x_m\,\forall y\,\forall y'\,
          \bigl((\varphi\land\varphi[y'/y])\to y=y'\bigr).
        \end{aligned}
       \]
 \item The \emph{unique-value premise relative to \(T\)} is:
       \(T\entails\operatorname{Tot}_{\varphi}\) and
       \(T\entails\operatorname{Uni}_{\varphi}\).
\end{enumerate}
When the unique-value premise holds, the passage from \(T\) to
\(T\cup\{\delta_f\}\) is a \emph{definitional extension of \(T\) by the
function symbol \(f\)} (by the constant symbol \(f\) when \(m=0\)).  The
symbol \(f\) is fresh because it is not in \(\Lang\); the defining formula
is admissible because it is an \(\Lang\)-formula with no free variable
outside \(x_1,\ldots,x_m,y\).  Both unique-value formulas and \(\delta_f\)
are sentences (\cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-unique-value}).  Under the
Axiom of Choice, soundness and completeness make the premise equivalent
to \(T\derives_{\mathsf{ND}_{\mathsf{FOL}}}\operatorname{Tot}_{\varphi}\)
and \(T\derives_{\mathsf{ND}_{\mathsf{FOL}}}\operatorname{Uni}_{\varphi}\).
\end{hbdefinition}

The premise is relative to \(T\): the same formula may define a function
over one theory and not over another.  Because \(y'\) lies outside
\(\operatorname{Var}(\varphi)\), the formula \(\varphi[y'/y]\) only renames
the free occurrences of \(y\).

\Cref{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-defined-relation},
\cref{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-instance} and
\cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-instance} use the standing data of the relation
case only through an \(\Lang\)-formula, a list of pairwise distinct
variables, and the inclusion of the formula's free variables in that list.
Their statements and annex proofs therefore apply unchanged to the present
\(\varphi\) with the list \(x_1,\ldots,x_m,y\) of length \(m+1\).  In this
section \(R^{\mathcal M}_{\varphi}\subseteq(\dom\mathcal M)^{m+1}\) is the
relation defined by \(\varphi\) with respect to that list,
\(\varphi(t_1,\ldots,t_m,t)\) is the corresponding instance, and
\(s[\bar x\mapsto\bar a][y\mapsto b]\) updates \(x_1,\ldots,x_m\) and then
\(y\).

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-defined-function}{Function defined by \(\varphi\), and the defining expansion}
Let \(\mathcal M\) be an \(\Lang\)-structure.  The formula \(\varphi\) is
\emph{functional in \(\mathcal M\)} if for every
\(\bar a\in(\dom\mathcal M)^m\) there is exactly one \(b\in\dom\mathcal M\)
with \((\bar a,b)\in R^{\mathcal M}_{\varphi}\).  In that case
\(f^{\mathcal M}_{\varphi}:(\dom\mathcal M)^m\to\dom\mathcal M\) sends
\(\bar a\) to that \(b\), and the \emph{defining expansion}
\(\mathcal M^{f}_{\varphi}\) is the \(\Lang_f\)-structure with domain
\(\dom\mathcal M\), the interpretations of \(\mathcal M\) for the symbols of
\(\Lang\), and \(f^{\mathcal M^{f}_{\varphi}}\defeq f^{\mathcal M}_{\varphi}\).
For \(m=0\), \(f^{\mathcal M}_{\varphi}\) is identified with its value, the
unique \(b\) with \(\mathcal M,s[y\mapsto b]\entails\varphi\) for every
assignment \(s\).
\end{hbdefinition}

\begin{hblemma}{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-unique-value}{proved=annex:p02-w02b-p02-018-unique-value}{The unique-value sentences}
The formulas \(\operatorname{Tot}_{\varphi}\), \(\operatorname{Uni}_{\varphi}\)
and \(\delta_f\) are sentences.  For every \(\Lang\)-structure
\(\mathcal M\):
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \(\mathcal M\entails\operatorname{Tot}_{\varphi}\) iff for every
       \(\bar a\in(\dom\mathcal M)^m\) there is at least one \(b\) with
       \((\bar a,b)\in R^{\mathcal M}_{\varphi}\);
 \item \(\mathcal M\entails\operatorname{Uni}_{\varphi}\) iff for every
       \(\bar a\in(\dom\mathcal M)^m\) there is at most one such \(b\);
 \item hence \(\varphi\) is functional in \(\mathcal M\) iff
       \(\mathcal M\entails\operatorname{Tot}_{\varphi}\) and
       \(\mathcal M\entails\operatorname{Uni}_{\varphi}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{hblemma}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-fn-expansion}{proved=annex:p02-w02b-p02-018-fn-expansion}{The defining expansion}
\begin{enumerate}
 \item An \(\Lang_f\)-structure \(\mathcal N\) satisfies \(\delta_f\) iff
       \(\varphi\) is functional in \(\mathcal N\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\Lang\)
       and \(f^{\mathcal N}=f^{\mathcal N\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\Lang}_{\varphi}\),
       that is, iff \(\varphi\) is functional there and
       \(\mathcal N=(\mathcal N\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\Lang)^{f}_{\varphi}\).
 \item An \(\Lang\)-structure \(\mathcal M\) has an \(\Lang_f\)-expansion
       satisfying \(\delta_f\) iff
       \(\mathcal M\entails\operatorname{Tot}_{\varphi}\) and
       \(\mathcal M\entails\operatorname{Uni}_{\varphi}\); it then has
       exactly one, namely \(\mathcal M^{f}_{\varphi}\).
 \item Under the unique-value premise, every \(\Lang\)-model \(\mathcal M\)
       of \(T\) has exactly one \(\Lang_f\)-expansion satisfying
       \(\delta_f\), namely \(\mathcal M^{f}_{\varphi}\), and that expansion
       is a model of \(T\cup\{\delta_f\}\).
 \item Without any premise,
       \(\{\delta_f\}\entails\operatorname{Tot}_{\varphi}\) and
       \(\{\delta_f\}\entails\operatorname{Uni}_{\varphi}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{hbtheorem}

A function symbol may occur nested inside terms, so an atomic formula
cannot be translated by replacing one occurrence of a symbol by a formula.
Each term is first replaced by a formula saying that a variable denotes the
value of the term.

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-graph-formula}{Graph formula of a term}
For \(t\in\mathsf{Term}(\Lang_f)\) and a variable \(v\notin\operatorname{Var}(t)\),
the \(\Lang\)-formula \(\operatorname{Gr}_{t}(v)\) is defined by recursion
on the construction of \(t\):
\begin{enumerate}
 \item if \(f\) does not occur in \(t\), then
       \(\operatorname{Gr}_{t}(v)\defeq(v=t)\);
 \item if \(f\) occurs in \(t\) and \(t=g(t_1,\ldots,t_k)\) with \(g\in\Lang\),
       then
       \[
        \operatorname{Gr}_{t}(v)\defeq
        \exists w_1\cdots\exists w_k\,
        \bigl(\operatorname{Gr}_{t_1}(w_1)\land\cdots\land
        \operatorname{Gr}_{t_k}(w_k)\land v=g(w_1,\ldots,w_k)\bigr);
       \]
 \item if \(t=f(t_1,\ldots,t_m)\), then
       \[
        \operatorname{Gr}_{t}(v)\defeq
        \exists w_1\cdots\exists w_m\,
        \bigl(\operatorname{Gr}_{t_1}(w_1)\land\cdots\land
        \operatorname{Gr}_{t_m}(w_m)\land\varphi(w_1,\ldots,w_m,v)\bigr).
       \]
\end{enumerate}
In clauses 2 and 3, \(w_1,\ldots,w_k\) (respectively \(w_1,\ldots,w_m\)) are
the least variables, in increasing order, outside
\(\operatorname{Var}(t)\cup\{v\}\).  A conjunction
\(\alpha_1\land\cdots\land\alpha_j\land\beta\) is right-associated,
\((\alpha_1\land(\alpha_2\land\cdots(\alpha_j\land\beta)\cdots))\); for
\(j=0\) it is \(\beta\), with no quantifier in front.  For \(m=0\), clause 3
reads \(\operatorname{Gr}_{f}(v)=\varphi(v)\).  In clause 2, \(k\geq1\)
because \(f\) occurs in \(t\).  Since \(w_i\notin\operatorname{Var}(t_i)\),
every recursive call is defined, and it is on an immediate subterm, so the
recursion terminates; by unique readability
(\cref{thm:fol-unique-readability}) exactly one clause applies.
\end{hbdefinition}

\begin{hblemma}{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-graph-formula}{proved=annex:p02-w02b-p02-018-graph-formula}{Graph formulas}
Let \(t\in\mathsf{Term}(\Lang_f)\) and \(v\notin\operatorname{Var}(t)\).
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \(\operatorname{Gr}_{t}(v)\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang)\) and
       \(\FV(\operatorname{Gr}_{t}(v))\subseteq\operatorname{Var}(t)\cup\{v\}\).
 \item If \(\varphi\) is functional in the \(\Lang\)-structure
       \(\mathcal M\), then for every assignment \(s\) in \(\mathcal M\),
       \[
        \mathcal M,s\entails\operatorname{Gr}_{t}(v)
        \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
        s(v)=\sem{t}^{\mathcal M^{f}_{\varphi}}_s .
       \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{hblemma}

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-function-translation}{Elimination of \(f\)}
For \(\psi\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang_f)\), the formula \(\psi^{\sharp}\) is
defined by recursion on \(c(\psi)\):
\begin{enumerate}
 \item if \(\psi\) is atomic and \(f\) does not occur in \(\psi\), then
       \(\psi^{\sharp}\defeq\psi\);
 \item if \(f\) occurs in \(r=t\), then, with \(v_1,v_2\) the two least
       variables outside \(\operatorname{Var}(r)\cup\operatorname{Var}(t)\),
       \[
        (r=t)^{\sharp}\defeq\exists v_1\,\exists v_2\,
        \bigl(\operatorname{Gr}_{r}(v_1)\land(\operatorname{Gr}_{t}(v_2)
        \land v_1=v_2)\bigr);
       \]
 \item if \(S\in\Lang\) is a relation symbol of arity \(k\) and \(f\) occurs
       in \(S(t_1,\ldots,t_k)\), then, with \(v_1,\ldots,v_k\) the least
       variables outside
       \(\operatorname{Var}(t_1)\cup\cdots\cup\operatorname{Var}(t_k)\),
       \[
        (S(t_1,\ldots,t_k))^{\sharp}\defeq
        \exists v_1\cdots\exists v_k\,
        \bigl(\operatorname{Gr}_{t_1}(v_1)\land\cdots\land
        \operatorname{Gr}_{t_k}(v_k)\land S(v_1,\ldots,v_k)\bigr);
       \]
 \item \(\bottom^{\sharp}\defeq\bottom\),
       \((\neg\chi)^{\sharp}\defeq\neg\chi^{\sharp}\),
       \((\chi\circ\eta)^{\sharp}\defeq(\chi^{\sharp}\circ\eta^{\sharp})\) for
       \(\circ\in\{\land,\lor,\to\}\), and
       \((Qu\,\chi)^{\sharp}\defeq Qu\,\chi^{\sharp}\) for
       \(Q\in\{\forall,\exists\}\) and every variable \(u\).
\end{enumerate}
The variables \(v_i\) are listed in increasing order and the conjunctions
are right-associated as in \cref{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-graph-formula}.  A
nullary relation symbol has no terms, so clause 1 covers it.
\end{hbdefinition}

\begin{hblemma}{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-fn-syntax}{proved=annex:p02-w02b-p02-018-fn-syntax}{The function translation lands in the old signature}
For every \(\psi\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang_f)\): \(\psi^{\sharp}\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang)\)
and \(\FV(\psi^{\sharp})\subseteq\FV(\psi)\); and if
\(\psi\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang)\), then \(\psi^{\sharp}=\psi\).
\end{hblemma}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-fn-satisfaction}{proved=annex:p02-w02b-p02-018-fn-satisfaction}{Function satisfaction translation}
If \(\varphi\) is functional in the \(\Lang\)-structure \(\mathcal M\), then
for every assignment \(s\) in \(\mathcal M\) and every
\(\psi\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang_f)\),
\[
 \mathcal M^{f}_{\varphi},s\entails\psi
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal M,s\entails\psi^{\sharp}.
\]
In particular this holds for every \(\Lang\)-model \(\mathcal M\) of \(T\)
when the unique-value premise holds.
\end{hbtheorem}

\begin{hbcorollary}{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-fn-conservativity}{proved=annex:p02-w02b-p02-018-fn-conservativity}{Conservativity for a function symbol}
\begin{enumerate}
 \item For every \(\psi\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang_f)\),
       \(\{\delta_f\}\entails\psi\leftrightarrow\psi^{\sharp}\).
 \item Assume the unique-value premise.  For every
       \(\Gamma\cup\{\psi\}\subseteq\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang_f)\), with
       \(\Gamma^{\sharp}\defeq\{\gamma^{\sharp}:\gamma\in\Gamma\}\),
       \[
        T\cup\{\delta_f\}\cup\Gamma\entails\psi
        \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
        T\cup\Gamma^{\sharp}\entails\psi^{\sharp}.
       \]
       In particular, for every \(\psi\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang)\), and so for
       every \(\Lang\)-sentence,
       \(T\cup\{\delta_f\}\entails\psi\) iff \(T\entails\psi\).
 \item Assume the unique-value premise and the Axiom of Choice.  Then for
       every \(\Gamma\cup\{\psi\}\subseteq\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang_f)\),
       \(T\cup\{\delta_f\}\derives_{\mathsf{ND}_{\mathsf{FOL}}}
       \psi\leftrightarrow\psi^{\sharp}\), and
       \(T\cup\{\delta_f\}\cup\Gamma\derives_{\mathsf{ND}_{\mathsf{FOL}}}\psi\)
       iff \(T\cup\Gamma^{\sharp}\derives_{\mathsf{ND}_{\mathsf{FOL}}}\psi^{\sharp}\),
       the derivations on the left being in \(\Lang_f\) and those on the
       right in \(\Lang\).
 \item The premise cannot be dropped: for every theory \(T\) in \(\Lang\),
       \(T\cup\{\delta_f\}\) is conservative over \(T\) for
       \(\Lang\)-sentences iff \(T\entails\operatorname{Tot}_{\varphi}\) and
       \(T\entails\operatorname{Uni}_{\varphi}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{hbcorollary}

Clause~4 makes the unique-value premise a necessary condition, not merely a
sufficient one: if \(T\) fails to entail one of the two sentences, that
sentence is an \(\Lang\)-sentence entailed by \(T\cup\{\delta_f\}\) and not
by \(T\).  For a relation symbol no premise is needed, because every
\(\Lang\)-structure has the expansion \(\mathcal M_{\varphi}\)
(\cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-consequences}(1)).

Definitions are usually introduced one after another, each defining
formula using the symbols defined before it.

\begin{hbcorollary}{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-iterated}{proved=annex:p02-w02b-p02-018-iterated}{Finite sequences of definitions}
Let \(\Lang_0=\Lang\) and \(T_0=T\), and for \(j=1,\ldots,k\) let
\(\Lang_j\) extend \(\Lang_{j-1}\) by one new symbol \(e_j\) and let
\(T_j=T_{j-1}\cup\{\delta_j\}\), where one of the following holds:
\(e_j\) is a relation symbol and \(\delta_j\) is its defining axiom as in
\cref{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-standing}, with \(\Lang_{j-1}\) and \(T_{j-1}\)
in place of \(\Lang\) and \(T\); or \(e_j\) is a function or constant symbol
and \(\delta_j\) is its defining axiom as in
\cref{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-function-standing}, with \(\Lang_{j-1}\) and
\(T_{j-1}\) in place of \(\Lang\) and \(T\), and the unique-value premise
holds relative to \(T_{j-1}\).  Then:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item every \(\Lang\)-model of \(T\) has exactly one \(\Lang_k\)-expansion
       that is a model of \(T_k\);
 \item for every \(\Gamma\cup\{\psi\}\subseteq\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang)\),
       \(T_k\cup\Gamma\entails\psi\) iff \(T\cup\Gamma\entails\psi\);
 \item every \(\psi\in\mathsf{Fm}(\Lang_k)\) has an \(\Lang\)-formula
       \(\psi^{[k]}\), obtained by applying the translations of steps
       \(k,k-1,\ldots,1\) in turn, with
       \(\FV(\psi^{[k]})\subseteq\FV(\psi)\) and
       \(T_k\entails\psi\leftrightarrow\psi^{[k]}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{hbcorollary}

Clause~3 is the replacement of each defined symbol by its defining formula
that interprets a definitional extension back into its base
(\cref{def:p05-interpretation-examples}); the unique-value conditions in
the function steps are the ones the interpretation needs
(\cref{tab:p14-strength-interpretations}).

\begin{hbexample}{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-function-example}{A defined least element}
Let \(\Lang=\{<\}\) with \(<\) binary, and let \(T\) consist of the
sentences \(\forall x_0\,\neg(x_0<x_0)\),
\(\forall x_0\forall x_1\forall x_2((x_0<x_1\land x_1<x_2)\to x_0<x_2)\),
\(\forall x_0\forall x_1(x_0<x_1\lor(x_0=x_1\lor x_1<x_0))\) and
\(\exists x_0\forall x_1(x_0<x_1\lor x_0=x_1)\): strict linear orders with a
least element.  Define a constant symbol \(f\) (\(m=0\)) by taking \(y\) to
be \(x_0\) and
\[
 \varphi\defeq\forall x_1\,(x_0<x_1\lor x_0=x_1).
\]
\emph{The premise.}  Here \(y'=x_2\), \(\operatorname{Tot}_{\varphi}\) is the
last axiom of \(T\), and \(\operatorname{Uni}_{\varphi}\) is
\(\forall x_0\forall x_2((\varphi\land\forall x_1(x_2<x_1\lor x_2=x_1))\to
x_0=x_2)\).  If \(a\) and \(a'\) are both least in a model of \(T\) and
\(a\ne a'\), then \(a<a'\) and \(a'<a\), so \(a<a\) by transitivity,
against irreflexivity; hence \(T\entails\operatorname{Uni}_{\varphi}\).
In each model \(\mathcal M\) of \(T\), \(f^{\mathcal M}_{\varphi}\) is the
least element.

\emph{A translation.}  Take \(\sigma\defeq\forall x_2\,\neg(x_2<f)\).  For the
atom \(x_2<f\), the two least variables outside \(\{x_2\}\) are
\(v_1=x_0\) and \(v_2=x_1\).  First, \(\operatorname{Gr}_{x_2}(x_0)\) is
\(x_0=x_2\).  Next, \(\operatorname{Gr}_{f}(x_1)=\varphi(x_1)\): the renaming
stage replaces \(x_0\) by \(x_2\), the least variable outside
\(\{x_0,x_1\}\), giving \(\forall x_1(x_2<x_1\lor x_2=x_1)\); inserting
\(x_1\) for \(x_2\) meets the binder \(x_1\), which occurs in the inserted
term, so the binder is renamed to \(x_0\), the least variable outside
\(\{x_1,x_2\}\).  Hence
\[
 \sigma^{\sharp}=\forall x_2\,\neg\exists x_0\,\exists x_1\,
 \bigl(x_0=x_2\land(\forall x_0\,(x_1<x_0\lor x_1=x_0)\land x_0<x_1)\bigr).
\]
The inner \(\forall x_0\) rebinds a variable that is free in the other
conjuncts; the proof of \cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-graph-formula} handles
this through formula coincidence, not through freshness of bound variables.
The sentence \(\sigma^{\sharp}\) says that nothing lies below an element
that is below or equal to everything, which holds in every model of \(T\)
by irreflexivity and transitivity.  By \cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-fn-conservativity}(2),
\(T\cup\{\delta_f\}\entails\sigma\).
\end{hbexample}

\begin{hbwarning}{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-function-failure}{Missing unique-value premise}
Let \(\Lang=\{<\}\) and let \(T\) be the theory of dense linear orders
without endpoints (\cref{ch:fol-theories}); \((\mathbb Q,<)\) is a model.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \emph{Uniqueness fails.}  For \(m=1\) and
       \(\varphi\defeq x_1<y\), every model of \(T\) satisfies
       \(\operatorname{Tot}_{\varphi}\) but not
       \(\operatorname{Uni}_{\varphi}\): above any \(a\) there is some \(b\),
       and density gives \(c\) with \(a<c<b\), so \(a\) has two distinct
       elements above it.  By
       \cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-fn-expansion}(4),
       \(T\cup\{\delta_f\}\) entails \(\operatorname{Uni}_{\varphi}\) and so
       has no model; it entails \(\bottom\), which \(T\) does not.
 \item \emph{Existence fails.}  For the constant case with
       \(\varphi\defeq\forall x_1(x_0<x_1\lor x_0=x_1)\) and \(y=x_0\), as
       in \cref{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-function-example}, \(T\) entails
       \(\operatorname{Uni}_{\varphi}\) but no model of \(T\) has a least
       element.  Again \(T\cup\{\delta_f\}\) entails
       \(\operatorname{Tot}_{\varphi}\) and has no model.
 \item \emph{Recursion equations are not explicit definitions.}  A function
       symbol introduced by equations that mention it on both sides, such as
       the recursion equations for multiplication, has no defining formula in
       the old signature, and the results of this section do not apply.
       Kleene shows that the multiplication symbol with its recursion
       equations is not eliminable from his number-theoretic system: by
       Presburger's result the system without it is simply consistent with a
       solvable decision problem, eliminability would carry both properties
       to the full system, and his Theorem~33 makes the decision problem of
       the simply consistent full system unsolvable
       \cite[\S\,74, Example~4, p.~407; \S\,61, Theorem~33, p.~314]{Kleene1952}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{hbwarning}

\section{Failure cases and limits}
\label{sec:p02-w02b-p02-018-limits}

\begin{hbwarning}{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-naive-replacement}{Replacing the defining variables one at a time, or textually}
With \(\varphi=\exists y\,(x_1<y\land y<x_2)\) as in
\cref{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-example}, two shortcuts give wrong
translations.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \emph{Sequential substitution without renaming.}  For
       \(R(x_2,x_1)\), computing \((\varphi[x_2/x_1])[x_1/x_2]\) first
       yields \(\exists y\,(x_2<y\land y<x_2)\) and then
       \(\exists y\,(x_1<y\land y<x_1)\).  This is false under every
       assignment in a strict order, whereas
       \((R(x_2,x_1))^{*}=\exists y\,(x_2<y\land y<x_1)\) is true in
       \((\mathbb Q,<)\) whenever \(x_2\) is assigned a number below the one
       assigned to \(x_1\).
       The second step altered the argument \(x_2\) inserted by the first;
       the renaming stage of \cref{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-instance} keeps the
       inserted arguments out of reach of later steps.
 \item \emph{Textual replacement.}  Writing \(y\) for \(x_1\) inside
       \(\varphi\) without renaming the binder gives
       \(\exists y\,(y<y\land y<x_2)\), in which the inserted \(y\) is
       captured; the formula is again false under every assignment in a
       strict order.  The capture-avoiding result computed in
       \cref{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-example} keeps the inserted \(y\) free.
\end{enumerate}
\end{hbwarning}

\begin{hbwarning}{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-limits}{What the translation does not cover}
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \emph{Without the defining axiom.}  The equivalence of \(\psi\) and
       \(\psi^{*}\) holds in \(\mathcal M_{\varphi}\) and, by
       \cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-consequences}(1), in no other expansion.
       In the order \((\mathbb Q,<)\), the expansion interpreting \(R\) as
       the empty relation falsifies \(R(x_1,x_2)\) under an assignment with
       \(x_1\mapsto0\) and \(x_2\mapsto1\), while
       \((R(x_1,x_2))^{*}=\exists y\,(x_1<y\land y<x_2)\) is true there.
 \item \emph{Parameters in the defining formula.}  If \(\varphi\) had a
       free variable \(u\) outside \(\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}\), the set of tuples
       satisfying \(\varphi\) would in general depend on the value of
       \(u\), so no single interpretation of \(R\) would serve every
       assignment, and \(\delta_R\) would not be a sentence.  The hypothesis
       \(\FV(\varphi)\subseteq\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}\) is used in
       \cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-instance}(3) and in
       \cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-consequences}(1).
 \item \emph{Defined function and constant symbols.}  The translation
       \(\psi\mapsto\psi^{*}\) eliminates one relation symbol.  A function
       symbol occurs inside terms, possibly nested, so the atomic clause of
       \cref{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-translation} has no direct counterpart,
       and its defining formula must in addition take exactly one value on
       the models of \(T\).  That case needs the graph formulas and the
       unique-value premise of \cref{sec:p02-w02b-p02-018-function-symbols};
       \cref{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-function-failure} shows what fails without
       the premise.
\end{enumerate}
\end{hbwarning}

\section{Relationships and sources}
\label{sec:p02-w02b-p02-018-relationships}

The existence and uniqueness of the expansion and the conservativity of
\(T\cup\{\delta_R\}\) over \(T\) for \(\Lang\)-sentences are
\cref{thm:definitional-conservativity}; this chapter extends them to
every formula of \(\Lang_R\) through the translation, and recovers the
sentence case as \cref{thm:p02-w02b-p02-018-consequences}(3).
\Cref{sec:p02-w02b-p02-018-function-symbols} supplies the function and
constant case, which \cref{thm:definitional-conservativity} does not
cover, and the finite sequences of definitions used when a definitional
expansion is interpreted back into its base
(\cref{def:p05-interpretation-examples},
\cref{tab:p14-strength-interpretations}).  The proofs
use capture-avoiding substitution and its complexity and free-variable
lemmas (\cref{sec:fol-syntax-semantics-04}), the term-substitution lemma
(\cref{lem:term-substitution}), formula coincidence
(\cref{lem:fol-coincidence}), the substitution lemma
(\cref{thm:fol-substitution}) and invariance under reduct
(\cref{lem:reduct-invariance}).  The definitions, notation and conventions
are those of \cref{ch:fol-semantics} and \cref{ch:fol-theories}.  The
only external work cited is Kleene's account of eliminability, used for
comparison; its page numbers are those of the 1974 seventh reprint of
\cite{Kleene1952}.  Kleene treats a constant as a function of zero
arguments in his section on descriptive definitions
\cite[\S\,74, p.~407]{Kleene1952}, as
\cref{def:p02-w02b-p02-018-function-standing} does.  His Theorem~42 adds
a function symbol together with an axiom saying that the defining formula
holds of the new term, under the hypothesis that unique existence of the
value is provable in the original system and a condition on any
additional axiom schemata holds, and establishes eliminability by a chain
of proof-theoretic lemmas; his Lemma~26 shows that the conjunction of the
unique-existence formula and that axiom is interdeducible with the
biconditional form used for \(\delta_f\) here \cite[\S\,74, Theorem~42 and Lemma~26,
pp.~408--409]{Kleene1952}.  The proofs in this chapter are semantic
instead, and reach derivability through soundness and completeness under
the Axiom of Choice.  No source-specific presentation of definitional
extensions is reproduced in this chapter.
