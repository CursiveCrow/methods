\chapter{Reading the Equivalence Glyphs Across the Book}
\chaptermark{Equivalence Glyphs Across the Book}
\label{sec:p01-w02b-p01-013-equivalence-audit}
\index{equivalence sign!typed roles}\index{definitional sign}
\index{symbol registry!equivalence glyphs}

\paragraph{Purpose.}  The triple bar \(\equiv\), the double arrow
\(\Leftrightarrow\) and the definitional sign \(\defeq\) occur in many parts
of the book, and no single meaning fits all of them.  The registry of
\cref{sec:p01-symbol-registry} therefore records each use as a typed role
with a scope.  This chapter states the roles that the earlier conventions of
\cref{sec:p01-symbol-registry,sec:p01-w02-p01-025-signs} do not yet name,
gives the rule by which a reader recovers the role of any occurrence from its
operands, proves the facts on which the new roles depend, and lists every
role of the three glyphs with the region of the book in which it is in force.
Because source presentations print the definitional sign as a bare
\(:=\), and the same two characters also write updates, substitutions,
program assignments and algorithm steps, the chapter records every role of
the colon-equals sign as well.

\paragraph{Inputs and conclusions.}  The input is one occurrence of
\(\equiv\) (with or without a subscript or superscript), of
\(\Leftrightarrow\), of the definitional sign, or of the colon-equals sign
\(:=\) or \(::=\), together with the section in which it stands.  The conclusion is the role of that occurrence: the kind of
its operands, the relation or connective it denotes, and whether it belongs to
the object language or to the metalanguage.

\section{Roles not covered by the earlier conventions}
\label{sec:p01-w02b-p01-013-new-roles}

The editorial defaults and the source-local roles recorded in
\cref{def:p01-w02-p01-025-equivalence-roles} remain in force.  The following
definition and five conventions complete them.

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-congruence-predicate}{Presburger congruence predicate}
Let \(m\geq1\) and \(0\leq r<m\) be natural numbers.  In the language
\(\{0,1,+,<\}\) of \cref{def:p05-presburger}, write \(\underline r\) for
\(0\) when \(r=0\) and for the term \(1+\cdots+1\) with \(r\) summands when
\(r\geq1\), and write \(m\cdot y\) for the term \(y+\cdots+y\) with \(m\)
summands.  The \emph{congruence predicate} \(x\equiv r\pmod m\) is the unary
predicate symbol \(C_{m,r}\) added to the language together with its explicit
definition
\[
  C_{m,r}(x)\ \leftrightarrow\ \exists y\,\bigl(x=\underline r+m\cdot y\bigr),
\]
where \(y\) is a variable distinct from \(x\).  In this role \(m\) and \(r\)
are fixed numerals of the metalanguage and the operand \(x\) is a term of the
object language; the sign \(\equiv\) is part of the name of a predicate
symbol.  The role is in force in \cref{def:p05-presburger},
\cref{thm:p05-presburger-qe} and \cref{def:p05-presburger-example}.
\index{congruence predicate}\index{Presburger arithmetic!congruence predicates}
\end{hbdefinition}

The metalinguistic congruence \(t\equiv a\pmod n\) of item~6 of
\cref{def:p01-w02-p01-025-equivalence-roles} relates two numbers; the
predicate above is a symbol of the object language whose interpretation in
\(\mathbb N\) is that relation with the second argument fixed
(\cref{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-congruence-definable}).  The same metalinguistic
congruence, with modulus \(2\), appears in \cref{sec:p09-presentation-n-c-012}.

\begin{hbconvention}{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-identically-equal}{Identically equal}
Let \(D\) be a nonempty set, let \(h:D\to\mathbb C\) be a function and let
\(c\in\mathbb C\).  Then \(h\equiv c\), read ``\(h\) is identically
\(c\)'', means \(h(y)=c\) for every \(y\in D\).  The left operand is a
function and the right operand a constant; the domain is the one declared for
\(h\).  The role is metalinguistic.  It is in force for \(h\equiv1/2\) in
\cref{thm:p15-conditional-examples}, for \(\varphi\equiv1\) in
\cref{thm:p15-characteristic-second-moment}, for \(\varphi\equiv1\) and
\(f\equiv0\) in \cref{sec:p15-characteristic-function-criteria-and-moments}
and its annex \cref{sec:annex-p15-characteristic-function-criteria-and-moments},
for \(\Psi_m\equiv m/\delta\) in
\cref{sec:annex-p15-bayes-factor-computation}, and in the following
places, each with the function and the constant named:
the predictive function \(f\) of the Kemeny--Carnap theorem in
\cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-030-kemeny-carnap} and its annex proof
\cref{annex:p15-w02b-p15-030-kemeny-carnap}, whose arguments are
natural-number pairs \((n_i,n)\) with \(0\leq n_i\leq n\): here
\(K\geq2\) is the fixed number of mutually exclusive and exhaustive
\(Q\)-predicates, and \(f\equiv1/K\) states that the value is
\(1/K\) at every such pair, including \((0,0)\);
the constant predictors \(f\equiv c\) of
\cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-064-squared-example} and the regression function
\(\eta\equiv1/2\) of \cref{annex:p15-w02b-p15-064-failures};
the labels \(Y\equiv0\), a random variable on the sample space, of
\cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-065-ch-failure} and
\cref{annex:p15-w02b-p15-065-ch-failure};
the Fisher information \(I\equiv1\), a function of the parameter, of
\cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-057-hodges} and \cref{annex:p15-w02b-p15-057.hodges};
the dominating function \(M\equiv2\) of
\cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-057-cauchy-check},
\cref{annex:p15-w02b-p15-057.cauchy-line} and
\cref{annex:p15-w02b-p15-057.cauchy-check};
the sum \(\sum_sp_s\equiv1\), a function of the parameter, in
\cref{annex:p15-w02b-p15-057.chisq-law};
the function \(h_K\equiv1\) of \cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-061-finite-error};
the dimensionless function \(\Phi\equiv1\) of
\cref{sec:p15-w02b-p15-073-dimensions};
the weight function \(w\equiv1\) of \cref{def:p15-w02b-p15-043-problem}
and \cref{annex:p15-w02b-p15-043-no-constraint};
the function \(h\equiv0\) of \cref{annex:p15-w02b-p15-008-ae-counterexample}
and the random variable \(V\equiv c\) of
\cref{annex:p15-w02b-p15-008-null-modification};
the difference \(D\equiv0\) of two characteristic functions in
\cref{annex:p15-w02b-p15-012-xv4-determinacy};
the bounded function \(f_j\equiv1\) of
\cref{annex:p15-w02b-p15-022-predictive};
the Jacobian factor \(j\equiv1\) of
\cref{annex:p15-w02b-p15-042-linear-change};
and the general \(h\equiv c\) and \(h'\not\equiv c\) of
\cref{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-identically-equal} and its annex proof
\cref{annex:p01-w02b-p01-013-identically-equal}.  Where the function
takes real values, \(c\) is real and \(h\) is read as a map into
\(\mathbb C\) through the inclusion of \(\mathbb R\) in \(\mathbb C\).
\index{identically equal}
\end{hbconvention}

\begin{hbconvention}{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-statement-iff}{Double arrow between assertions}
When both operands of \(\Leftrightarrow\) are assertions of the metalanguage,
such as \(x\in A\), \(\mathsf D\derives\sigma\) or
\((M,\in)\entails\varphi(\vec a)\), the sign is the biconditional of the
metalanguage: the displayed assertions are both true or both false.  When both
operands name formulas compared as expressions, \(\varphi\Leftrightarrow\psi\) is the editorial formula
equivalence of \cref{sec:p01-symbol-registry}.  The operands decide between
the two readings after any declared semantic abbreviations have been
expanded.  In a fixed structure with specified parameters,
\(\varphi(\bar a)\) may abbreviate the assertion that the structure
satisfies \(\varphi\) at \(\bar a\); it then has the assertion reading,
not the role of a formula named as a syntactic object.  Thus a display
\(\varphi(c_i;b_j)\Leftrightarrow i<j\) in a fixed model states a truth
pattern.  Likewise \((1)\Leftrightarrow(2)\) relates the numbered
assertions, not their numerical labels.  When no interpretation is fixed,
one cannot infer a truth assertion merely from a formula's shape.  The
assertion reading is in force throughout the book; it is used, for example, in
\cref{sec:p04-complexity}, \cref{sec:p04-turing-degrees},
\cref{sec:p04-frontiers-computability}, \cref{def:p06-finite-character},
\cref{thm:p06-suslin}, \cref{def:p06-absoluteness} and
\cref{def:p01-w02-p01-022-setting}.
\index{biconditional!metalinguistic}
\end{hbconvention}

\begin{hbconvention}{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-mention}{Displayed register entries}
In the register tables of \cref{sec:p01-symbol-registry},
\cref{sec:p01-w02-p01-024-notation},
\cref{sec:p14-symbols-terminology-and-glossary},
\cref{sec:p01-w02b-p01-013-table} and the notation appendix
\cref{app:notation} (whose register row for \(a\mathrel{:=}b\) stands
inside a restoration scope), and in the typed-role
convention of \cref{sec:p01-w02-p01-025-overview}, an equivalence glyph or
a form of the colon-equals sign is
displayed together with its registered role and scope, in the same table row
or the same sentence.  Such a display names the glyph and asserts no relation.
Its role is the one printed beside it.  The same holds inside the
typed-role convention \cref{def:p01-w02-p01-025-equivalence-roles} and the
remarks that follow it, where each glyph is displayed in the item that
states its role and region.  It holds as well for the register rows of the
registry extracts in \cref{sec:p01-w02b-p01-012-extracts}, and for the
section ``Calculi, notation, and source conventions'' of the front matter
chapter ``How to Use This Handbook'', where \(\mathrel{:=}\),
\(\mathrel{:\equiv}\) and \(\equiv\) are each displayed in the sentence
that states their role, and for \cref{def:p15-approximation-relations},
where \(\equiv\) is displayed with the reading ``equality by definition''
that Mahajan's grading of informal equalities gives it.  Finally, it holds
for this chapter, \cref{sec:p01-w02b-p01-013-equivalence-audit}, outside the
lemmas of \cref{sec:p01-w02b-p01-013-facts} and their annex proofs: there
each occurrence of a glyph either shows the schematic form of a role,
beside the statement of that role, or quotes a line of another region,
beside the name of that region, and then it has the role that the line has
in that region.  The lemmas themselves use the glyphs in the roles listed
for them in \cref{tab:p01-w02b-p01-013-roles}.

It holds also for the chapter \cref{sec:p01-w02c-p01-018-motives} on motive
letters and for its annex proofs \cref{sec:annex-p01-w02c-p01-018-motives},
with one exception stated at the end of this paragraph.  There an
occurrence of \(:=\) does one of three things.  It names the sign itself,
or names a row of \cref{tab:p01-w02b-p01-013-roles} by the form printed in
that row's first column (\(a:=b\), \(p:=s\)).  It shows a schematic form,
\(p(z_1,\ldots,z_k):=s\), \(p\,z:=s\), \(p(\vec z):=s\) or \(Q\,n:=s\),
which stands for any line of that form; the display asserts nothing, and a
line of that form has the role that
\cref{def:p01-w02c-p01-018-motive-reading} assigns to it.  Or it quotes a
line of a Part~12 or Part~7 proof, such as \(P(z):=(z=x)\),
\(\mathsf{no\_conf}\ n:=\ldots\), \(M:=S\,n\), \(B_n:=\ldots\) or
\(P(x):=x=1_I\), in the
paragraph or list item that names the proof or result the line comes from;
the quoted line then has the role that it has in that region, and the row
of \cref{tab:p01-w02b-p01-013-roles} for that role lists the region.  The
exception is items~1--3 of \cref{thm:p01-w02c-p01-018-equality-instances}.
The phrases ``with \(P:=\lambda z.\,s\)'' there are not quotations: each
gives the letter \(P\) of the eliminator \(E_{A,a,P}\) displayed in the
lemma a value for one use, which is role~(b) of
\cref{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-colon-equals}, and the row of that role lists
them.
The source-printing examples of
\cref{def:p01-w02-p01-025-equivalence-roles} also quote a sign rather
than introducing a new abbreviation.  The program readings chapter
\cref{sec:p01-w02c-p01-017-readings} repeats the cited source grammars
and definitions of the box; those quotations keep the grammar-production
and definitional readings of the cited presentation.
\end{hbconvention}

\begin{hbconvention}{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-slashed}{Slashed signs}
A slashed sign \(a\not\equiv b\) or \(a\not\equiv_X b\) is read from the
role that the unslashed sign \(a\equiv b\) or \(a\equiv_X b\) has at the same
place.  If that role is a relation of the metalanguage, the slashed sign
asserts that the relation fails for the displayed operands.  Thus
\(A\to B\not\equiv B\to A\) in \cref{thm:p02-w02-p02-005-idiom-equivalences}
denies \(\mathsf{CPL}\) equivalence, \(f(b)\not\equiv_{E_0}b\) in
\cref{thm:p12-completion-worked} denies that the two terms are related by
the congruence generated by \(E_0\), and \(h'\not\equiv c\) in
\cref{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-identically-equal} denies identical equality.
If the role is a connective of an object language, the slashed sign is the
negated biconditional, itself a formula of that language: in the causal
theories of \cref{sec:p16-nonmonotonic-causal-theories} and
\cref{sec:p16-cplus-action-language}, with their annex proofs,
\(F\not\equiv G\) is the formula \(\neg(F\equiv G)\).  One region departs
from both readings.  In \cref{sec:p13-street-2004-modal-tables} the
slashed sign joins a mode-prefixed categorical proposition to the
proposition that the source's tables list as its contradictory; it asserts
a contradictory pairing, which is stronger than the failure of equivalence
(\cref{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-contradictory}).
\index{slashed equivalence sign}\index{contradictory pairing}
\end{hbconvention}

\begin{hbconvention}{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-colon-equals}{The colon-equals sign}
The two characters \(:=\) occur in six roles, and the doubled sign \(::=\)
in a seventh.  The printed form and the position of the sign fix the role;
\cref{tab:p01-w02b-p01-013-roles} lists the regions of each.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)] \emph{Definitional sign printed bare.}  Source presentations
  print the definitional sign as \(:=\) in the notation of their source,
  outside the restoration scopes: for example
  \(\mathcal C:=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}\) in \cref{def:p04-wc-tupling}, and the
  defined connectives \(\neg\varphi:=\varphi\to\bot\), \(\top:=\neg\bot\),
  \(\varphi\lor\psi:=\neg(\neg\varphi\land\neg\psi)\),
  \(\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi:=(\varphi\to\psi)\land(\psi\to\varphi)\) and
  \(?\varphi:=\varphi\mathbin{\vee\!\vee}\neg\varphi\) of
  \cref{def:p08-inq-language}.  The left side is a new name: a constant, a
  function or predicate symbol applied to distinct argument variables, a
  connective applied to formula letters, or an abbreviation local to a
  proof.  The right side does not contain the new name, except in a
  definition by recursion, where the name occurs on the right only applied to
  structurally smaller arguments, as in
  \(\mathcal A_x(UV):=\mathsf S\mathcal A_x(U)\mathcal A_x(V)\) in
  \cref{sec:p07-combinatory-algorithms}.  The role is that of item~3 of
  \cref{def:p01-w02-p01-025-equivalence-roles} and of the editorial
  \(\mathrel{:\equiv}\): the left expression abbreviates the right one from
  the definition to the end of its region.
  \item[(b)] \emph{Instantiation.}  After ``with'', ``taking'' or
  ``instantiate'', or in the justification column of a derivation,
  \(p:=s\) gives a letter \(p\) of a rule, schema or result already stated the
  value \(s\) for one application, unless the line applies a substitution
  rule to a variable of a displayed context, which is role~(d).  The letter is not a new name, and the
  same letter takes other values in other applications, as in the
  syllogism derivations of \cref{annex:p02-w02-p02-002-twelve}.  The
  same role covers a witness: ``the witness \(z:=y\)'' in the proof of
  \cref{lem:q-dichotomy} gives the bound variable \(z\) of the existential
  formula being proved the value \(y\) for that one inference, and
  ``applied with \(f_0:=f\)'' in \cref{sec:qe-categoricity-02} gives the
  letter \(f_0\) of \cref{thm:back-and-forth-test} the map \(f\).  Inside
  the restoration scopes these lines are printed with the source's
  definitional macro, but their left sides are not new names.  A predicate
  letter that an eliminator or an induction principle binds in its
  displayed statement is such a letter: in
  \cref{annex:p12.har.prime-equality} the lines \(P(z):=(z=x)\),
  \(P(z):=(f x=f z)\) and \(P(z):=(x=z)\) give the letter \(P\) of the
  equality eliminator \(E_{A,x,P}\) three values in three uses, and
  \cref{def:p01-w02c-p01-018-motive-reading} separates such motive lines
  from lines that name a new predicate.  A type-family letter \(P\) with
  values in a universe, bound by a displayed induction principle of a
  higher inductive type, is such a letter as well: ``induct in
  \(P(x):=f(x)=g(x)\)'' in \cref{annex:p07-hit-circle-universal} gives the
  letter \(P\) of the circle eliminator of \cref{def:p07-hit-circle} a
  value for that one induction.  So is the bound letter of an existential
  clause of a stated result: in \cref{annex:p01-w02-p01-023-invariant-principle}
  the line \(P:=\operatorname{Reach}\) names the witness for the inductive
  property \(P\) of clause~3 of
  \cref{thm:p01-w02-p01-023-invariant-principle}.  The same distinction
  applies to interpretation data: when a proof supplies
  \(\chi,\theta_0,\theta\) for one instance of the existence condition
  in \cref{def:p03-w03-p03-046-b-interpretation}, the data letters have
  role~(b).  Freshly named helper formulas in that proof have role~(a).
  In \cref{annex:p03-w03-p03-046-b-permutations} the several reductions
  supply different values for \(\theta\), each for its own interpretation;
  they are not competing definitions of one permanent abbreviation.
  \item[(c)] \emph{Update.}  A bracket written after an expression for a
  function, tuple, sequence, assignment, valuation or environment, as in
  \(\bar a[i{:=}b]\), the coordinate replacement
  \(x[k_0:=a_0,\ldots,k_{m-1}:=a_{m-1}]\) of \cref{sec:p08-n-b-006-02}, or
  \(\eta[Z:=U]\) in \cref{sec:p09-n-c-018-bradfield-stirling}, denotes the
  function that agrees with the given one except at the listed arguments,
  where it takes the listed values.  The phrases ``under \(X:=S\)'' and
  ``with \(Z:=U\)'' in \cref{sec:p09-presentation-n-c-018-emerson-jutla}
  and \cref{sec:p09-n-c-018-bradfield-stirling} evaluate a formula
  or play a game in the updated valuation.  An assignment can also be
  specified inside a satisfaction assertion:
  \(\mathcal A\models\varphi[\bar v:=\bar a]\) in
  \cref{annex:p03-w03-p03-038-pointed-ef} means that an assignment sends
  \(v_i\) to the domain element \(a_i\).  The displayed variables contain
  all free variables of \(\varphi\), so the other coordinates do not
  affect truth.  This is a semantic assignment specification; the domain
  elements need not be denoted by terms of the language.
  \item[(d)] \emph{Substitution.}  A bracket written after a formula or
  another syntactic object, as in \(B[x:=t]\) in
  \cref{sec:p12-small-clause-standard}, denotes the result of replacing the
  free occurrences of the variable by the term under the substitution rules
  of the region; the inline phrase ``the substitution \(x_0:=\bar n\)''
  names the same operation.  A relation letter can similarly be replaced
  by a formula of matching arity, with capture avoided, as in the
  substitutions of \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-060-substitution} and its annex
  proof; the result is a formula, not a changed interpretation of the
  original formula.  So does an inline phrase that applies the
  substitution rule of a calculus to a sequent or derivation whose
  variable context is displayed, with a variable declared in that context
  on the left and a term on the right.  In \cref{annex:p10.adjoint.2} the
  phrases ``first \(x:=M\) \ldots\ then \(y:=N\)'', ``substitute
  \(x':=x\)'', ``instantiate \(x:=M\)'' (twice) and ``substitute \(z:=M\)
  \ldots\ and then \(w:=N\)'' each apply the rule Subst of
  \cref{def:p10-contextual-first-order} to such a sequent or derivation:
  the variable is replaced by the term throughout the sequent and its
  declaration leaves the context.  The verb ``instantiate'' does not make
  such a line role~(b), because the variable is declared in the context of
  the sequent and is not a letter of the displayed statement of a rule.
  \item[(e)] \emph{Assignment command.}  In the program text of a
  programming language, \(x:=e\) is a command of the object language.  It
  denotes a change of state: the state after it sends \(x\) to the value
  that \(e\) has in the state before it, and leaves every other variable
  unchanged.  The heap lookup \(x:=[e]\) reads the cell at address \(e\);
  the heap mutation \([e]:=v\), such as \([i+1]:=j\) in the list-reversal
  program of \cref{sec:p09-presentation-n-c-020}, writes it.  A variable or
  cell may be assigned again, and the right side may contain it, as in
  \(i:=i+1\) in \cref{sec:p09-presentation-n-c-019}.
  \item[(f)] \emph{Algorithm step.}  In an algorithm stated in the
  metalanguage, the step \(t:=t+1\) of the depth-first search in
  \cref{sec:p02-w02-p02-019-two-sat} or
  the step \(C:=E(C)\) of the procedure in
  \cref{sec:p08-ranked-models-rational-closure} stores a value under a name
  of the algorithm; later steps may replace it.  The role is the same inside
  the restoration scopes: in the pseudocode listing of the DPLL procedure in
  \cref{sec:automated-reasoning-01}, the step
  \(r:=\mathrm{DPLL}(\ldots)\) stores the result of the first recursive
  call, and every call of the procedure stores its own result under \(r\).
  \item[(g)] \emph{Grammar production.}  \(C::=\alpha_1\mid\cdots\mid
  \alpha_k\) states that the syntactic category \(C\) is the least set of
  expressions closed under the alternatives \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k\); the
  category may recur on the right.  A production may name several
  metavariables of one category on its left, as \(A,B::=\alpha\mid A\to B\)
  for the types of \cref{sec:normalization-curry-howard-02}.  The role is
  the same inside and outside the restoration scopes; inside them the
  productions are \(\varphi::=p_n\mid\bottom\mid\neg\varphi\mid\cdots\) in
  \cref{sec:prop-syntax-semantics-01}, the type grammar
  \(\alpha,\beta::=\iota\mid o\mid\alpha\to\beta\) in
  \cref{sec:simple-type-theory-01}, the type and term grammars of
  \cref{sec:normalization-curry-howard-02}, and the formula grammars of
  \cref{sec:modal-temporal-epistemic-02}, \cref{sec:relevance-linear-02}
  and \cref{sec:relevance-linear-03}.
\end{enumerate}
Only role~(a), together with the printing \(\mathrel{:=}\) of the
definitional sign inside the restoration scopes, is the definitional sign.
It differs from the others as follows.  It introduces a name that was not available before and fixes its
meaning for the whole region, and replacing the name by its definiens never
changes a truth value (part~1 of \cref{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-colon-equals}).
In~(b) the letter already has its place in a stated schema, and its value
lasts for one application.  In~(c) and~(d) the sign is part of an operation
symbol: the bracket expression denotes a new function or a new syntactic
object, introduces no name, and leaves the expression before the bracket
unchanged (part~2).  In~(e) and~(f) the left side names a storage place
whose content changes; read as a definition, \(i:=i+1\) would define a
number equal to its own successor, and there is none (part~3).  In~(g) a
set is defined inductively, so the left side recurs on the right without a
smaller argument.
\index{colon-equals sign!roles}\index{update of a function}
\index{assignment command}\index{grammar production}
\end{hbconvention}

\section{Recovering the role of an occurrence}
\label{sec:p01-w02b-p01-013-procedure}

\begin{hbmethod}{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-role-reading}{Reading the role of a sign}
\emph{Input:} an occurrence of \(\equiv\), \(\Leftrightarrow\), the
definitional sign, \(:=\) or \(::=\), and its section.
\begin{enumerate}
  \setcounter{enumi}{-1}
  \item If the sign is slashed, run steps~1--7 on the unslashed sign at the
  same place and then apply \cref{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-slashed}.  Stop.
  \item If the sign is printed \(\mathrel{:\equiv}\), \(:=\) or \(::=\),
  decide its role by its printed form and position.  The sign
  \(\mathrel{:\equiv}\) is the definitional sign.  The sign \(::=\) is a
  grammar production, role~(g) of \cref{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-colon-equals},
  inside and outside the restoration scopes described in item~3 of
  \cref{def:p01-w02-p01-025-equivalence-roles}.  A sign \(:=\), inside or
  outside those scopes, takes role~(c) or~(d) of
  \cref{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-colon-equals} if it stands inside a bracket
  written after an expression (update when that expression denotes a
  function, tuple, sequence, assignment, valuation or environment;
  substitution when it is a formula or another syntactic object).
  First recognize a bracket inside a satisfaction assertion that specifies
  domain elements for the variables: this is the assignment reading of
  role~(c), not a substitution of terms.  Role~(d) applies
  also if it stands in an inline phrase that applies the substitution rule
  of the region's calculus to a sequent or derivation whose variable
  context is displayed, with a variable declared in that context on its
  left, whatever verb introduces the phrase (as ``substitute \(z:=M\)'' and
  ``instantiate \(x:=M\)'' in \cref{annex:p10.adjoint.2}); role~(e)
  if it stands in the program text of a programming language; role~(f) if
  it is a step of an algorithm, including a step of a pseudocode listing;
  role~(b) if its left side is a letter of a rule, schema or result applied
  at that place, or the bound variable for which a witness is named; and
  otherwise, with a new name on its left, it is the definitional sign:
  printed \(\mathrel{:=}\) inside the restoration scopes, and role~(a)
  outside them.  For the definitional sign in every printing, the left
  expression is defined to abbreviate the right one.  Stop.
  \item If the sign carries a subscript or superscript, read it as one
  symbol and look it up in the registry under that full form.  The subscript
  is part of a name (\(\equiv_\alpha\), \(\equiv_m\), \(\equiv_{\mathrm j}\),
  \(\equiv_{\mathrm W}\)) or an index of a declared family
  (\(\equiv_E^n\), \(\equiv_k\), \(\equiv_Q\)); it is never a modulus.  Stop.
  \item If a modulus is displayed by \(\pmod{\cdot}\), the role is
  congruence: metalinguistic between numbers (item~6 of
  \cref{def:p01-w02-p01-025-equivalence-roles}) or the object-language
  predicate of \cref{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-congruence-predicate} inside the
  Presburger chapter.  Stop.
  \item If both operands are structures, the role is elementary equivalence.
  Stop.
  \item If the left operand is a function and the right a constant, the role
  is \cref{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-identically-equal}.  Stop.
  \item If the sign is \(\Leftrightarrow\), apply
  \cref{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-statement-iff}, unless the section declares it
  as a connective of its object language (as the internal higher-order
  language of \cref{sec:p10-n-d-023-source-03} does).  Stop.
  \item Otherwise the operands are formulas, terms of an object language,
  concepts, or objects of another kind for which the section declares the
  sign.  If the section's object language lists \(\equiv\) among its
  connectives, it is that connective.  If the section defines \(\equiv\)
  as a relation symbol between terms of its object language, as
  \cref{def:p08-sa-concepts} does for whole individual concepts, it is that
  defined relation.  If the section declares \(\equiv\) as a relation of the
  metalanguage between objects that are neither structures nor formulas, as
  \cref{def:p15-w02b-p15-045-pair} does for measures with the same null
  sets, it is that declared relation.  Otherwise it is the equivalence of
  the section's declared semantics: the two sides hold at the same points of
  every model of that semantics.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Side conditions.}  Step~7 needs the semantics or object language to be
declared before the occurrence; \cref{tab:p01-w02b-p01-013-roles} names the
declaration for every region of the book.  Steps~4 and~5 read the operand
types from the declarations of the operands, not from their letters.  Step~1
reads the kind of the expression before a bracket from its declaration, and
it reads program text only where the section declares the programming
language.  An occurrence in a region of
\cref{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-mention} that is displayed beside a statement of
its role is not run through the steps: its role is the stated one.
\emph{Output:} the role of the occurrence.
\end{hbmethod}

The steps are tried in order.  Step~0 depends only on the printed form of
the sign and hands the occurrence back to the other steps.  Of steps~1--7
at most one applies to a given occurrence, because their conditions exclude
one another: the definitional sign, the forms of \(:=\) and the
subscripted signs are separate printed forms, step~1 assigns each form of
\(:=\) exactly one role because its clauses are tried in the stated order,
and among the bare signs numbers
with a displayed modulus, structures, a function against a constant,
assertions and formulas are five different kinds of object.  Where a section
uses \(\equiv\) both for the equivalence of its semantics and to introduce a
derived operator, as the temporal presentations of
\cref{sec:p09-presentation-n-c-012} do with \(FA\equiv\top\mathbin{\mathsf
U}A\), the two readings agree: once the operator is introduced by the line,
the two sides are true at the same points of every model.

\section{Facts behind the new roles}
\label{sec:p01-w02b-p01-013-facts}

\begin{hblemma}{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-congruence-definable}{proved=annex:p01-w02b-p01-013-congruence-definable}{Congruence predicates are definitional}
Let \(m\geq1\) and \(0\leq r<m\), and let \(C_{m,r}\) be the predicate of
\cref{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-congruence-predicate}.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item For every \(n\in\mathbb N\),
  \(\mathbb N\entails\exists y\,(x=\underline r+m\cdot y)\) under the
  assignment \(x\mapsto n\) iff \(m\) divides the integer \(n-r\).
  \item For every \(n\in\mathbb N\) there is exactly one \(r<m\) for which
  the condition of part~1 holds.
  \item Let \(\mathbb N^{+}\) be the expansion of \((\mathbb
  N;0,1,+,<)\) in which each \(C_{m,r}\) is interpreted by
  \(\{n\in\mathbb N:m\text{ divides }n-r\}\).  For a formula \(\chi\) of the
  expanded language let \(\chi^{\circ}\) replace each atom \(C_{m,r}(t)\) by
  \(\exists y\,(t=\underline r+m\cdot y)\), where \(y\) is a variable that does
  not occur in \(\chi\).  Then for every assignment \(s\),
  \(\mathbb N^{+}\entails\chi[s]\) iff \(\mathbb N\entails\chi^{\circ}[s]\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{hblemma}

Part~3 is what makes the predicates a definitional expansion: adding them
changes no truth value of a formula of the original language and makes no
new relation definable, so \cref{thm:p05-presburger-qe} is a statement about
the original additive structure.

\begin{hblemma}{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-identically-equal}{proved=annex:p01-w02b-p01-013-identically-equal}{Identical equality is pointwise}
Let \(D\) be a nonempty set, \(h:D\to\mathbb C\) and \(c\in\mathbb C\).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \(h\equiv c\) iff \(h\) is the constant function \(\kappa_c\) with
  \(\kappa_c(y)=c\) for all \(y\in D\).
  \item Let \((D,\mathcal D,\mu)\) be a measure space, let \(y_0\in D\) with
  \(\{y_0\}\in\mathcal D\) and \(\mu\{y_0\}=0\), and let \(c'\neq c\).  If
  \(h\equiv c\) and \(h'\) agrees with \(h\) except that \(h'(y_0)=c'\), then
  \(h'=h\) \(\mu\)-almost everywhere and \(h'\not\equiv c\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{hblemma}

Part~2 is the situation of item~3 of \cref{thm:p15-conditional-examples}:
the version \(h\equiv1/2\) and the version altered at \(0\) are both versions
of the conditional mean, but only the first is identically \(1/2\).

\begin{hblemma}{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-contradictory}{proved=annex:p01-w02b-p01-013-contradictory}{Contradictory pairing and nonequivalence}
Let \(C\) be a nonempty set of cases and let \(P,Q\) be propositions, each
true or false in each case.  Call \(P\) and \(Q\) \emph{equivalent over
\(C\)} if they have the same truth value in every case, and
\emph{contradictories over \(C\)} if in every case exactly one of them is
true.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If \(P\) and \(Q\) are contradictories over \(C\), they are not
  equivalent over \(C\).
  \item If some case makes both \(P\) and \(Q\) false, they are not
  contradictories over \(C\); if in addition some case makes exactly one of
  them true, they are neither equivalent nor contradictories over \(C\).
  \item \(Q\) is a contradictory of \(P\) over \(C\) iff \(Q\) is equivalent
  over \(C\) to the negation \(\neg P\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{hblemma}

By parts~1 and~2, the contradictory pairing of
\cref{sec:p13-street-2004-modal-tables} says more than the denial of an
equivalence; by part~3 it is the assertion of an equivalence with a
negation.  So the slashed sign there is not the slashed sign of
\cref{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-slashed} read for a relation.

\begin{hblemma}{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-colon-equals}{proved=annex:p01-w02b-p01-013-colon-equals}{Definition, update and assignment differ}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Let \(\mathcal L\) be a first-order language, let \(t\) be a closed
  \(\mathcal L\)-term and let \(c\) be a constant symbol not in
  \(\mathcal L\), introduced by \(c:=t\).  For an \(\mathcal L\)-structure
  \(\mathcal M\) let \(\mathcal M^{c}\) be its expansion to
  \(\mathcal L\cup\{c\}\) with \(c^{\mathcal M^{c}}=t^{\mathcal M}\).  For a
  formula \(\chi\) of \(\mathcal L\cup\{c\}\) let \(\chi^{t}\) be the result
  of replacing every occurrence of \(c\) in \(\chi\) by \(t\).  Then for
  every assignment \(s\), \(\mathcal M^{c}\entails\chi[s]\) iff
  \(\mathcal M\entails\chi^{t}[s]\).
  \item Let \(f:D\to E\) be a function, let \(a,a'\in D\) and
  \(b,b'\in E\), and let \(f[a:=b]\) be the function on \(D\) with value
  \(b\) at \(a\) and value \(f(y)\) at every \(y\neq a\).  Then
  \(f[a:=f(a)]=f\); \(f[a:=b][a:=b']=f[a:=b']\); if \(a\neq a'\), then
  \(f[a:=b][a':=b']=f[a':=b'][a:=b]\); and \(f[a:=b]=f\) iff \(f(a)=b\).
  \item Let \(V\) be a set of program variables with \(x\in V\), let a state
  be a function \(\sigma:V\to\mathbb N\), and let the command \(x:=x+1\)
  denote the map \(\sigma\mapsto\sigma[x:=\sigma(x)+1]\).  Then the image of
  every state differs from that state, and no natural number \(n\)
  satisfies \(n=n+1\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{hblemma}

Part~1 is the eliminability of a definition: the defined name adds nothing
that its definiens does not already say.  Part~2 shows that an update is an
operation that yields a new function and leaves the old one as it was; the
last clause shows that the update changes nothing exactly when it writes the
value already there.  Part~3 shows that an assignment changes the state, and
that the same line read as a definition would require a number equal to its
successor.

\begin{hbexample}{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-example}{Eleven lines read by the procedure}
(1)~In \cref{def:p05-presburger-example}, \(x\equiv1\pmod3\) carries a
displayed modulus, so step~3 of \cref{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-role-reading}
applies inside the Presburger chapter: it is the atom \(C_{3,1}(x)\).  By
part~3 of \cref{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-congruence-definable} it may be replaced
by \(\exists y\,(x=1+(y+y+y))\) without changing truth in \(\mathbb N\); the
example's formula \(\exists y\,(x=3y+1)\) is this formula with the sum
\(y+y+y\) written \(3y\) and the order of the summands exchanged, which does
not change its value.
(2)~In \cref{thm:p15-characteristic-second-moment}, \(\varphi\equiv1\) has a
characteristic function on the left and a constant on the right, so step~5
applies: \(\varphi(t)=1\) for every real \(t\).
(3)~In \cref{thm:p15-conditional-examples}, \(h\equiv1/2\) likewise means
\(h(y)=1/2\) for every \(y\in[0,1]\), not merely for almost every \(y\).
(4)~In \cref{sec:p08-n-b-006-01}, \(\tau\equiv\sigma\) has two terms as
operands and the section defines the sign as the boxed local equality
\(\Box(\tau=\sigma)\), so step~7 gives whole-concept equality: the two
partial concepts have the same domain of definition and the same values.
It is not elementary equivalence, which step~4 would give only for two
structures.
(5)~\(A\to B\not\equiv B\to A\) in
\cref{thm:p02-w02-p02-005-idiom-equivalences} is slashed; step~0 runs the
procedure on \(A\to B\equiv B\to A\), which falls under the \(\mathsf{CPL}\)
row of \cref{tab:p01-w02b-p01-013-roles}, so the line says that some
valuation gives the two formulas different values.  By contrast
\(X_1bj^a\not\equiv Abj^o\) in \cref{sec:p13-street-2004-modal-tables}
lies in the one region where the slashed sign is the contradictory
pairing.
(6)~In \cref{def:p04-wc-tupling}, \(\mathcal C:=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}\) has
the new name \(\mathcal C\) on its left and stands in no bracket, program
or algorithm, so step~1 gives role~(a): \(\mathcal C\) abbreviates the set
of binary streams wherever it occurs later in the section.  Likewise
\(\neg\varphi:=\varphi\to\bot\) and \(?\varphi:=\varphi\mathbin{\vee\!\vee}
\neg\varphi\) in \cref{def:p08-inq-language} define connectives, and by
part~1 of \cref{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-colon-equals} each defined connective
can be removed by writing out its definiens.
(7)~In \cref{sec:p08-n-b-006-02}, \(x[k_0:=a_0,\ldots,k_{m-1}:=a_{m-1}]\)
stands in a bracket after the sequence \(x\), so step~1 gives role~(c): the
sequence that agrees with \(x\) except at the coordinates
\(k_0,\ldots,k_{m-1}\), where it has the values \(a_0,\ldots,a_{m-1}\).
Nothing is defined, and \(x\) keeps its value (part~2).  In the same way
\(\eta[Z:=U]\) in \cref{sec:p09-n-c-018-bradfield-stirling} is the valuation
\(\eta\) with \(Z\) sent to \(U\).
(8)~In \cref{sec:p09-presentation-n-c-020}, \([i+1]:=j\) stands in the program
text of the heap language, so step~1 gives role~(e): the command writes the
value of \(j\) into the cell at address \(i+1\), the second cell of the
record at \(i\).  The loop runs it once for each record of the list, each
time at another address and with another value, which a definition could
not do.
(9)~In \cref{sec:p09-presentation-n-c-019}, \(i:=i+1\) is a command
(role~(e)); by part~3 of \cref{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-colon-equals} it changes
every state, and read with role~(a) it would assert \(i=i+1\), which no
number satisfies.
(10)~In \cref{sec:automated-reasoning-01}, a restoration scope, the line
\(r:=\mathrm{DPLL}(\ldots)\) of the listing of the DPLL procedure is a step
of an algorithm, so step~1 gives role~(f) and not the definitional sign:
\(r\) holds the answer of the first recursive call, and the next line tests
whether that answer is \(\mathrm{SAT}\).  The procedure calls itself, so the
line is executed once in each call that branches, and the values stored
under \(r\) in different calls may differ.
(11)~In \cref{sec:simple-type-theory-01}, also a restoration scope,
\(\alpha,\beta::=\iota\mid o\mid\alpha\to\beta\) is printed \(::=\), so
step~1 gives role~(g): the types form the least set that contains
\(\iota\) and \(o\) and contains \(\alpha\to\beta\) whenever it contains
\(\alpha\) and \(\beta\).  The letters \(\alpha,\beta\) on the left recur on
the right, and nothing is abbreviated.
\end{hbexample}

\begin{hbwarning}{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-failure}{Readings that change the statement}
Reading \(h\equiv1/2\) as equality almost everywhere weakens item~3 of
\cref{thm:p15-conditional-examples}: by part~2 of
\cref{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-identically-equal} the altered version is equal to
\(h\) almost everywhere, yet the theorem uses the difference between the two
versions at the null point \(0\).  Reading \(x\equiv r\pmod m\) in the
Presburger chapter as a metalinguistic statement about a number loses the
fact that quantifier elimination needs these atoms in the object language.
Reading any of these lines as elementary equivalence or as formula equivalence
is a type error, since their operands are neither two structures nor two
formulas.  Reading \(X_1bj^a\not\equiv Abj^o\) in
\cref{sec:p13-street-2004-modal-tables} as the mere failure of equivalence
weakens it: by part~2 of \cref{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-contradictory} two
propositions can fail to be equivalent without being contradictories.
Reading \(\tau\equiv\sigma\) in \cref{sec:p08-n-b-006-01} as local
equality at one index loses the replacement property that
\cref{thm:p08-sa-descriptions} grants only to whole-concept equality.
Reading the assignment \(i:=i+1\) of \cref{sec:p09-presentation-n-c-019}
as a definition makes the line false of every number (part~3 of
\cref{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-colon-equals}); reading the bare
\(\mathcal C:=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}\) of \cref{def:p04-wc-tupling} as an
assignment suggests that \(\mathcal C\) could later hold another set, which
the section never allows; and reading the update \(g[x:=d]\) of
\cref{sec:p11-kripke-fixed-points} as a definition of \(g\) would change the
assignment \(g\) that the quantifier clause keeps fixed (part~2).  Inside
the restoration scopes the printing alone does not decide the role: reading
the step \(r:=\mathrm{DPLL}(\ldots)\) of \cref{sec:automated-reasoning-01}
as a definition would give \(r\) one fixed value for the whole section,
whereas each call of the procedure stores its own answer, and reading the
witness \(z:=y\) in the proof of \cref{lem:q-dichotomy} as a definition
would introduce a new name where the inference only needs a value for the
bound variable \(z\).  The procedure has a limit: it reads operand types from
declarations, so an occurrence whose operands are not declared before it has
no determined role, and the registry gives none.
\end{hbwarning}

\section{All roles of the three glyphs}
\label{sec:p01-w02b-p01-013-table}

\Cref{tab:p01-w02b-p01-013-roles}, with the additional occurrence regions
in \cref{tab:p01-w02b-p01-013-route-instances}, lists every role in which the three
glyphs and the colon-equals sign occur in the reading text, with or without
a slash, subscript or superscript, together with the kind of operands and the regions where the
role is in force.  A region named by a section or chapter includes its
subsections; annex proofs are named where the role is used in them.  An
occurrence outside the listed regions with a given operand kind does not
have that role.  Inside a listed region an occurrence whose operands are of
another kind takes the role that the procedure of
\cref{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-role-reading} gives for that kind; for
instance, two structures related by \(\equiv\) in the sections of
\cref{sec:p02-w02-p02-005-idioms} that follow Barwise are elementarily
equivalent, although the chapter is a region of the \(\mathsf{CPL}\) role.
The definitional sign has a single role throughout.  It is printed
\(\mathrel{:\equiv}\) in editorial text, \(\mathrel{:=}\) inside the
restoration scopes, and a bare \(:=\) in the source presentations named in
its third row; the six other roles of \(:=\) and \(::=\) of
\cref{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-colon-equals} follow it, and inside a region of
several of them step~1 of \cref{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-role-reading} decides
each occurrence.  For the colon-equals sign the position of the occurrence
plays the part of the operand kind.  The rows of roles~(b), (f) and~(g)
name their regions inside the restoration scopes as well as outside them:
the second row gives the definitional sign for the restoration scopes as a
whole, and an occurrence inside those scopes that stands in an algorithm,
is a grammar production or names an instance or a witness lies in one of
the regions listed in the row of its role.  The one remaining printing of
\(a\mathrel{:=}b\) inside the restoration scopes, in the register of
\cref{app:notation}, is a displayed register entry
(\cref{def:p01-w02b-p01-013-mention}).  An occurrence in a region of that
convention which displays, names or quotes a sign takes the role that the
convention gives it, and the rows below list only the regions where a sign
is used in its role; thus the chapter \cref{sec:p01-w02c-p01-018-motives}
appears in the row of role~(b) for the uses in items~1--3 of
\cref{thm:p01-w02c-p01-018-equality-instances} alone.

\paragraph{Relationships.}  The roles that relate formulas are linked by the
passages of \cref{thm:p01-w02-p01-025-equiv-bridges}: first-order equivalence
restricts to CPL equivalence on propositional formulas, alpha-equivalence
implies first-order equivalence, and equivalent sentences keep their truth
value between elementarily equivalent structures.  The Presburger predicate
and the metalinguistic congruence are linked by part~1 of
\cref{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-congruence-definable}.  Identical equality implies
equality almost everywhere and not conversely, by
\cref{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-identically-equal}.  Whole-concept equality
\(\tau\equiv\sigma\) implies the boxed local equality and hence the local
equality \(\tau=\sigma\) at each index, and not conversely, by
\cref{thm:p08-sa-incidence} and \cref{thm:p08-sa-descriptions}.  A
contradictory pairing implies the failure of equivalence of the paired
propositions and is the equivalence of one with the negation of the other,
by \cref{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-contradictory}.  Each slashed row denies the
relation of the row it is read from.  The three printings of the
definitional sign are one role; an update or a substitution may occur on
the right of a definition, and an assignment command may occur inside a
defined program, but none of them is itself a definition, by
\cref{thm:p01-w02b-p01-013-colon-equals}.  The update row has as instances the
registered coordinate updates \(\bar a[i{:=}b]\) of
\cref{def:p02-w02-p02-008-bits} and \(x[n:=y]\) of
\cref{def:p08-montague-pragmatic-extension}.  No other passage between two rows of
\cref{tab:p01-w02b-p01-013-roles} holds in general, because the operands
are of different kinds.

and the contradictory pairing are recorded as they are fixed in
