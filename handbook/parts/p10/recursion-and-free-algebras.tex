\subsubsection{Recursion objects and free equational algebras}
\label{sec:p10-recursion-free-algebras}

Write \(\operatorname{End}(\mathcal E)\) for the category of
objects equipped with an endomap; its morphisms commute with
the endomaps. This notation avoids confusing the source's
endomap-category symbol with a subobject classifier.
The source's free-recursion assumption supplies a free object
\(X\times\omega\) over each \(X\).
At \(X=1\) it gives an object \(\omega\), a zero map, and successor,
with the natural numbers recursion universal property.

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p10-recursion-lifting}{proved=annex:p10-recursion-lifting}{Geometric transport of recursion}
If \(\mathcal E\) has the stated recursion object and
\(f:\mathcal F\to\mathcal E\) is a geometric morphism, then
\(f^*\omega\), with the transported zero and successor,
is a recursion object in \(\mathcal F\).
The free endomap object over \(B\in\mathcal F\) is
\(B\times f^*\omega\).
The source's additional requirement that the forgetful functor
not be an equivalence holds exactly when the target topos is
nondegenerate. It is not preserved into a degenerate target.
\end{hbtheorem}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p10-free-equational-algebras}{proved=annex:p10-free-equational-algebras}{Free groups and rings internally}
In a topos with a natural numbers object, every object has a free
algebra for a fixed finite signature of finite-arity operations
and a fixed finite family of equational axioms.
In particular free groups, free unital rings, and free
commutative unital rings exist.
Their forgetful functors have the corresponding free left adjoints.
Geometric inverse image preserves these free algebras and their
specified generators.
This statement does not assert the existence of free objects for
nonequational classes such as fields.
\end{hbtheorem}

The finite-lattice construction of
\cref{thm:p10-spectrum-lattice} did not need this additional
recursion assumption: its idempotent lattice operations allowed
a finite-subobject construction directly. The group and ring
construction below instead uses finite words and their parsing,
for which the natural numbers object supplies the required
internal recursion.
