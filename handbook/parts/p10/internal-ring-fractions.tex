\subsubsection{Internal fractions and locality}
\label{sec:p10-internal-ring-fractions}

Work in the topos and internal intuitionistic language of the preceding
development. A ring object is commutative and has a multiplicative unit,
and ring homomorphisms preserve that unit.
A multiplicative subobject \(S\hookrightarrow A\) contains \(1\) and is
closed under multiplication. No complement, decidability of membership,
or choice of representatives is assumed.

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p10-internal-ring-fractions}{proved=annex:p10-internal-ring-fractions}{Internal localization by a multiplicative subobject}
On \(A\times S\), define
\[
 (a,s)\sim(b,t)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists u\in S\;\;u(at-bs)=0.
\]
This is an internal equivalence relation. Its effective quotient,
denoted \(S^{-1}A\), is a ring object with
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 a/s+b/t&=(at+bs)/(st),&
 (a/s)(b/t)&=(ab)/(st),\\
 -(a/s)&=(-a)/s,&
 0&=0/1,\qquad 1=1/1.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The arrow \(i:A\to S^{-1}A\), \(i(a)=a/1\), inverts every member of \(S\).
For every ring homomorphism \(h:A\to B\) whose restriction to \(S\)
takes values in the units of \(B\), there is exactly one ring
homomorphism \(\bar h:S^{-1}A\to B\) with \(\bar h\,i=h\);
it is given by
\(\bar h(a/s)=h(a)h(s)^{-1}\).
The construction is preserved, up to its canonical isomorphism,
by inverse-image functors of geometric morphisms.
\end{hbtheorem}

Here \(a/s\) denotes the equivalence class of the pair \((a,s)\),
not an operation requiring a previously chosen inverse in \(A\).
Representatives of quotient elements are available locally through
the quotient epimorphism; no global section of that epimorphism is used.

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p10-internal-prime-locality}{proved=annex:p10-internal-prime-locality}{Localization at a prime predicate is local}
Suppose \(S\hookrightarrow A\) satisfies the four prime-predicate
conditions of \cref{def:p10-lawvere-sheaves-prime}:
\(1\in S\), \(0\notin S\),
\(ab\in S\) if and only if \(a,b\in S\), and
\(a+b\in S\) implies \(a\in S\) or \(b\in S\).
Then, internally,
\[
 a/s\text{ is a unit in }S^{-1}A
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\in S.
\]
The unit subobject of \(S^{-1}A\) is prime in the same sense.
In particular \(0\neq1\), and the ring is local according to the
source's definition.

If \(B\) is a local ring object and \(h:A\to B\) is a unital
homomorphism, its inverse image of the unit subobject is such a prime
subobject \(S\). The factorization
\(A\to S^{-1}A\to B\) given by localization is unique, and the second
homomorphism reflects units as well as preserving them.
\end{hbtheorem}

These statements give fractions and locality for an actual prime
subobject, including prime subobjects varying over another topos.
The universal global spectrum additionally requires the generic prime
and the comparison between its sheaf presentation and these
localizations. That global classification is a distinct assertion
from the universal property of a localization at a specified \(S\).
