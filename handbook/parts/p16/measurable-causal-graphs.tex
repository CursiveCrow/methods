% P16: density-free Markov construction and the general mediation bridge.
\section{Measurable causal graphs and their global Markov property}
\label{sec:p16-measurable-causal-graphs}

Lauritzen formulates the equivalence of directed Markov properties using
recursive probability kernels, separately from the special case of
densities \cite[Theorems~1.28, 2.44 and 2.49; Propositions~2.46--2.48,
pp.~19--20, 65--68]{Lauritzen2019GraphicalLectures2020}.
This section supplies the complete standard Borel construction and closes
the general-space graphical premise in the mediation treatment. Its graphs
have finitely many vertices; their coordinate spaces need not be finite,
countable, or dominated by a common product measure.

\subsection{Conditional independence as a kernel identity}

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p16-measurable-ci}{Conditional independence of random elements}
Let \(X,Y,Z\) be random elements in nonempty standard Borel spaces on
\((\Omega,\mathcal F,P)\). Write \(X\perp Y\mid Z\) when
\[
 E[u(X)v(Y)\mid Z]=E[u(X)\mid Z]E[v(Y)\mid Z]
\]
almost surely for every bounded measurable real \(u,v\).
The sigma-algebra in \(E[\cdot\mid Z]\) is \(\sigma(Z)\).
For vertex sets, this notation applies to their indexed tuples; the empty
tuple is a constant. Regular conditional laws mean the common probability
kernels of \cref{sec:p15-regular-conditional-laws}.
\end{hbdefinition}

The same relation for sub-sigma-algebras of an arbitrary probability
space, with its inference rules proved without any standard Borel
hypothesis, is \cref{def:p15-w02c-p15-009-sigma-ci}; item~7 of
\cref{thm:p15-w02c-p15-009-sigma-ci-criteria} shows that the two
definitions agree for standard Borel random elements.

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p16-measurable-ci-calculus}{proved=annex:p16.measurable-ci-calculus}{Common product kernels and the semigraphoid operations}
Conditional independence is equivalent to both of the following:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(E[u(X)\mid Y,Z]=E[u(X)\mid Z]\) for all bounded measurable \(u\).
\item A regular conditional law of \((X,Y)\) given \(Z=z\) equals
the product of its two conditional marginal laws, outside one null set
of \(z\), simultaneously as probability measures.
\end{enumerate}
For any additional standard Borel random element \(W\), it satisfies
symmetry, decomposition, weak union and contraction:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 X\perp(Y,W)\mid Z&\Longrightarrow X\perp Y\mid Z,\\
 X\perp(Y,W)\mid Z&\Longrightarrow X\perp Y\mid(Z,W),\\
 X\perp Y\mid Z,\quad X\perp W\mid(Y,Z)
     &\Longrightarrow X\perp(Y,W)\mid Z.
\end{aligned}
\]
Measurable functions may be applied to either independent tuple, also
using the conditioning tuple as an argument: if \(X\perp Y\mid Z\) and
\(g:S_X\times S_Z\to S'\), \(h:S_Y\times S_Z\to S''\) are jointly
measurable into standard Borel spaces, then
\(g(X,Z)\perp h(Y,Z)\mid Z\). Outside one null set of \(z\), the regular
conditional law of \((g(X,Z),h(Y,Z))\) given \(Z=z\) is the image of the
conditional product law at \(z\) under
\((x,y)\mapsto(g(x,z),h(y,z))\). Taking \(h(y,z)=y\) transforms only
one side. No positivity, intersection axiom or assumption of a density
is required.
\end{hbtheorem}

\subsection{A finite graph of probability kernels}

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p16-measurable-dag-kernels}{An everywhere specified kernel model}
Let \(G=(N,E)\) be a finite DAG and let \(S_v\) be a nonempty standard
Borel space for every \(v\in N\). Assign a probability kernel
\(K_v:S_{\operatorname{pa}(v)}\leadsto S_v\), specified at every parent
argument. Products of coordinate spaces carry their product sigma-algebras.
The empty coordinate product is a singleton; the empty graph carries its
point-mass law.
Starting in a topological order, successively combine the current joint
measure with \(K_v(x_{\operatorname{pa}(v)},dx_v)\) using
\cref{thm:p15-kernel-joint-measure}. Denote the resulting law by \(P_K\).
The notation
\[
 P_K(dx_N)=\mathop{\prod}_{v\in N}^{\mathrm{top}}
                         K_v(x_{\operatorname{pa}(v)},dx_v)
\]
means this iterated kernel construction, not multiplication of densities.
A local kernel on a parent stratum absent under \(P_K\) is still part of
this model. An observed probability law alone does not specify that choice.
\end{hbdefinition}

Lauritzen calls kernels of this kind a system adapted to the DAG
\(\mathcal D\): each \(P^v\) is a Markov kernel from
\(\mathcal X_{\operatorname{pa}(v)}\) to \(\mathcal X_v\). Their recursive
combination \(P=\circledast_{v\in V}P^v\), formed along a topological
numbering, is well defined and does not depend on the numbering (his
Proposition~2.46), and his Definition~2.47 names the result: the
\emph{Bayesian network} \((\mathcal D,P)\) generated by \(\mathcal D\)
and \((P^v)_{v\in V}\) is the recursive combination of the adapted
kernels with respect to \(\mathcal D\)
\cite[Proposition~2.46 and Definition~2.47, p.~66]{Lauritzen2019GraphicalLectures2020}.
In the notation here, \(\mathcal D=G\), \(V=N\),
\(\mathcal X_v=S_v\), \(P^v=K_v\) and \(P=P_K\), and
\cref{thm:p16-measurable-kernel-construction} supplies the existence and
order independence of Proposition~2.46. The object of the source's
definition is the pair of the graph and the combined law; the kernels
generate it but are not retained in it. The model of
\cref{def:p16-measurable-dag-kernels} departs from that definition by
keeping the kernels as part of the model, because an intervention
evaluates a kernel at its specified arguments, including arguments that
the combined law never produces. Two kernel models may therefore generate
the same Bayesian network and still differ as causal models. For
instance, let \(G\) be \(a\to b\) with \(S_a=S_b=\{0,1\}\),
\(K_a=\delta_0\) and \(K_b(0,\cdot)=\delta_0\) in both models, and
\(K_b(1,\cdot)=\delta_0\) in one model and \(\delta_1\) in the other.
In both, the combination gives \(P_K=\delta_{(0,0)}\), since the first
coordinate is \(0\) with probability one and only \(K_b(0,\cdot)\) is
used. Replacing \(K_a\) by \(\delta_1\), the constant intervention
\(a=1\) of \cref{thm:p16-measurable-interventions}, gives
\(\delta_{(1,0)}\) in the first model and \(\delta_{(1,1)}\) in the
second. This is the measurable form of item~2 of
\cref{thm:p16-causal-nonidentification}. When only the observational
Markov properties below are in question, the kernels enter only through
\(P_K\), and the source's pair \((G,P_K)\) carries all that is used.
\index{Bayesian network}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p16-measurable-kernel-construction}{proved=annex:p16.measurable-kernel-construction}{Existence, order independence and ancestral marginals}
The iterated construction gives a unique probability measure and does not
depend on the topological order. Nonnegative measurable functions admit
the corresponding iterated integral; signed functions do so when absolutely
integrable. If \(D\subseteq N\) is ancestral, its marginal is the kernel
model on \(G[D]\) with exactly the retained kernels. In particular every
topological prefix has that marginal, and
\(K_v(X_{\operatorname{pa}(v)},\cdot)\) is a conditional law of \(X_v\)
given all its predecessors. All assertions hold for singular laws.
\end{hbtheorem}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p16-measurable-kernel-randomization}{proved=annex:p16.measurable-kernel-randomization}{Jointly measurable realization of an entire kernel}
For any probability kernel \(K:T\leadsto S\), with \(S\) nonempty
standard Borel and \(T\) measurable, there is a jointly measurable
\(F:T\times(0,1)\to S\) such that \(F(t,U)\) has law \(K(t,\cdot)\)
for every \(t\), when \(U\) is uniform on \((0,1)\).
Consequently every finite standard Borel kernel model has a representation
\[
 X_v=F_v(X_{\operatorname{pa}(v)},U_v)
\]
with mutually independent uniform \(U_v\). This is an existence result;
the representation, and its joint coupling of different intervention
worlds, need not be unique.
\end{hbtheorem}

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p16-measurable-markov-properties}{Ordered, local and global Markov properties}
For a topological order let \(\operatorname{pr}(v)\) be the predecessors
of \(v\), and let \(\operatorname{nd}(v)\) exclude both \(v\) and its
strict descendants. The ordered property is
\[
 X_v\perp X_{\operatorname{pr}(v)\setminus\operatorname{pa}(v)}
               \mid X_{\operatorname{pa}(v)}.
\]
The local property replaces predecessors by nondescendants. The global
property asserts \(X_A\perp X_B\mid X_C\) whenever the disjoint sets
\(A,B,C\) satisfy d-separation in \(G\). Paths, colliders and ancestral
moral separation have exactly their earlier graph-theoretic definitions.
\end{hbdefinition}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p16-measurable-global-markov}{proved=annex:p16.measurable-global-markov}{Global, local, ordered and recursive-kernel equivalence}
For a probability law on the finite product of the declared standard
Borel spaces, the global, local and ordered Markov properties are
equivalent to existence of an adapted recursive kernel representation.
In particular every law \(P_K\) is globally Markov. The proof handles
a conditioned terminal vertex explicitly using weak union and contraction;
it never conditions by dividing a joint density.
\end{hbtheorem}

\subsection{The same equivalence for an abstract semigraphoid}

\Cref{thm:p16-measurable-global-markov} concerns a probability law.
Lauritzen states the equivalence of the global, local and ordered
properties for any independence model obeying four formal axioms, and
proves it from those axioms alone; the probabilistic case is then one
instance \cite[Sec.~2.5.1, p.~52; Sec.~2.6.2, pp.~64--66;
Theorem~2.49, p.~67]{Lauritzen2019GraphicalLectures2020}.
Such a model may come from a probability law, from graph separation,
or from another relation of irrelevance; no measure, kernel or
conditional expectation is involved. This subsection keeps the source's
notation: \(D\) is the DAG, \(V\) its finite vertex set and
\(\perp_\sigma\) the independence model.

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p15-w02b-p15-009-semigraphoid}{Independence models and semigraphoids}
Let \(V\) be a finite set. An \emph{independence model}
\(\perp_\sigma\) over \(V\) is a ternary relation on subsets of \(V\);
write \(A\perp_\sigma B\mid C\) when the triple \((A,B,C)\) is in the
relation. It is a \emph{semigraphoid} if, for all mutually disjoint
\(A,B,C,D\subseteq V\), empty sets included,
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(S1)] \(A\perp_\sigma B\mid C\) implies \(B\perp_\sigma A\mid C\)
  (symmetry);
\item[(S2)] \(A\perp_\sigma(B\cup D)\mid C\) implies
  \(A\perp_\sigma B\mid C\) and \(A\perp_\sigma D\mid C\)
  (decomposition);
\item[(S3)] \(A\perp_\sigma(B\cup D)\mid C\) implies
  \(A\perp_\sigma B\mid(C\cup D)\) (weak union);
\item[(S4)] \(A\perp_\sigma B\mid C\) and
  \(A\perp_\sigma D\mid(B\cup C)\) imply
  \(A\perp_\sigma(B\cup D)\mid C\) (contraction).
\end{enumerate}
A single vertex \(\alpha\) in a slot stands for \(\{\alpha\}\).
For a probability law on a product indexed by \(V\), the relation
defined by \(A\perp_\sigma B\mid C\) when \(X_A\perp X_B\mid X_C\) in
the sense of \cref{def:p16-measurable-ci} is a semigraphoid, by
\cref{thm:p16-measurable-ci-calculus}; the source records the same
fact \cite[Example~2.24, pp.~52--53]{Lauritzen2019GraphicalLectures2020}.
No intersection or composition axiom is assumed.
\end{hbdefinition}

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p15-w02b-p15-009-directed-markov}{Directed Markov properties of an independence model}
Let \(D\) be a DAG with vertex set \(V\). Write \(\operatorname{pa}(\alpha)\)
for the parents of \(\alpha\), \(\operatorname{de}(\alpha)\) for the
vertices reached from \(\alpha\) by directed paths of positive length,
and \(\operatorname{nd}(\alpha)=V\setminus(\{\alpha\}\cup\operatorname{de}(\alpha))\).
For a topological order \(<\) put
\(\operatorname{pr}(\alpha)=\{\beta\in V:\beta<\alpha\}\).
An independence model \(\perp_\sigma\) over \(V\) obeys
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(O)] the ordered Markov property relative to \((D,<)\) if
  \(\alpha\perp_\sigma\operatorname{pr}(\alpha)\setminus\operatorname{pa}(\alpha)
   \mid\operatorname{pa}(\alpha)\) for every \(\alpha\in V\);
\item[(L)] the local Markov property relative to \(D\) if
  \(\alpha\perp_\sigma\operatorname{nd}(\alpha)\setminus\operatorname{pa}(\alpha)
   \mid\operatorname{pa}(\alpha)\) for every \(\alpha\in V\);
\item[(G)] the global Markov property relative to \(D\) if
  \(A\mathrel{\perp_D}B\mid S\) implies \(A\perp_\sigma B\mid S\)
  for all pairwise disjoint \(A,B,S\subseteq V\).
\end{enumerate}
Here \(A\mathrel{\perp_D}B\mid S\) is d-separation in \(D\) as in
\cref{def:p16-active-path}. By \cref{thm:p16-moral-separation-bridge}
it is equivalent to separation of \(A\) from \(B\) after deleting \(S\)
from the moral graph of the subgraph induced on the ancestors of
\(A\cup B\cup S\).
\end{hbdefinition}

\begin{hblemma}{thm:p15-w02b-p15-009-semigraphoid-trivial}{proved=annex:p15-w02b-p15-009-sg-trivial}{Statements with an empty side follow from the ordered property}
Let \(\perp_\sigma\) be a semigraphoid over a nonempty \(V\) that obeys
(O) relative to some topological order of a DAG \(D\) on \(V\). Then
\(A\perp_\sigma\varnothing\mid C\) and \(\varnothing\perp_\sigma A\mid C\)
for all disjoint \(A,C\subseteq V\).
\end{hblemma}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p15-w02b-p15-009-semigraphoid-markov}{proved=annex:p15-w02b-p15-009-sg-markov}{Markov equivalence for a semigraphoid}
Let \(D\) be a DAG on a nonempty finite set \(V\), let \(<\) be a
topological order of \(D\), and let \(\perp_\sigma\) be a semigraphoid
over \(V\). Then (G), (L) and (O) relative to \((D,<)\) are
equivalent. The implications (G)\(\Rightarrow\)(L),
(L)\(\Rightarrow\)(O) and (O)\(\Rightarrow\)(L) hold also when
\(V=\varnothing\). Only (S1)--(S4) are used; no intersection axiom,
positivity or probability is assumed
\cite[Theorem~2.44, pp.~65--66]{Lauritzen2019GraphicalLectures2020}.
\end{hbtheorem}

\begin{hbcorollary}{thm:p15-w02b-p15-009-order-invariance}{proved=annex:p15-w02b-p15-009-sg-order}{The ordered property does not depend on the order}
If a semigraphoid \(\perp_\sigma\) obeys (O) relative to one
topological order of \(D\), it obeys (O) relative to every
topological order of \(D\)
\cite[Corollary~2.45, p.~66]{Lauritzen2019GraphicalLectures2020}.
This includes \(V=\varnothing\), where (O) is vacuous.
\end{hbcorollary}

For a probability law, \cref{def:p15-w02b-p15-009-semigraphoid} and
\cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-009-semigraphoid-markov} give the equivalence of
(G), (L) and (O) in \cref{thm:p16-measurable-global-markov} without any
kernel; kernels enter only for the recursive representation. The source
derives its probabilistic Theorem~2.49 from Theorem~2.44 in this way
\cite[Theorem~2.49, p.~67]{Lauritzen2019GraphicalLectures2020}.
When \(V=\varnothing\) the probabilistic relation contains
\(\varnothing\perp_\sigma\varnothing\mid\varnothing\), since the empty
tuple is a constant.

\begin{hbexample}{def:p15-w02b-p15-009-chain-derivation}{A global statement from the axioms}
Take the chain \(a\to b\to c\to d\) with order \(a<b<c<d\).
Property (O) asserts
\(a\perp_\sigma\varnothing\mid\varnothing\),
\(b\perp_\sigma\varnothing\mid a\),
\(c\perp_\sigma a\mid b\) and
\(d\perp_\sigma\{a,b\}\mid c\).
The set \(\{b\}\) d-separates \(a\) from \(\{c,d\}\), so (G) requires
\(a\perp_\sigma\{c,d\}\mid b\). It follows from the axioms in four steps.
(S1) applied to \(c\perp_\sigma a\mid b\) gives
\(a\perp_\sigma c\mid b\). (S3) applied to
\(d\perp_\sigma\{a,b\}\mid c\), keeping \(\{a\}\) and moving \(\{b\}\)
into the conditioning set, gives \(d\perp_\sigma a\mid\{b,c\}\); then
(S1) gives \(a\perp_\sigma d\mid\{b,c\}\). Finally (S4), with
\(\{a\},\{c\},\{b\},\{d\}\) in the axiom's slots \(A,B,C,D\), gives
\(a\perp_\sigma\{c,d\}\mid b\). The derivation holds in every
semigraphoid, whether its statements come from a probability law, from
graph separation, or from another irrelevance relation. By contrast,
in the collider \(a\to b\leftarrow c\) the path through \(b\) is active
given \(\{b\}\), so (G) asks nothing of \(a\perp_\sigma c\mid b\) there.
\end{hbexample}

\begin{hbwarning}{def:p15-w02b-p15-009-empty-vertex-set}{The empty vertex set}
The hypothesis \(V\neq\varnothing\) in
\cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-009-semigraphoid-markov} cannot be dropped for
an arbitrary semigraphoid. The empty relation over \(V=\varnothing\)
satisfies (S1)--(S4) vacuously, and (O) and (L) are vacuous. But
\(\varnothing\mathrel{\perp_D}\varnothing\mid\varnothing\) holds, since
there is no path between empty sets, so (G) requires
\(\varnothing\perp_\sigma\varnothing\mid\varnothing\), which the empty
relation lacks. The source's statement does not single out this case.
For \(V=\varnothing\) the three properties are equivalent exactly for
the semigraphoids containing
\(\varnothing\perp_\sigma\varnothing\mid\varnothing\). For nonempty
\(V\), \cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-009-semigraphoid-trivial} derives every
statement with an empty side from (O).
\end{hbwarning}

\subsection{The contrast with undirected graphs, and densities}

Lauritzen introduces the directed properties with the remark that, unlike
the undirected ones, they are all equivalent without any further
regularity assumption; his Theorem~2.44 is the precise statement
\cite[Sec.~2.6.2, p.~65]{Lauritzen2019GraphicalLectures2020}, and
\cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-009-semigraphoid-markov} is its version here. For
an undirected graph he defines the global property by graph separation
(his Definition~2.35, p.~59) and adds the pairwise and local properties
(p.~60). He proves that the global property implies the local, and the
local the pairwise, for every distribution and indeed for every
semigraphoid (Proposition~2.37, p.~60). The converse implications are his
Theorem~2.38, which he credits to Pearl and Paz (1987): the three
properties are equivalent when the independence model of the distribution
is a graphoid, that is, when it also satisfies the intersection axiom
(S5) (pp.~52 and~60--61). The regularity in question is this extra
hypothesis. The definition and theorem below state the undirected
properties in the present measurable language and show, by a three-vertex
example that is this handbook's, that without it the undirected
properties come apart.

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p15-w02c-p15-009-undirected-markov}{Undirected Markov properties}
Let \(\mathcal G=(V,E)\) be a finite undirected graph and let
\((X_\alpha)_{\alpha\in V}\) be random elements in nonempty standard
Borel spaces, with \(X_A\) the indexed tuple for \(A\subseteq V\). For a
vertex \(\alpha\), the boundary \(\operatorname{bd}(\alpha)\) is the set
of vertices adjacent to \(\alpha\), and the closure is
\(\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)=\{\alpha\}\cup\operatorname{bd}(\alpha)\).
For pairwise disjoint \(A,B,S\subseteq V\), \(S\) \emph{separates}
\(A\) from \(B\) when, after the vertices of \(S\) and their incident
edges are deleted, no path joins a vertex of \(A\) to a vertex of \(B\),
as in \cref{def:p16-ancestral-moral-graph}. With conditional independence
as in \cref{def:p16-measurable-ci}, the family obeys
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(P)] the \emph{pairwise} Markov property relative to \(\mathcal G\)
  if \(X_\alpha\perp X_\beta\mid X_{V\setminus\{\alpha,\beta\}}\) for every
  pair of distinct nonadjacent vertices \(\alpha,\beta\);
\item[(L)] the \emph{local} Markov property relative to \(\mathcal G\) if
  \(X_\alpha\perp X_{V\setminus\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)}
  \mid X_{\operatorname{bd}(\alpha)}\) for every vertex \(\alpha\);
\item[(G)] the \emph{global} Markov property relative to \(\mathcal G\) if
  \(X_A\perp X_B\mid X_S\) whenever \(S\) separates \(A\) from \(B\).
\end{enumerate}
These are the source's (P), (L) and (G) for an undirected graph
\cite[Definition~2.35, p.~59; p.~60]{Lauritzen2019GraphicalLectures2020}.
\index{Markov property!undirected}
\end{hbdefinition}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p15-w02c-p15-009-undirected-gap}{proved=annex:p15-w02c-p15-009-undirected-gap}{The undirected properties need a regularity assumption}
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every finite undirected graph and every family as in
  \cref{def:p15-w02c-p15-009-undirected-markov}, (G) implies (L) and (L)
  implies (P).
\item Let \(V=\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}\), \(E=\varnothing\), let \(B\) be
  a fair bit, and put \(X_\alpha=X_\beta=X_\gamma=B\). This family obeys
  (P) relative to the edgeless graph but neither (L) nor (G). Its
  conditional independence relation satisfies
  \(X_\alpha\perp X_\beta\mid X_\gamma\) and
  \(X_\alpha\perp X_\gamma\mid X_\beta\) but not
  \(X_\alpha\perp(X_\beta,X_\gamma)\), so it violates the intersection
  axiom.
\item For a DAG, by contrast, the global, local and ordered properties of
  \cref{def:p16-measurable-markov-properties} are equivalent for every
  probability law on the product of the coordinate spaces, with no
  positivity, density or intersection hypothesis.
\end{enumerate}
\end{hbtheorem}

Item~1 is the source's Proposition~2.37 and item~3 restates
\cref{thm:p16-measurable-global-markov}. Item~2 shows how a degenerate
law defeats the undirected converse: each variable is a function of
every other, so conditioning on any one of them makes the
other two constant, while unconditionally they are perfectly dependent.
The same law obeys none of the directed properties for the edgeless DAG
on \(V\), whose ordered property already asks that \(X_\beta\) be
independent of \(X_\alpha\); the directed equivalence is not a claim
that every law is Markov for every DAG, only that the directed
properties never separate from one another.

Lauritzen then turns to densities
\cite[p.~68]{Lauritzen2019GraphicalLectures2020}. A Bayesian network
defined by structural equations need not have a density with respect to
any product measure, but when it has one the results can be strengthened.
He says that \(P\) admits a \emph{recursive density factorization}, or
has property (F), relative to \(\mathcal D\) when there are nonnegative
functions \(k^\alpha\) on \(\mathcal X_\alpha\times
\mathcal X_{\operatorname{pa}(\alpha)}\), each integrating to one in its
first argument with respect to a measure \(\mu_\alpha\), such that \(P\)
has density \(f(x)=\prod_{\alpha\in V}k^\alpha(x_\alpha,x_{\operatorname{pa}(\alpha)})\)
with respect to \(\mu=\bigotimes_{\alpha\in V}\mu_\alpha\). His
Proposition~2.51 identifies (F) with the statement that the \(k^\alpha\)
are densities of the conditional distributions of \(X_\alpha\) given its
parents; his Lemma~2.52 states that (F) gives a factorization along the
moral graph \(\mathcal D^m\) and hence the undirected global property
relative to \(\mathcal D^m\); and his Theorem~2.53 adds (F) to the
equivalent conditions (R), (G), (L) and (O) for every \(P\) that has a
density with respect to a product measure (p.~68). These are the source's
statements; this section proves none of them and uses none. In the finite
setting of \cref{sec:p16-finite-causal-models} every law has a density
with respect to counting measure on the product of the value sets, which
is a product measure, and for a model with mutually independent
disturbances the factorization of \cref{thm:p16-causal-factorization} is
property (F) for that measure, with the structural kernels as the
functions \(k^\alpha\). The measurable results above need no such
density: the chain law of \cref{thm:p16-measurable-graph-examples} has
none with respect to any sigma-finite product of coordinate measures, yet
it is globally Markov.

\subsection{Structural interventions and response backgrounds}

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p16-measurable-scm}{A measurable structural model and interventions}
For the same finite DAG, let \(U_v\) be mutually independent random
elements in nonempty standard Borel spaces with specified laws, and let
\(f_v(x_{\operatorname{pa}(v)},u_v)\) be jointly measurable total functions.
An intervention on a fixed set \(I\) replaces its equations by constants
\(t\in S_I\). All other functions and the joint background law remain
fixed. A history-based stochastic intervention may instead use a specified
probability kernel on earlier coordinates, with independent new randomness.
The augmented graph includes exactly the permitted earlier inputs of
those replacement kernels, and is required to remain acyclic.
\end{hbdefinition}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p16-measurable-interventions}{proved=annex:p16.measurable-interventions}{Measurable solutions and intervened kernel laws}
Every such model and intervention has a unique solution. For constant
interventions that solution is jointly measurable in \((t,U)\), and its
law is a probability kernel in \(t\). Its law is the recursive kernel
construction with the acted-on kernels replaced by \(\delta_{t_v}\);
along a topological order, \(K_v(X^t_{\operatorname{pa}(v)},\cdot)\),
or \(\delta_{t_v}\) at an intervened vertex, is a regular conditional
law of \(X^t_v\) given all its predecessors. Here
\(K_v(x_{\operatorname{pa}(v)},B)=\int\mathbf 1_B(f_v(x_{\operatorname{pa}(v)},u))\,\lambda_v(du)\),
with \(\lambda_v\) the law of \(U_v\).
History-based replacement kernels also admit the independent-noise
construction: with a fresh independent uniform variable for each replaced
equation, the intervened law is the kernel model on the augmented graph
whose kernels are the unmodified \(K_w\) and the replacement kernels,
and it obeys the global Markov property of that graph.
Consistency and composition at attained values hold pointwise. Structural
interventions are defined even when the prescribed event had zero
observed probability.
\end{hbtheorem}

\begin{hblemma}{thm:p15-w02c-p15-009-root-exposure}{proved=annex:p15-w02c-p15-009-root-exposure}{Exposing private disturbance roots preserves d-separation}
Let \(G\) be a finite DAG on \(N\), and let \(G^+\) be obtained by adding,
for each \(v\) in some subset of \(N\), a new vertex \(\varepsilon_v\) and
the single edge \(\varepsilon_v\to v\). For pairwise disjoint
\(A,B,C\subseteq N\),
\(A\mathrel{\perp_G}B\mid C\) if and only if
\(A\mathrel{\perp_{G^+}}B\mid C\). Consequently, by
\cref{thm:p16-moral-separation-bridge} applied to \(G^+\), the separation
\(A\mathrel{\perp_G}B\mid C\) implies that no component of the moral
graph of \(G^+\) restricted to
\(\operatorname{an}_{G^+}(A\cup B\cup C)\), after deletion of \(C\),
meets both \(A\) and \(B\).
\end{hblemma}

\begin{hblemma}{thm:p16-measurable-response-component}{proved=annex:p16.measurable-response-component}{A separated response component gives a uniform response family}
Consider a family of measurable structural models with the same finite
DAG \(H\), same independent disturbances, and a standard Borel parameter
\(t\) in a nonempty parameter space entering their jointly measurable functions.
Let \(Y\) be a response vertex and \(B,C\) disjoint vertex sets excluding
\(Y\). Suppose \(B,C\) are pointwise unchanged as \(t\) varies and \(Y\) is d-separated
from \(B\) given \(C\) in \(H\). Expose each disturbance as a private
root. In the ancestral moral graph for \(Y,B,C\), remove \(C\) and
take the component containing \(Y\). Let \(R\) be the tuple of private
disturbances in that component. Then there is one jointly measurable
function \(F\) with
\[
 Y^t=F(t,C,R)\quad\hbox{pointwise for every }t,
 \qquad R\perp B\mid C.
\]
Thus one conditional product law of \((R,B)\) given \(C\) licenses
integration of this entire family at a measurable random parameter
depending on \((B,C)\). Precisely, let \(\kappa\) be a regular
conditional law of \(R\) given \(C\), let \(\lambda\) be one of \(B\)
given \(C\), and let \(\theta\) be a measurable map from the space of
\((B,C)\) to the parameter space. Then \(\kappa(C,\cdot)\) is also a
regular conditional law of \(R\) given \((B,C)\), and for every bounded
measurable \(\varphi\), almost surely,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E\bigl[\varphi(Y^{\theta(B,C)})\bigm|B,C\bigr]
   &=\int\varphi(F(\theta(B,C),C,r))\,\kappa(C,dr),\\
 E\bigl[\varphi(Y^{\theta(B,C)})\bigm|C\bigr]
   &=\int\!\!\int\varphi(F(\theta(b,C),C,r))\,
                  \kappa(C,dr)\,\lambda(C,db),
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(Y^{\theta(B,C)}\) is the family evaluated at the random
parameter, outcome by outcome. The same holds for real \(\varphi\) with
\(E|\varphi(Y^{\theta(B,C)})|<\infty\), using signed inner integrals
where the absolute ones are finite. Separate exceptional sets for each
parameter are not substituted along a diagonal.
\end{hblemma}

\subsection{The general-space mediation graph corollary}

\begin{hbdefinition}{def:p16-measurable-mediation-data}{Graph, observations and common support measures}
Use the measurable structural model above. Let \(A,Y\) be distinct
observed vertices, with \(Y\) real-valued, and \(M\) a nonempty set of
other observed intermediate vertices, each a descendant of \(A\) and
an ancestor of \(Y\). Let observed covariate sets \(W_0,W_1,W_2,W_3\)
exclude \(A,M,Y\). Assume exactly the four separations in
\cref{thm:p16-mediation-four-set-criterion}, with
\(W_0,W_1,W_3\) nondescendants of \(A\) and \(W_2\) nondescendants
of \(M\). For the formulas, remove from \(W_1,W_3\) vertices already
in \(W_0\), and from \(W_2\) vertices already in \(W_0\cup W_1\).
This leaves each of the four conditioning sets unchanged. Put
\(C=W_0\), \(S=(W_0,W_1)\), and \(T=(A,W_0,W_1,W_2)\).
Fix exposure values \(a,b\). Define the jointly evaluated variables
\(M_b,Y_{a,m},Y_{a,M_b}\) using the intervention construction.

Let \(\nu\) be the law of \(C\), and choose regular observed kernels
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \gamma_1(c,dw_1)&=\mathcal L(W_1\mid C=c),\\
 \gamma_3(c,dw_3)&=\mathcal L(W_3\mid C=c),\\
 \gamma_2^a(c,w_1,dw_2)&=\mathcal L(W_2\mid A=a,C=c,W_1=w_1),\\
 \rho_a(c,w_1,w_2,dm)&=\mathcal L(M\mid A=a,C=c,W_1=w_1,W_2=w_2),\\
 Q^{\mathrm{obs}}_a(c,w_1,w_2,m,dy)
   &=\mathcal L(Y\mid A=a,C=c,W_1=w_1,W_2=w_2,M=m).
\end{aligned}
\]
The covariate reductions make the input tuples have distinct coordinates.
Require positive conditional exposure atoms
\[
 P(A=a\mid C,W_1)>0\quad\nu\gamma_1\hbox{-almost everywhere},
 \qquad P(A=b\mid C,W_3)>0\quad\nu\gamma_3\hbox{-almost everywhere}.
\]
The candidate reference-mediator kernel is
\[
 \eta_b(c,dm)=\int\mathcal L(M\mid A=b,C=c,W_3=w_3)(dm)
                                                    \gamma_3(c,dw_3).
\]
Require
\[
 \eta_b(c,\cdot)\ll\rho_a(c,w_1,w_2,\cdot)
 \quad \nu\gamma_1\gamma_2^a\hbox{-almost everywhere}.        \tag{MS}
\]
Every kernel denotes a probability measure at each argument, with arbitrary
measurable defaults at null observation arguments. Condition (MS) transfers
the relevant common almost-everywhere equalities to the target measure.
\end{hbdefinition}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p16-measurable-mediation-fields}{proved=annex:p16.measurable-mediation-fields}{The graph supplies the uniform premises, not merely pointwise independence}
Under the stated structural graph and its nondescendant/separation
conditions, there are standard Borel disturbance tuples \(R_0,R_1,R_2\)
and jointly measurable response functions such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(Y_{a,m}=F_0(a,m,C,R_0)\) for all \(a,m\), and
      \(R_0\perp M_b\mid C\) for each fixed \(b\).
\item \(Y_{a,m}=F_1(a,m,S,R_1)\) for all \(a,m\), and
      \(R_1\perp A\mid S\).
\item \(Y_m=F_2(m,T,R_2)\) for all \(m\), and
      \(R_2\perp M\mid T\).
\end{enumerate}
Each independence is an equality of conditional joint measures outside
one conditioning-null set. The first statement gives the uniform
cross-world condition of \cref{thm:p16-mediation-kernel-formula}; the
second and third license the two different observational replacements.
Also \(M_b\perp A\mid(C,W_3)\), so the displayed observed kernel
\(\eta_b\) is a version of \(\mathcal L(M_b\mid C)\) under the
exposure support condition.
\end{hbtheorem}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p16-measurable-mediation-corollary}{proved=annex:p16.measurable-mediation-corollary}{The exact general mediation integral under the source graph criterion}
Under the graph, observation and support hypotheses above, every bounded
measurable \(\varphi\) satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E[\varphi(Y_{a,M_b})]
 =\int\!\int\!\int\!\int\!\int
 &\varphi(y)Q^{\mathrm{obs}}_a(c,w_1,w_2,m,dy)\eta_b(c,dm)\\
 &\gamma_2^a(c,w_1,dw_2)\gamma_1(c,dw_1)\nu(dc).
\end{aligned}
\]
This identifies the entire nested response law. For the mean require
\(E|Y|<\infty\) and finiteness of the same displayed absolute response
integral. Applying the result to the exposure pairs needed in the
definitions identifies the corresponding natural direct and indirect
effects. Only a finite union of conditioning-null sets is needed for
the finitely many chosen pairs.

The variables and mediator may be continuous, discrete, mixed, or
deterministically related. A fixed exposure point with no conditional
atom is outside this observational corollary's stated support domain;
no arbitrary conditional version at that point is claimed identifiable.
The structural intervention and the experimental kernel formula still
exist there. This extends the earlier finite graphical corollary without
assuming a product dominating measure or pointwise-to-random-index substitution.
\end{hbtheorem}

\subsection{Singular examples and the boundary of observational support}

\begin{hbtheorem}{thm:p16-measurable-graph-examples}{proved=annex:p16.measurable-graph-examples}{A singular Markov model and a continuous-mediator calculation}
Let \(U\) be uniform on \((0,1)\), and define \(X=U,Z=X,Y=Z\)
on the chain \(X\to Z\to Y\). Its law is globally Markov and is
concentrated on the diagonal. It is not absolutely continuous with
respect to any sigma-finite product of three coordinate measures.
Nevertheless its kernels and all fixed structural interventions exist.

For a mediation example take independent \(A\), a fair bit, and
uniform \(U\in(0,1)\), with
\[
 M=\begin{cases}U,&A=0,\\\sqrt U,&A=1,\end{cases}
 \qquad Y=A+M+AM.
\]
The mediator laws have densities \(1\) and \(2m\) on \((0,1)\),
and the response is deterministic conditional on its parents. The general
corollary applies with empty covariate sets and \(a_0=0,a_1=1\), and gives
\[
 \theta_{00}=1/2,\quad\theta_{01}=2/3,
 \quad\theta_{10}=2,\quad\theta_{11}=7/3.
\]
Thus PNDE is \(3/2\), TNIE is \(1/3\), and TE is \(11/6\).
Point-mediator observational identification is a different question from
this supported integral over mediator values.

Even a strictly positive observed density need not identify a fixed
non-atomic exposure point. For independent uniform \(A,M\in(0,1)\)
and an independent Bernoulli\((1/4)\) bit \(E\), compare
\(Y=E\oplus\mathbf1_{\{A=1/2\}}\) with \(Y=E\).
The same permitted mediation graph satisfies the four separations and
the observed laws agree with positive densities. Their natural direct
effects from \(1/4\) to \(1/2\) are respectively \(1/2\) and zero.
Thus a point-density assertion cannot replace the exposure-atom or
other explicitly justified identifying premises.
\end{hbtheorem}

The standard Borel and finite-graph hypotheses identify the exact
existence and graphical scope proved here. Arbitrary measurable target
spaces, infinite graphs, cyclic equations, and unrestricted conditioning
at null exposure values are separate mathematical problems.
