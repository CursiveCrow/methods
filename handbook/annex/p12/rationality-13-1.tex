\begin{annexitem}{annex:p12.r131.checkerboard}{thm:p12-r131-checkerboard}{The checkerboard invariant}
\begin{proof}
Index cells by $(i,j)\in\{1,\ldots,8\}^2$ and color them by
the parity of $i+j$. Every row has four cells of each parity, so
the intact board has $32$ of each. The removed cells $(1,8)$ and
$(8,1)$ both have odd parity. There remain $32$ even-parity and $30$
odd-parity cells. Two edge-adjacent cells differ by $1$ in exactly one
coordinate, so have opposite parity. A disjoint exact tiling by $31$
dominoes would therefore have $31$ cells of each parity, contrary to
the actual counts. Rotation of a permitted domino does not alter
this invariant; nonadjacent or overlapping coverage is outside its
assumptions.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p12.r131.free-ride}{thm:p12-r131-free-ride}{The Euler consequence and its limits}
\begin{proof}
If $x\in R\cap Q$, then $x\in R\subseteq P$ and $x\in Q$,
giving $x\in P\cap Q$, contrary to the empty-intersection premise.
Thus no such $x$ exists. To refute nonemptiness, let $R=\varnothing$,
$P=\{a\}$ and $Q=\{b\}$ in $U=\{a,b\}$. Both premises hold,
but $R$ is empty and not equal to $P$. Even when nonemptiness is added,
$U=\{a,b,c\}$, $R=\{a\}$, $P=\{a,c\}$ and $Q=\{b\}$
satisfy the premises without $R=P$. These countermodels also show
why curve sizes cannot silently be read as additional set assertions.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p12.r131.cardinality}{thm:p12-r131-cardinality}{Reading the shaded spider example in both directions}
\begin{proof}
The missing zones with membership in $R$ but not in $P$ force
$R\setminus P=\varnothing$. The drawn $P\cap Q$ zone is shaded
and no spider has a foot there. Every spider's habitat is disjoint
from that zone; the shading condition therefore makes it empty.
Let $s_i$ now denote the element assigned by an admissible spider
map to the handle with that index. Injectivity makes these three
elements pairwise distinct. The first two habitats put $s_1,s_2$
in $R$. The third puts $s_3$ in $(P\setminus R)\cup Q$;
since $R\subseteq P$ and $P\cap Q=\varnothing$, it is outside
$R$. Shading of $R$ requires $R\subseteq\{s_1,s_2,s_3\}$.
Hence $R=\{s_1,s_2\}$.

Conversely, suppose the displayed set and existential assertion
holds. Assign each spider handle to the corresponding witness.
Pairwise distinctness supplies injectivity; the stated memberships
supply the habitat conditions. Inclusion makes every omitted
$R$-outside-$P$ zone empty. Disjointness makes the shaded overlap
empty, and $R=\{s_1,s_2\}$ gives its shading condition. There
are no other shaded constraints. Thus the map witnesses diagram
truth. Extra elements outside these shaded regions violate none of
the conditions. For example, taking an additional element in
$P\setminus R$ leaves the assertion true, so $P$ is not thereby
bounded by the number of spider handles.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p12.r131.rules}{thm:p12-r131-rules}{The actual selected spider transformations}
\begin{proof}
For AddFeet, fix any interpretation satisfying $d$ and an injective
witness map $\Sigma$. Enlarging one habitat enlarges its interpreted
union, so its original witness still belongs. The witness set, shading
and missing zones are unchanged, as are every other habitat. The same
map proves the conclusion. For nonreversibility, take disjoint
$A=\{a\}$, $B=\{b\}$ and one spider whose original habitat is
$A\cap B$. That assertion is false. Enlarge its habitat to include
$A\setminus B$: the new assertion is true by choosing $a$.
All four Boolean zones can be drawn, including the empty intersection,
so this is a legitimate full-zone unshaded example.

For SplitSpider, the distinguished witness in a model of $d$ lies
in the union of the zones in $\eta(s)$. The partition into $r$ and
$\eta(s)\setminus r$ is exhaustive. It therefore lies in at least
one corresponding interpreted union, and the same witness map proves
that branch. Conversely, a map for either branch satisfies the original
wider habitat and all unchanged conditions, so proves $d$. The
nonempty proper-subset side condition makes both replacement habitats
syntactically legitimate. Witness maps need not be shared between the
two existential alternatives.

For RemoveShading, an original witness map satisfies an upper-bound
condition on a union containing the smaller shaded union; therefore
it satisfies the latter as well. No other truth condition changes.

For IntroduceShadedZone, the formerly missing zone denotes the empty
set in every model of the premise, so drawing it shaded with no new
foot leaves the same witness map valid. Conversely, in a model of the
conclusion every witness still inhabits only an original zone. Boolean
membership zones are pairwise disjoint, so none of those witnesses can
belong to the newly drawn zone. Its shading condition forces it to be
empty. It therefore meets the former missing-zone condition as well.
All remaining conditions agree, establishing both directions.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p12.r131.hyperproof}{thm:p12-r131-hyperproof}{The necessary graphical observation}
\begin{proof}
The observation supplies $\operatorname{Dodec}(c)$. By the implication
premise, $\operatorname{Dodec}(d)$ follows. Under the declared convention
that two blocks with this shape satisfy $\operatorname{SameShape}$,
the requested conclusion follows. This inference has not treated an
unknown graphical property as observed.

For a countermodel to the sentences alone, let $c$ be a small cube and
$d$ a tetrahedron. Then $\operatorname{Small}(c)$ is true and
$\operatorname{Dodec}(c)$ is false, so
$\operatorname{Dodec}(c)\to\operatorname{Dodec}(d)$ is true. The
shapes are different. Thus the extra licensed observation, not
smallness or the vacuous implication, does the essential work.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p12.r131.odd-sum}{thm:p12-r131-odd-sum}{A verified recursive lcut, not extrapolation from a picture}
\begin{proof}
Let $D_0=\varnothing$. For $k\geq1$, the difference
$L_k=D_k\setminus D_{k-1}$ consists of the row
$\{(i,k):1\leq i\leq k\}$ and the column
$\{(k,j):1\leq j<k\}$. These two sets are disjoint with
$k$ and $k-1$ elements respectively. Thus $L_k$ has $2k-1$
elements, and $D_k$ is the disjoint union of $D_{k-1}$ and $L_k$.

Define the proof-producing procedure at input $0$ to return the empty
partition of $D_0$. At input $n+1$, apply the stated disjoint split
to $D_{n+1}$, recursively produce the partition of $D_n$, and append
$L_{n+1}$. Induction on the input proves termination, since every
recursive call decreases it, and correctness: the base case partitions
the empty set; if the recursive output partitions $D_n$, appending
the disjoint difference partitions $D_{n+1}$. The procedure neither
duplicates nor discards any dot. This proves the uniform condition
for all standard natural-number inputs; the recurrence is not merely
postulated to have that condition.

Counting this established partition gives
$n^2=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(2k-1)$, because $D_n$ has $n^2$ ordered
pairs. At $n=0$ both sides are zero. The version stopping at $D_1$
uses it as the final one-dot layer and makes exactly $n-1$ splits
for positive $n$, as is verified by the same decreasing recurrence.
In particular, input $6$ uses five cuts before the one-dot base. This
establishes the actual generalization obligation without confusing
finite examples, a guessed program and the verified recursive family.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p12.r131.speedith}{thm:p12-r131-speedith}{Both proof branches and the converse countermodel}
\begin{proof}
Choose distinct initial witnesses $t_1,t_2$, with $t_2\in A\cap B$.
If $t_1\in A\setminus B$, assign $u_1=t_1$ and $u_2=t_2$.
If $t_1\in B\setminus A$, assign $u_1=t_2$ and $u_2=t_1$.
In each exhaustive case $u_1\in A$, $u_2\in B$ and the witnesses
are distinct. The two cases are the SplitSpider alternatives. In
each case the two habitat enlargements are licensed AddFeet steps:
a singleton exclusive zone is enlarged to the corresponding whole
set, and the intersection zone to the other whole set. Renaming
handles does not change an existential assertion. Thus both branches
weaken to the same target, and their disjunction collapses by classical
idempotency. The implication of that target by itself is true under
every interpretation, giving the source's final closing step.

For failure of the converse let $U=\{a,b\}$, $A=\{a\}$ and
$B=\{b\}$. The final assertion has distinct witnesses $u_1=a$
and $u_2=b$. The initial assertion cannot have its required witness
in $A\cap B=\varnothing$. Consequently the sequence loses
information exactly as its habitat-weakening steps suggest.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}
