\label{sec:annex-p15-rationality-4-4}

These are explicit finite semantic reconstructions of the chapter's

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.rat44.coherence}{thm:p15-rat44-coherence-method}{Finite separation and zero-weight recursion}
\begin{proof}
For unconditional events a portfolio has gain \(s\cdot(q-p)\) at
the constituent with vector \(q\). If \(p\) is a convex combination
of the constituent vectors, the weighted mean of each such gain is
zero, so its range contains zero. Conversely let \(p\) be outside
their compact convex hull, and choose the closest hull point \(y\)
in Euclidean norm. Put \(d=p-y\ne0\). For every hull point \(q\),
the segment from \(y\) to \(q\) stays in the hull. Minimality at
its initial endpoint gives \(d\cdot(q-y)\leq0\). Hence
\(d\cdot(q-p)\leq-\|d\|^2<0\) at every constituent. The stake
vector \(d\) gives a sure loss, contradicting coherence.
This proves the convex-hull and linear-weight characterizations.

For a conditional family let
\(g(\omega)=(\mathbf1_{H_i}(\mathbf1_{E_i}-p_i))_i\).
For a stake vector \(s\), the gain is
\(G_s(\omega)=s\cdot g(\omega)\), and it vanishes at every
\(\omega\) outside the active union \(H_s\), since every term
with \(s_i\ne0\) then has \(\mathbf1_{H_i}(\omega)=0\).

\emph{Necessity: a coherent family is never rejected.} Let the family
be coherent, and let \(J\) be the nonempty set of assessments remaining
at some stage, with union \(H_J=\bigcup_{i\in J}H_i\), which is
nonempty because every \(H_i\) is. The stage's system asks for a
probability vector \(\lambda\) on \(H_J\) with
\(\sum_{\omega\in H_J}\lambda_\omega g_J(\omega)=0\), where
\(g_J\) keeps the coordinates in \(J\); that is, it asks whether
\(0\) lies in the convex hull \(K_J\) of the finite set
\(\{g_J(\omega):\omega\in H_J\}\). Suppose it does not.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(K_J\) is compact and convex, so it has a point \(y\) of least
      Euclidean norm, and \(y\ne0\). Put \(d=-y\).
\item For \(q\in K_J\) and \(t\in(0,1]\) the point \(y+t(q-y)\) lies
      in \(K_J\), so
      \(\|y+t(q-y)\|^2-\|y\|^2=2t\,y\cdot(q-y)+t^2\|q-y\|^2\geq0\).
      Dividing by \(t\) and letting \(t\to0\) gives
      \(y\cdot(q-y)\geq0\), hence
      \(d\cdot q\leq d\cdot y=-\|y\|^2<0\).
\item Take the stakes \(s_i=d_i\) for \(i\in J\) and \(s_i=0\)
      otherwise. This portfolio is nonzero because \(d\ne0\). For every
      \(\omega\in H_J\), step~2 with \(q=g_J(\omega)\) gives
      \(G_s(\omega)=d\cdot g_J(\omega)\leq-\|y\|^2<0\).
\item The active union satisfies \(H_s\subseteq H_J\) and is nonempty.
      By step~3, \(\max_{H_s}G_s<0\), so the portfolio is a sure loss on
      its active union. Its bets are a finite subfamily of the coherent
      family, contradicting the definition of coherence.
\end{enumerate}
Hence every stage reached from a coherent family is feasible. At the
first stage \(J\) is the whole family. At each later stage \(J\) is the
set of assessments whose conditioning event has maximum weight zero
over the previous stage's solution set; this set is again a subfamily
of the original family, and a portfolio on it is a portfolio of the
original family with zero stakes elsewhere and the same active union.
So the argument applies at every stage, and the procedure never
reaches the rejection branch. Each stage removes at least one assessment
(shown in the next paragraph), so it ends with no assessment remaining,
which is the acceptance branch. The first stage alone does not decide
coherence: its condition is necessary, but it can be met while a
subfamily whose conditioning events all receive weight zero is
incoherent, which is why the later stages exist. For instance, the
prices \(p(A)=0\), \(p(B\mid A)=0.3\) and
\(p((A\mathbin{\&}B)\mid A)=0.7\) pass the first stage with all
weight on \(\sim A\), but the stage on \(A\) needs a probability on
\(A\) giving \(B\) both weight \(0.3\) and weight \(0.7\), which is
impossible; directly, staking \(1\) on the second conditional and
\(-1\) on the third yields the sure gain \(0.4\) throughout \(A\).

At any feasible stage, for every conditioning event not forced to have
zero weight, choose a feasible solution giving it positive weight.
Averaging these finitely many solutions produces one feasible measure
positive on all such events simultaneously. At least one exists: a
normalized measure on the union of the remaining events cannot assign
zero to every event in that union. Record this measure as a stratum
and recurse on the events forced to weight zero. Each iteration removes
at least one assessment, proving termination in at most the family size.
If the procedure accepts, every \(H_i\) has a first stratum giving
it positive weight, and at that stratum
\[
 \mu(E_i\mathbin{\&}H_i)=p_i\mu(H_i).
\]
For any nonzero portfolio choose the earliest stratum positive on any
of its conditioning events. Every later-first event has zero weight
there; every earlier-first event is absent from this portfolio by
earliestness; and each first-at-this-stage bet has zero mean gain.
The portfolio therefore has mean zero under this stratum, with
positive mass on its active union. Its gain cannot be strictly positive
or strictly negative throughout that union. This proves coherence.

This argument also proves a useful construction principle: any finite
sequence of normalized measures covering every assessed conditioning
event defines coherent prices by its first-positive-stratum ratios.
Later measures may put weight on events covered earlier; they do not
change those already determined prices. Such strata are mathematical
witnesses of conditional coherence, not an assumption of positive
ordinary mass on every possible event.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.rat44.laws}{thm:p15-rat44-basic-laws}{Complement, identity, and ordinary conditional ratios}
\begin{proof}
On \(A\), the gain for \(A\mid A\) is \(s(1-p)\); coherence
for arbitrary signed stakes forces \(p=1\). For \(\sim A\mid A\)
the gain is \(-sp\), forcing \(p=0\). For complementary
conditionals with prices \(q,r\), the unit-stake sum is the constant
\(1-q-r\) on \(A\). It can avoid a strict sign only if
\(q+r=1\). The convex-hull representation on unconditional
constituents gives normalization, nonnegativity, and finite additivity,
hence the ordinary event identities.

Include \(A,A\mathbin{\&}B\), and \(B\mid A\) in one assessed
family. The first feasible constituent measure in the conditional
method has the specified unconditional event probabilities and
satisfies
\(\mu(A\mathbin{\&}B)-p(B\mid A)\mu(A)=0\).
If \(p(A)>0\), division gives the stated quotient. If \(p(A)=0\),
both unconditional terms are zero and the equation alone imposes
no conditional value. The recursive test still applies on \(A\):
if \(A\) logically implies \(B\), its price must be one; if their
intersection is impossible, the price must be zero. Null probability
is therefore not permission to disregard logical constraints.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15-w02b-p15-038-dutch-book-bridge}{thm:p15-w02b-p15-038-dutch-book-bridge}{Stake reversal, full conditioning events, and called-off gains}
\begin{proof}
Throughout, for a stake vector \(s\) the gain is
\(G_s=\sum_is_i\mathbf1_{H_i}(\mathbf1_{E_i}-p_i)\) and
\(H_s=\bigcup_{i:s_i\ne0}H_i\). If \(s\ne0\), some \(s_i\ne0\) and
\(H_i\ne\varnothing\), so \(H_s\) is a nonempty finite set and
\(G_s\) attains a minimum and a maximum on it.

\emph{Item 1.} The stake vector \(-s\) has the same active event,
\(H_{-s}=H_s\), and gain \(G_{-s}=-G_s\). Coherence fails exactly when
some nonzero \(s\) has \(\max_{H_s}G_s<0\) or \(\min_{H_s}G_s>0\).
In the first case \(G_s<0\) throughout \(H_s\). In the second case
\(G_{-s}<0\) throughout \(H_{-s}\), and \(-s\ne0\). Conversely, if a
nonzero \(s\) has \(G_s<0\) at every point of the nonempty finite set
\(H_s\), then \(\max_{H_s}G_s<0\) and coherence fails. Hence the family
is coherent if and only if no nonzero \(s\) has \(G_s<0\) throughout
\(H_s\).

\emph{Item 2.} Let every \(H_i=\Omega\). For \(s\ne0\),
\(H_s=\Omega\), and \(\mathbf1_{H_i}=\mathbf1\) gives
\(G_s=\sum_is_i(\mathbf1_{E_i}-p_i\mathbf1)=\sum_is_ib_{E_i}\). Every
portfolio \(x=\sum_{j=1}^ma_jb_{A_j}\) of
\cref{def:p17-finite-price-coherence} built from the listed events
equals some \(G_s\): group the terms by event and let \(s_i\) be the
sum of the coefficients \(a_j\) with \(A_j=E_i\). A portfolio with
\(x<0\) in every state is not zero, since the zero portfolio vanishes,
so the corresponding \(s\) is nonzero. Conversely each \(G_s\) is
such a portfolio. By item~1, coherence is therefore equivalent to the
absence of a portfolio negative in every state of \(\Omega\). When the
family lists every \(E\subseteq\Omega\) once, put \(S=\Omega\) and
\(q(E)=p(E\mid\Omega)\). The previous sentence says that coherence is
coherence of \(q\) against a strict sure loss in the sense of
\cref{def:p17-finite-price-coherence}, and
\cref{thm:p17-finite-price-coherence} says that this holds if and only
if \(q\) is a probability measure on \(2^\Omega\).

\emph{Item 3.} If \(\omega\notin U\), then
\(\mathbf1_{H_i}(\omega)=0\) for every \(i\), so \(G_s(\omega)=0\).
Since \(\Omega\setminus U\) is nonempty, no \(G_s\) is negative at
every state of \(\Omega\). For the example, the family is
\(B\mid A\), \(\sim B\mid A\) with both prices \(0.6\). For stakes
\((s_1,s_2)\),
\[
 G_s=\mathbf1_A\bigl(s_1\mathbf1_B+s_2\mathbf1_{\sim B}
        -0.6(s_1+s_2)\bigr),
\]
which is zero off \(A\), and \(\Omega\setminus A\ne\varnothing\).
For \(s=(1,1)\), \(\mathbf1_B+\mathbf1_{\sim B}=\mathbf1\) gives
\(G_s=\mathbf1_A(1-1.2)=-0.2\,\mathbf1_A\), and \(H_s=A\ne\varnothing\).
So \(\max_{H_s}G_s=-0.2<0\) and the family is not coherent.

\emph{Item 4.} For the single assessment \(p(E\mid\Omega)=0\) and a
stake \(s\ne0\), \(H_s=\Omega\) and \(G_s=s\mathbf1_E\). Since
\(E\ne\varnothing\) and \(\Omega\setminus E\ne\varnothing\), \(G_s\)
takes both values \(s\) and \(0\). If \(s>0\) its minimum is \(0\)
and maximum \(s\); if \(s<0\) its minimum is \(s\) and maximum \(0\).
Either way \(\min\leq0\leq\max\), so the assessment is coherent. The
stake \(-1\) gives \(G_{-1}=-\mathbf1_E\), which is \(0\) off \(E\) and
\(-1\) on \(E\).

The same stake, placed on an assessment \(p(A\mid\Omega)=0\) inside
any larger family with zero stakes elsewhere, has active event
\(\Omega\) and gain \(-\mathbf1_A\), nonpositive everywhere and
negative on \(A\) whenever \(A\ne\varnothing\). A criterion that
excluded every portfolio nonpositive on its active event and negative
somewhere on it would therefore reject every family containing such an
assessment, which is the claim in the comparison following the theorem.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.rat44.paradox}{thm:p15-rat44-paradox}{Explicit endpoint witnesses and material implication}
\begin{proof}
Let the four \(A,B\) constituents be possible. For any desired
\(x,z\in[0,1]\), let the first measure be supported on \(\sim A\),
with weights \(x,1-x\) on \(\sim A\mathbin{\&}B\) and
\(\sim A\mathbin{\&}\sim B\). Let the next measure be supported
on \(A\), with weights \(z,1-z\) on
\(A\mathbin{\&}B\) and \(A\mathbin{\&}\sim B\).
The first-positive-stratum construction gives \(p(B)=x\) and
\(p(B\mid A)=z\), including both endpoints. Thus no narrower
conditional interval is possible.

The event \(\sim A\lor B\) contains \(B\), so its probability
is at least \(x\) and at most one. Every \(r\in[x,1]\) is
attained by ordinary weights \(x,r-x,1-r\) on
\(A\mathbin{\&}B\), \(\sim A\mathbin{\&}\sim B\), and
\(A\mathbin{\&}\sim B\), respectively; the fourth weight is zero.
These sum to one and give material-conditional probability \(r\).

Under positive-probability quotient conditioning, probability-zero
\(B\) has zero intersection with \(A\), forcing conditional zero;
probability-one \(B\) has the same intersection probability as
\(A\), forcing conditional one. For \(0<x<1\) and any target
\(z\in[0,1]\), choose sufficiently small \(a>0\) such that
\(az\leq x\) and \(a(1-z)\leq1-x\). Use weights
\(az,a(1-z),x-az,1-x-a(1-z)\) on the four constituents.
They are nonnegative, give \(p(A)=a>0,p(B)=x\), and give the
conditional quotient \(z\). This establishes the interior and
endpoint contrast precisely.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.rat44.connexive}{thm:p15-rat44-connexive}{The declared probability interpretation and classical reductions}
\begin{proof}
For a contingent \(A\), both \(A\) and \(\sim A\) are admissible
conditioning events. The basic identities give the four Aristotle
assessments. Narrow-scope negation of its contrary-consequent
conditional replaces that consequent by its complement, giving the
probability-one identity conditional. For Boethius, the consequent of
the first principle is accepted exactly when
\(p(\sim\sim B\mid A)>h\), the identical condition to its
antecedent \(p(B\mid A)>h\). The second uses the identical
condition for \(\sim B\). These are implications between
high-assessment predicates, not a new nested conditional semantics.
If both opposed conditionals were accepted with threshold greater
than one-half, their probabilities would sum to more than one,
contradicting the complement law. This proves the Abelard reading.

With material implication, ``if \(\sim A\) then \(A\)'' is
\(A\lor A=A\), so its negation is \(\sim A\); the other
Aristotle negation is \(\sim\sim A=A\). The first Boethius
formula becomes
\[
 \sim(\sim A\lor B)\lor\sim(\sim A\lor\sim B)
 =(A\mathbin{\&}\sim B)\lor(A\mathbin{\&}B)=A.
\]
The second gives the same two disjuncts in reverse order. The
conjunction of opposed material conditionals is
\((\sim A\lor B)\mathbin{\&}(\sim A\lor\sim B)=\sim A\),
so its negation is \(A\). These explicit reductions prove the
classical comparison without conflating negation conventions.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15-w02b-p15-038-alberic}{thm:p15-w02b-p15-038-alberic}{Forced values, a coherent witness, and where the chain breaks}
\begin{proof}
First a fact about a single conditional \(E\mid H\) with \(H\) possible.
If \(H\subseteq E\), a unit stake has gain \(1-p\) at every point of
\(H\), its active union; coherence requires a value \(\leq0\) and a value
\(\geq0\) there, so \(p=1\). If \(H\cap E=\varnothing\), the gain is
\(-p\) throughout \(H\), so \(p=0\).

Item~1. Logical independence makes \(D=p\mathbin{\&}\sim q\) and
\(\sim p\) possible. Since \(D\subseteq\sim q\) and \(D\subseteq p\),
the fact gives \(p(\sim q\mid D)=p(p\mid D)=1\). Since
\(D\cap\sim p=\varnothing\) and \(D\cap\sim D=\varnothing\), it gives
\(p(\sim p\mid D)=p(\sim D\mid D)=0\). Since \(D\subseteq p\), we have
\(\sim p\subseteq\sim D\), and the fact gives \(p(\sim D\mid\sim p)=1\).

Item~2. Apply the contraposition part of the proof of
\cref{thm:p15-rat44-noninformation} with \(p\) in the role of \(A\),
\(q\) in the role of \(B\), and target \(z\). In each of its four cases
it supplies a finite sequence of normalized measures on the four
\(p,q\)-constituents in which some measure is positive on \(p\) and
some is positive on \(\sim q\): for \(x,z<1\) the single measure gives
\(p\mathbin{\&}\sim q\) positive weight; for \(x=1,z<1\) the first
measure sits on \(p\mathbin{\&}q\) and the second on \(\sim q\); for
\(z=1,x<1\) the first sits on \(\sim p\mathbin{\&}\sim q\) and the
second on \(p\); for \(x=z=1\) the single measure is positive on
\(p\mathbin{\&}q\) and on \(\sim p\mathbin{\&}\sim q\). The
first-positive-stratum ratios of that sequence are \(p(q\mid p)=x\) and
\(p(\sim p\mid\sim q)=z\). Append one further measure, uniform on the
four constituents. It is positive on \(D\) and on \(\sim p\), so every
assessed conditioning event now has a first positive stratum, and
appending a later measure leaves the prices already fixed unchanged. By
the construction principle proved for
\cref{thm:p15-rat44-coherence-method}, the first-positive-stratum
ratios are coherent prices for the whole family. For a conditioning
event \(D\) or \(\sim p\), let \(\mu\) be its first positive stratum.
The inclusions used for item~1 give
\(\mu(\sim q\mathbin{\&}D)=\mu(p\mathbin{\&}D)=\mu(D)\),
\(\mu(\sim p\mathbin{\&}D)=\mu(\sim D\mathbin{\&}D)=0\) and
\(\mu(\sim D\mathbin{\&}\sim p)=\mu(\sim p)\), so the ratios are
exactly the five values of item~1. This proves item~2.

Consequences. Acceptance needs a value \(>h>1/2\). By item~1,
\(D\supset\sim D\) has value \(0\) and is never accepted, while the
projections \(D\supset\sim q\), \(D\supset p\) and the second
contraposition \(\sim p\supset\sim D\) have value \(1\) and are
accepted. By item~2 with \(x>h\geq z\), the premise \(p\supset q\) is
accepted and \(\sim q\supset\sim p\) is not. By item~2 with
\(x,z>h\), both \(D\supset\sim q\) (value \(1\)) and
\(\sim q\supset\sim p\) (value \(z\)) are accepted while
\(D\supset\sim p\) has value \(0\).
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.rat44.noninformation}{thm:p15-rat44-noninformation}{All three noninformative argument forms}
\begin{proof}
For strengthening, use a first measure supported on
\(B\mathbin{\&}\sim P\), with \(F\)-probability \(x\), and a
second supported on \(B\mathbin{\&}P\), with \(F\)-probability
the arbitrary desired \(z\). First-positive-stratum prices give
\(p(F\mid B)=x,p(F\mid(B\mathbin{\&}P))=z\), for every \(x,z\in[0,1]\).

For contraposition, when \(x<1,z<1\), give unnormalized weights
\(x/(1-x),1,z/(1-z),0\) to
\(A\mathbin{\&}B,A\mathbin{\&}\sim B,
  \sim A\mathbin{\&}\sim B,\sim A\mathbin{\&}B\), respectively,
then normalize.
Both conditioning events have positive probability and their ratios
are \(p(B\mid A)=x\) and \(p(\sim A\mid\sim B)=z\).
For \(x=1,z<1\), put the first measure on \(A\mathbin{\&}B\), and the
second on \(\sim B\) with \(\sim A\)-probability \(z\).
For \(z=1,x<1\), put the first measure on \(\sim A\mathbin{\&}\sim B\)
and the second on \(A\) with \(B\)-probability \(x\).
For \(x=z=1\), equal ordinary weights on \(A\mathbin{\&}B\) and
\(\sim A\mathbin{\&}\sim B\) suffice. The stratum principle proves coherence
in the boundary cases as well.

For transitivity use a first measure on \(\sim A\mathbin{\&}B\)
with \(C\)-probability \(y\). Use a second measure on \(A\),
giving \(B\)-probability \(x\) and \(C\)-probability arbitrary
\(z\); for example use their two-coordinate product weights
\(xz,x(1-z),(1-x)z,(1-x)(1-z)\) on that stratum.
The resulting prices are \(p(B\mid A)=x,p(C\mid B)=y,p(C\mid A)=z\).
The product is only a chosen witness for attainability, not an
independence hypothesis imposed on the inference. The first stratum
makes \(A\) null, preserving the freedom of its conditional price
even when \(x=y=1\). Every value \(z\) has now been witnessed
for each argument form, proving the full noninformative intervals.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.rat44.propagation}{thm:p15-rat44-propagation}{Sharp AND and cumulative bounds without independence}
\begin{proof}
For any coherent conditional family whose conditions are all \(A\),
the gain-vector feasibility argument on \(A\) supplies normalized
constituent weights representing its prices. Write
\(b=p(B\mid A),c=p(C\mid A),r=p((B\mathbin{\&}C)\mid A)\).
The four conditional constituent weights are
\(r,b-r,c-r,1-b-c+r\); their nonnegativity is exactly
\(\max\{0,b+c-1\}\leq r\leq\min\{b,c\}\).
For interval premises the smallest feasible \(r\) is
\(\max\{0,x_L+y_L-1\}\), attained at \(b=x_L,c=y_L\).
The largest is \(\min\{x_U,y_U\}\), attained at
\(b=x_U,c=y_U\). The displayed four weights construct each
endpoint, and convex mixtures of the two endpoint distributions
preserve the premise intervals and cover every intermediate value.
Put ordinary positive mass on \(A\) to realize these as coherent
conditional prices.

For CUT include the assessments
\(B\mid A,C\mid(A\mathbin{\&}B),C\mid A\)
and use feasibility on their active union \(A\). A representing
measure there has \(\mu(B)=x\), \(\mu(C)=z\), and
\(\mu(B\mathbin{\&}C)=y\mu(B)=xy\). Here the events are restricted
to the local constituent space \(A\). Hence
\(z=xy+\mu(\sim B\mathbin{\&}C)\), with
\(0\leq\mu(\sim B\mathbin{\&}C)\leq1-x\), proving the bounds.
For their sharpness, choose within \(A\) the four weights
\[
             xy,\quad x(1-y),\quad(1-x)t,\quad(1-x)(1-t),
             \qquad t\in[0,1],
\]
on \(B\mathbin{\&}C,B\mathbin{\&}\sim C,
      \sim B\mathbin{\&}C,\sim B\mathbin{\&}\sim C\).
If \(x>0\), they
give all prices by positive conditioning and yield
\(z=xy+(1-x)t\). If \(x=0\), the first measure is supported on
\(A\mathbin{\&}\sim B\), with \(C\)-probability \(t\). Add a second
measure on \(A\mathbin{\&}B\) with \(C\)-probability \(y\). The stratum
principle supplies the same prices and every \(z=t\), verifying
the otherwise problematic zero-conditioning endpoint. At \(x=1\)
the interval collapses correctly to \(\{y\}\).

Substitute \(A=\top\) in this construction. Then \(B\mid A\)
is unconditional \(B\), \(C\mid(A\mathbin{\&}B)\) is \(C\mid B\), and
\(C\mid A\) is unconditional \(C\). This proves MP and its
endpoint constructions, rather than assuming a ratio on null \(B\).
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15-w02b-p15-038-threshold-and}{thm:p15-w02b-p15-038-threshold-and}{A witness below the threshold and the value-one case}
\begin{proof}
Given \(0\leq h<1\), put \(x=(1+3h)/4\). Then \(x>h\) because
\(1+3h>4h\), and \(x\leq1\). Within \(A\), give the four constituents
\(B\mathbin{\&}C\), \(B\mathbin{\&}\sim C\), \(\sim B\mathbin{\&}C\),
\(\sim B\mathbin{\&}\sim C\) the weights \(r\), \(x-r\), \(x-r\),
\(1-2x+r\), where \(r=\max\{0,2x-1\}\); these are the endpoint weights
of the proof of \cref{thm:p15-rat44-propagation} with \(b=c=x\). They
are nonnegative: \(r\geq0\); \(x-r\) equals \(x\) or \(1-x\); and
\(1-2x+r\) equals \(1-2x\geq0\) when \(r=0\), or \(0\) otherwise. They
sum to one. Logical independence of \(B,C\) given \(A\) makes all four
constituents possible, so this is a normalized measure on \(A\), and a
single measure positive on the one conditioning event \(A\) gives
coherent quotient prices by the construction principle for
\cref{thm:p15-rat44-coherence-method}. The prices are
\(p(B\mid A)=p(C\mid A)=x>h\) and \(p((B\mathbin{\&}C)\mid A)=r\).
If \(r=0\), then \(r\leq h\). Otherwise
\(r=2x-1=(3h-1)/2\), and \((3h-1)/2<h\) because \(h<1\). In both cases
the conjunction is not accepted at threshold \(h\).

If \(p(B\mid A)=p(C\mid A)=1\), the interval of
\cref{thm:p15-rat44-propagation} with all four premise bounds equal to
\(1\) is \([\max\{0,1\},\min\{1,1\}]=\{1\}\).
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15-w02b-p15-038-collapse}{thm:p15-w02b-p15-038-collapse}{The two derivations and the penguin countermodel}
\begin{proof}
Assume the relation \(\mathrel{|\!\sim}\) is closed under Reflexivity
(\(\alpha\mathrel{|\!\sim}\alpha\)), Left Logical Equivalence (from
\(\models\alpha\leftrightarrow\alpha'\) and
\(\alpha\mathrel{|\!\sim}\gamma\) infer
\(\alpha'\mathrel{|\!\sim}\gamma\)) and Right Weakening (from
\(\models\beta\to\gamma\) and \(\alpha\mathrel{|\!\sim}\beta\) infer
\(\alpha\mathrel{|\!\sim}\gamma\)), with \(\models\) relative to the
fixed universe of \cref{def:p08-klm-language}. Let
\(\alpha\mathrel{|\!\sim}\gamma\) and let \(\beta\) be any formula.

\emph{With Transitivity.} Reflexivity gives
\(\alpha\wedge\beta\mathrel{|\!\sim}\alpha\wedge\beta\), and Right
Weakening with \(\models\alpha\wedge\beta\to\alpha\) gives
\(\alpha\wedge\beta\mathrel{|\!\sim}\alpha\). Transitivity with
\(\alpha\mathrel{|\!\sim}\gamma\) gives
\(\alpha\wedge\beta\mathrel{|\!\sim}\gamma\).

\emph{With Contraposition.} Contraposition gives
\(\neg\gamma\mathrel{|\!\sim}\neg\alpha\). Right Weakening with
\(\models\neg\alpha\to\neg(\alpha\wedge\beta)\) gives
\(\neg\gamma\mathrel{|\!\sim}\neg(\alpha\wedge\beta)\).
Contraposition again gives
\(\neg\neg(\alpha\wedge\beta)\mathrel{|\!\sim}\neg\neg\gamma\).
Left Logical Equivalence with
\(\models\neg\neg(\alpha\wedge\beta)\leftrightarrow\alpha\wedge\beta\)
gives \(\alpha\wedge\beta\mathrel{|\!\sim}\neg\neg\gamma\), and Right
Weakening with \(\models\neg\neg\gamma\to\gamma\) gives
\(\alpha\wedge\beta\mathrel{|\!\sim}\gamma\).

In both cases the relation is closed under Monotonicity.

\emph{The countermodel.} The model of \cref{thm:p08-klm-penguins} has
two states with \(s_0\prec s_1\), labelled
\[
 (b,p,f)(s_0)=(1,0,1),\qquad(b,p,f)(s_1)=(1,1,0).
\]
It is smooth:
in a finite strict partial order, if \(s\in X\) is not minimal in \(X\),
pick \(t_1\prec s\) in \(X\), then \(t_2\prec t_1\) in \(X\) while
\(t_1\) is not minimal, and so on; by transitivity each \(t_k\prec s\)
and \(t_k\prec t_j\) for \(j<k\), so irreflexivity forbids repeats and
finiteness ends the chain at a minimal \(t\prec s\). By
\cref{thm:p08-klm-soundness} its relation is closed under Reflexivity,
Left Logical Equivalence and Right Weakening. The \(b\)-states are
\(s_0,s_1\), whose only minimal state is \(s_0\), which satisfies \(f\);
so \(b\mathrel{|\!\sim}f\). The only \(b\wedge p\)-state is \(s_1\),
which falsifies \(f\); so \(b\wedge p\mathrel{|\!\sim}f\) fails, and the
relation is not closed under Monotonicity. If it were closed under
Contraposition or under Transitivity, the derivations above would close
it under Monotonicity. Hence it is closed under none of the three.
\end{proof}
