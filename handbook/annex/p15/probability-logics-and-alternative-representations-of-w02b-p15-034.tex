\section{Partition updates and conglomerability}
\label{sec:annex-p15-w02b-p15-034-partition-updates}

Throughout, \(\underline P\) is a coherent lower prevision on
\(\mathcal L(\Omega)\). By \cref{thm:p15-ip-linear-domain} and
\cref{thm:p15-ip-natural-extension} it satisfies
\(\inf h\leq\underline P(h)\leq\sup h\), positive homogeneity,
superadditivity, monotonicity and
\(|\underline P(h)-\underline P(k)|\leq\|h-k\|_\infty\). Two consequences
are used repeatedly. First, \(\underline P(0)=0\) and
\(\underline P(B)\geq\inf I_B\geq0\) for every event. Second, for all
gambles \(u,v\),
\[
 \sup_{\Omega}(u-v)\geq\underline P(u)-\underline P(v),\tag{\(*\)}
\]
because superadditivity gives
\(\underline P(v)\geq\underline P(u)+\underline P(v-u)
\geq\underline P(u)+\inf(v-u)=\underline P(u)-\sup(u-v)\).

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.w02b-p15-034.cell-facts}{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-cell-facts}{Cellwise facts}
\begin{proof}
(1) Applying (C2) with \(\lambda=2\) to \(f=0\) gives
\(\underline P(0\mid B)=2\underline P(0\mid B)\), so
\(\underline P(0\mid B)=0\). By (C3),
\(\underline P(f\mid B)+\underline P(-f\mid B)\leq\underline P(0\mid B)=0\),
so by (C1)
\(\underline P(f\mid B)\leq-\underline P(-f\mid B)\leq-\inf_B(-f)=\sup_Bf\).
The lower bound is (C1). If \(f=g\) on \(B\), then (C3) and (C1) give
\(\underline P(f\mid B)\geq\underline P(g\mid B)+\underline P(f-g\mid B)
\geq\underline P(g\mid B)+\inf_B(f-g)=\underline P(g\mid B)\), and the
reverse inequality follows by exchanging \(f\) and \(g\). The bounds give
\(|\underline P(f\mid B_\omega)|\leq\|f\|_\infty\) for every \(\omega\), so
\(\underline P(f\mid\mathcal B)\) is bounded. Finally, on \(B\) the gambles
\(I_Bf\) and \(f\) agree, so \(G(I_Bf\mid\mathcal B)=f-\underline P(f\mid B)\)
there; on a cell \(B'\ne B\), \(I_Bf\) vanishes, so
\(\underline P(I_Bf\mid B')=\underline P(0\mid B')=0\) and
\(G(I_Bf\mid\mathcal B)=0\) there. Hence
\(G(I_Bf\mid\mathcal B)=I_B(f-\underline P(f\mid B))=G(f\mid B)\).

(2) By (C3) and (C1),
\(\underline P(g\mid B)\geq\underline P(f\mid B)+\underline P(g-f\mid B)
\geq\underline P(f\mid B)+\inf_B(g-f)\). Thus
\(\sup_B(f-g)=-\inf_B(g-f)\geq\underline P(f\mid B)-\underline P(g\mid B)\),
which is the claim.

(3) For real \(q,q'\),
\(\|I_B[f-q]-I_B[f-q']\|_\infty\leq|q-q'|\), so the Lipschitz property of
\(\underline P\) gives \(|F(q)-F(q')|\leq|q-q'|\). For \(\delta>0\),
\(I_B[f-q]=I_B[f-q-\delta]+\delta I_B\); superadditivity and positive
homogeneity give \(F(q)\geq F(q+\delta)+\delta\underline P(B)\). Since
\(\underline P(B)\geq0\), \(F\) is nonincreasing, and if
\(\underline P(B)>0\) it is strictly decreasing, so it has at most one
root. In that case \(F(\inf_Bf)=\underline P(I_B[f-\inf_Bf])\geq0\) because
the gamble is nonnegative, and \(F(\sup_Bf)\leq\sup I_B[f-\sup_Bf]\leq0\).
By continuity and the intermediate value theorem a root exists in
\([\inf_Bf,\sup_Bf]\).

(4) Let \(B_1,\ldots,B_k\) be distinct cells with
\(\underline P(B_i)\geq1/m\). They are disjoint, so
\(I_{B_1\cup\cdots\cup B_k}=\sum_iI_{B_i}\leq1\). Superadditivity and the
upper bound give
\(1\geq\underline P(I_{B_1\cup\cdots\cup B_k})\geq\sum_i\underline P(B_i)\geq k/m\),
so \(k\leq m\). The set of cells of positive lower probability is the union
over \(m\geq1\) of the sets \(\{B:\underline P(B)\geq1/m\}\), each of which
has at most \(m\) members; it is finite or countably infinite.

(5) For disjoint \(B_1,\ldots,B_k\),
\(I_{B_1\cup\cdots\cup B_k}f=\sum_iI_{B_i}f\), and superadditivity gives
\(\underline P(I_{B_1\cup\cdots\cup B_k}f)\geq\sum_i\underline P(I_{B_i}f)\geq0\).
If only finitely many cells have positive lower probability, there is no
infinite sequence of distinct such cells, and the condition of
\cref{def:p15-w02b-p15-034-conglomerable} holds vacuously. A finite partition
has finitely many cells; a partition whose cells all have lower probability
zero has none of positive lower probability.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.w02b-p15-034.necessity}{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-necessity}{Two portfolios and one infinite portfolio}
\begin{proof}
(1) Fix \(f\) and \(B\), and put \(h=G(f\mid B)=I_B(f-\underline P(f\mid B))\).
By \cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-cell-facts}(1),
\(G(I_Bf\mid\mathcal B)=h\). Apply (JC1) with \(f_1=0\), \(g_1=I_Bf\) and
\(f_2=h\). Since \(G(0)=0-\underline P(0)=0\), the net gain is
\(h-(h-\underline P(h))=\underline P(h)\), a constant, so (JC1) gives
\(\underline P(h)\geq0\). Apply (JC2) with \(f_1=h\), \(g_1=0\),
\(g_2=f\) and \(B_0=B\). Since \(G(0\mid\mathcal B)=0\) by
\cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-cell-facts}(1), the net gain is
\(Z=(h-\underline P(h))-h=-\underline P(h)\), again a constant. Every set
in the prescribed family is nonempty, so its supremum over any of them is
\(-\underline P(h)\), and (JC2) gives \(\underline P(h)\leq0\). Hence
\(\underline P(h)=0\).

(2) If \(\underline P(B)>0\), item (1) says that
\(\underline P(f\mid B)\) is a root of \(q\mapsto\underline P(I_B[f-q])\),
and \cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-cell-facts}(3) says that this root is
unique. If \(\underline P(B)=0\), the bound is (C1).

(3) Let \(B_1,B_2,\ldots\) be distinct cells with
\(\underline P(B_n)>0\) and let \(\underline P(I_{B_n}f)\geq0\) for all
\(n\). Put \(F_n(q)=\underline P(I_{B_n}[f-q])\) and
\(r_n=\underline P(f\mid B_n)\). By (1), \(F_n(r_n)=0\). If \(r_n<0\),
\cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-cell-facts}(3) would give
\(F_n(0)\leq F_n(r_n)-(0-r_n)\underline P(B_n)<0\), contradicting
\(F_n(0)=\underline P(I_{B_n}f)\geq0\). So \(r_n\geq0\) for all \(n\).

Let \(D=\bigcup_nB_n\) and \(f'=I_Df\). For each \(n\), \(f'=f\) on
\(B_n\), so \(\underline P(f'\mid B_n)=r_n\geq0\) by
\cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-cell-facts}(1). A cell \(B\) not among the
\(B_n\) is disjoint from \(D\), so \(f'=0\) on \(B\) and
\(\underline P(f'\mid B)=\underline P(0\mid B)=0\). Hence the gamble
\(\underline P(f'\mid\mathcal B)\) is nonnegative everywhere. Apply (JC1)
with \(f_1=0\), \(g_1=f'\), \(f_2=f'\). The net gain is
\[
 G(f'\mid\mathcal B)-G(f')
 =\bigl(f'-\underline P(f'\mid\mathcal B)\bigr)-\bigl(f'-\underline P(f')\bigr)
 =\underline P(f')-\underline P(f'\mid\mathcal B),
\]
whose supremum over \(\Omega\) is
\(\underline P(f')-\inf_\omega\underline P(f'\mid B_\omega)\). (JC1) makes it
nonnegative, so
\(\underline P(I_Df)=\underline P(f')\geq\inf_\omega\underline P(f'\mid B_\omega)\geq0\).
This is the conglomerability inequality. The portfolio used is active on
every cell \(B_n\) at once.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.w02b-p15-034.existence}{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-existence}{Constructing the least update under conglomerability}
\begin{proof}
If a partition update exists, \(\underline P\) is
\(\mathcal B\)-conglomerable by
\cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-necessity}(3).

Conversely assume \(\mathcal B\)-conglomerability.
\emph{Well defined.} For a cell with \(\underline P(B)>0\), the root
exists and is unique by \cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-cell-facts}(3).

\emph{Separate coherence.} On a cell of lower probability zero,
\(\inf_B\) satisfies (C1) with equality, (C2) because \(\lambda>0\), and
(C3) because \(\inf_B(f+g)\geq\inf_Bf+\inf_Bg\). Let
\(\underline P(B)>0\), write \(F_f(q)=\underline P(I_B[f-q])\) and let
\(q_f\) be its root. For (C1), suppose \(q_f<\inf_Bf\); the strict
decrease in \cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-cell-facts}(3) would give
\(F_f(\inf_Bf)\leq F_f(q_f)-(\inf_Bf-q_f)\underline P(B)<0\), whereas
\(F_f(\inf_Bf)\geq0\) because \(I_B[f-\inf_Bf]\geq0\). For (C2),
\(F_{\lambda f}(\lambda q_f)=\lambda F_f(q_f)=0\), so
\(q_{\lambda f}=\lambda q_f\) by uniqueness. For (C3), superadditivity gives
\(F_{f+g}(q_f+q_g)\geq F_f(q_f)+F_g(q_g)=0\); if \(q_{f+g}<q_f+q_g\),
strict decrease would give \(F_{f+g}(q_f+q_g)<F_{f+g}(q_{f+g})=0\). Hence
\(q_{f+g}\geq q_f+q_g\).

\emph{Every conditional gain is almost acceptable.} Fix \(g\) and put
\(G=G(g\mid\mathcal B)\), computed with \(\underline P_{\mathcal B}\). By
\cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-cell-facts}(4) the cells of positive lower
probability can be listed without repetition as \(B_1,B_2,\ldots\), a finite
or infinite list; let \(D\) be their union (empty if there are none). For
each listed cell, \(I_{B_n}G=G(g\mid B_n)\) and
\(\underline P(I_{B_n}G)=F_g(q_g)=0\) at that cell. If the list is
infinite, \(\mathcal B\)-conglomerability applied to the gamble \(G\) gives
\(\underline P(I_DG)\geq0\). If it is finite and nonempty,
\cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-cell-facts}(5) gives the same inequality; if it
is empty, \(I_DG=0\). On a cell \(B\) of lower probability zero,
\(G=g-\inf_Bg\geq0\). Therefore \(G\geq I_DG\) pointwise and, by
monotonicity,
\[
                  \underline P(G(g\mid\mathcal B))\geq\underline P(I_DG)\geq0.
\]

\emph{Each cell's own gain has lower prevision zero.} Let
\(k=G(g_2\mid B_0)\). If \(\underline P(B_0)>0\),
\(\underline P(k)=0\) by the definition of the root. If
\(\underline P(B_0)=0\), then \(k=I_{B_0}(g_2-\inf_{B_0}g_2)\), so
\(0\leq k\leq cI_{B_0}\) with \(c=\sup k\geq0\); monotonicity and
homogeneity give \(0\leq\underline P(k)\leq c\,\underline P(B_0)=0\).

\emph{(JC1).} Put \(u=f_1+G(g_1\mid\mathcal B)\). The net gain is
\(u-f_2-\underline P(f_1)+\underline P(f_2)\). By \((*)\) its supremum over
\(\Omega\) is at least
\(\underline P(u)-\underline P(f_1)
\geq\underline P(f_1)+\underline P(G(g_1\mid\mathcal B))-\underline P(f_1)\geq0\).

\emph{(JC2).} Let \(k=G(g_2\mid B_0)\) as above, so
\(\underline P(k)=0\). If \(f_1\ne0\), \(\Omega\) belongs to the prescribed
family. With \(u\) as before, \(Z=u-k-\underline P(f_1)\), and by \((*)\)
\(\sup_\Omega Z\geq\underline P(u)-\underline P(k)-\underline P(f_1)
\geq\underline P(G(g_1\mid\mathcal B))\geq0\). If \(f_1=0\), then
\(G(f_1)=0\) and \(B_0\) belongs to the family. On \(B_0\),
\(Z=(g_1-\underline P_{\mathcal B}(g_1\mid B_0))-(g_2-\underline P_{\mathcal B}(g_2\mid B_0))\),
and \cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-cell-facts}(2) gives
\(\sup_{B_0}Z\geq0\).

So \(\underline P_{\mathcal B}\) is a partition update. For any partition
update, \cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-necessity}(2) forces the root on cells of
positive lower probability and the bound \(\geq\inf_Bf\) on the others,
which are the displayed relations.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.w02b-p15-034.counterexample}{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-counterexample}{The finitely additive mixture computed in full}
\begin{proof}
(1) On \(\mathcal L(\mathbb N_+)\) let
\(Q(h)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}h(n)\). Then \(Q(h)\geq\inf h\),
\(Q(th)=tQ(h)\) for \(t>0\), and
\(\liminf(h+k)\geq\liminf h+\liminf k\). By
\cref{thm:p15-ip-linear-domain}, applied with the power-set algebra of
\(\mathbb N_+\), \(Q\) is coherent; in particular it avoids sure loss, so by
\cref{thm:p15-ip-representation} its compatible set is nonempty. Choose
\(\nu\) in it: \(\nu\) is positive, normalized and linear, and
\(\nu(h)\geq\liminf h\). For finite \(F\), \(\nu(I_F)\geq\liminf I_F=0\),
and
\(\nu(I_F)=1-\nu(I_{\mathbb N_+\setminus F})\leq1-\liminf I_{\mathbb N_+\setminus F}=0\).

(2) The series defining \(\mu\) converges absolutely, and
\[
 |\mu(f)|\leq\|f\|_\infty\sum_n2^{-n}=\|f\|_\infty;
\]
\(\mu\) is linear,
positive and \(\mu(1)=1\). The map \(f\mapsto(n\mapsto f(n,1))\) is linear,
positive and sends \(1\) to \(1\), so \(\nu_1\) has the same three
properties, and so does their average \(P\). For such a functional
\(P(g)-\inf g=P(g-\inf g)\geq0\), positive homogeneity holds and
superadditivity holds with equality, so \(P\) is coherent by
\cref{thm:p15-ip-linear-domain}; its conjugate is
\(-P(-g)=P(g)\).

Now \(\mu(I_{B_n})=2^{-n}\) and \(\nu_1(I_{B_n})=\nu(I_{\{n\}})=0\), so
\(P(B_n)=2^{-n-1}\) and \(\sum_nP(B_n)=1/2\). Next
\(\mu(I_{Y_1})=\sum_n2^{-n}/2=1/2\) and \(\nu_1(I_{Y_1})=\nu(1)=1\), so
\(P(Y_1)=3/4\). Finally
\(P(I_{B_n}I_{Y_1})=\tfrac12\bigl(2^{-n}/2+\nu(I_{\{n\}})\bigr)=2^{-n-2}\),
whose ratio to \(P(B_n)\) is \(1/2\).

(3) Here \(f(n,0)=1\) and \(f(n,1)=-1\). Then
\(\mu(I_{B_n}f)=2^{-n}(1-1)/2=0\) and
\(\nu_1(I_{B_n}f)=\nu(-I_{\{n\}})=0\), so \(P(I_{B_n}f)=0\). Also
\(\mu(f)=0\) and \(\nu_1(f)=\nu(-1)=-1\), so \(P(f)=-1/2\). The sequence
\(B_1,B_2,\ldots\) consists of distinct cells of positive probability, each
with \(P(I_{B_n}f)\geq0\), and its union is \(\Omega\); but
\(P(I_\Omega f)=-1/2<0\). So \(P\) is not \(\mathcal B\)-conglomerable, and
by \cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-existence} it has no partition update.

(4) The average \(a_n\) is linear, \(a_n(1)=1\), and
\(a_n(g)\geq\inf_{B_n}g\), so it satisfies (C1)--(C3) on \(B_n\). For real
\(q\),
\[
 P(I_{B_n}[g-q])=\tfrac12\Bigl(2^{-n}\bigl(a_n(g)-q\bigr)
 +\bigl(g(n,1)-q\bigr)\nu(I_{\{n\}})\Bigr)=2^{-n-1}\bigl(a_n(g)-q\bigr),
\]
whose only root is \(a_n(g)\). For the gamble \(f\) of item (3),
\(a_n(f)=0\) for every \(n\), so
\(G(f\mid\mathcal B)=f\), while \(G(f)=f-P(f)=f+1/2\). The net gain
\(G(f\mid\mathcal B)-G(f)\) is the constant \(-1/2\); this is the
failure of (JC1) with \(f_1=0\), \(g_1=f_2=f\).

(5) Let \(J\subseteq\mathbb N_+\) index a sequence of distinct cells and
\(D=\bigcup_{n\in J}B_n\). Since \(I_Df\) agrees with \(f\) on \(B_n\) for
\(n\in J\) and vanishes on the other cells, the defining series gives
\(\mu(I_Df)=\sum_{n\in J}2^{-n}a_n(f)=\sum_{n\in J}\mu(I_{B_n}f)\), an
absolutely convergent sum of nonnegative terms whenever each
\(\mu(I_{B_n}f)\geq0\). So \(\mu\) is \(\mathcal B\)-conglomerable. It is
coherent by the argument in (2), and \(\mu(B_n)=2^{-n}>0\). Since
\(\mu(I_{B_n}[g-q])=2^{-n}(a_n(g)-q)\), the least partition update of
\(\mu\) is \(a_n\) on \(B_n\). Finally \(\mu(Y_1)=1/2=a_n(I_{Y_1})\).
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.w02b-p15-034.williams}{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-williams}{Finitely supported portfolios have expectation zero}
\begin{proof}
Every allowed conditioning event has positive probability:
\(P(\Omega)=1\) and \(P(B_n)=2^{-n-1}\). For each \(i\),
\(P\bigl(I_{C_i}(f_i-P(f_i\mid C_i))\bigr)=0\): for \(C_i=\Omega\) this is
\(P(f_i)-P(f_i)=0\) by linearity, and for \(C_i=B_n\) it is the displayed
identity in item (4) of the preceding proof at \(q=a_n(f_i)\). Let \(H\) be
the bracketed gamble. It is a finite linear combination of these gambles,
so \(P(H)=0\). Put \(U=C_0\cup\cdots\cup C_k\); \(H\) vanishes outside
\(U\). Suppose \(\sup_UH=-\epsilon<0\). Then \(H\leq-\epsilon I_U\)
pointwise, and positivity and linearity give
\(P(H)\leq-\epsilon P(U)\leq-\epsilon P(C_0)<0\), a contradiction. Hence
\(\sup_UH\geq0\).
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\section{Evidence models and independence in decisions}
\label{sec:annex-p15-w02b-p15-034-evidence-independence}

In this section \(W\) and \(\Omega\) are finite. Two facts are used
repeatedly. First, if \(\mathcal M\) is a nonempty compact convex set of
probability masses and \(\underline E_{\mathcal M}(g)=\min_{p\in\mathcal
M}E_p(g)\), then \(\underline E_{\mathcal M}\) is coherent on all gambles
and its compatible set is \(\mathcal M\): the compatible set contains
\(\mathcal M\), so the assessment avoids sure loss, its natural extension is
the minimum over the compatible set, which is at most the minimum over
\(\mathcal M\) and at least \(\underline E_{\mathcal M}\) by the definition
of compatibility, and \cref{thm:p15-ip-representation} then gives
coherence and, by its uniqueness clause, that the compatible set is
\(\mathcal M\). Second, for a gamble \(g\) and a real constant \(c\),
\(E_p(g-c)=E_p(g)-c\) for every \(p\), so lower and upper envelopes are
translated by \(c\).

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.w02b-p15-034.bel-ext}{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-belief-extension}{The gamble extension of a belief function}
\begin{proof}
(1) By item~4 of \cref{thm:p14-evidence-finite-measures}, \(\mathcal C_m\)
is nonempty and \(\min_{p\in\mathcal C_m}P_p(A)=\operatorname{Bel}_m(A)\)
for every \(A\), the minimum being attained. So every \(p\in\mathcal C_m\)
satisfies \(P_p(A)\geq\operatorname{Bel}_m(A)\) for all \(A\), that is,
\(\mathcal C_m\subseteq\mathcal M(\operatorname{Bel}_m)\). Hence
\(\mathcal M(\operatorname{Bel}_m)\ne\varnothing\) and the assessment
avoids sure loss by \cref{thm:p15-ip-representation}. For each \(A\),
\[
 \operatorname{Bel}_m(A)\leq\min_{p\in\mathcal M(\operatorname{Bel}_m)}P_p(A)
 \leq\min_{p\in\mathcal C_m}P_p(A)=\operatorname{Bel}_m(A),
\]
the first inequality by the definition of \(\mathcal M(\operatorname{Bel}_m)\).
The input therefore equals the minima of \cref{thm:p15-ip-representation}
on its domain, so it is coherent.

(2) Put \(s_m(g)=\sum_{m(B)>0}m(B)\min_Bg\). For each focal set \(B\) choose
\(w_B\in B\) with \(g(w_B)=\min_Bg\), and let \(p^*\) be the allocation
with \(q_B\) the unit mass at \(w_B\). Then \(p^*\in\mathcal C_m\) and
\(E_{p^*}(g)=s_m(g)\). Every allocation \(p=\sum_Bm(B)q_B\) has
\(E_p(g)=\sum_Bm(B)E_{q_B}(g)\geq s_m(g)\), since \(q_B\) is a probability
mass on \(B\). So \(\min_{p\in\mathcal C_m}E_p(g)=s_m(g)\).

By \cref{thm:p15-ip-representation} the natural extension is
\(\underline E_m(g)=\min_{p\in\mathcal M(\operatorname{Bel}_m)}E_p(g)\).
Since \(p^*\in\mathcal M(\operatorname{Bel}_m)\) by item~(1),
\(\underline E_m(g)\leq s_m(g)\). For the reverse inequality let
\(p\in\mathcal M(\operatorname{Bel}_m)\), put \(c=\min_Wg\) and
\(h=g-c\geq0\). For each \(w\),
\(h(w)=\int_0^\infty I[h(w)\geq t]\,dt\), the integrand vanishing for
\(t>\max h\). Multiplying by \(p(w)\) and summing over the finite set
\(W\),
\[
 E_p(h)=\int_0^\infty P_p(\{h\geq t\})\,dt
 \geq\int_0^\infty\operatorname{Bel}_m(\{h\geq t\})\,dt .
\]
For a nonempty \(B\), \(B\subseteq\{h\geq t\}\) holds exactly when
\(\min_Bh\geq t\). Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \int_0^\infty\operatorname{Bel}_m(\{h\geq t\})\,dt
 &=\sum_{m(B)>0}m(B)\int_0^\infty I[\min_Bh\geq t]\,dt\\
 &=\sum_{m(B)>0}m(B)\min_Bh=s_m(g)-c,
\end{aligned}
\]
using \(\min_Bh=\min_Bg-c\) and \(\sum_Bm(B)=1\). So
\(E_p(g)=E_p(h)+c\geq s_m(g)\), and \(\underline E_m(g)=s_m(g)\).

(3) Let \(x_1>\cdots>x_k\) be the distinct values of \(g\) and
\(A_i=\{g\geq x_i\}\). If \(k=1\), \(g\) is the constant \(x_1\) and
both \(V_{\operatorname{Bel}_m}(g)\) and \(s_m(g)\) equal \(x_1\). If
\(k\geq2\), take \(h=g-x_k\) in the identity of item~(2). For
\(1\leq i\leq k-1\) and \(t\in(x_{i+1}-x_k,\ x_i-x_k]\),
\(h\geq t\) holds exactly when \(g\geq x_i\), because \(g\) takes no value
strictly between \(x_{i+1}\) and \(x_i\); so \(\{h\geq t\}=A_i\) on an
interval of length \(x_i-x_{i+1}\). For \(t>x_1-x_k\),
\(\{h\geq t\}=\varnothing\) and \(\operatorname{Bel}_m(\varnothing)=0\).
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 s_m(g)&=x_k+\int_0^\infty\operatorname{Bel}_m(\{h\geq t\})\,dt\\
 &=x_k+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(x_i-x_{i+1})\operatorname{Bel}_m(A_i)
 =V_{\operatorname{Bel}_m}(g).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.w02b-p15-034.bel-prev}{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-belief-not-prevision}{Same belief function on events, different gamble prices}
\begin{proof}
The masses are nonnegative and sum to \(3/10+3/5+1/10=1\). Summing the
masses of subsets gives \(\operatorname{Bel}_m(\varnothing)=0\),
\(\operatorname{Bel}_m(\{i\})=1/10\),
\(\operatorname{Bel}_m(\{i,j\})=1/10+1/10+1/5=2/5\) and
\(\operatorname{Bel}_m(W)=1\). These are the common event lower
probabilities stated in \cref{thm:p15-ip-event-separation}. By item~2 of
\cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-belief-extension}, for \(f=(2,1,0)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \underline E_m(f)&=\tfrac1{10}(2+1+0)
  +\tfrac15\bigl(\min\{2,1\}+\min\{2,0\}+\min\{1,0\}\bigr)\\
 &\quad+\tfrac1{10}\min\{2,1,0\}\\
 &=\tfrac3{10}+\tfrac15=\tfrac12 .
\end{aligned}
\]
\Cref{thm:p15-ip-event-separation} gives \(\min_CE_pf=1/2\) and
\(\min_DE_pf=7/10\). The set \(D\) is the convex hull of finitely many
masses, hence nonempty compact and convex, so by the first fact above its
lower envelope \(\underline E_D\) is coherent on all gambles. Its values on
events are the event lower probabilities of \(D\), which by
\cref{thm:p15-ip-event-separation} are those of \(\operatorname{Bel}_m\).
But \(\underline E_D(f)=7/10\ne1/2=\underline E_m(f)\), so
\(\underline E_D\) is not the natural extension of
\(\operatorname{Bel}_m\).
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.w02b-p15-034.classes}{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-strict-classes}{Necessity measures form a proper subclass of belief functions}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.w02b-p15-034.dempster}{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-dempster}{Dempster conditioning is not the coherent update}
\begin{proof}
(1) In \cref{def:p15-r47-combination} with \(m_1=m\) and \(m_2=m^B\),
\(K=\sum_{E\cap B\ne\varnothing}m(E)=\operatorname{Pl}_m(B)>0\), so the
combination is defined, and by \cref{thm:p15-r47-combination} it is a
normalized mass assignment. For nonempty \(C\),
\(m_B(C)=\sum_{E:E\cap B=C}m(E)/\operatorname{Pl}_m(B)\). Each \(E\) with
\(E\cap B\ne\varnothing\) contributes to exactly one \(C\), namely
\(C=E\cap B\), and \(C\cap A\ne\varnothing\) then means
\(E\cap(A\cap B)\ne\varnothing\). Hence
\[
 \operatorname{Pl}_{m_B}(A)=\sum_{C\cap A\ne\varnothing}m_B(C)
 =\frac{1}{\operatorname{Pl}_m(B)}\sum_{E\cap(A\cap B)\ne\varnothing}m(E)
 =\frac{\operatorname{Pl}_m(A\cap B)}{\operatorname{Pl}_m(B)} .
\]
Item~3 of \cref{thm:p14-evidence-finite-measures}, applied to \(m_B\),
gives \(\operatorname{Bel}_{m_B}(A)=1-\operatorname{Pl}_{m_B}(A^c)\),
which is the second formula.

If \(m\) is the nested mass assignment of \(\pi\), then
\(\operatorname{Pl}_m=\operatorname{Poss}_\pi\) by
\cref{thm:p15-ip-possibility-bridge}, so
\(\operatorname{Pl}_m(B)=\operatorname{Poss}_\pi(B)>0\) and
\[
 \operatorname{Pl}_{m_B}(A)=\frac{\max_{w\in A\cap B}\pi(w)}{\operatorname{Poss}_\pi(B)}
 =\max_{w\in A}\pi_B(w)=\operatorname{Poss}_{\pi_B}(A),
\]
because \(\pi_B\) vanishes off \(B\), with both sides zero when
\(A\cap B=\varnothing\). The function \(\pi_B\) takes values in
\([0,1]\) and has maximum \(\max_B\pi/\operatorname{Poss}_\pi(B)=1\), so it
is a possibility distribution, and
\(\operatorname{Bel}_{m_B}(A)=1-\operatorname{Poss}_{\pi_B}(A^c)
=\operatorname{Nec}_{\pi_B}(A)\).

(2) Both focal sets meet \(B\), so \(\operatorname{Pl}_m(B)=1\), with
\(\{a,c\}\cap B=\{a\}\) and \(\{b\}\cap B=\{b\}\). Thus
\(m_B(\{a\})=m_B(\{b\})=1/2\), and
\(\operatorname{Bel}_{m_B}(\{a\})=\operatorname{Pl}_{m_B}(\{a\})=1/2\).

The belief function has
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \operatorname{Bel}_m(\{a\})=\operatorname{Bel}_m(\{c\})=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{Bel}_m(W)=1,\\
 \operatorname{Bel}_m(E)=1/2\ \text{for } E=\{b\},\{a,b\},\{a,c\},\{b,c\}.
\end{gathered}
\] A mass \(p\) lies in
\(\mathcal M(\operatorname{Bel}_m)\) exactly when \(p(b)\geq1/2\) and
\(p(a)+p(c)\geq1/2\), the remaining constraints then following; since
the total is one, both hold with equality. So
\(\mathcal M(\operatorname{Bel}_m)=\{p_t=(t,1/2,1/2-t):0\leq t\leq1/2\}\)
in the order \((a,b,c)\). By \cref{thm:p15-ip-representation},
\(\underline E_m(B)=\min_t(t+1/2)=1/2>0\). By
\cref{thm:p15-ip-conditional-models,thm:p15-ip-generalized-bayes}, the
generalized Bayes update is then the regular extension, which here is
\[
 \min_{0\leq t\leq1/2}\frac{p_t(a)}{p_t(B)}=\min_{0\leq t\leq1/2}\frac{t}{t+1/2}=0,
\]
and its conjugate upper value on \(B\) is the maximum of the same
increasing function of \(t\), namely \(1/2\), at \(t=1/2\). The model
\(p_{1/2}=(1/2,1/2,0)\) maximizes \(p_t(B)=t+1/2\), and its conditional
probability of \(a\) given \(B\) is \(1/2\), the Dempster value.

(3) Order \(a,b,c\) by decreasing \(\pi\), so the possibility values are
\(1,1,1/2\). The nested mass assignment of
\cref{thm:p15-ip-possibility-bridge} is \(m(\{a\})=0\),
\(m(\{a,b\})=1/2\), \(m(W)=1/2\). Here
\(\operatorname{Poss}_\pi(B)=1\), so by item~(1)
\(\operatorname{Pl}_m(\{b\}\Vert B)=\operatorname{Poss}_\pi(\{b\})=1\) and
\(\operatorname{Bel}_m(\{b\}\Vert B)=1-\operatorname{Poss}_\pi(\{c\})=1/2\).

If an event \(E\) omits \(a\) or \(b\), its complement contains a point
of possibility one and \(\operatorname{Nec}_\pi(E)=0\); otherwise
\(\operatorname{Nec}_\pi(\{a,b\})=1-\pi(c)=1/2\) and
\(\operatorname{Nec}_\pi(W)=1\). So
\(\mathcal M(\operatorname{Nec}_\pi)=\{p:p(a)+p(b)\geq1/2\}\). It contains
\((1,0,0)\), with \(p(B)=0\), and \((0,1,0)\), with \(p(B)=1\); hence the
natural extension gives \(B\) lower probability \(0\) and upper probability
\(1\). By \cref{thm:p15-ip-conditional-models}, natural conditioning is then
vacuous, giving \(\{b\}\) the conditional bounds \(\inf_BI_{\{b\}}=0\) and
\(\sup_BI_{\{b\}}=1\). Regular extension takes the infimum and supremum of
\(p(b)/p(B)\) over the members with \(p(B)>0\); the member
\((1/2,0,1/2)\) gives \(0\) and \((0,1,0)\) gives \(1\), and the ratio lies
in \([0,1]\), so its bounds are also \(0\) and \(1\).

(4) Let \(C(\cdot\mid B)\) be a separately coherent conditional lower
prevision on all gambles, jointly coherent with the natural extension. In
item~(2) the lower probability of \(B\) is positive, so
\cref{thm:p15-ip-conditional-bounds} gives \(C=\underline R\) and
\(C(I_{\{a\}}\mid B)=0\ne1/2\). In item~(3) the lower probability of \(B\)
is zero and its upper probability is positive, so the same theorem gives
\(C(I_{\{b\}}\mid B)\leq\underline R(I_{\{b\}}\mid B)=0<1/2\).
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

For the remaining two items write \(t_X(p)=p_{10}+p_{11}\) and
\(t_Y(p)=p_{01}+p_{11}\) for the marginal probabilities of value \(1\), and
for a gamble \(h\) on \(\{0,1\}\) write \(\hat h\) and \(\check h\) for
its lifts \(\hat h(x,y)=h(x)\) and \(\check h(x,y)=h(y)\). Then
\(E_p(\hat h)=t_X(p)h(1)+(1-t_X(p))h(0)\), an affine function of
\(t_X(p)\), and likewise for \(\check h\). The set \(\mathcal Q\) of
products \(p_X\otimes p_Y\) with \(r=p_X(1)\) and \(s=p_Y(1)\) in
\([2/5,3/5]\) is the image of the square \([2/5,3/5]^2\) under a
continuous map, hence compact. In finite dimension the convex hull of a
compact set is compact, so \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{str}}=\operatorname{conv}
\mathcal Q\), and each of its members is a finite convex combination of
products.

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.w02b-p15-034.ind-models}{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-independence-models}{What each independence judgement fixes}
\begin{proof}
(1) A product has \(p_{1y}=r\,p(Y{=}y)\) and \(p_{x1}=s\,p(X{=}x)\) with
\(r,s\in[2/5,3/5]\), so it satisfies the inequalities defining
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ei}}\). Those inequalities, with nonnegativity and
total mass one, are finitely many nonstrict linear conditions, so
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ei}}\) is closed and convex; it therefore contains
\(\operatorname{conv}\mathcal Q=\mathcal M_{\mathrm{str}}\). Adding the two
\(y\)-inequalities gives
\(\tfrac25\leq p_{10}+p_{11}=t_X(p)\leq\tfrac35\), and adding the two
\(x\)-inequalities gives the same bounds for \(t_Y(p)\); so
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ei}}\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{mg}}\).

Let \(\mathcal M\) be any closed convex set with
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{str}}\subseteq\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{mg}}\),
in particular any of the three sets. Then
\(\{t_X(p):p\in\mathcal M\}\subseteq[2/5,3/5]\), and it contains \(2/5\)
and \(3/5\), the values at the products with \(r=2/5\) and \(r=3/5\),
which lie in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{str}}\subseteq\mathcal M\). Since
\(E_p(\hat h)\) is affine in \(t_X(p)\), its minimum over \(\mathcal M\)
is the smaller of its values at \(t=2/5\) and \(t=3/5\), which is
\(\underline P_1(h)\), the minimum of the same affine function over
\([2/5,3/5]\). The same argument applies to \(Y\).

(2) A mass \(p\) is compatible with the two marginal assessments when
\(E_p(\hat h)\geq\underline P_1(h)\) and
\(E_p(\check h)\geq\underline P_1(h)\) for every \(h\). If
\(p\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{mg}}\), then \(t_X(p)\in[2/5,3/5]\) and
\(E_p(\hat h)\geq\underline P_1(h)\) by the definition of
\(\underline P_1\); likewise for \(Y\). Conversely, \(h=I_{\{1\}}\) gives
\(t_X(p)\geq\underline P_1(I_{\{1\}})=2/5\) and \(h=-I_{\{1\}}\) gives
\(-t_X(p)\geq\underline P_1(-I_{\{1\}})=-3/5\); likewise for \(Y\). So the
compatible set is \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{mg}}\), which is nonempty, and by
\cref{thm:p15-ip-representation} the natural extension is
\(\underline E_{\mathrm{mg}}\).

(3) Let \(\mathcal M\) be closed and convex with
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{str}}\subseteq\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ei}}\).
For \(p\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ei}}\) the \(x\)-inequalities give
\(p_{x1}\geq\tfrac25p(X{=}x)\) and
\(p_{x0}=p(X{=}x)-p_{x1}\geq\tfrac25p(X{=}x)\); summing over \(x\) gives
\(p(Y{=}1)\geq2/5\) and \(p(Y{=}0)\geq2/5\). The products with
\(s=2/5\) and \(s=3/5\) attain these bounds, so each event \(\{Y=y\}\) has
lower probability \(2/5\) under \(\underline E_{\mathcal M}\); the
\(y\)-inequalities give the same for \(\{X=x\}\).

By the first fact above, \(\underline E_{\mathcal M}\) is coherent with
compatible set \(\mathcal M\). The partition \(\{\{Y=0\},\{Y=1\}\}\) is
finite, so \(\underline E_{\mathcal M}\) is conglomerable for it by item~5
of \cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-cell-facts}, and by
\cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-existence} it has a partition update; since both
cells have positive lower probability, every partition update equals the
generalized Bayes value there. By \cref{thm:p15-ip-generalized-bayes} that
value is the regular extension, which, since every member of \(\mathcal M\)
gives \(\{Y=y\}\) probability at least \(2/5\), is
\[
 \underline R(\hat h\mid Y{=}y)=\min_{p\in\mathcal M}
 \bigl[c_y(p)h(1)+(1-c_y(p))h(0)\bigr],\qquad
 c_y(p)=\frac{p_{1y}}{p(Y{=}y)},
\]
the minimum being attained because \(\mathcal M\) is compact and the
expression is continuous on it. The \(y\)-inequalities give
\(c_y(p)\in[2/5,3/5]\) on \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ei}}\), and the products
with \(r=2/5\) and \(r=3/5\) give \(c_y=2/5\) and \(c_y=3/5\). As in
item~(1), the minimum of the affine expression is therefore
\(\underline P_1(h)\), which by item~(1) is also the marginal lower
prevision of \(\hat h\) under \(\underline E_{\mathcal M}\). So learning
\(Y=y\) leaves every lower prevision of an \(X\)-gamble unchanged: \(Y\) is
epistemically irrelevant for \(X\). Exchanging the roles of the two
variables, with \(d_x(p)=p_{x1}/p(X{=}x)\) and the \(x\)-inequalities,
shows that \(X\) is epistemically irrelevant for \(Y\).

(4) Let \(\mathcal M\) be closed and convex, with marginal lower previsions
\(\underline P_1\) and with \(X\) and \(Y\) epistemically independent
under generalized-Bayes updates. The lower probability of \(\{Y=y\}\) is
the marginal value \(\underline P_1(I_{\{y\}})=2/5>0\), so every
\(p\in\mathcal M\) gives it probability at least \(2/5\), and by
\cref{thm:p15-ip-generalized-bayes} the update is the regular extension
\(\min_{p\in\mathcal M}[c_y(p)h(1)+(1-c_y(p))h(0)]\). Irrelevance makes this
equal to \(\underline P_1(h)\). For \(h=I_{\{1\}}\) this gives
\(\min_{\mathcal M}c_y=2/5\), and for \(h=-I_{\{1\}}\) it gives
\(\min_{\mathcal M}(-c_y)=-3/5\). So \(2/5\leq c_y(p)\leq3/5\) for every
\(p\in\mathcal M\), and multiplying by \(p(Y{=}y)>0\) gives the
\(y\)-inequalities. The \(x\)-inequalities follow in the same way. Hence
\(\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ei}}\), and
\(\underline E_{\mathcal M}\geq\underline E_{\mathrm{ei}}\) pointwise,
being a minimum over a smaller set. By item~(3) with
\(\mathcal M=\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ei}}\), \(\underline E_{\mathrm{ei}}\)
itself has these properties.

(5) In \(p^-\), \(p(Y{=}1)=p(Y{=}0)=p(X{=}1)=p(X{=}0)=1/2\), and
\(p_{11}=1/5\), \(p_{10}=3/10\), \(p_{01}=3/10\), \(p_{00}=1/5\); the
ratios \(c_1=2/5\), \(c_0=3/5\), \(d_1=2/5\), \(d_0=3/5\) lie in
\([2/5,3/5]\), so \(p^-\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ei}}\). Four of the defining
inequalities hold with equality at \(p^-\):
\[
 \tfrac35p_{11}-\tfrac25p_{01}\geq0,\quad
 \tfrac35p_{11}-\tfrac25p_{10}\geq0,\quad
 \tfrac35p_{00}-\tfrac25p_{10}\geq0,\quad
 \tfrac35p_{00}-\tfrac25p_{01}\geq0,
\]
these being the lower \(y=1\) bound, the lower \(x=1\) bound, the upper
\(y=0\) bound and the upper \(x=0\) bound, rearranged. If
\(p^-=\tfrac12(u+v)\) with \(u,v\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ei}}\), each of
these linear functions is nonnegative at \(u\) and at \(v\) and zero at
their midpoint, so it is zero at both. At \(u\) the equalities give
\(u_{01}=u_{10}=\tfrac32u_{11}\) and \(u_{00}=\tfrac23u_{10}=u_{11}\), and
total mass one gives \(5u_{11}=1\); so \(u=p^-\), and likewise \(v=p^-\).
Thus \(p^-\) is extreme in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ei}}\). Every product
satisfies \(p_{00}p_{11}=p_{01}p_{10}\), while
\(p^-_{00}p^-_{11}=1/25\ne9/100=p^-_{01}p^-_{10}\); so \(p^-\notin\mathcal Q\).
If \(p^-\) were in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{str}}\), it would be a convex
combination of products with positive weights, all of them in
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ei}}\); extremality in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ei}}\)
would force each of them to equal \(p^-\), which is not a product. Hence
\(p^-\notin\mathcal M_{\mathrm{str}}\). This agrees with
\cref{thm:p17-mmeu-product-priors}, by which every extreme member of the
product set is a product.

(6) For a product, \(E_p(f)=rs+(1-r)(1-s)=\tfrac12+2(r-\tfrac12)(s-\tfrac12)\).
With \(|r-\tfrac12|,|s-\tfrac12|\leq\tfrac1{10}\) the last term lies in
\([-1/50,1/50]\), with the endpoints attained at \((r,s)=(3/5,2/5)\) and
\((3/5,3/5)\). Every member of \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{str}}\) is a convex
combination of products, so \(E_p(f)\) lies in \([12/25,13/25]\) on
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{str}}\), and both endpoints are attained.

On \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ei}}\), \(E_p(f)=p_{00}+p_{11}\). The
\(y\)-inequalities give \(p_{11}\geq\tfrac25p(Y{=}1)\) and
\(p_{00}=p(Y{=}0)-p_{10}\geq\tfrac25p(Y{=}0)\), so \(E_p(f)\geq2/5\); they
also give \(p_{11}\leq\tfrac35p(Y{=}1)\) and \(p_{00}\leq\tfrac35p(Y{=}0)\),
so \(E_p(f)\leq3/5\). The value \(2/5\) is attained at \(p^-\). The value
\(3/5\) is attained at \(p^+=(3/10,1/5,1/5,3/10)\), which lies in
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ei}}\) because its four marginal probabilities are
\(1/2\) and its ratios are \(c_1=3/5\), \(c_0=2/5\), \(d_1=3/5\),
\(d_0=2/5\).

On \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{mg}}\) the bounds \(0\leq E_p(f)\leq1\) are
trivial; the
masses \((0,1/2,1/2,0)\) and \((1/2,0,0,1/2)\) have both marginal
probabilities \(1/2\), so they lie in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{mg}}\), and
they give \(E_p(f)=0\) and \(1\).
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.w02b-p15-034.ind-decision}{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-independence-decision}{The comparison of the agreement bet with a sure payment}
\begin{proof}
Since \(g\) is the constant \(9/20\), \(E_p(f-g)=E_p(f)-9/20\) for every
\(p\), and \(\min_{p}E_p(g)=9/20\) over any set. The bounds of item~6 of
\cref{thm:p15-w02b-p15-034-independence-models} therefore give the lower
and upper values of \(f-g\): \(12/25-9/20=3/100\) and \(13/25-9/20=7/100\)
under \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{str}}\); \(2/5-9/20=-1/20\) and
\(3/5-9/20=3/20\) under \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{ei}}\); \(-9/20\) and
\(11/20\) under \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{mg}}\).

(1) Every \(p\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{str}}\) has
\(E_p(f)-E_p(g)\geq3/100>0\), so \(f\succeq_{\rm rob}g\) in the sense of
\cref{def:p17-robust-dominance} and \(g\succeq_{\rm rob}f\) fails. The
maxmin value of \(f\) is \(\underline E_{\mathrm{str}}(f)=12/25>9/20\), so
the maxmin rule chooses \(f\).

(2) The member \(p^-\) has \(E_{p^-}(f)=2/5<9/20\), so
\(f\succeq_{\rm rob}g\) fails; the member \(p^+\) has
\(E_{p^+}(f)=3/5>9/20\), so \(g\succeq_{\rm rob}f\) fails. The maxmin value
of \(f\) is \(\underline E_{\mathrm{ei}}(f)=2/5<9/20\), so the maxmin rule chooses
\(g\).

(3) The members \((0,1/2,1/2,0)\) and \((1/2,0,0,1/2)\) of
\(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{mg}}\) give \(f\) the expectations \(0<9/20\) and
\(1>9/20\), so neither act robustly dominates the other, and
\(\underline E_{\mathrm{mg}}(f)=0<9/20\).

(4) Each product in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{str}}\) gives
\(E_p(f)\geq12/25>9/20\); the single model \(p^-\) gives \(2/5<9/20\). As a
precise model \(p^-\) makes \(X\) and \(Y\) dependent, since its
conditional probability of \(X=1\) given \(Y=1\) is \(2/5\) while its
marginal probability of \(X=1\) is \(1/2\).
\end{proof}
