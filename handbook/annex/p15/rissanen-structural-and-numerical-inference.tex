\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.rmdl.gibbs}{thm:p15-rmdl-gibbs}{Gibbs for finite or countable finite-mass sequences}
\begin{proof}
The case \(M=0\) has all entries zero. Otherwise an entry
\(q_i=0<p_i\) gives infinite divergence. Assume no such entry.
For \(t>0\), \(t-1-\ln t\geq0\), with equality only at one:
its derivative \(1-1/t\) is negative below one and positive above.
For every \(p_i>0\), put
\(c_i=p_i[-\ln(q_i/p_i)+q_i/p_i-1]\geq0\).
A negative divergence term has magnitude
\(p_i\ln(q_i/p_i)\leq q_i-p_i\leq q_i\), so all negative terms
are summable. Finite partial sums and then the countable limit give
\[
 D(p,q)=\sum_{p_i>0}c_i+M-\sum_{p_i>0}q_i\geq0.
\]
Equality forces every \(c_i=0\) and no \(q\)-mass outside the positive
support of \(p\), hence \(p=q\). Conversely identical sequences give
zero. For probabilities with finite entropy, subtracting that finite
entropy from the nonnegative cross-entropy sum is legitimate and gives
the stated identity. No difference of two infinite entropies was taken.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.rmdl.types}{thm:p15-rmdl-types}{Counting, actual finite lengths and the dependent countercase}
\begin{proof}
Choose positions for each successive symbol. The binomial factors
multiply to \(N!/\prod_i k_i!\), also when some counts vanish.
For an injective binary assignment, replacing a used longer word by an
unused shorter word reduces total length. A minimum therefore uses
the shortest \(S\) words. There are \(2^l\) words of length \(l\).
For \(b=\lfloor\log_2(S+1)\rfloor\), complete levels below \(b\)
have \(2^b-1\) words, and the remaining \(S-2^b+1\) have length
\(b\). Induction gives
\(\sum_{l=0}^{b-1}l2^l=(b-2)2^b+2\). Dividing their total length
by \(S\) gives the body formula. Since \(S\geq2^b-1\), its
subtracted fraction is at most two. Thus the mean is at least
\(b-2>\log_2S-3\).

For a finite prefix family, extend each word to a common depth. Their
disjoint descendant sets occupy fractions \(2^{-l}\) of that level,
so their sum is at most one. Countable families follow by finite partial
sums. Normalize these weights by their sum \(W\leq1\); Gibbs applied
to the uniform law on the \(S\) strings gives mean length at least
\(\log_2S-\log_2W\geq\log_2S\).
At most \((N+1)^a\) count tuples are possible. Give them distinct
fixed-length headers of length \(\lceil a\log_2(N+1)\rceil\).
After a header, send a rank in that type in \(\lceil\log_2S\rceil\)
bits, or an empty rank if \(S=1\). The header determines the rank
length. Different headers have equal length, and ranks within one
header have equal length, so no message is a proper prefix of another.
This constructs the claimed code, rather than merely assuming one.

For \(k\geq1\), monotonicity yields
\[
 \int_1^k\ln x\,dx\leq\ln(k!)\leq\int_1^{k+1}\ln x\,dx.
\]
The evaluated integrals bound the magnitude of the difference from
\(k\ln k-k\) by \(\ln(k+1)+1\); the same bound holds at zero
with \(0\ln0=0\). Apply these bounds to \(N!\) and each \(k_i!\).
The linear terms cancel, giving the entropy estimate after division by
\(N\ln2\), with a constant depending only on \(a\).
Continuity of \(-z\log_2z\) at zero and on \([0,1]\) proves the
frequency-limit conclusion.

For the countercase choose a fair bit \(Z\) and set
\(X_t=Z\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}((t-1)\bmod2)\). A shift swaps the
two phases and preserves their equal probabilities. Only the empty and
full phase sets are invariant under this swap, so the process is stationary
and ergodic. Each marginal has entropy one, but every nonempty block
determines only the phase and also has entropy one. Its frequencies
converge to one half and its entropy rate is zero. With \(N\) known,
encode the two alternating strings by \(00,01\), and all other
strings by \(1\) followed by their \(N\) literal bits. This is
prefix-free and codes either realized phase in two bits. It does not
contradict the uniform type-class bound: almost all strings of that
type have probability zero under this dependent process.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.rmdl.dependence}{thm:p15-rmdl-dependence}{Dependence identity and an additive minimizer bound}
\begin{proof}
A positive joint mass has positive marginal mass in every coordinate.
Expanding the logarithm of the product and summing other coordinates gives
\[
 \sum_zP(z)\log_2\frac{P(z)}{\prod_tP_t(z_t)}
       =-H_2(P)+\sum_tH_2(P_t).
\]
The product law is a probability distribution, so Gibbs gives
nonnegativity and equality exactly when the laws coincide, namely
independence. A finite bijection permutes the list of masses and preserves
joint entropy. The exact minimization comparison follows.
For the approximate statement, let \(J_s\) be joint entropy,
\(A_s\) the marginal-entropy sum and \(R_s=A_s-J_s\).
If \(\lvert J_s-J\rvert\leq\eta\) and \(s_*\) minimizes \(A_s\),
then for every \(s\)
\(R_{s_*}-R_s=A_{s_*}-A_s+J_s-J_{s_*}\leq2\eta\).
The assertion applies when such a minimizer exists, as in a finite
family; it does not infer a limiting minimizer from pointwise convergence.
Relative error need not imply vanishing absolute error: \(J_N=N\),
\(\eta_N=1\) gives a ratio tending to zero. Finally, mapping
\(\{0,1,2,3\}\) by \(e=x/10\) is invertible before rounding, but
rounding to the nearest integer collapses all four points. The original
distinctions cannot then be recovered. This proves the stated quantization
countercase without a continuous-entropy assumption.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.rmdl.variance}{thm:p15-rmdl-variance}{Exact differentiation and the degenerate boundary}
\begin{proof}
Abbreviate \(E_N(\eta)=E\) and fix everything else. The variance
part is \((N/2+1)\ln r+E/(2r)\), with derivative
\(((N+2)r-E)/(2r^2)\). For \(E>0\), it is negative below
\(E/(N+2)\) and positive above, giving the unique minimum.
At zero the reciprocal term dominates the negative logarithm and the
cost tends to positive infinity; at infinity the logarithm tends to
positive infinity. Substitution gives \(E/(2\rho)=(N+2)/2\) and
the exact profile in the body. Removing transmission cost \(\ln r\)
instead gives \(E/N\). For \(E=0\), the positive multiple of
\(\ln r\) tends to negative infinity as \(r\downarrow0\), with
no positive minimizer. That approximation failure is not a legitimate
negative-length prefix description or a positive-variance Gaussian model.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.rmdl.precision}{thm:p15-rmdl-precision}{Rounding, width optimization and dimensional constants}
\begin{proof}
Along the segment to \(\widehat\xi+A\), Taylor's formula and
stationarity give \(\Phi(\widehat\xi+A)=\Phi(\widehat\xi)+A^THA/2+R(A)\),
with \(\lvert R(A)\rvert\leq M\|A\|^3/6\).
Centered independent uniforms have variance \(\delta_i^2/12\) and
zero mixed second moments. Taking expectations gives the stated diagonal
term. Their support has
\(\|A\|\leq(\sum_i\delta_i^2)^{1/2}/2\), yielding the remainder
bound. The support box must lie in the stipulated controlled region.

Each leading width term is
\(g_i(\delta)=-\ln\delta+h_i\delta^2/24\).
Its derivative \(-1/\delta+h_i\delta/12\) changes from negative
to positive at \(\delta^2=12/h_i\); both endpoint costs tend to
positive infinity. Its unique minimum value is
\((\ln h_i+1-\ln12)/2\). Admissible-width constraints can exclude
this unconstrained point; the exact expectation also contains the remainder.

In the defined source surrogate \(Q\), hold the fit and curvatures fixed.
Substituting \(h_0=(N+2)/(2\rho^2)\) and
\(h_i=(N+2)G_i/2\) gives the source widths. Each of the \(d=p+1\)
quadratic terms at its width is \(1/2\). The precision logarithms are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 -\ln\delta_0&=-\ln\rho+\tfrac12\ln(N+2)-\tfrac12\ln24,\\
 \ln(\lvert\widehat\eta_i\rvert/\delta_i)
 &=\ln\lvert\widehat\eta_i\rvert+\tfrac12\ln(N+2)
                         +\tfrac12\ln G_i-\tfrac12\ln24.
\end{aligned}
\]
Insert these into the proved profile and add the \(d/2\) rounding cost.
The log-variance coefficient becomes \(N/2\). Multiplication by two gives
\[
 2Q_*=N\ln\rho+\sum_i\ln(\widehat\eta_i^2G_i)+d\ln(N+2)
       +2\kappa_{n,m}+2K_N+(N+2)+d(1-\ln24),
\]
the asserted identity. At fixed \(N,\varepsilon\), the first two
discarded additions are common to structures, but the \(d\)-term is not.

These steps do not make the curvature substitution exact. Direct
differentiation at \(r=\rho\) gives the stated variance second derivative,
but numerical-parameter entries also include magnitude-code curvature,
and mixed variance/parameter entries need not vanish. The stipulated
error law removes mixed terms in expectation, not from the Hessian.
Accuracy of likelihood-curvature replacement needs a separate family bound.
For a zero curvature the width cost \(-\ln\delta\) has no finite
unconstrained minimum, and negative curvature makes the quadratic
surrogate unbounded below at large widths. Finally
\(\delta_0/\rho=\sqrt{24/(N+2)}<1\) exactly for \(N>22\).
The chain rule under \(\psi_i=c_i\eta_i\) gives
\(G_{\psi_i}=G_i/c_i^2\), preserving \(\eta_i^2G_i\) and scaling
width by \(\lvert c_i\rvert\). Translation keeps that second
derivative but changes the squared magnitude, proving the claimed contrast.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.rmdl.leading}{thm:p15-rmdl-leading}{The fixed-dimensional conditional limit}
\begin{proof}
The hypotheses make all logarithms eventually defined. Integrating
\(1/(1+t)\) gives \(u-u^2/2\leq\ln(1+u)\leq u\) for \(u\geq0\).
Thus
\(N\ln\rho=N\ln(E_N/N)-N\ln(1+2/N)
=N\ln(E_N/N)-2+O(N^{-1})\).
Also \(d\ln(N+2)=d\ln N+O(N^{-1})\), since \(d\) is fixed.
Continuity of logarithms at positive limits makes the curvature/magnitude
sum tend to \(\sum_i\ln(c_i^2g_i)\). The structural term is fixed.
Combining these identities proves the formula. No stochastic convergence
or growing-dimension guarantee was inferred merely from this algebra.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p15.rmdl.occam-case}{thm:p15-rmdl-occam-case}{An exact fixed-code comparison of the two sampling regimes}
\begin{proof}
The model headers \(0,10,110\) and literal header \(111\) form a
prefix tree, and each literal suffix has known length \(N\).
A model header tells the decoder to apply its fixed function to the known
inputs. The family is decodable and prefix-free, though different messages
may describe the same data. No post-sample dictionary is concealed.
On all-zero labels, \(h_0\)'s one bit beats \(f\)'s three and literal
cost \(N+3\). On all-one labels, \(h_1\)'s two bits beat those
alternatives. On mixed labels both constants are inconsistent, while
\(f\)'s three bits beat literal cost. These are exactly the minimum
consistent descriptions, in a fixed class containing the true target.

With all training inputs equal to \(Z\), all labels are \(Z\), so
the selected function is a constant. Either constant disagrees with
\(f\) on exactly one equally likely future input, giving error one half
for every realization and every \(N\). With independent inputs, a
constant is selected exactly on the disjoint events that every input is
zero or every input is one. Each event has probability \(2^{-N}\),
so the error-one-half probability is \(2^{1-N}\). Otherwise \(f\)
is selected with zero error. The minimum-code fact holds in both regimes;
the exponentially decreasing future-error probability uses independence.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}
