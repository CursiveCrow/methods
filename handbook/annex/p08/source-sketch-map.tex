\begin{annexitem}{annex:p8.s5i.strong-completeness}{thm:p08-s5i-strong-completeness}{Soundness, maximal extension, existence, and canonical truth}
\begin{proof}
Throughout this proof, derivability is that of
Definition~\ref{def:p08-s5i-completeness-setting} and the subscript on
\(\derives\) is suppressed.

\emph{Soundness.} Propositional tautologies and modus ponens preserve truth
at a world. Distribution holds because truth of both a conditional and its
antecedent at every \(i\)-successor gives truth of its consequent there.
Reflexivity validates factivity. Transitivity validates positive
introspection: every successor of an \(i\)-successor is already an
\(i\)-successor of the original world. For negative introspection,
suppose \(K_iB\) fails at \(w\), witnessed by \(w\sim_i u\) with
\(B\) false at \(u\). If \(w\sim_i v\), equivalence gives
\(v\sim_i u\); hence \(K_iB\) fails at \(v\). Thus
\(\neg K_iB\) holds at every \(i\)-successor of \(w\).
Theorem-only necessitation is sound because a theorem is true at every
world of every such model, including all successors. Formally, induction
on the finite proof tree, including the separate no-premise proofs used
by necessitation, first proves validity of theorems and then preservation
of truth of premises along a premise derivation. A one-world equivalence
frame shows that \(\bot\) is not a theorem.

\emph{Deduction and maximal extension.} The deduction theorem holds:
if \(\Delta\cup\{B\}\derives C\), then
\(\Delta\derives B\to C\). To verify it, induct on a derivation of
\(C\). The discharged assumption uses the tautology \(B\to B\).
An axiom, a member of \(\Delta\), or a necessitation conclusion already
proved without premises can first be derived from \(\Delta\), and then
the tautology \(C\to(B\to C)\) gives the required conditional.
For a modus ponens step, use the tautology
\((B\to(D\to C))\to((B\to D)\to(B\to C))\).
These are all cases: a necessitation step cannot depend on the discharged
assumption. The converse of the deduction theorem follows by modus ponens.

Call \(\Delta\) consistent if \(\Delta\not\derives\bot\).
Consistency has finite character, since every proof uses finitely many
premises. The union of a chain of consistent supersets of a fixed
consistent set is consistent: all premises of a purported contradiction
belong to one member of the chain. Zorn's lemma therefore extends any
consistent set to a maximal consistent set. This argument applies to the
set of all formulas for an arbitrary set of agents; no enumeration of the
language is needed.

If \(w\) is maximal consistent, then for every formula \(B\) exactly
one of \(B,\neg B\) belongs to \(w\). They cannot both belong, by
classical logic. If \(B\notin w\), maximality makes
\(w\cup\{B\}\) inconsistent; deduction gives \(w\derives\neg B\).
Whenever \(w\derives C\), adding \(C\) preserves consistency, since
each use of \(C\) in a contradiction proof can be replaced by its finite
derivation from \(w\). Maximality then gives \(C\in w\). Applying
this closure property to \(C=\neg B\) proves the claim. In particular,
\(w\) contains every theorem, is closed under modus ponens, and does not
contain \(\bot\).

These facts give the Boolean membership clauses. Negation is membership
complement. A conjunction belongs to \(w\) exactly when both conjuncts
do, using the two projection tautologies and the conjunction-introduction
tautology. A disjunction belongs exactly when at least one disjunct does:
either disjunct implies it, and if neither belongs, both negations do,
which contradict the disjunction by a classical tautology. Finally,
\(B\to C\) belongs exactly when \(B\notin w\) or \(C\in w\):
either alternative implies the conditional by a tautology, while the
conditional together with \(B\in w\) gives \(C\in w\).
For a biconditional, its tautological equivalence with the conjunction of
the two implications gives membership exactly when both sides have the
same membership value. Truth and falsity constants have their usual
clauses; any other defined Boolean connectives may be expanded by their
classical definitions.

\emph{Finite modal lifting.} If \(C_1,\ldots,C_m\derives B\),
successive uses of deduction give the theorem
\(C_1\to(C_2\to\cdots(C_m\to B)\cdots)\).
Necessitate this theorem for a fixed \(i\), and apply distribution and
modus ponens successively with \(K_iC_1,\ldots,K_iC_m\). The result is
\(K_iB\). When \(m=0\), necessitate the theorem \(B\) itself. Thus
\(K_iC_1,\ldots,K_iC_m\derives K_iB\), without ever necessitating
a formula derived only under these premises.

\emph{Canonical model and existence.} Let \(W\) be the set of all
maximal consistent sets; it is nonempty by consistency of the empty set
and the extension argument. Put
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 D_i(w)=\{B:K_iB\in w\},\qquad V(p)=\{w:p\in w\},\\
 w\sim_i v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad D_i(w)\subseteq v.
 \end{gathered}
\]
If \(\neg K_iB\in w\), the set \(D_i(w)\cup\{\neg B\}\)
is consistent. Otherwise a finite subset
\(C_1,\ldots,C_m\) of \(D_i(w)\), together with \(\neg B\),
would derive \(\bot\). Deduction and classical double-negation
elimination give \(C_1,\ldots,C_m\derives B\). Finite modal lifting
would then put \(K_iB\) in \(w\), a contradiction. Extend
\(D_i(w)\cup\{\neg B\}\) to a maximal consistent set \(v\).
It satisfies \(w\sim_i v\) and \(\neg B\in v\). This is the
modal existence lemma, including the case of no \(C_j\).

\emph{Frame properties.} Factivity gives \(D_i(w)\subseteq w\),
so \(w\sim_i w\). If \(w\sim_i v\), \(v\sim_i u\), and
\(K_iB\in w\), positive introspection puts \(K_iK_iB\) in
\(w\), hence \(K_iB\in v\) and \(B\in u\). This proves
transitivity. For Euclideanness suppose \(w\sim_i v\) and
\(w\sim_i u\). If \(K_iB\in v\) but \(K_iB\notin w\),
maximality and negative introspection put \(K_i\neg K_iB\) in
\(w\), hence \(\neg K_iB\in v\), a contradiction. Therefore
\(K_iB\in v\) implies \(K_iB\in w\) and then \(B\in u\).
Thus \(v\sim_i u\). Reflexivity and Euclideanness also give symmetry:
from \(w\sim_i v\) and \(w\sim_i w\) infer \(v\sim_i w\).
Each relation is consequently an equivalence. Every calculation fixes
one agent; none requires a relation between distinct agents.

\emph{Truth lemma and completeness.} Structural induction proves
\(\mathcal M,w\entails B\) exactly when \(B\in w\).
Atoms follow from the definition of \(V\); the Boolean steps are the
membership clauses already established. If \(K_iB\in w\), every
\(i\)-successor contains \(B\), so the induction hypothesis makes
\(B\) true at each successor. If \(K_iB\notin w\), maximality
gives \(\neg K_iB\in w\) and modal existence supplies a successor
containing \(\neg B\); there \(B\) is false by induction. These
prove both directions of the modal step.

If \(\Gamma\not\derives A\), then
\(\Gamma\cup\{\neg A\}\) is consistent. Otherwise deduction
would give \(\Gamma\derives\neg\neg A\), and classical logic would
give \(\Gamma\derives A\). Extend it to a maximal consistent world
\(w\). The truth lemma makes all of \(\Gamma\) true and \(A\)
false at \(w\) in the canonical equivalence model. Hence semantic
consequence implies derivability. Soundness supplies the converse.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p8.s5i.finite-countermodel}{thm:p08-s5i-finite-countermodel}{Truth under equivalence filtration}
\begin{proof}
The truth profile of a world is the subset of \(\Sigma\) true there.
Two worlds have the same profile exactly when they are
\(\equiv_\Sigma\)-equivalent, so there are at most
\(2^{\lvert\Sigma\rvert}\) classes. They form a nonempty set. Since
the original \(\sim_i\) is reflexive and symmetric, its image
\(E_i\) is reflexive and symmetric. Its finite-path closure
\(R_i^\Sigma\) is reflexive, symmetric (reverse a path), and
transitive (concatenate paths). The filtered model therefore remains in
the required frame class. Its valuation is well-defined because any atom
in \(\Sigma\) has constant truth value on a profile class.

We prove preservation by induction on \(B\in\Sigma\). Subformula
closure makes each induction hypothesis available. The atom case follows
from \(V_\Sigma\); constants retain their fixed truth values. Negation,
conjunction, disjunction, and implication follow by applying the induction
hypotheses to their Boolean truth clauses. It remains to treat \(K_iC\).

First observe the following fact about the original model. If
\(K_iC\in\Sigma\), a class \(c\) has \(K_iC\) true, and
\(cE_i d\), then both \(K_iC\) and \(C\) are true throughout
\(d\). Indeed choose witnesses \(x\in c,y\in d\) with
\(x\sim_i y\). Since equivalence-related worlds have identical
\(i\)-successor sets, truth of \(K_iC\) at \(x\) implies its
truth at \(y\); also \(C\) holds at \(y\). Both formulas belong
to \(\Sigma\), so their truth is constant on \(d\).

Suppose \(\mathcal M,w\entails K_iC\) and
\([w]_\Sigma R_i^\Sigma d\). Along a positive-length \(E_i\)-path
from \([w]_\Sigma\) to \(d\), the preceding observation propagates
\(K_iC\) and makes \(C\) true in the final class. Along a
length-zero path, reflexivity of the original relation already makes
\(C\) true at \(w\), and hence throughout its class. In both cases
the induction hypothesis makes \(C\) true at \(d\) in the filtered
model. Thus \(K_iC\) is true at \([w]_\Sigma\) there.

Conversely, if \(\mathcal M,w\not\entails K_iC\), choose
\(v\) with \(w\sim_i v\) and \(\mathcal M,v\not\entails C\).
Then \([w]_\Sigma E_i[v]_\Sigma\), so these classes are also
\(R_i^\Sigma\)-related. The induction hypothesis makes \(C\) false
at \([v]_\Sigma\) in the filtered model, witnessing failure of
\(K_iC\) at \([w]_\Sigma\). This completes the induction. Taking
the equivalence closure has added successors, but the propagation argument
has checked every added successor, which is the necessary S5 filtration
step.

For finite \(\Gamma\), strong completeness supplies a countermodel to
\(\Gamma\derives A\) when that derivation fails. Apply the construction
to the finite subformula closure of \(\Gamma\cup\{A\}\).
Preservation keeps all premises true and the conclusion false at the
chosen class. For satisfiability, start with a world satisfying \(A\)
and use its subformula closure instead. The same counting bound applies.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p8.s5i.finite-decision}{thm:p08-s5i-finite-decision}{Finite-input decision}
\begin{proof}
For a finite premise set \(\Gamma\) and conclusion \(A\), compute
the subformula closure \(\Sigma\) of \(\Gamma\cup\{A\}\), the
finite set of occurring atoms, and the finite set \(J\) of occurring
agent names. All are obtained by traversing the finite syntax trees. Set
\(N=2^{\lvert\Sigma\rvert}\). For each integer \(1\leq n\leq N\),
enumerate all binary relations on \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) for each agent
in \(J\), retain exactly the reflexive, symmetric, transitive ones,
and enumerate all subsets of this world set as valuations of each
occurring atom. This is a finite search, including when \(J\) is empty.
Assign the identity relation to agents outside \(J\), and the empty
valuation to atoms not occurring in the input. These assignments extend
each candidate to a model of the full language without changing the
truth of any input formula, by structural induction.

At every world of each candidate, evaluate each input formula recursively.
Boolean operations are finite truth-table calculations, and a modal clause
checks finitely many related worlds. If some world satisfies \(\Gamma\)
and falsifies \(A\), return that countermodel and answer that the
derivation fails. Soundness makes this answer correct. If the finite
search ends without such a world, answer that the derivation exists:
otherwise Theorem~\ref{thm:p08-s5i-finite-countermodel} would supply a
countermodel within the enumerated bound, and some bijective numbering of
its worlds would occur among the candidates. Hence this second answer is
also correct, and the procedure terminates.

Validity is the case \(\Gamma=\varnothing\). For satisfiability, compute
the subformulas of \(A\), search the corresponding finite candidates,
and answer yes exactly when \(A\) holds at some enumerated world. A
witness proves the yes answer; filtration of any model of \(A\) shows
that failure to find a witness proves unsatisfiability. The procedure is
an exhaustive decision argument, not a claimed optimal complexity bound.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}
