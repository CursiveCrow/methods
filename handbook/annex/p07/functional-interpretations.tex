\begin{annexitem}{annex:p7.functional.1}{thm:p07-lj-disjunction-last-rule}{Cut-free LJ disjunction extraction}
\begin{proof}
Let \(D\) be a derivation on
\hyperref[sheet:lj]{\texttt{sheet:lj}} of
\(\Rightarrow\varphi\lor\psi\), and suppose \(D\) contains neither
Cut nor Mix.  Inspect its last inference.

It is not Id, whose conclusion has its succedent formula in the antecedent,
and not \(\bottom L\), whose conclusion has \(\bottom\) in the antecedent.
It is not left weakening, contraction, or a left logical or quantifier
inference: every such conclusion has a displayed principal formula in its
antecedent, whereas the antecedent here is empty.  The calculus has no right
weakening or right contraction.  It is not a right rule for conjunction,
implication, or a quantifier, because those rules produce a conclusion whose
succedent has the corresponding outermost connective.  Cut and Mix are
excluded by hypothesis.

The only remaining rules on the sheet whose conclusion has empty antecedent
and disjunctive succedent are \(\lor R_1\) and \(\lor R_2\).  In the first
case the unique premise of the last inference is
\(\Rightarrow\varphi\); in the second it is \(\Rightarrow\psi\).  That premise
is a derivation on the same sheet and gives the required alternative.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}
