\hbannexchapter{10}{Algebraic and Categorical Logic}\label{annex:p10}

The following proofs develop term evaluation, equational completeness,
free algebras, subdirect representation, Stone duality, and the
countable atomless Boolean algebra.
The later proofs develop Boolean operators, canonical extensions,
descriptive frames, and modal algebraic completeness.
The ordered-lattice proofs supply prime-filter separation,
Priestley and Esakia duality, and intuitionistic algebraic and
Kripke completeness, alongside the distinct Rasiowa--Sikorski lemma.
The categorical proofs give the elementary universal constructions,
Yoneda, and adjunctions, followed by typed context categories and
the reconstruction of a small category from its internal language.
The Lawvere-theory proofs give the algebra/model equivalence,
named presentations, restriction along theory morphisms, and
free models with their precise carrier conventions.
The predicate-doctrine proofs supply quantifier adjunctions,
substitution stability, parameterized equality, and the distinction
between unrestricted and inhabited sort interpretations.
