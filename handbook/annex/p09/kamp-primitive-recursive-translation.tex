% Explicit coding, family growth and bounded evaluation for Kamp's Exp function.

\section{Primitive recursiveness of Kamp's translation}
\label{sec:annex-p09-kamp-primitive-recursive-translation}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p9.kamp.pr.codes}{thm:p09-kamp-pr-codes}{Finite codes and every basic syntax operation}
\begin{proof}
For a positive list code \(c\), repeatedly halve it until it is odd.
The number of halvings is its head token \(a\); the remaining odd
number is \(2b+1\), giving tail \(b\). This is a bounded
iteration: at most \(c\) halvings suffice, with the state held
fixed once odd. The tail is strictly smaller than \(c\).
Consequently a scan of at most the original code many tail steps
decodes the entire list; unused steps hold the completed state fixed.
Halving, parity, addition, multiplication, exponentiation and bounded
iteration are primitive recursive. Thus the projections and decoding
are primitive recursive. Encoding is primitive recursion on a finite
list, using the displayed constructor.

If a list has tokens \(a_0,\ldots,a_{r-1}\), expansion of its
code is a sum of powers of two with strictly increasing exponents
from the outside toward the end. Its largest exponent is
\(\sum_i a_i+r-1\). Hence its code is strictly below
\(2^{\sum_i a_i+r}\), and in particular below \(2^{w(a)}\).
The empty list satisfies the claimed bound as well. This supplies
a numerical bound whenever a data-weight bound is used below.

Prefix parsing scans the finite token list with a stack of required
operand counts. Predicate and order tags consume their indicated
indices; connective and quantifier tags push exactly their arities.
Reject an out-of-range tag, an incomplete operand or trailing tokens
after the unique root is complete. This scan is bounded by the decoded
length. Store each node's start, end and children; postorder and
subformula occurrence lists are then bounded scans of that finite table.
The table also computes free-variable sets by finite union and removal
at quantifier nodes. Every candidate index is at most the largest
index occurring in the input, so a bit-vector or finite sorted list
implements these sets with bounded loops.

To freshen a formula, first list its free and bound indices, then
assign its bound occurrences distinct indices above the maximum
forbidden one, in prefix order. A bounded traversal with the binding
stack replaces exactly the occurrences governed by each binder.
For substitution, freshen the replacement's binders away from the
receiving formula, list the original target occurrences, and replace
them once. Do not visit inserted copies as new substitution targets.
This is a bounded scan of the original occurrence table followed by
finite concatenation. Successive substitutions use their specified
finite target list. The same operation on temporal trees has no
binding stack.

Finite lists are compared lexicographically by scanning their common
prefix and then their first difference or lengths. Sorting can use
a bounded double-loop comparison sort. Finite subsets are enumerated
by masks below \(2^k\); functions on finite domains by bounded
tuples of their values; and binary relations on \(k\) tags by
masks below \(2^{k^2}\). Reflexivity, transitivity, totality,
endpoint identifications and the restrictions defining a fusion are
checked by bounded loops over at most triples of tags. Ordered
partitions can be obtained in the same way, without an unbounded
search for an order type. Boolean normal forms are generated by
the finite truth table of their Boolean skeleton, treating the
listed non-Boolean constituents as distinct propositional inputs.
Their evaluation is a postorder finite-tree computation, not a
test of first-order truth in a structure.

All these implementations use composition, primitive recursion and
bounded tests. Their declared defaults make every numerical operation
total. A final input validity test returns the one-free-variable input flag;
outside that flag the translator returns the fixed temporal code
\([0,0]^\#=3\). This total extension imposes no semantic
requirement on a malformed code.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p9.kamp.pr.local.envelope}{thm:p09-kamp-pr-local-envelope}{Bounded batches, repeated sweeps and local expansion}
\begin{proof}
We make explicit the implementation granularity of a local phase.
A \emph{sweep} reads a frozen finite collection of input objects,
enumerates its prescribed finite candidates, and writes their results
to a new collection. Newly written objects are not used as new inputs
until the next sweep. Index loops are bounded by the input tables or
the masks of their finite subsets, tuples or relation matrices.

For data weight \(t\), there are at most \(t\) token
positions, named tags and original labels in a single frozen input.
The following estimates apply to one sweep:
\[
\begin{array}{l|l}
 \text{operation}&\text{candidate or expansion estimate}\\ \hline
 \text{parsing, renaming, matching}&\text{at most polynomially many bounded scans}\\
 \text{one substitution}&\text{at most }t\text{ copies of a replacement of weight at most }t\\
 \text{Boolean DNF or CNF}&2^t\text{ rows, at most }t\text{ literals per row}\\
 \text{ordered partitions or fusions}&2^{t^2}\text{ matrices, at most }t^3\text{ local checks each}\\
 \text{word cuts and cut-subset tuples}&2^{t^2}\text{ choices, with polynomial-size records}\\
 \text{distribution or concatenation}&\text{finite products of at most }t\text{ listed choices}.
\end{array}
\]
In the scan rows one can use at most \((t+16)^8\) scan positions
or constructor positions before the bounded candidate enumeration;
the parsing tables, triples of relation tags and copied label trees
fit this explicit polynomial allowance. In the last row the lists are part of the frozen input, so the
sum of their lengths and contents is at most \(t\); their
Cartesian product has at most \(2^{t^2}\) entries. A fusion's
label requirements refer to at most the original point and interval
tags, and hence have polynomial total size per candidate. A new
fresh name is obtained from the current forbidden maximum plus
the next finite counter. Allowing candidate identifiers and fresh-name
records gives the conservative combined estimate
\(2^{2t^2}(t+16)^{16}\), which is less than
\(2^{(t+16)^4}\). Thus these counters and the weights of all
retained input and generated output are bounded by
\(2^{(t+16)^4}\). The quartic bound dominates the products
of candidate counts, scans, labels and index weights just listed.

With the chosen list encoding the corresponding object codes are
below \(2^{2^{(t+16)^4}}\). Bounded decoding by repeated
halving, list scans, comparisons and constructor operations can
therefore be implemented with a construction-step bound below
\(R(t)=2^{2^{2^{(t+16)^4}}}\). In particular the estimate
allows the finite code-arithmetic loops; it does not treat an
unbounded search or a semantic oracle as a constant-time operation.
Output and temporary data weight are below this same enlarged bound.

We also bound the number of successive sweeps in one nonrecursive
phase. A renaming or substitution phase has a fixed original target
list of length at most the input weight. A limiting-word recipe
processes at most its input word positions. A segmented assembly
cuts each original edge at each original named vertex, at most
\(t^2\) stages; each stage applies its cut to all current
branches in one batch. Fusion and concatenation then process the
fixed edge/vertex lists, again at most a quadratic number of stages.
The eleven-branch construction has a fixed number of preliminary
groupings and final products. Boolean conversions, sorting and
relation enumerations are the single-batch operations described
above. Including preprocessing, target-list scans, empty cases and
the retention of input/history data, fewer than \(32(t+1)^2\)
successive sweeps suffice. At each stage its current data is bounded
by one application of \(R\) to the preceding bound.

This count refers only to the fixed local recipes just enumerated.
A recursive call to Lemma~8 is not one of these sweeps. Nor does
this count include the separate visits to all original nodes of
an outer syntax tree. Those two kinds of iteration are bounded
in the following proofs. Iterating \(R\) the stated number
of times, starting at \(t+16\), bounds every local phase by
\(A(t)\). All loops have numerical limits given before they
start; \(R\), its iteration and \(A\) are primitive
recursive by their explicit recurrences.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p9.kamp.pr.lemma8}{thm:p09-kamp-pr-lemma8}{Length levels, whole-tree growth and bounded evaluation}
\begin{proof}
Use the direct occurrence-word version of the completed Lemma~8
construction. Its pure recursive input consists only of At trees
and oriented basic words; the optional preliminary renaming of
every occurrence is not repeated inside this kernel. Each input
member is enumerated once. At a nonbase node one currently maximal
basic member is deleted. A remaining word of length \(l\)
has \(l-2\) possible cuts. Any selected subset contributes
at most that many residual words, each of length at most \(l\).
Their total mass is at most
\((l-2)(l+1)\leq l(l+1)\), at most \(M\) times that
original word's contribution \(l+1\). A native basic word
contributes just itself. At trees are retained, and the one fresh
atom contributes two units. The offset in the mass definition
then gives, conservatively,
\[
                         \mu(G)\leq(M+3)(\mu(F)+1).
 \tag{1}
\]
Removing duplicate records can only reduce this mass. Orientation
reversal preserves word lengths and cannot increase it.

At a fixed current maximum length \(m\), each edge that
retains that maximum decreases its multiplicity by at least one.
The multiplicity at entry to the level is at most the entry
mass \(q\). Thus after at most \(q\) edges the branch
has left that level or reached a base case. It can never return
to that or a larger level, because residual lengths do not increase.
This is the additional structural fact that a general lexicographic
recursion need not have with controlled input growth.

Put \(a=M+3\). Iterating the affine bound \(g(q)=a(q+1)\)
for \(k\) steps gives
\[
             g^{[k]}(q)=a^kq+a\frac{a^k-1}{a-1}
                         \leq a^k(q+2).
\]
For \(k\leq q\), this is at most \(a^{2(q+1)}\),
because \(q+2\leq a^{q+2}\) and \(a\geq4\).
The bound is monotone, so it controls all intermediate masses in
that level as well as the exit mass. At most \(M+1\)
levels, including the final base, can occur. The displayed
recurrence for \(b_i\) therefore gives the bound \(B\)
for every input mass along a branch. The sum of all level lengths
and its terminal node is below \(h=(M+1)(B+1)+1\).
No appeal to mere termination is used for these numerical bounds.

There are at most 11 boundary cases and at most three universal
regions per case. For one region the complete subset tuple has
at most \(2^{M\mu(F)}\) members, since every original word
contributes at most \(M\) cut positions. The deterministic
implementation translates each region/tuple once, before distributing
products of returned packages. Thus it has at most
\(33\,2^{M\mu(F)}\) immediate recursive calls. This is
below the declared global \(c\). With depth at most \(h\),
the geometric count of all recursive nodes is less than
\((c+1)^{h+1}=z\). Repeated equal subproblems may still be
evaluated separately; this bound does not rely on memoization.

The one predicate placeholder in a child is chosen above the
maximum index of its own parent family. It is absent from the
additional label, which uses only parent letters and the fixed
constant letter. Hence along a branch every predicate index is
at most \(p+h\). Separate children may reuse such a temporary
index because it is removed before their outputs are combined.
Recursive family records use fixed endpoint slots. At data and
word records of mass at most \(B\), their indices and their
finite orientation/length fields have serialized weight below
\(s_*=64(B+h+p+M+16)^2\). Expanded bound names for output
formulas are generated only in their local construction phase,
where the preceding local envelope bounds them. They are not
an uncontrolled global counter fed into later child families.

All child input lists and boundary-support data are produced by
a nonrecursive phase on such a family record, bounded by
\(A(s_*)\). Suppose each child's returned data has weight
at most \(u_i\). There are at most \(c\) children, so
the parent's complete postprocessing input has weight at most
\(A(s_*)+c(u_i+1)+16\). Its nonrecursive substitutions,
products, edge cuts, fusions and concatenations are bounded by
one further application of \(A\). This is precisely the
recurrence for \(u_{i+1}\). The base case is bounded by
\(u_0=A(s_*)\). Induction on remaining tree height proves
that \(u_h\) bounds every returned representation and every
local phase's data and construction steps.

Multiply this local bound by the number of nodes and allow the
finite stack, child-index and data-copy bookkeeping. The stated
square in \(L_8\) dominates these sums and products, so
\(L_8(M,s,p)\) bounds the full kernel's construction steps
and its output weight. The positive parameters in the definitions
also cover an empty or point-only input, which returns immediately.
Every numerical function used in this argument is obtained by
composition and the displayed ordinary primitive recursions.

Finally the evaluator can be implemented by the stated explicit
stack frames. Its transition dispatches by a finite tag, reads
the next indexed child, performs a local finite-list operation,
pushes a child frame or returns a completed result. Each transition
is primitive recursive by the coding lemma and the local implementations.
Hold a halted state fixed. Iterating this transition exactly
\(L_8(M,s,p)\) times, starting with the input frame, and
projecting the output gives the kernel's value. This is bounded
iteration of a fixed primitive-recursive transition with a
primitive-recursive bound. It proves primitive recursiveness of
the complete kernel, including its recursive structure.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p9.kamp.primitive.recursive.translation}{thm:p09-kamp-primitive-recursive-translation}{All outer stages, exact schema padding and the final numerical function}
\begin{proof}
We first justify every outer bound, using the actual finite
constructions already proved. A local node of the Bet-to-interval
compiler combines previously returned representations by Lemma~5
or Lemma~9. Its nonrecursive preprocessing has bound \(A(t)\).
In the universal case it creates one basic skeleton family for
Lemma~8 of weight at most \(A(t)\), so the kernel bound
is at most \(L(A(t))\). All subsequent proper substitutions
and combinations have bound \(A\) on the combined data.
The definition of \(J(t)\) bounds the whole node, including
retained data and its work. An existential node needs only local
assembly and has a smaller bound. Thus \(J\) bounds both
source quantifier constructors without treating Lemma~8 as an
unbounded subroutine.

The following structural services each make at most
\(4(t+1)^2\) such node visits on original input of weight
\(t\): the first-order standard-form construction; signed
interval Boolean normalization; the Bet/positive-interval representation
compiler; translation of an elementary history to a temporal formula;
and formation of its \(D\)-image. Here are their individual
recursion justifications. Prenex standardization freshens the original
binders and moves each through its finite Boolean context; it then
processes at most the original number of quantifiers, as its proof
states, without processing newly generated regional components as
new original quantifiers. The signed interval normalization visits
proper original component histories; the new Bet leaves it constructs
are outputs, not recursive inputs. The Bet representation compiler
visits its original stage tree, and each universal node has the
justified \(J\) bound. The elementary translator visits the
original history tree; a substitution node performs a finite
substitution on returned children and does not recursively evaluate
the inserted output as a new history. The \(D\) translation
visits the original temporal tree and uses finite freshening and
the fixed strict \(S,U\) clauses. Postorder tables retain
their original node identities in all these services. Processing
all nodes, their operand slots and the fixed setup/finalization
passes fits the stated quadratic visit bound.

Each visit is bounded by \(J\) on its current retained data,
and \(J\) dominates \(A\). Iterating it gives \(S(t)\)
as a bound for the output, temporary data and construction steps
of every listed structural service. This also covers source
retention steps when histories rather than minimal stages are
supplied; their finite occurrences are part of the input size.
No recursion on a newly expanded output has been hidden in this
fixed original-node count.

A primitive Bef/Aft conversion has at most six successive services:
parsing and classification; outer quantifier negation when needed;
interval negation normalization; its finite DNF conversion; interval
representation of the resulting positive expression; and the appropriate
guarded closure followed by outer negation. Each service is bounded
by \(S\) on the current total data. Thus \(C(t)\) is
an explicit bound. Existential primitive roots skip some services
and satisfy the same bound. All disjuncts, endpoint factors and
empty-region conditions are included in these services.

For a node of maximal-proper-region restoration, all recursive children
are proper original regional subtrees. After their returned elementary
histories are available, freshening and construction of the primitive
skeleton have bound \(A(t)\); its primitive conversion has
bound \(C(A(t))\). The finite target list of proper substitutions
and their expansions is bounded by \(A\) on the combined data.
Hence \(V(t)\) bounds that whole node. The restoration tree
and the final Boolean combination of the standard form have no
more nodes than the original standard-form data weight, plus a
final root. Iterating \(V\) therefore gives \(W(t)\).
Fresh indices chosen after the child results are bounded by the
current data weight and the finite target count, as included in
the local envelope; they are not omitted from the size estimate.

We can now bound the complete pipeline. Initial validity testing,
expansion of implication and biconditional, and freshening are
bounded by \(n_1=A(n)\). The first-order standard form is
bounded by \(n_2=S(n_1)\). Regional elementary construction
is bounded by \(n_3=W(n_2)\), and its temporal translation
by \(n_4=S(n_3)\). The number of permitted predicate indices
is at most the original weight \(n\). Appending one
tautological conjunct for each missing designated letter, in
increasing order, is a bounded scan and finite tree construction,
bounded by \(n_5=A(n_4+n+16)\). Translation of that final
schema into the literal \(D\)-image has bound \(n_6=S(n_5)\).
Summing these stage bounds with their setup and concatenation costs
gives the stated \(T(n)\). The atomic early-return case is
smaller and satisfies it as well.

All functions defining these limits are explicit compositions and
primitive recursions. In particular \(S\) iterates a fixed
function \(J\), \(C\) is six iterations of \(S\),
and \(W\) iterates the fixed function \(V\). None defines
a function by substituting a new unbounded recursive argument into
itself. These outer bounds therefore remain primitive recursive,
rather than appealing to a general closure under well-founded
recursion, which would be invalid.

Use the deterministic stack program for all the named operation
tags, with the finite-list transitions described above. Its
single transition is primitive recursive, and after halting it
keeps the output unchanged. The output projection after exactly
\(T(w(c))\) transitions is therefore a primitive-recursive
function of the input code \(c\). Weight and input validity
are primitive recursive by the coding lemma. The bound may be
computed as a separate primitive-recursive function; no search
for a stopping time or a semantically equivalent formula is used.
The code estimate in that lemma gives output and \(D\)-image
codes below \(2^{T(w(c))}\).

Logical correctness is the already proved constructive Theorem~III.2.
The deterministic ordering choices select among its full finite
alternatives and never discard a required alternative. Its source
constant convention and all properly eliminated temporary letters
give an output using only the permitted \(q_i\), for positive
source arity. The final padding is a conjunction with formulas
true at every point under every valuation, so it changes no truth
condition. It ensures that every permitted letter occurs. Hence
the output is an actual n-place schema under the source's exact
definition, not merely a formula using a subset of those letters.
Its \(D\)-image is obtained by the original strict clauses,
and the \(\mathsf{Com}\) equivalence is preserved.

For the possible nullary reading, an induction on the temporal
grammar shows that every formula contains a proposition letter:
an atomic formula contains its own letter, and each of the four
positive-arity constructors retains the letters of its operand or
operands. Thus there is no variable-free formula, hence no
zero-place schema in that literal grammar. A zero-predicate input
can still have its truth condition represented by the preceding
construction with an inessential \(q_0\), independently of
its valuation, but the result is a unary schema. This explicitly
states the syntactic boundary if the source's unspecified arity
parameter is taken to include zero; it does not change either
language or falsely label the output.

Finally \([0,0,0]^\#=2(2(1)+1)+1=7\), and
\([0,0]^\#=3\). Their weights are 4 and 3. The atomic
branch returns \(q_0\), which already contains the sole
required letter, and its literal image is \(P_0(t_0)\).
For the family example, the displayed mass calculation gives 7.
Removing its only basic word leaves \(P_0\); the maximum original
predicate index is 3, so the prescribed placeholder is \(P_4\).
The actual child mass is \(1+2+2=5\), and (1) of the
kernel proof gives the uniform upper bound 48 for one edge. The affine
iteration bound at \(M=3,s=7\) is \(6^{16}\), as stated.
These verify a concrete translation and a nontrivial family-growth
instance without claiming that the very coarse general estimates
describe practical running times \cite[ch.~II, pp.~21--23,28]{Kamp1968}.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}
