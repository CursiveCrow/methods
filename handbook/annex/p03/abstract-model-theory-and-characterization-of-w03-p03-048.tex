\section{Proofs for guarded translations and the Karp route}
\label{sec:annex-p03-w03-p03-048-barwise}

Throughout, \(\mathcal L^{\ast}\) is a guarded logic on a category of languages
\(\mathcal C\) in the sense of
\cref{def:p03-w03-p03-048-b74-languages,def:p03-w03-p03-048-b74-logic},
and clause~1 of \cref{def:p03-w03-p03-048-standing} holds.  ``New'' symbols
are chosen as described after \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-first-order}: by
clause (iii) of the definition of a category of languages a finite language
of prescribed kinds and arities has a copy in \(\mathcal C\) whose only
common symbol with a given language is \(\mathbf V\), and by clause (i) the
union of the two is in \(\mathcal C\).  A \(0\)-term interpretation between
languages of \(\mathcal C\) that is the identity except at finitely many
symbols is a morphism by clause (iv); every renaming or relativization used
below is of this kind.  First-order satisfaction is Tarski's, also on the
empty structure: \(\exists x\,\chi\) is false and \(\forall x\,\chi\) true
there.  We write \(\top\) for \(\forall x\,(x=x)\) and \(\bot\) for
\(\neg\top\).

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p03-w03-p03-048-basic}{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-basic}{Reducts and invertible translations}
\begin{proof}
(1) By the Occurrence Axiom \(L_\varphi\) is in \(\mathcal C\).  The inclusion
\(i:L_\varphi\subseteq K\) is a morphism (clause (ii)), and by the Occurrence
Axiom \(i^{\ast}\) is the inclusion of \(L_\varphi^{\ast}\) in \(K^{\ast}\);
so \(\varphi=i^{\ast}(\varphi)\in K^{\ast}\).

(2) Let \(i:L_\varphi\subseteq K\).  Then \(\varphi^{i}=\varphi\).  An
inclusion is a simple interpretation: it has no terms and sends
\(\mathbf V\) to \(\mathbf V\).  The pull-back of a \(K\)-structure
\(\mathfrak M\) along \(i\) has universe \(\mathbf V^{\mathfrak M}=M\) and
interprets each symbol of \(L_\varphi\) as \(\mathfrak M\) does, so it is the
reduct \(\mathfrak M\restriction L_\varphi\), a total structure; thus
\(\mathfrak M\) is \(i\)-invertible.  The Translation Axiom gives
\(\mathfrak M\models\varphi\) iff \(\mathfrak M\restriction L_\varphi\models
\varphi\).  The same holds for \(\mathfrak M'\) and \(K'\), and the reducts
coincide.

(3) Let \(i:L_\varphi\subseteq L\) and \(\beta=\alpha i\); \(\beta\) is a
morphism of \(\mathcal C\), being a composite of morphisms, and it is the
restriction of \(\alpha\) to \(L_\varphi\) with the same terms.  By
restriction coherence and the Occurrence Axiom,
\(\varphi^{\beta}=\alpha^{\ast}(i^{\ast}(\varphi))=\alpha^{\ast}(\varphi)=
\varphi^{\alpha}\).  The pull-backs along \(\beta\) and \(\alpha\) have the
same universe (both are computed from \(\mathbf V^{\alpha}\) and the same
terms) and interpret each symbol of \(L_\varphi\) in the same way, so
\(\mathfrak N^{-\beta}=\mathfrak N^{-\alpha}\restriction L_\varphi\), which
is a total structure since \(\mathfrak N^{-\alpha}\) is.  By the Translation
Axiom for \(\beta\), \(\mathfrak N\models\varphi^{\alpha}\) iff
\(\mathfrak N^{-\alpha}\restriction L_\varphi\models\varphi\), iff
\(\mathfrak N^{-\alpha}\models\varphi\) by part~2.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p03-w03-p03-048-first-order}{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-first-order}{First-order sentences inside \(\mathcal L^{\ast}\)}
\begin{proof}
Induction on \(\chi\), for all lists of variables and constants at once.
For a term \(t\) of \(K\) with variables among \(\bar x\), let \(t(\bar d)\)
be the result of putting \(d_i\) for \(x_i\); an induction on \(t\) shows that
\(t(\bar d)\) takes in \((\mathfrak M,\bar a)\) the value that \(t\) takes in
\(\mathfrak M\) at \(x_i\mapsto a_i\).

\emph{Atomic \(\chi\).}  \(\theta=\chi(\bar d)\) is an atomic sentence of
\(K(\bar d)\); it lies in \(K(\bar d)^{\ast}\) with the usual truth
condition by the closure hypothesis, and by the remark on terms
\((\mathfrak M,\bar a)\models\theta\) iff \(\mathfrak M\models\chi[\bar a]\).

\emph{\(\neg\chi_1\), \(\chi_1\land\chi_2\).}  Apply the negation and
conjunction of \(K(\bar d)^{\ast}\) to the sentences given by the induction
hypothesis.  Disjunction, implication and \(\forall\) are treated as
abbreviations: \(\chi_1\lor\chi_2\) as \(\neg(\neg\chi_1\land\neg\chi_2)\) and
\(\forall y\,\chi_1\) as \(\neg\exists y\,\neg\chi_1\).

\emph{\(\chi=\exists y\,\chi_1\).}  If \(y\) is \(x_i\), let \(\theta_1\)
serve \(\chi_1\) with the same list and put \(\theta=q(d_i,\theta_1)\in
K(\bar d)^{\ast}\).  By the truth condition of \(q\),
\((\mathfrak M,\bar a)\models\theta\) iff some \(b\in M\) gives
\((\mathfrak M,\bar a[i:=b])\models\theta_1\), iff some \(b\) gives
\(\mathfrak M\models\chi_1[\bar a[i:=b]]\), iff
\(\mathfrak M\models\chi[\bar a]\); here \(\bar a[i:=b]\) is \(\bar a\) with
\(b\) in place \(i\).  If \(y\) is not in the list, take a new constant
symbol \(e\) with \(K(\bar d,e)\) in \(\mathcal C\), let
\(\theta_1\in K(\bar d,e)^{\ast}\) serve \(\chi_1\) with the list
\(\bar x,y\), and put \(\theta=q(e,\theta_1)\).  Since \(e\) does not occur
in \(\theta\), \(L_\theta\subseteq K(\bar d)\), and \(\theta\in
K(\bar d)^{\ast}\) by part~1 of \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-basic}.  By the
truth condition of \(q\), read as in clause~1 of
\cref{def:p03-w03-p03-048-standing}, \((\mathfrak M,\bar a)\models\theta\)
iff some \(b\) gives \((\mathfrak M,\bar a,b)\models\theta_1\), iff
\(\mathfrak M\models\chi[\bar a]\).

No case needs an element of \(M\) beyond those in \(\bar a\), so the claim
holds for the empty structure when \(n=0\).  For the last sentence of the
lemma: if \(K\) is the set of symbols of \(\chi\) together with \(\mathbf V\),
the construction takes place in \(K(\bar d)\) and in languages
\(K(\bar d,e,\ldots)\) whose new constants are removed by \(q\), so
\(L_\theta\subseteq K(\bar d)\).
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p03-w03-p03-048-iso-closed}{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-iso-closed}{Pull-back classes are closed under isomorphism}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathfrak N\models\psi\), let \(N_0=(\mathbf V^{\gamma})^{\mathfrak N}\),
and let \(G:\mathfrak N^{-\gamma}\cong\mathfrak A\).  Choose a set \(Z\)
disjoint from \(A\) and a bijection \(h:N\setminus N_0\to Z\), and let
\(H=G\cup h\), a bijection from \(N\) onto \(A\cup Z\).  Let \(\mathfrak N'\)
be the \(K\)-structure on \(A\cup Z\) obtained by transporting \(\mathfrak N\)
along \(H\).  Then \(H:\mathfrak N\cong\mathfrak N'\), so
\(\mathfrak N'\models\psi\) by the Isomorphism Axiom, and \(\mathfrak N'\) is
\(\gamma\)-invertible since \(\mathfrak N\) is.  The pull-back
\(\mathfrak N'^{-\gamma}\) has universe \(H[N_0]=G[N_0]=A\); since \(\gamma\)
has no terms, each symbol \(s\neq\mathbf V\) of \(L\) is interpreted in it by
the restriction to \(A\) of \((s^{\gamma})^{\mathfrak N'}\), which is the
\(H\)-image, hence the \(G\)-image, of the restriction of
\((s^{\gamma})^{\mathfrak N}\) to \(N_0\), that is, of \(s^{\mathfrak
N^{-\gamma}}\).  This is \(s^{\mathfrak A}\).  So \(\mathfrak A\) is a
pull-back of a model of \(\psi\).

For the consequence, let \(P\) be the class of pull-backs and \(A\) the class
of ordinals \(\alpha\) with \((\alpha,<)\cong\mathfrak B\) for some
\(\mathfrak B\in P\).  Each member of \(P\) is in \(\mathcal K_A\), and each
member of \(\mathcal K_A\) is isomorphic to a member of \(P\), so lies in
\(P\) by closure.  Hence \(P=\mathcal K_A\), and \(A\) is \(\Sigma^1_1\).
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p03-w03-p03-048-finite-omega}{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-finite-omega}{Finite sets, \((\omega,<)\) and an infinite accessible ordinal}
\begin{proof}
\emph{(2)\(\Rightarrow\)(3).}  The class \(\{\omega\}\) of ordinals is
\(\Sigma^1_1\) by (2), since \(\mathcal K_{\{\omega\}}\) is the class of
structures isomorphic to \((\omega,<)\).

\emph{(3)\(\Rightarrow\)(1).}  Let \(A\) be \(\Sigma^1_1\) with an infinite
member \(\alpha_0\), witnessed by \(\psi\in K^{\ast}\) and a one-one
\(0\)-term morphism \(\gamma:L_{<}\to K\); write \(W\) for
\(\mathbf V^{\gamma}\) and \(\prec\) for \(<^{\gamma}\).  So for every model
\(\mathfrak N\) of \(\psi\), \((W^{\mathfrak N},\prec^{\mathfrak N}\cap
(W^{\mathfrak N})^{2})\) is a well-ordering, and some model makes it of type
\(\alpha_0\).  Take a new unary \(U\) and let
\(\varphi=\psi\land\chi^{\bullet}\in K(U)^{\ast}\), where \(\chi\) is the
conjunction of
\begin{gather*}
\forall x\,(U(x)\to W(x)),\qquad
\forall x\,\forall y\,\bigl(U(x)\land W(y)\land y\prec x\to U(y)\bigr),\\
\forall x\,\Bigl(U(x)\land\exists y\,(W(y)\land y\prec x)\to\exists y\,
\bigl(W(y)\land y\prec x\land\forall z\,(W(z)\to\neg(y\prec z\land z\prec
x))\bigr)\Bigr),\\
\neg\exists x\,U(x)\ \lor\ \exists x\,\bigl(U(x)\land\forall y\,(U(y)\to
(y\prec x\lor y=x))\bigr).
\end{gather*}
The language \(\{\mathbf V\}\) is in \(\mathcal C\): it is the intersection of
a language with a copy whose only common symbol with it is \(\mathbf V\)
(clauses (iii) and (i)).  Let \(\varepsilon:\{\mathbf V\}\to K(U)\) send
\(\mathbf V\) to \(U\); it is a morphism by clause (iv), and every
\(K(U)\)-structure is \(\varepsilon\)-invertible because \(\{\mathbf V\}\)
has no function symbols.  The pull-back of a model \(\mathfrak N\) of
\(\varphi\) is the set \(U^{\mathfrak N}\).  It is finite: if it is nonempty,
\(U^{\mathfrak N}\) with the order of \(W^{\mathfrak N}\) is a well-ordering
with a largest element in which, by the third clause and downward closure,
every element other than the least has an immediate predecessor; its order
type is therefore a successor ordinal \(\delta+1\) all of whose nonzero
members are successors, and if \(\delta+1>\omega\) the member \(\omega\)
would be a nonzero limit; so \(\delta+1\) is finite.  Conversely, in a model
of \(\psi\) with \((W,\prec)\) of type \(\alpha_0\geq\omega\), interpreting
\(U\) as the set of the first \(n\) elements (\(n<\omega\), \(n=0\)
allowed) satisfies \(\chi\) and, by part~2 of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-basic}, \(\psi\).  So the pull-backs are finite
sets of every finite size, and by \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-iso-closed}
(whose argument applies verbatim to \(\varepsilon\)) they form the class of
all finite sets.

\emph{(1)\(\Rightarrow\)(2).}  Let \(\varphi\in K^{\ast}\) and
\(\varepsilon:\{\mathbf V\}\to K\) with \(\varepsilon(\mathbf V)=U\) witness
(1).  For each \(n<\omega\) there is a model \(\mathfrak N_n\) of
\(\varphi\) with \(U^{\mathfrak N_n}\) of size at least \(n\); by the
Isomorphism Axiom we may take \(U^{\mathfrak N_n}=\{0,\ldots,k_n-1\}\) with
\(k_n\geq n\).  By clause (v) of the definition of a category of languages
there are a constant symbol \(d\notin K\), a language \(K_1\) in
\(\mathcal C\) containing \(d\), and a one-one \(1\)-term interpretation
\(\delta:K\to K_1\) with term \(d\) that is a morphism.  For a symbol \(s\) of
\(K\) write \(s^{\delta}\); thus \(\mathbf V^{\delta}\) and \(U^{\delta}\) are
binary, an \(n\)-ary \(R\) goes to an \((n+1)\)-ary \(R^{\delta}\), and an
\(n\)-ary \(f\) (constants included) to an \((n+1)\)-ary \(f^{\delta}\).

\emph{Indexed union.}  Let \(\Omega=\omega\cup\bigcup_n N_n\), fix
\(o\in\Omega\), and let \(\mathfrak U\) be the \(K_1\)-structure with
universe \(\Omega\) in which \(\mathbf V^{\delta}(a,i)\) iff \(i<\omega\) and
\(a\in N_i\); \(R^{\delta}(\bar a,i)\) iff \(i<\omega\) and \(\bar a\in
R^{\mathfrak N_i}\); \(f^{\delta}(\bar a,i)=f^{\mathfrak N_i}(\bar a)\) if
\(i<\omega\) and \(\bar a\in N_i^{n}\), and \(=o\) otherwise; all other
symbols of \(K_1\), \(d\) included, are interpreted arbitrarily.  This is
well defined because \(\delta\) is one-one.  For \(i<\omega\), the structure
\((\mathfrak U,i)\), with \(d\) interpreted by \(i\), is
\(\delta\)-invertible: the pull-back has universe \(N_i\), which is closed
under \(\bar a\mapsto f^{\delta}(\bar a,i)\) and contains each
\(c^{\delta}(i)\); and the pull-back is \(\mathfrak N_i\).  By the
Translation Axiom, \((\mathfrak U,i)\models\varphi^{\delta}\).

\emph{The sentence.}  Take new symbols: \(W\) unary, \(\prec\) binary and a
constant \(e\).  Let \(\theta\in K_1(W,\prec)^{\ast}\) be the conjunction of
\(\chi_0^{\bullet}\), where \(\chi_0\) says that \(\prec\) is a linear
ordering of \(W\) (\(\prec\subseteq W^{2}\), irreflexive, transitive,
connected on \(W\)), that \(W\) is nonempty and that \(W\) has no largest
element, and of the sentence
\[
\forall x\,\Bigl(W(x)\to\exists y\,\bigl(\varphi^{\delta}(y)\land\forall z\,
(z\prec x\to\mathbf V^{\delta}(z,y)\land U^{\delta}(z,y))\bigr)\Bigr),
\]
formed as \(\neg q\bigl(e,W(e)\land\neg q(d,\varphi^{\delta}\land
\chi_1^{\bullet})\bigr)\), where \(\chi_1^{\bullet}\in K_1(W,\prec,e)^{\ast}\)
comes from \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-first-order} for the first-order
formula \(\forall z\,(z\prec x\to\mathbf V^{\delta}(z,y)\land
U^{\delta}(z,y))\) with \(e,d\) for \(x,y\).

\emph{\(\theta\) has a model of type \(\omega\).}  Expand \(\mathfrak U\) by
\(W=\omega\) and \(\prec\) the natural order of \(\omega\).  \(\chi_0\)
holds.  Given \(x<\omega\), take \(y=n\) with \(n>x\): then
\((\mathfrak U,n)\models\varphi^{\delta}\), and every \(z<x\) is in
\(\{0,\ldots,k_n-1\}=U^{\mathfrak N_n}\subseteq N_n\).

\emph{Every model is of type \(\omega\).}  Let \(\mathfrak B\models\theta\)
and \(x\in W^{\mathfrak B}\).  Some \(a\) has
\((\mathfrak B,a)\models\varphi^{\delta}\) with every \(\prec\)-predecessor
\(z\) of \(x\) satisfying \(\mathbf V^{\delta}(z,a)\land U^{\delta}(z,a)\).
By the Translation Axiom \((\mathfrak B,a)\) is \(\delta\)-invertible and
\(\mathfrak M=(\mathfrak B,a)^{-\delta}\models\varphi\); its universe is
\(\{z:\mathbf V^{\delta}(z,a)\}\) and \(U^{\mathfrak M}=\{z\in
M:U^{\delta}(z,a)\}\), which contains every predecessor of \(x\).  Since
\(U^{\mathfrak M}\) is finite, \(x\) has finitely many predecessors.  So
\((W^{\mathfrak B},\prec)\) is a nonempty linear order without a largest
element in which every element has finitely many predecessors.  The map
\(x\mapsto|\{z:z\prec x\}|\) is then strictly increasing (if \(x\prec x'\),
the predecessors of \(x\) together with \(x\) are predecessors of \(x'\)),
its range is closed downward (the predecessors of \(x\) are linearly ordered
and realize all smaller counts), and its range is unbounded since there is
no largest element; so it is an isomorphism onto \((\omega,<)\).

\emph{Conclusion.}  The morphism \(\gamma:L_{<}\to K_1(W,\prec)\) sending
\(\mathbf V\) to \(W\) and \(<\) to \(\prec\) is one-one and \(0\)-term, and
every structure is \(\gamma\)-invertible.  The pull-backs of models of
\(\theta\) are the structures \((W^{\mathfrak B},\prec^{\mathfrak B})\), all
isomorphic to \((\omega,<)\), and \((\omega,<)\) itself is one of them up to
isomorphism.  By \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-iso-closed} the class of
pull-backs is the class of structures isomorphic to \((\omega,<)\), which
is therefore \(\Sigma^1_1\).
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p03-w03-p03-048-sup-accessible}{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-sup-accessible}{Downward closure and suprema}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\psi\in K^{\ast}\) and a one-one \(0\)-term morphism
\(\gamma:L_{<}\to K\) witness that \(A\) is \(\Sigma^1_1\); write \(U\) for
\(\mathbf V^{\gamma}\) and \(\prec_0\) for \(<^{\gamma}\).

(1) Take new symbols \(U'\) unary and \(\prec'\) binary, and let
\(\psi'=\psi\land\chi^{\bullet}\) with \(\chi\) the conjunction of
\(\forall x\,(U'(x)\to U(x))\) and \(\forall x\,\forall y\,(x\prec'y\leftrightarrow
U'(x)\land U'(y)\land x\prec_0 y)\).  Let \(\gamma':L_{<}\to K(U',\prec')\)
send \(\mathbf V\) to \(U'\) and \(<\) to \(\prec'\).  The pull-back of a
model is a subset of the well-ordering \((U,\prec_0)\) with the induced
order.  A subset \(X\) of a well-ordering of type \(\beta\) has type at most
\(\beta\): if \(g\) is the isomorphism of \(X\) onto its type and \(\iota\)
the isomorphism of the whole order onto \(\beta\), an induction along \(X\)
gives \(g(x)\leq\iota(x)\) for all \(x\in X\), so the type of \(X\) is at
most \(\beta\).  Conversely, for \(\alpha\leq\beta\in A\), a model of
\(\psi\) of type \(\beta\) with \(U'\) its initial segment of type
\(\alpha\) and \(\prec'\) the induced order is a model of \(\psi'\) (part~2
of \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-basic}).  By
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-iso-closed}, \(A'\) is \(\Sigma^1_1\).

(2) If \(A=\emptyset\) then \(\beta=0\), and \(0\) is accessible: the class
\(\{0\}\) is \(\Sigma^1_1\) by the identity morphism of \(L_{<}\) and the
sentence \((\neg\exists x\,x=x)^{\bullet}\), whose only model is the empty
\(L_{<}\)-structure \((0,<)\).  Let \(A\neq\emptyset\).  By (1) we may
replace \(A\) by \(A'\), which is a set with the same supremum.  If
\(\beta\in A'\) we are done.  Otherwise \(A'\), being closed downward with
supremum \(\beta\notin A'\), is the set \(\beta\) of ordinals below
\(\beta\), and \(\beta>0\).  So assume \(A=\beta\).

For each \(x<\beta\), \(x\in A\), so by \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-iso-closed}
there is a model \(\mathfrak N_x\) of \(\psi\) whose pull-back is
\((x,\in)\) itself: \(U^{\mathfrak N_x}=x\) and \(\prec_0\) restricted to
\(x\) is the ordinal order.  Let \(\delta:K\to K_1\) be a one-one
\(1\)-term morphism with term \(d\) (clause (v)), and form the indexed
union \(\mathfrak U\) of the \(\mathfrak N_x\), \(x<\beta\), on
\(\Omega=\beta\cup\bigcup_x N_x\) as in the proof of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-finite-omega}; for each \(x<\beta\),
\((\mathfrak U,x)\) is \(\delta\)-invertible with pull-back
\(\mathfrak N_x\), so \((\mathfrak U,x)\models\psi^{\delta}\).

Take new symbols \(W\) unary, \(\prec\) binary and a constant \(e\).  Let
\(\theta\) be the conjunction of \(\chi_0^{\bullet}\), \(\chi_0\) saying
that \(\prec\) is a linear ordering of \(W\), and of
\[
\forall x\,\Bigl(W(x)\to\exists y\,\bigl(\psi^{\delta}(y)\land\forall z\,
\bigl(z\prec x\to\mathbf V^{\delta}(z,y)\land U^{\delta}(z,y)\land
\forall z'\,(z'\prec x\to(z\prec z'\leftrightarrow
\prec_0^{\delta}(z,z',y)))\bigr)\bigr)\Bigr),
\]
formed with \(q\), \(e\) and \(d\) as in the proof of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-finite-omega}.

\emph{A model of type \(\beta\).}  Expand \(\mathfrak U\) by \(W=\beta\) and
\(\prec\) the order of \(\beta\).  For \(x<\beta\) take \(y=x\): the
predecessors of \(x\) are the members of \(x=U^{\mathfrak N_x}\subseteq
N_x\), and on them \(\prec\) and \(\prec_0^{\mathfrak N_x}\) are both the
ordinal order.

\emph{Every model is well-ordered.}  Let \(\mathfrak B\models\theta\) and
\(x\in W^{\mathfrak B}\).  Some \(a\) has \((\mathfrak B,a)\models
\psi^{\delta}\); its pull-back \(\mathfrak M\models\psi\), so
\((U^{\mathfrak M},\prec_0^{\mathfrak M})\) is a well-ordering, and the
set \(P_x\) of \(\prec\)-predecessors of \(x\) is a subset of
\(U^{\mathfrak M}\) on which \(\prec\) agrees with \(\prec_0^{\mathfrak M}\).
So each \((P_x,\prec)\) is well-ordered.  If \(X\subseteq W^{\mathfrak B}\)
is nonempty, pick \(x\in X\); either \(x\) is least in \(X\), or
\(X\cap P_x\neq\emptyset\) has a least element, which is least in \(X\),
because every element of \(X\) below it is in \(P_x\).  So
\((W^{\mathfrak B},\prec)\) is a well-ordering.

With \(\gamma'':L_{<}\to K_1(W,\prec)\) sending \(\mathbf V\) to \(W\) and
\(<\) to \(\prec\), \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-iso-closed} shows that the
class of order types of models of \(\theta\) is \(\Sigma^1_1\); it contains
\(\beta\).  So \(\beta\) is accessible.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p03-w03-p03-048-levels}{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-levels}{Finite-level types}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Closure.}  \(J_{m+1}\subseteq J_m\) by definition.  \(J_0\) is closed
under subsets, since the restriction of a partial isomorphism to a subset of
its domain is one.  If \(J_m\) is closed under subsets, \(p\subseteq q\in
J_{m+1}\) gives \(p\in J_m\), and the extensions of \(q\) required by the
definition of \(J_{m+1}\) also extend \(p\); so \(p\in J_{m+1}\).  The
identity map of a finite subset of \(M\) is in every \(J_m(\mathfrak M,
\mathfrak M)\), by induction on \(m\).

\emph{The formulas.}  Let \(\Lambda_n\) be the finite set of formulas
\(x_i=x_j\), \(R(x_{i_1},\ldots,x_{i_k})\) for \(k\)-ary \(R\in L\), and
\(f(x_{i_1},\ldots,x_{i_k})=x_j\) for \(k\)-ary \(f\in L\), \(k\geq0\), with
indices in \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\).  Let \(\tau^{0}_{\mathfrak M,\bar a}\) be the
conjunction of the members of \(\Lambda_n\) true in \(\mathfrak M\) at
\(\bar a\) and the negations of the others (\(\top\) if \(\Lambda_n\) is
empty), and
\[
\tau^{m+1}_{\mathfrak M,\bar a}=\tau^{0}_{\mathfrak M,\bar a}\land
\bigwedge_{\lambda\in X}\exists x_{n+1}\,\lambda\land\forall x_{n+1}\,
\bigvee_{\lambda\in X}\lambda,\qquad
X=\{\tau^{m}_{\mathfrak M,\bar aa}:a\in M\},
\]
with \(\bigwedge\emptyset=\top\) and \(\bigvee\emptyset=\bot\).  By induction
on \(m\), \(\Phi_{m,n}=\{\tau^{m}_{\mathfrak M,\bar a}\}\) is finite: for
\(m=0\) there are at most \(2^{|\Lambda_n|}\) formulas, and
\(\tau^{m+1}_{\mathfrak M,\bar a}\) is fixed by a member of \(\Phi_{0,n}\) and a
subset \(X\) of the finite set \(\Phi_{m,n+1}\); so \(X\) is finite and the
displayed formula is first-order.

\emph{Characterization, \(m=0\).}  \(\mathfrak N\models\tau^0_{\mathfrak M,
\bar a}[\bar b]\) iff \(\bar a\) and \(\bar b\) satisfy the same members of
\(\Lambda_n\).  The equalities say exactly that \(p=\{(a_i,b_i)\}\) is a
well-defined one-one map; the relation formulas and the function formulas
say exactly that \(p\) preserves each relation and each graph on its domain
\(\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}\).  So the condition is \(p\in J_0\).

\emph{Step.}  Suppose the claim for \(m\) and all lengths.  If \(p\in
J_{m+1}\), then \(\tau^0\) holds at \(\bar b\).  For \(a\in M\) there is
\(q\in J_m\) with \(p\subseteq q\) and \(a\in\operatorname{dom}q\); by
closure \(p\cup\{(a,q(a))\}\in J_m\), and by the induction hypothesis
\(\mathfrak N\models\tau^{m}_{\mathfrak M,\bar aa}[\bar b\,q(a)]\); this gives
the conjunction.  For \(b\in N\) the back clause gives similarly some \(a\)
with \(\mathfrak N\models\tau^{m}_{\mathfrak M,\bar aa}[\bar bb]\); this gives
the disjunction.  Conversely, if \(\mathfrak N\models\tau^{m+1}_{\mathfrak M,
\bar a}[\bar b]\), then \(p\in J_0\); for \(a\in M\) the conjunct for
\(\tau^m_{\mathfrak M,\bar aa}\) gives \(b\) with \(p\cup\{(a,b)\}\in J_m\) by
the induction hypothesis; and for \(b\in N\) the disjunction gives \(a\) with
the same.  So \(p\in J_{m+1}\).  Taking \(\mathfrak N=\mathfrak M\),
\(\bar b=\bar a\) and the identity map shows
\(\mathfrak M\models\tau^m_{\mathfrak M,\bar a}[\bar a]\).

\emph{Consequence.}  Write \(\tau^m_{\mathfrak M}\) for \(n=0\).  Let
\(\rho=\bigvee\{\tau^m_{\mathfrak M}:\mathfrak M\models\varphi\}\), a finite
disjunction of sentences of \(L\) (\(\bot\) if \(\varphi\) has no model).
If \(\mathfrak M\models\varphi\), then \(\mathfrak M\models\tau^m_{\mathfrak
M}\), so \(\mathfrak M\models\rho\).  If \(\mathfrak N\models\rho\), some
\(\mathfrak M\models\varphi\) has \(\mathfrak N\models\tau^m_{\mathfrak M}\),
that is, \(\emptyset\in J_m(\mathfrak M,\mathfrak N)\); by hypothesis
\(\mathfrak N\models\varphi\).
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p03-w03-p03-048-fo-karp}{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-fo-karp}{First-order and infinitary logic have the Karp property}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Terms.}  Claim: for each term \(t(\bar x)\), each \(F\in I\) and
\(\bar a\in(\operatorname{dom}F)^{n}\) there is \(G\in I\) with
\(F\subseteq G\), \(t^{\mathfrak M}[\bar a]\in\operatorname{dom}G\) and
\(G(t^{\mathfrak M}[\bar a])=t^{\mathfrak N}[F\bar a]\).  For a variable take
\(G=F\).  Let \(t=f(t_1,\ldots,t_k)\), \(k\geq0\).  Apply the claim to
\(t_1\) and \(F\), then to \(t_2\) and the map obtained, and so on; since
each step extends the previous map, the final \(G_k\supseteq F\) contains
the values \(c_j=t_j^{\mathfrak M}[\bar a]\) in its domain with
\(G_k(c_j)=t_j^{\mathfrak N}[F\bar a]\).  By the forth property take
\(G\supseteq G_k\) in \(I\) with \(f^{\mathfrak M}(\bar c)\in
\operatorname{dom}G\).  As \(G\) preserves the graph of \(f\) on its domain,
\(f^{\mathfrak N}(G\bar c)=G(f^{\mathfrak M}(\bar c))\), which is the claim.

\emph{Formulas.}  Induction on the formation of \(\chi\) in
\(\mathcal L_{\infty\omega}\), which is well founded, for all \(F\in I\) and
\(\bar a\).
For \(R(t_1,\ldots,t_k)\) and \(t_1=t_2\), extend \(F\) as above to \(G\)
containing the values of all \(t_j\); \(G\) preserves \(R\) and is one-one,
and \(G\bar a=F\bar a\).  The connectives are immediate; for
\(\bigwedge\Phi\) and \(\bigvee\Phi\) the induction hypothesis holds for
every member of \(\Phi\) at the same \(F\) and \(\bar a\), so the conjunction
(disjunction) holds at \(\bar a\) in \(\mathfrak M\) iff it holds at
\(F\bar a\) in \(\mathfrak N\).  If
\(\mathfrak M\models\exists y\,\chi_1[\bar a]\) with witness \(a\), take
\(G\supseteq F\) in \(I\) with \(a\in\operatorname{dom}G\); by the induction
hypothesis \(\mathfrak N\models\chi_1[G\bar a,G(a)]\), and \(G\bar a=F\bar
a\).  The converse uses the back property.  For sentences, \(I\neq\emptyset\)
provides some \(F\).
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p03-w03-p03-048-countable}{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-countable}{Countable back and forth}
\begin{proof}
Enumerate \(M=\{a_k:k<\mu\}\) and \(N=\{b_k:k<\nu\}\) with
\(\mu,\nu\leq\omega\).  Build \(F_0\subseteq F_1\subseteq\cdots\) in \(I\):
at stage \(2k+1\), if \(k<\mu\), use the forth property to put \(a_k\) into
the domain; at stage \(2k+2\), if \(k<\nu\), use the back property to put
\(b_k\) into the range; otherwise \(F_{i+1}=F_i\).  Let
\(F=\bigcup_iF_i\).  As a union of a chain of one-one maps, \(F\) is a
one-one map; its domain is \(M\) and its range is \(N\).  For \(\bar a\in
M^{n}\) and \(b\in M\), all lie in the domain of one \(F_i\), which preserves
each relation and each graph of a function on its domain; so
\(R^{\mathfrak M}(\bar a)\) iff \(R^{\mathfrak N}(F\bar a)\), and
\(f^{\mathfrak M}(\bar a)=b\) iff \(f^{\mathfrak N}(F\bar a)=F(b)\).  Hence
\(F\) is an isomorphism.  (If \(M=\emptyset\), every member of \(I\) is
empty, and the back property forces \(N=\emptyset\).)
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p03-w03-p03-048-lowenheim-karp}{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-lowenheim-karp}{The L\"owenheim property gives the Karp property}
\begin{proof}
Suppose \(I:\mathfrak M_0\cong_{p}\mathfrak M_1\) for \(L\)-structures,
\(\varphi\in L^{\ast}\), \(\mathfrak M_0\models\varphi\) and
\(\mathfrak M_1\not\models\varphi\).  Put \(L_0=L_\varphi\), finite by
clause~2 of \cref{def:p03-w03-p03-048-standing}.  By part~2 of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-basic} the reducts to \(L_0\) still disagree on
\(\varphi\), and \(I\) is still a back and forth system between the reducts;
so we may assume \(L=L_0\).  By the Isomorphism Axiom we may assume
\(M_0\cap M_1=\emptyset\) (replace \(\mathfrak M_1\) by an isomorphic copy
and compose the members of \(I\) with the isomorphism).  Let
\(\neg\varphi\) be formed with the negation of \(L_0^{\ast}\), so
\(\mathfrak M_1\models\neg\varphi\).

\emph{Language and translations.}  For each symbol \(s\neq\mathbf V\) of
\(L_0\) take a new symbol \(s''\) of the same kind and arity, and take new
symbols \(U,W,S\) unary and \(E\) ternary; let \(K\) be \(L_0\) with all of
these.  Let \(\alpha:L_0\to K\) send \(\mathbf V\) to \(U\) and fix the other
symbols, and \(\beta:L_0\to K\) send \(\mathbf V\) to \(W\) and each \(s\) to
\(s''\).  Both are \(0\)-term morphisms.  For a \(K\)-structure
\(\mathfrak N\), \(\mathfrak N^{-\alpha}\) is the partial structure on
\(U^{\mathfrak N}\) with the restrictions of the symbols of \(L_0\), and
\(\mathfrak N^{-\beta}\) the partial structure on \(W^{\mathfrak N}\) with
the restrictions of the doubled symbols, read as symbols of \(L_0\).

\emph{The sentence.}  Let \(\psi\in K^{\ast}\) be the conjunction of
\(\varphi^{\alpha}\), \((\neg\varphi)^{\beta}\) and \(\chi^{\bullet}\), where
\(\chi\) is the conjunction of the following first-order sentences:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(P1)] \(\exists s\,S(s)\);
\item[(P2)] \(\forall s\,\forall x\,\forall y\,(E(s,x,y)\to S(s)\land U(x)\land
      W(y))\);
\item[(P3)] \(\forall s\,\forall x\,\forall y\,\forall x'\,\forall y'\,
      (E(s,x,y)\land E(s,x',y')\to(x=x'\leftrightarrow y=y'))\);
\item[(P4)] for each \(k\)-ary relation symbol \(R\neq\mathbf V\) of \(L_0\):
      \(\forall s\,\forall\bar x\,\forall\bar y\,(\bigwedge_{i\leq k}E(s,x_i,y_i)
      \to(R(\bar x)\leftrightarrow R''(\bar y)))\);
\item[(P5)] for each \(k\)-ary function symbol \(f\) of \(L_0\), \(k\geq0\):
      \(\forall s\,\forall x_1\ldots x_{k+1}\,\forall y_1\ldots y_{k+1}\,
      (\bigwedge_{i\leq k+1}E(s,x_i,y_i)\to(f(x_1,\ldots,x_k)=x_{k+1}
      \leftrightarrow f''(y_1,\ldots,y_k)=y_{k+1}))\);
\item[(P6)] \(\forall s\,\forall u\,\bigl(S(s)\land U(u)\to\exists t\,(S(t)\land
      \forall x\,\forall y\,(E(s,x,y)\to E(t,x,y))\land\exists w\,E(t,u,w))\bigr)\);
\item[(P7)] \(\forall s\,\forall w\,\bigl(S(s)\land W(w)\to\exists t\,(S(t)\land
      \forall x\,\forall y\,(E(s,x,y)\to E(t,x,y))\land\exists u\,E(t,u,w))\bigr)\).
\end{enumerate}
Since \(L_0\) is finite, \(\chi\) is a single first-order sentence.

\emph{\(\psi\) has a model.}  Let \(\{\iota_F:F\in I\}\) be distinct objects
outside \(M_0\cup M_1\), and let \(\mathfrak N\) have universe
\(N=M_0\cup M_1\cup\{\iota_F:F\in I\}\), nonempty since \(I\neq\emptyset\);
\(U=M_0\), \(W=M_1\), \(S=\{\iota_F\}\), \(E=\{(\iota_F,x,F(x)):F\in I,\
x\in\operatorname{dom}F\}\); \(R^{\mathfrak N}=R^{\mathfrak M_0}\),
\(R''^{\mathfrak N}=R^{\mathfrak M_1}\); \(f^{\mathfrak N}\) agrees with
\(f^{\mathfrak M_0}\) on \(M_0^{k}\), \(f''^{\mathfrak N}\) with
\(f^{\mathfrak M_1}\) on \(M_1^{k}\), and both take a fixed value elsewhere
(constants: \(c^{\mathfrak N}=c^{\mathfrak M_0}\),
\(c''^{\mathfrak N}=c^{\mathfrak M_1}\), which exist because an
\(L_0\)-structure interprets its constants).  Then \(U\) is closed under the
functions of \(L_0\) and contains its constants, so \(\mathfrak N\) is
\(\alpha\)-invertible with \(\mathfrak N^{-\alpha}=\mathfrak M_0\);
likewise \(\mathfrak N^{-\beta}=\mathfrak M_1\).  By the Translation Axiom
\(\mathfrak N\models\varphi^{\alpha}\) and
\(\mathfrak N\models(\neg\varphi)^{\beta}\).  (P1) holds as
\(I\neq\emptyset\); (P2) and (P3) hold because each \(F\) is a one-one map
from part of \(M_0\) into \(M_1\); (P4) and (P5) because each \(F\) is a
partial isomorphism; (P6) and (P7) by the back and forth property.

\emph{A countable model gives a contradiction.}  By the L\"owenheim property
\(\psi\) has a model \(\mathfrak N^{\ast}\) of power at most \(\aleph_0\).  By
the Translation Axiom it is \(\alpha\)- and \(\beta\)-invertible,
\(\mathfrak A_0=\mathfrak N^{\ast-\alpha}\models\varphi\) and
\(\mathfrak A_1=\mathfrak N^{\ast-\beta}\models\neg\varphi\), so
\(\mathfrak A_1\not\models\varphi\).  For \(s\in S^{\mathfrak N^{\ast}}\) let
\(F_s=\{(x,y):E(s,x,y)\}\).  By (P2) and (P3), \(F_s\) is a one-one map from
a subset of \(U^{\mathfrak N^{\ast}}\) into \(W^{\mathfrak N^{\ast}}\); by
(P4) and (P5) it preserves each relation and each graph of a function on its
domain, the relation and function of \(\mathfrak A_1\) being those of the
doubled symbols; so it is a partial isomorphism from \(\mathfrak A_0\) to
\(\mathfrak A_1\).  By (P1), \(I^{\ast}=\{F_s\}\) is nonempty, and by (P6)
and (P7) it has the back and forth property.  \(\mathfrak A_0\) and
\(\mathfrak A_1\) are countable, so by
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-countable} they are isomorphic, and the
Isomorphism Axiom contradicts \(\mathfrak A_0\models\varphi\),
\(\mathfrak A_1\not\models\varphi\).
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p03-w03-p03-048-karp-omega}{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-karp-omega}{Karp property and boundedness by \(\omega\)}
\begin{proof}
\emph{(\(\Rightarrow\)).}  Assume \(\mathcal L_{\omega\omega}\geq
\mathcal L^{\ast}\).  \emph{Karp property}: let
\(I:\mathfrak M\cong_{p}\mathfrak N\) and \(\varphi\in L^{\ast}\); take a
first-order \(\rho\) in the symbols of \(L_\varphi\) with the same
\(L_\varphi\)-models.  \(I\) is a back and forth system between the reducts
to \(L_\varphi\), so by \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-fo-karp} the reducts agree
on \(\rho\), hence on \(\varphi\), and by part~2 of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-basic} so do \(\mathfrak M\) and \(\mathfrak N\).
\emph{Boundedness}: let \(A\) be \(\Sigma^1_1\), witnessed by \(\psi\in
K^{\ast}\) and \(\gamma:L_{<}\to K\), with \(U=\mathbf V^{\gamma}\),
\(\prec=<^{\gamma}\); take a first-order \(\rho\) in the symbols of
\(L_\psi\) with the same \(L_\psi\)-models as \(\psi\).  Suppose some
\(\alpha\in A\) has \(\alpha\geq\omega\).  Let \(c_0,c_1,\ldots\) be new
constants (an ordinary first-order language; it need not be in
\(\mathcal C\)), and \(T=\{\rho\}\cup\{U(c_i),\ U(c_{i+1})\land
c_{i+1}\prec c_i:i<\omega\}\), where \(U(x)\) is read as \(x=x\) if
\(U=\mathbf V\).  A model of \(\psi\) with pull-back of type \(\alpha\) is
nonempty and satisfies any finite part of \(T\) when finitely many \(c_i\)
name a descending chain in its first \(\omega\) elements.  By
\cref{thm:fol-compactness}, \(T\) has a model \(\mathfrak B\).  Its reduct to
\(L_\psi\) satisfies \(\rho\), hence \(\psi\), so the pull-back of
\(\mathfrak B\restriction K\) along \(\gamma\) is isomorphic to an ordinal;
but \(c_0^{\mathfrak B},c_1^{\mathfrak B},\ldots\) descend in it.  So every
member of \(A\) is \(<\omega\).

\emph{(\(\Leftarrow\)).}  Assume the Karp property and boundedness by
\(\omega\), and suppose \(\varphi\in L^{\ast}\) has no first-order
equivalent in the sense of clause~4 of \cref{def:p03-w03-p03-048-standing}.
Put \(L_0=L_\varphi\), finite.  By \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-levels}, for
each \(m\geq1\) there are \(L_0\)-structures \(\mathfrak M_m\models\varphi\)
and \(\mathfrak N_m\not\models\varphi\) with \(\emptyset\in
J_m(\mathfrak M_m,\mathfrak N_m)\).

\emph{Language.}  Take new symbols: \(s''\) for each \(s\neq\mathbf V\) of
\(L_0\); \(U,W_0,W_1,S\) unary; \(<_U,H\) binary; \(E\) ternary; let \(K\)
be \(L_0\) with these.  \(\alpha:L_0\to K\) sends \(\mathbf V\) to \(W_0\)
and fixes the rest; \(\beta\) sends \(\mathbf V\) to \(W_1\) and \(s\) to
\(s''\).

\emph{The sentence.}  \(\psi\in K^{\ast}\) is the conjunction of
\(\varphi^{\alpha}\), \((\neg\varphi)^{\beta}\) and \(\chi^{\bullet}\), with
\(\chi\) the conjunction of: (Q1) \(<_U\) is a linear ordering of \(U\);
(Q2) \(E(s,x,y)\to S(s)\land W_0(x)\land W_1(y)\) and \(H(s,b)\to
S(s)\land U(b)\), universally closed; (Q3)--(Q5) the clauses (P3)--(P5) of
the proof of \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-lowenheim-karp}; and
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(Q6)] \(\forall b\,\bigl(U(b)\to\exists s\,(H(s,b)\land\forall x\,
      \forall y\,\neg E(s,x,y))\bigr)\);
\item[(Q7)] \(\forall s\,\forall b\,\forall b'\,\forall u\,\bigl(H(s,b)\land
      b'<_Ub\land W_0(u)\to\exists t\,(H(t,b')\land\forall x\,\forall y\,
      (E(s,x,y)\to E(t,x,y))\land\exists w\,E(t,u,w))\bigr)\);
\item[(Q8)] the same with \(W_1(w)\) given and \(\exists u\,E(t,u,w)\)
      required.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{(i) In every model \((U,<_U)\) is well-ordered.}  Let
\(\mathfrak N\models\psi\) and suppose \(X\subseteq U^{\mathfrak N}\) is
nonempty without a least element.  Let \(I=\{F_s:H(s,b)\text{ for some
}b\in X\}\), \(F_s=\{(x,y):E(s,x,y)\}\).  As in the proof of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-lowenheim-karp}, each \(F_s\) is a partial
isomorphism from \(\mathfrak N^{-\alpha}\) to \(\mathfrak N^{-\beta}\), the
pull-backs being \(L_0\)-structures by the Translation Axiom.  \(I\) is
nonempty by (Q6).  If \(H(s,b)\), \(b\in X\), pick \(b'\in X\) with
\(b'<_Ub\); by (Q7) and (Q8) there are extensions \(F_t\supseteq F_s\) with
\(H(t,b')\), so \(F_t\in I\), containing any prescribed element of
\(W_0\) in the domain, respectively of \(W_1\) in the range.  So
\(I:\mathfrak N^{-\alpha}\cong_p\mathfrak N^{-\beta}\), and by the Karp
property the two agree on \(\varphi\); but \(\mathfrak N^{-\alpha}\models
\varphi\) and \(\mathfrak N^{-\beta}\models\neg\varphi\).

\emph{(ii) For each \(m\geq1\) there is a model with \((U,<_U)\) of type
\(m\).}  Take \(\mathfrak M=\mathfrak M_m\) and \(\mathfrak N'\) an
isomorphic copy of \(\mathfrak N_m\) disjoint from it (with \(J_m\)
transported).  The universe consists of \(M\), \(N'\), \(m\) further objects
\(u_0,\ldots,u_{m-1}\) and one object \(\pi_{b,p}\) for each \(b<m\) and
\(p\in J_b(\mathfrak M,\mathfrak N')\), all distinct.  Put
\(U=\{u_b\}\) with \(u_b<_Uu_{b'}\) iff \(b<b'\); \(W_0=M\), \(W_1=N'\);
\(S=\{\pi_{b,p}\}\); \(H(\pi_{b,p},u_b)\); \(E(\pi_{b,p},x,p(x))\) for
\(x\in\operatorname{dom}p\); the symbols of \(L_0\) and their doubles as in
the proof of \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-lowenheim-karp}.  (Q6): \(\emptyset\in
J_m\subseteq J_b\).  (Q7): if \(p\in J_b\) and \(b'<b\), then \(p\in
J_{b'+1}\), so for \(u\in M\) some \(q\in J_{b'}\) extends \(p\) with \(u\in
\operatorname{dom}q\), and \(\pi_{b',q}\) is the required position; (Q8)
likewise.  (Q3)--(Q5) hold since every \(p\in J_0\) is a partial
isomorphism, and \(\varphi^{\alpha}\), \((\neg\varphi)^{\beta}\) hold by
the Translation Axiom as before.

\emph{Conclusion.}  Choose \(<\) so that \(L_{<}\) is in \(\mathcal C\), and
let \(\gamma:L_{<}\to K\) send \(\mathbf V\) to \(U\) and \(<\) to \(<_U\).
Every \(K\)-structure is \(\gamma\)-invertible.  By (i) and
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-iso-closed}, the class \(A\) of the order types of
\((U,<_U)\) in models of \(\psi\) is \(\Sigma^1_1\); by (ii) it contains
every \(m\) with \(1\leq m<\omega\); by boundedness \(A\subseteq\omega\).  So
\(A\) is a set with \(\sup A=\omega\), and by part~2 of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-sup-accessible} \(\omega\) is accessible: it
belongs to some \(\Sigma^1_1\) class, against boundedness by \(\omega\).
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p03-w03-p03-048-lindstrom}{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-lindstrom}{Lindstr\"om's theorem in Barwise's form}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-lowenheim-karp}, \(\mathcal L^{\ast}\) has the
Karp property.  Suppose \(\mathcal L_{\omega\omega}\not\geq\mathcal L^{\ast}\).
By \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-karp-omega}, \(\mathcal L^{\ast}\) is not
bounded by \(\omega\): some \(\Sigma^1_1\) class of ordinals has a member
\(\geq\omega\).  By \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-finite-omega},
\((\omega,<)\) is \(\Sigma^1_1\): there are \(\psi\in K^{\ast}\) and a
one-one \(0\)-term morphism \(\gamma:L_{<}\to K\), with \(W=\mathbf
V^{\gamma}\) and \(\prec=<^{\gamma}\), such that the pull-backs of the models
of \(\psi\) are exactly the structures isomorphic to \((\omega,<)\).

\emph{Against (b).}  Take a new constant \(c\).  For \(n\geq1\) let
\(\theta_n\) be the first-order sentence ``there are \(n\) distinct \(x\)
with \(W(x)\land x\prec c\)'', and let
\(T=\{\psi\land(W(c))^{\bullet}\}\cup\{\theta_n^{\bullet}:n\geq1\}\), a
countable subset of \(K(c)^{\ast}\).  A finite part of \(T\) holds in a model
of \(\psi\) with \(c\) interpreted by an element of \(W\) with enough
predecessors (part~2 of \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-basic}).  If \(T\) had a
model, \(c\) would be an element of \(W\) with infinitely many
\(\prec\)-predecessors in \(W\), while \((W,\prec)\) restricted to \(W\) is
isomorphic to \((\omega,<)\).

\emph{Against (a).}  A model of \(\psi\) exists, so by the L\"owenheim
property there is one, \(\mathfrak N\), of power at most \(\aleph_0\); its
\(W\) is countably infinite.  Take a new unary function symbol \(f\) and
\(\theta=\psi\land\xi^{\bullet}\in K(f)^{\ast}\), with \(\xi\) the sentence
\(\forall x\,\forall y\,(f(x)=f(y)\to x=y)\land\forall x\,W(f(x))\).
Interpreting \(f\) as an injection of \(N\) into \(W^{\mathfrak N}\) gives an
infinite model of \(\theta\).  Every model of \(\theta\) injects its universe
into its \(W\), which is countable; so \(\theta\) has no uncountable model.

Either way the hypothesis fails, so \(\mathcal L_{\omega\omega}\geq
\mathcal L^{\ast}\).  The converse statement is
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-first-order} with \(n=0\): \(\chi^{\bullet}\) has
the same \(L\)-structures as models as \(\chi\), and, when
\(L_\chi\cup\{\mathbf V\}\) is a language of \(\mathcal C\), its symbols lie
in that language, which is the requirement of clause~4 of
\cref{def:p03-w03-p03-048-standing} for \(\mathcal L^{\ast}\geq
\mathcal L_{\omega\omega}\).  The empty structure is covered throughout,
since \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-levels} and
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-first-order} allow it.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p03-w03-p03-048-levels-transfinite}{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-levels-transfinite}{Transfinite types and their count}
\begin{proof}
\emph{(1)}  Closure under subsets, by induction on \(\beta\): for
\(\beta=0\) and for successors the argument is the one in the proof of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-levels}; at a limit \(\lambda\), an intersection
of sets closed under subsets is closed under subsets.  Monotonicity, by
induction on \(\gamma\geq\beta\): \(J_\beta\subseteq J_\beta\); if
\(\beta\leq\delta\) then \(J_{\delta+1}\subseteq J_\delta\subseteq J_\beta\);
if \(\gamma>\beta\) is a limit then \(J_\gamma\subseteq J_\beta\) by
definition.  By induction on \(\beta\), the identity map of a finite subset
of \(M\) lies in \(J_\beta(\mathfrak M,\mathfrak M)\): it is a partial
isomorphism; at a successor the identity map on the enlarged finite set is
the required extension; at a limit use the induction hypothesis for every
smaller level.

\emph{(2)}  Define \(\tau^{\beta}_{\mathfrak M,\bar a}\) by recursion on
\(\beta\).  \(\tau^{0}\) and \(\tau^{\beta+1}\) are given by the formulas of
the proof of \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-levels}, where now
\(X=\{\tau^{\beta}_{\mathfrak M,\bar aa}:a\in M\}\) may be infinite; we read
\(\tau^{\beta+1}_{\mathfrak M,\bar a}\) as the conjunction of the set
consisting of \(\tau^0_{\mathfrak M,\bar a}\), the formulas
\(\exists x_{n+1}\,\lambda\) for \(\lambda\in X\), and \(\forall x_{n+1}\,
\bigvee X\).  For a limit \(\lambda\), \(\tau^{\lambda}_{\mathfrak M,\bar a}=
\bigwedge\{\tau^{\beta}_{\mathfrak M,\bar a}:\beta<\lambda\}\).  Let
\(\Phi_{\beta,n}\) be the class of all \(\tau^{\beta}_{\mathfrak M,\bar a}\)
with \(\bar a\) of length \(n\).  It is a set, by induction: \(\Phi_{0,n}\) is
finite; every member of \(\Phi_{\beta+1,n}\) is determined by a member of
\(\Phi_{0,n}\) and a subset of the set \(\Phi_{\beta,n+1}\); every member of
\(\Phi_{\lambda,n}\) is the conjunction of the range of a function in
\(\prod_{\beta<\lambda}\Phi_{\beta,n}\).  The characterization is proved by
induction on \(\beta\).  For \(0\) and for successors the argument of the
proof of \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-levels} applies word for word: it uses
closure under subsets and the induction hypothesis at the previous level,
and never the finiteness of \(X\).  At a limit \(\lambda\),
\(\mathfrak N\models\tau^{\lambda}_{\mathfrak M,\bar a}[\bar b]\) iff for
every \(\beta<\lambda\) the map \(p=\{(a_i,b_i)\}\) belongs to \(J_\beta\),
iff \(p\in J_\lambda\).  For finite \(\beta\) the recursion is that of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-levels}.  Taking \(\mathfrak N=\mathfrak M\) and the
identity map gives \(\mathfrak M\models\tau^{\beta}_{\mathfrak M,\bar a}[\bar
a]\).

\emph{(3)}  Write \(c_\beta=\beth_{\beta+1}(\aleph_0)\).  Facts about the
\(\beth\) function: \(\beth_\alpha(\lambda)<\beth_{\alpha+1}(\lambda)\) by
Cantor's theorem, so \(\alpha\mapsto\beth_\alpha(\lambda)\) is strictly
increasing (at limits it is a supremum); \(\alpha\leq\beth_\alpha(\aleph_0)\)
as ordinals, by induction (a strict inequality \(\beth_\alpha>\alpha\) at a
successor step gives \(\beth_{\alpha+1}\geq\alpha+1\), and suprema preserve
the inequality); and \(\beth_\alpha(\beth_\gamma)=\beth_{\gamma+\alpha}\), by
induction on \(\alpha\), the limit case because \(\{\gamma+\alpha':\alpha'<
\alpha\}\) is cofinal in the limit \(\gamma+\alpha\).  Now induct on
\(\beta\), for all \(n\) at once.  \(\Phi_{0,n}\) is finite and each
\(\tau^0\) has finitely many subformulas.  At a successor,
\(|\Phi_{\beta+1,n}|\leq|\Phi_{0,n}|\cdot2^{|\Phi_{\beta,n+1}|}\leq\aleph_0
\cdot2^{c_\beta}=c_{\beta+1}\); the subformulas of
\(\tau^{\beta+1}_{\mathfrak M,\bar a}\) are the formula itself,
\(\forall x_{n+1}\bigvee X\), \(\bigvee X\), the subformulas of
\(\tau^0_{\mathfrak M,\bar a}\), and, for each \(\lambda\in X\),
\(\exists x_{n+1}\lambda\) and the subformulas of \(\lambda\), so there are at
most \(3+\aleph_0+|X|\cdot(1+c_\beta)\leq c_\beta\leq c_{\beta+1}\) of them,
as \(|X|\leq|\Phi_{\beta,n+1}|\leq c_\beta\).  At a limit \(\lambda\), let
\(d=\sup_{\beta<\lambda}c_\beta=\beth_\lambda(\aleph_0)\); then
\(|\lambda|\leq\lambda\leq d\), \(|\Phi_{\lambda,n}|\leq\prod_{\beta<\lambda}
c_\beta\leq d^{|\lambda|}\leq d^{d}=2^{d}=c_\lambda\), and
\(|\tau^{\lambda}_{\mathfrak M,\bar a}|\leq1+|\lambda|\cdot d=d\leq
c_\lambda\).

If \(\kappa=\beth_\kappa>\omega\) and \(\beta<\kappa\), then, since
\(\aleph_0=\beth_\omega\), \(c_\beta=\beth_{\beta+1}(\beth_\omega)=
\beth_{\omega+\beta+1}\).  As \(\kappa\) is an uncountable cardinal and
\(|\omega+\beta+1|=|\beta|+\aleph_0<\kappa\), we have
\(\omega+\beta+1<\kappa\), so \(c_\beta<\beth_\kappa=\kappa\).

\emph{(4)}  Let \(\Phi'=\{\tau^{\beta}_{\mathfrak M}:\mathfrak M\models
\varphi\}\subseteq\Phi_{\beta,0}\), a set, and \(\rho=\bigvee\Phi'\)
(\(\rho\) is false everywhere if \(\Phi'=\emptyset\)).  If
\(\mathfrak M\models\varphi\), then \(\mathfrak M\models\tau^{\beta}_{\mathfrak
M}\) by (2), so \(\mathfrak M\models\rho\).  If \(\mathfrak N\models\rho\),
then \(\mathfrak N\models\tau^{\beta}_{\mathfrak M}\) for some \(\mathfrak M
\models\varphi\), so \(\emptyset\in J_\beta(\mathfrak M,\mathfrak N)\) by
(2), and \(\mathfrak N\models\varphi\) by hypothesis.  The symbols of
\(\rho\) are those of \(L\).  Under the hypothesis on \(\kappa\) and
\(\beta\), \(|\rho|\leq1+|\Phi_{\beta,0}|\cdot c_\beta=c_\beta<\kappa\) by
(3), so \(\rho\in\mathcal L_{\kappa\omega}\).
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p03-w03-p03-048-karp-kappa}{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-karp-kappa}{From the Karp property to \(\mathcal L_{\kappa\omega}\) and \(\mathcal L_{\infty\omega}\)}
\begin{proof}
For \(\kappa=\omega\) part~1 is the direction \((\Leftarrow)\) of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-karp-omega}; the argument below covers that case
as well.  Let \(\varphi\in L^{\ast}\) have no equivalent in
\(\mathcal L_{\kappa\omega}\) (part~1), respectively in
\(\mathcal L_{\infty\omega}\) (part~2), in the sense of
\cref{def:p03-w03-p03-048-infinitary}, clause~3.  Put \(L_0=L_\varphi\),
finite by clause~2 of \cref{def:p03-w03-p03-048-standing}.

\emph{Pairs at every level.}  For every ordinal \(\zeta\geq1\) with
\(\zeta<\kappa\) (part~1), respectively every ordinal \(\zeta\geq1\)
(part~2), there are \(L_0\)-structures \(\mathfrak M_\zeta\models\varphi\)
and \(\mathfrak N_\zeta\not\models\varphi\) with \(\emptyset\in
J_\zeta(\mathfrak M_\zeta,\mathfrak N_\zeta)\).  Otherwise
\(\varphi\) would have the same \(L_0\)-structures as models as a sentence
in the symbols of \(L_0\) that is first-order (the consequence in
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-levels}, when \(\kappa=\omega\)), lies in
\(\mathcal L_{\kappa\omega}\) (part~4 of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-levels-transfinite}, when \(\kappa>\omega\)), or
lies in \(\mathcal L_{\infty\omega}\) (the same part~4, in part~2).

\emph{The sentence.}  Let \(\psi\in K^{\ast}\) be the sentence built from
\(\varphi\) in the proof of the direction \((\Leftarrow)\) of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-karp-omega}, with clauses (Q1)--(Q8); it depends
on \(\varphi\) alone.  Step~(i) of that proof shows, using only the Karp
property and those clauses, that in every model of \(\psi\) the relation
\(<_U\) well-orders \(U\).

\emph{A model of type \(\zeta\).}  Let \(\zeta\geq1\) be one of the
ordinals for which a pair exists, \(\mathfrak M=\mathfrak M_\zeta\), and
\(\mathfrak N'\) an isomorphic copy of \(\mathfrak N_\zeta\) disjoint from
\(\mathfrak M\), with \(J_\zeta\) transported along the isomorphism.  Take
pairwise distinct new objects \(u_b\) for \(b<\zeta\) and \(\pi_{b,p}\) for
\(b<\zeta\) and \(p\in J_b(\mathfrak M,\mathfrak N')\).  The
\(K\)-structure has universe \(M\cup N'\) together with these objects;
\(U=\{u_b:b<\zeta\}\) with \(u_b<_Uu_{b'}\) iff \(b<b'\);
\(W_0=M\), \(W_1=N'\); \(S=\{\pi_{b,p}\}\); \(H(\pi_{b,p},u_b)\); and
\(E(\pi_{b,p},x,p(x))\) for \(x\in\operatorname{dom}p\).  The symbols of
\(L_0\) and their doubles are interpreted as in the proof of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-lowenheim-karp}, so that the pull-backs along
the morphisms \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) of the proof of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-karp-omega} are \(\mathfrak M\) and \(\mathfrak N'\); by the
Translation and Isomorphism Axioms \(\varphi^{\alpha}\) and
\((\neg\varphi)^{\beta}\) hold.  (Q1) holds since \((U,<_U)\) has type
\(\zeta\), and (Q2) by construction.  (Q3)--(Q5) hold because each
\(p\in J_b\subseteq J_0\) is a finite partial isomorphism.  (Q6): for
\(b<\zeta\), \(\emptyset\in J_\zeta\subseteq J_b\) by part~1 of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-levels-transfinite}, and \(\pi_{b,\emptyset}\) has
level \(u_b\) and empty \(E\)-section.  (Q7): let \(p\in J_b\), \(b'<b\) and
\(u\in M\).  Then \(b'+1\leq b\), so \(p\in J_{b'+1}\), and some \(q\in
J_{b'}\) with \(p\subseteq q\) has \(u\in\operatorname{dom}q\); the position
\(\pi_{b',q}\) has level \(u_{b'}\), its \(E\)-section contains that of
\(\pi_{b,p}\), and \(E(\pi_{b',q},u,q(u))\).  (Q8) follows in the same way
from the back clause of \(J_{b'+1}\), with \(w\in N'\) put into the range.

\emph{Conclusion.}  Let \(\gamma:L_{<}\to K\) send \(\mathbf V\) to \(U\) and
\(<\) to \(<_U\); every \(K\)-structure is \(\gamma\)-invertible, \(L_{<}\)
having no function symbols.  By the well-ordering step and
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-iso-closed}, the class \(A\) of order types of
\((U,<_U)\) in models of \(\psi\) is \(\Sigma^1_1\), and by the previous step
it contains every \(\zeta\) with \(1\leq\zeta<\kappa\) (part~1),
respectively every \(\zeta\geq1\) (part~2).  In part~1, boundedness by
\(\kappa\) puts every member of \(A\) below \(\kappa\); so \(A\) is a set
with supremum \(\kappa\), \(\kappa\) being a limit ordinal (it is
\(\omega\) or an uncountable cardinal).  By part~2 of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-sup-accessible}, \(\kappa\) belongs to some
\(\Sigma^1_1\) class of ordinals, against boundedness by \(\kappa\).  In
part~2, \(A\) contains every nonzero ordinal and is not a set, against
boundedness.  So \(\varphi\) has the required equivalent.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p03-w03-p03-048-kappa-forward}{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-kappa-forward}{From \(\mathcal L_{\kappa\omega}\) and \(\mathcal L_{\infty\omega}\) to the Karp property and boundedness}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Three facts about cardinals.}  (a) If \(\kappa=\beth_\kappa>\omega\)
and \(\mu<\kappa\), then \(2^{\mu}<\kappa\).  For \(\kappa\) is an infinite
cardinal, hence a limit ordinal, so \(\mu<\beth_\kappa=\sup_{\gamma<\kappa}
\beth_\gamma\) gives \(\mu<\beth_\gamma\) for some \(\gamma<\kappa\), and
\(2^{\mu}\leq\beth_{\gamma+1}<\beth_\kappa=\kappa\), by strict monotonicity
(proof of part~3 of \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-levels-transfinite}).
(b) If \(\kappa=\beth_\kappa>\omega\) and \(\psi'\in\mathcal L_{\kappa\omega}\),
let \(\mu=|\psi'|+\aleph_0<\kappa\) and \(\lambda=\mu^{+}\), a regular
cardinal.  Then \(|\psi'|<\lambda\), so \(\psi'\in\mathcal L_{\lambda\omega}\);
\(\lambda\leq2^{\mu}<\kappa\) and \(2^{\lambda}<\kappa\) by (a), so
\((2^{\lambda})^{+}\leq\kappa\).  (c) For every cardinal \(\mu\) there is
\(\kappa=\beth_\kappa>\mu+\aleph_0\).  Put \(\gamma_0=\mu+\omega+1\) and
\(\gamma_{n+1}=\beth_{\gamma_n}\).  Since \(\beth_\gamma\geq\gamma\), the
sequence is nondecreasing; let \(\kappa=\sup_n\gamma_n\).  If
\(\gamma_{n+1}=\gamma_n\) for some \(n\), then \(\kappa=\gamma_n=
\beth_{\gamma_n}\).  Otherwise \(\kappa\) is a limit ordinal, and
\(\beth_\kappa=\sup_{\alpha<\kappa}\beth_\alpha=\sup_n\beth_{\gamma_n}=
\sup_n\gamma_{n+1}=\kappa\), since the \(\gamma_n\) are cofinal in
\(\kappa\) and \(\beth\) is monotone.  In both cases \(\kappa\geq\gamma_0\).

\emph{Karp property (both parts).}  Let \(I:\mathfrak M\cong_p\mathfrak N\)
and \(\varphi\in L^{\ast}\), and take \(\psi'\) in
\(\mathcal L_{\kappa\omega}\), respectively \(\mathcal L_{\infty\omega}\),
built from the symbols of \(L_\varphi\), with the same
\(L_\varphi\)-structures as models.  The members of \(I\) are partial
isomorphisms between the reducts to \(L_\varphi\), and \(I\) keeps the back
and forth property; by \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-fo-karp} the reducts agree
on \(\psi'\), so on \(\varphi\), and by part~2 of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-basic} \(\mathfrak M\) and \(\mathfrak N\) agree
on \(\varphi\).

\emph{Boundedness.}  Let \(A\) be \(\Sigma^1_1\), witnessed by \(\psi\in
K^{\ast}\) and \(\gamma:L_{<}\to K\), and let \(\psi'\) be an equivalent of
\(\psi\) as above, built from the symbols of \(L_\psi\).  For a
\(K\)-structure \(\mathfrak N\), \(\mathfrak N\models\psi\) iff
\(\mathfrak N\restriction L_\psi\models\psi\) (part~2 of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-basic}) iff \(\mathfrak N\restriction L_\psi
\models\psi'\) iff \(\mathfrak N\models\psi'\), Tarskian truth depending
only on the symbols that occur.  So \(\psi\) and \(\psi'\) have the same
\(K\)-structures as models, and \(A\) is the class of order types of the
pull-backs of models of \(\psi'\).  In part~1, (b) gives a regular
\(\lambda\) with \(\psi'\in\mathcal L_{\lambda\omega}\) and
\((2^{\lambda})^{+}\leq\kappa\); \(A\) is \(\Sigma^1_1\) in
\(\mathcal L_{\lambda\omega}\), and by
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-lopez-escobar} every member of \(A\) is below
\((2^{\lambda})^{+}\leq\kappa\).  So \(\mathcal L^{\ast}\) is bounded by
\(\kappa\).  In part~2, (c) gives \(\kappa=\beth_\kappa>|\psi'|+\aleph_0\),
so \(\psi'\in\mathcal L_{\kappa\omega}\), and the argument of part~1 puts
every member of \(A\) below \(\kappa\); so \(A\) is a set, and
\(\mathcal L^{\ast}\) is bounded.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p03-w03-p03-048-host-karp}{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-host-karp}{Barwise's Theorem~2.2 in the regular relational package}
\begin{proof}
Suppose \(I:A\cong_pB\), \(A\models\varphi\) and \(B\not\models\varphi\) for a
sentence \(\varphi\) of \(\tau\).  Let \(\tau_0=\tau_\varphi\), finite.
Satisfaction depends only on the reduct to \(\tau_0\), and the members of
\(I\) are partial isomorphisms between the reducts with the back and forth
property, so we may assume \(\tau=\tau_0\).  By isomorphism invariance we may
replace \(B\) by an isomorphic copy disjoint from \(A\), composing the members
of \(I\) with the isomorphism.  By closure under negation,
\(B\models\neg\varphi\).

\emph{Signature and sentence.}  For each \(R\in\tau_0\) take a new relation
symbol \(R''\) of the same arity, and let \(\rho\) be the renaming
\(R\mapsto R''\).  For a \(\tau_0''\)-structure \(C\) let \(C^{\rho}\) be the
\(\tau_0\)-structure on the same universe with \(R^{C^{\rho}}=(R'')^{C}\); by
the renaming clause of the package, \(C\models\rho(\neg\varphi)\) iff
\(C^{\rho}\models\neg\varphi\).  Take new unary \(U,W,S\) and a ternary \(E\),
and let \(\sigma=\tau_0\cup\tau_0''\cup\{U,W,S,E\}\).  For a
\(\sigma\)-structure \(\mathfrak N\) with \(U^{\mathfrak N}\neq\emptyset\), let
\(\mathfrak N[U]\) be the \(\tau_0\)-structure induced on \(U^{\mathfrak N}\);
by the relativization clause, \(\mathfrak N\models\varphi^{U}\) iff
\(\mathfrak N[U]\models\varphi\).  Likewise \(\mathfrak N[W]\) is the
\(\tau_0''\)-structure induced on \(W^{\mathfrak N}\).  Let
\[
\psi=\varphi^{U}\land(\rho(\neg\varphi))^{W}\land\chi,
\]
regarded as a sentence of \(\sigma\) by the occurrence and reduct clauses,
where \(\chi\) is the first-order conjunction of \(\exists x\,U(x)\),
\(\exists x\,W(x)\) and the clauses (P1)--(P4), (P6) and (P7) of the proof
of \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-lowenheim-karp}, with (P4) taken for each
\(R\in\tau_0\) and its double \(R''\); there are no function symbols, so (P5)
is absent.

\emph{\(\psi\) has a model.}  Let \(\mathfrak N\) have universe
\(A\cup B\cup\{\iota_F:F\in I\}\) (new distinct objects \(\iota_F\)),
\(U=A\), \(W=B\), \(S=\{\iota_F\}\), \(E=\{(\iota_F,x,F(x)):x\in
\operatorname{dom}F\}\), \(R^{\mathfrak N}=R^{A}\) and
\((R'')^{\mathfrak N}=R^{B}\).  Then \(\mathfrak N[U]=A\models\varphi\) and
\(\mathfrak N[W]^{\rho}=B\models\neg\varphi\); \(\chi\) holds because \(A\),
\(B\) and \(I\) are nonempty and \(I\) is a back and forth set of partial
isomorphisms.

\emph{A countable model.}  By hypothesis~2, \(\psi\) has a countable model
\(\mathfrak N^{\ast}\).  By \(\chi\), \(U\) and \(W\) are nonempty there;
\(A_0=\mathfrak N^{\ast}[U]\models\varphi\) and \(B_0=\mathfrak
N^{\ast}[W]^{\rho}\models\neg\varphi\), so \(B_0\not\models\varphi\).  For
\(s\in S^{\mathfrak N^{\ast}}\) the set \(F_s=\{(x,y):E(s,x,y)\}\) is, by
(P2)--(P4), a one-one map from part of \(A_0\) into \(B_0\) that preserves and
reflects each \(R\in\tau_0\), the relation \(R\) of \(B_0\) being
\((R'')^{\mathfrak N^{\ast}}\) on \(W\).  By (P1), (P6) and (P7),
\(\{F_s\}\) is a nonempty back and forth set of partial isomorphisms from
\(A_0\) to \(B_0\).  Both are countable, so they are isomorphic by
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-countable}, whose proof applies to relational
structures; isomorphism invariance contradicts \(A_0\models\varphi\),
\(B_0\not\models\varphi\).  A potential isomorphism is a set \(I\) of the
kind used here, so the last sentence of the lemma follows.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}

\begin{annexitem}{annex:p03-w03-p03-048-host-route}{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-host-route}{\Cref{thm:p03-lindstrom} along the Karp route}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Part~1.}  Let \(\tau_0=\tau_\varphi\).  For each \(m\geq1\) there are
\(\tau_0\)-structures \(\mathfrak M_m\models\varphi\) and \(\mathfrak N_m
\not\models\varphi\) with \(\emptyset\in J_m(\mathfrak M_m,\mathfrak N_m)\).
Otherwise, for some \(m\), let \(\vartheta=\bigvee\{\tau^{m}_{\mathfrak M}:
\mathfrak M\models\varphi\}\), a finite disjunction of relational first-order
sentences of \(\tau_0\) by \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-levels} (the
\(\tau^{m}\) of a relational language use only equality and the relations).
If \(\mathfrak M\models\varphi\) then \(\mathfrak M\models\tau^m_{\mathfrak M}\),
so \(\mathfrak M\models\vartheta\); if \(\mathfrak N\models\vartheta\), then
\(\emptyset\in J_m(\mathfrak M,\mathfrak N)\) for some
\(\mathfrak M\models\varphi\), so \(\mathfrak N\models\varphi\).  So
\(\varphi\) would be equivalent over \(\tau_0\) to \(\vartheta\).

Take \(\tau_0''\) and \(\rho\)-readings as in the proof of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-host-karp}, new unary \(U,W_0,W_1,S\), binary
\(<_U,H\) and ternary \(E\); \(\sigma\) is \(\tau_0\cup\tau_0''\) with these.
Let \(\psi=\varphi^{W_0}\land(\rho(\neg\varphi))^{W_1}\land\chi\), with
\(\chi\) the first-order conjunction of \(\exists x\,W_0(x)\),
\(\exists x\,W_1(x)\) and the clauses (Q1)--(Q4) and (Q6)--(Q8) of the proof
of \cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-karp-omega} (no function symbols, so no (Q5)).

\emph{Well-ordering.}  Let \(\mathfrak N\models\psi\), and suppose
\(X\subseteq U^{\mathfrak N}\) is nonempty with no least element.  Put
\(A_0=\mathfrak N[W_0]\models\varphi\) and \(B_0=\mathfrak
N[W_1]^{\rho}\models\neg\varphi\).  For a position \(s\) let
\(F_s=\{(x,y):E(s,x,y)\}\); by (Q2)--(Q4) it is a partial isomorphism from
\(A_0\) to \(B_0\).  Let \(I=\{F_s:H(s,b)\text{ for some }b\in X\}\).  By
(Q6), \(I\neq\emptyset\).  If \(H(s,b)\) with \(b\in X\), choose \(b'\in X\)
with \(b'<_Ub\); by (Q7), for every \(u\in W_0\) some \(t\) with
\(H(t,b')\) has \(F_s\subseteq F_t\) and \(u\in\operatorname{dom}F_t\), and
\(F_t\in I\); (Q8) gives the back clause.  So \(I:A_0\cong_pB_0\), and
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-host-karp} contradicts \(A_0\models\varphi\),
\(B_0\not\models\varphi\).

\emph{Type \(m\).}  For \(m\geq1\), build the \(\sigma\)-structure from
\(\mathfrak M_m\), a disjoint copy \(\mathfrak N'\) of \(\mathfrak N_m\), the
objects \(u_b\) (\(b<m\)) and \(\pi_{b,p}\) (\(b<m\), \(p\in
J_b(\mathfrak M_m,\mathfrak N')\)), as in the proof of
\cref{thm:p03-w03-p03-048-karp-kappa} with \(\zeta=m\), the relations of
\(\mathfrak N'\) going to the doubled symbols.  Its universe is nonempty,
\(\mathfrak N[W_0]=\mathfrak M_m\models\varphi\), and \(\mathfrak N[W_1]^{\rho}
=\mathfrak N'\models\neg\varphi\) by isomorphism invariance.  (Q1)--(Q4) and
(Q6)--(Q8) hold by the verification given there, which uses only
\(\emptyset\in J_m\subseteq J_b\) and \(J_b\subseteq J_{b'+1}\) for
\(b'<b\).  So \((U,<_U)\) has type \(m\).

\emph{Part~2.}  Suppose \(\psi\) has both properties.  Take new unary
\(P_i\) (\(i<\omega\)).  Let \(\delta_0\) say that exactly one element is in
\(P_0\) and it is in \(U\), and \(\delta_{i+1}\) say that exactly one
element is in \(P_{i+1}\), it is in \(U\), and it is \(<_U\)-below the
element of \(P_i\).  These are relational first-order sentences, hence
\(\mathcal L^{\ast}\)-sentences, and
\(T=\{\psi\}\cup\{\delta_i:i<\omega\}\) is countable.  A finite subset lies
in \(\{\psi,\delta_0,\ldots,\delta_k\}\); take a model of \(\psi\) in which
\((U,<_U)\) has type \(k+1\), \(u_0<_U\cdots<_Uu_k\), and interpret \(P_i\)
as \(\{u_{k-i}\}\) for \(i\leq k\) and as \(\emptyset\) for \(i>k\).  The
expansion still satisfies \(\psi\) by the reduct clause, and it satisfies
\(\delta_0,\ldots,\delta_k\).  By hypothesis~1, \(T\) has a model
\(\mathfrak N\).  If \(c_i\) is the element of \(P_i\), then
\(c_0>_Uc_1>_U\cdots\) in \(U\), so \(\{c_i:i<\omega\}\) has no least
element; but \(\mathfrak N\models\psi\), so \(<_U\) well-orders \(U\).

\emph{Conclusion.}  If some \(\varphi\) had no first-order equivalent over
\(\tau_\varphi\), part~1 would give a sentence with the two properties that
part~2 excludes.  First-order sentences belong to \(\mathcal L^{\ast}\) by
the definition of the package.
\end{proof}
\end{annexitem}
