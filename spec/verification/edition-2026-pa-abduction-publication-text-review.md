## Exactness and proof audit

The P5 text uses three different levels consistently: the numerical direct
checker, a particular finite primitive recursive description, and the arithmetic
formula obtained by expanding that description. The graph-construction
dependency specifies arities, initial functions, composition, recursion,
beta-entry formulas and fresh variables. The first-order variable order and
least-fresh substitution convention are available in the earlier syntax chapter.
The new declaration conventions specify the order and expansion of tests,
patterns, folds and intermediate fields. No unspecified search over formulas,
axiom matrices or ND witnesses is used to select the new graph.

The command bound for substitution is justified by counting main node visits,
preliminary renaming visits, assembly and continuation commands in annex 79–90.
The alpha comparison bound and the meaning of its free/bound occurrence tags
are supplied in annex 92–101. The numerical equivalence is then established
against the actual inherited operations, rather than inferred from the fact
that some primitive recursive implementation exists.

For the internal joining theorem, the proof establishes equations of the
selected list/table graphs. In particular it proves the sentence-subformula
fact inside PA before using it to show that the new consequent is a sentence.
It tracks the order of premise references and of surviving open occurrences.
The existential-elimination case preserves the required all-variable test,
including bound occurrences, at annex 408–417. The new final row is tag 8 with
the antecedent first and the implication second. Old roots acquire exactly one
parent, and the new root acquires none.

The proof does not need an internal semantic correctness theorem for arbitrary
substitution or alpha comparison: corresponding copied rows call the same
fixed descriptions on literally identical numerical arguments, so their
unique graph outputs agree. The same argument applies to the reused direct
axiom tests. This is stated at annex 306–310, 387–393 and 479–488 and supports
the precise invariant being proved. Standard-model agreement between arbitrary
representatives is not substituted for those internal equations.

For P16, the restriction/extension argument identifies finite valuations with
the existing global consequence relation. The candidate restriction is tested
separately from consistency and entailment. The minimality proofs include the
empty explanation and the fact that a consistent explanatory subset makes a
one-atom deletion explanatory whenever it lies between that subset and a
known explanation. The enumeration proof states and proves both flag
invariants and both output-filter characterizations. The examples include
negative cases, not merely a list of successful candidates.
