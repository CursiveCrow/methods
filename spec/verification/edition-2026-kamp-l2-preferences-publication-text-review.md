## Located examination: Kamp Lemma 2

The actual reader route is `main.tex:33` to `parts/p09.tex:4`, then
`parts/p09/logic-in-computer-science.tex:950`, then
`parts/p09/presentations/n-c-012.tex:149-150`. Lemma 1 immediately precedes
Lemma 2. The P9 host's fourth numbered section begins at line 612 and
contains the input at line 950, supporting the new registry's P9.4 scope.
The annex route is `main.tex:55` to `annex/p09.tex:7-8`, in the same lemma
order. The Lemma 1 body and annex hashes match the unchanged dependency
identities reported in the author intake; both complete files were read as
the actual interface used by this review.

The host defines nonempty strict linear orders, the first-order language,
the temporal language, the witness-first arguments of S/U, and the fresh
variable D-translation before either lemma. Lemma 1 then defines the
predicate-to-proposition hat, named-variable expressibility, binary patterns,
their guarded closures, occurrence words, and finite realizations. Lemma 2
uses precisely those interfaces. The distinction between endpoint variables
and point assignments is preserved, and substitutions explicitly avoid
capture.

Body 13-48 gives H', G', P', F' as temporal abbreviations and the two partial
operators as first-order formula operations. Annex 21-61 derives their
strict-cut meanings rather than assuming a general substitution theorem.
The endpoint vacuity is included in both derivations. The base predicates
are tested at the quantified witness, and body 75-91 specifies the free
variable and interval-count conventions of the positive stages. There is
no undefined basic stage zero or silent retention of preceding stages.

Body 94-125 defines both whole-word formulas and the shorter suffix/prefix
before the paired recurrence. The one-letter case is stated explicitly.
Annex 63-98 verifies that the actual guarded limiting formulas have exactly
the advertised whole-chain conditions. The basic same-word equivalence is
used only through the already supplied Lemma 1 interface.

The past reverse implication at annex 116-165 permits every index
`0 <= k < n`, including `k = n - 1`, and equality of the next old point
with the current point. Its new gap is contained in the corresponding old
gap, so it inherits precisely the required interval predicate. The future
reverse implication at 182-219 supplies its own prefix construction and
permits every `1 <= k <= n`, including both extreme indices and equality.
Each splice retains n + 1 points and their correct indexed endpoint
predicates. The proof covers n = 1 explicitly and obtains all inserted
points from the stated premises. Density, endpointlessness, and order
completeness are not hidden assumptions.

The simultaneous induction at 221-247 reaches the exact displayed temporal
formulas and preserves the input alphabet, even when letters repeat. The
endpoint, adjacency, singleton, and empty-gap calculations at 249-261 are
consistent with the strict quantifiers. The two one-interval examples at
263-271 demonstrate why the extra conjunct is needed. The numerical splice
examples at 273-284 check the claimed extreme positions. The separately
named CurF comparison at 286-305 proves its disjunction for a last point
as well as a point with a future, then verifies the real-line separation
from the witness-point base.

No claim in the target pair turns this lemma into all of III.2. Body
205-212 and annex 307-310 identify the remaining source dependency and
preserve Lin as the present domain. The surrounding host paragraph
140-160 identifies the larger source theorem with citations; the inserted
lemma's own limitation remains explicit.

The quotient is initially introduced as a family of explicitly defined
subsets. Its total-order and representative-independence theorem precedes
the rank construction. This avoids assuming an unproved quotient order.
Completeness includes diagonal pairs, while antisymmetry is required only
of the class order. The number k counts classes; r counts lower classes;
u0 assigns their ranks to alternatives. None of these counts silently
counts alternatives inside a tie class.

The uniqueness theorem fixes its unique f on u(X), and the proof establishes
that exact domain before extending to the real line. The extension treats
zero, one, and at least two attained values separately; the interpolation
formulas agree at overlapping endpoints and have positive slopes. The
nonuniqueness argument changes an exterior slope while preserving all
attained values. Its non-affine example does not introduce a proof cycle:
the later tied-example proof verifies v directly from its table.

The generic C_R is defined for any supplied relation and any menu, so the
cyclic counterexample is within its stated domain. Nonempty choice is
claimed only for a nonempty finite menu under the weak-preference
hypotheses. The restriction equality assumes that an original maximizer
survives in the smaller menu; the proof uses that very witness. No empty
extremum is selected. A retained tie set is distinguished from a selection
of one alternative.

The source-specific key Rubinstein2015LectureNotes is used in both body and
annex. The canonical record at bibliography 6608-6620 states the 2012
second edition and the author-hosted revision of 11 January 2015; its 2007
URL filename is not used as the edition date. The body identifies its
glyph convention, one-to-zero rank shift, added converse and finite
extension, empty-menu extension, and additional tied table. The ordinal
boundary is proved on the original alternatives. It asserts no lottery
preferences, intensity measure, empirical adequacy, or full decision-theory
completion. Exact source identity and passage fidelity remain the separate
primary-source review's responsibility.
