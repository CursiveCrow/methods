## Numerators, nulls and source-report anomalies

- 1989 recruited10 (5male/5female), removed one with no protocols/no solved
  problems, retained9, then omitted middle scorer for4+4 extreme comparison.
  Group membership selected using all19 problem scores.82%/46% is consequently
  a defining outcome contrast, not an independently assigned treatment effect.
  The article's reported p thresholds are preserved only for actual selected
  results, not recomputed from incomplete records. Declarative-law component
  equality5.5/5.5 does not exclude unarticulated prior understanding (pp.168–169).
  Reference-episode difference2.7/6.7 is only marginal (`p<.10`,p.174);
  mapping/checking episode differences not found. No randomized no-explanation
  control. 'Far transfer' means the seven same-chapter nonisomorphic tasks,
  not distant-domain or delayed everyday transfer.
- 1994 main sample14+10, pilots11 separate. The method describes volunteers
  deliberately spanning CAT scores but **does not report random assignment**.
  Prompted mean study125min versus control66min despite planned rough matching.
  Gain percentages are changes on percentage-correct scale (percentage points),
  not relative growth rates.32versus22 and22.6versus12.5 are source-reported
  gains. High/low overall38versus27, `t(6)=2.14,p<.07`, is not a conventional
  `.05` positive result; the difficult-question contrast33versus17 is separate.
  Four+four explanation-count extremes exclude14(outlying CAT) and13(noCAT).
  All4 high and1/4low reached double-loop2. Prompted8/14 versus newly reaching
  control2/9 is not the unconditional control posttest3/10: student19 started
  with the correct model. Preserve both denominators. Designed four categories
  of14 questions imply56, but p.458 calls the posttest54; no silent reconciliation
  or item-level reanalysis. Coarse/fine recoding by project members is not a
  reported blinded independent reliability coefficient. Some unprompted
  students could self-explain covertly. Diagram counts are not randomized
  diagram-only conditions. Aboutone-quarter erroneous inferences and unchanged
  correct-only association do not prove that false explanation is harmless.
- 2014 actual groups23/19; posttest76.4/68.9 are **ANCOVA-adjusted** means,
  not raw scores; `F(1,39)=4.98,p=.03,eta^2=.086` is the authors' report.
  Do not relabel their eta-squared as partial eta-squared or claim reconstructible
  uncertainty. Paper849 says25pretest items plus6extra yields29; this is
  internally inconsistent. Reported posttest breakdown10inter+8micro+2macro+
  9other sums29, but raw items unavailable here; exact denominator unresolved.
  Printed PDF confirms this is not extraction/OCR error. Mean duration not
  reported, so twice-reading is a time-control intention, not verified equality.
  The eleven specific simulation prompts occur only in explanation condition;
  no factorial isolation of generic explaining from that directed attention.
  Macro-inference correlation `p=.23` and paraphrase correlations not reliable;
  negative overall macro correlation not a causal detrimental effect.
  No random assignment to micro/inter/macro explanation types. Multiple
  correlations/extreme-tail comparisons are exploratory; coder blinding and
  interrater reliability are not reported in the held8page article. The reported
  Asterhan/Schwarz2009 natural-selection null is **not independently qualified**;
  it is not used as a new experimental evidence row.
