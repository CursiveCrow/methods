## Notes and complete cited-work inventory

The complete references contain **27 entries**. Their role in this
selected extent is as follows; separate full treatments are not claimed.

1. Aristotle 1999, *Nicomachean Ethics*, Irwin second edition: the
   historical account and note 2's translation provenance; note 1's
   narrow/broad praxis distinction is preserved.
2. Broome 2013, *Rationality through Reasoning*: mental-state requirements,
   distinct requirement conception (note 4), and reduction objections.
3. Edgington 1995, *On Conditionals*: proposed conditional credences.
4. Friedman 2013, *Suspended Judgment*: distinctive suspension attitude.
5. Hájek 2005, *Scotching Dutch Books?*: note 5's external discussion,
   not an imported formal proof.
6. Harman 1986, *Change in View*: plan/intention revision as the fundamental
   practical object of assessment.
7. Hume 1739/1740, *Treatise of Human Nature*, II.iii.3, Hume Texts Online:
   the inspiration for contemporary instrumental/desire-based approaches,
   with its contested interpretation retained.
8. Jeffrey 1983, *The Logic of Decision*, second edition: a
   preference-centered formal approach.
9. Joyce 1998, *A Nonpragmatic Vindication of Probabilism*: the source's
   interpretation of degrees of inaccuracy; no specific scoring theorem
   is stated or attributed as locally proved.
10. Joyce 1999, *Foundations of Causal Decision Theory*: preference-centered
    formal theory and rejection of compulsory pragmatist belief reduction.
11. Joyce 2010, *A Defense of Imprecise Credences in Inference and Decision
    Making*: the proposed precise/imprecise attitude distinction.
12. Kant 1900ff., *Gesammelte Schriften*: original volume/page locators.
13. Kant 1992, *Logic*, edited by Jäsche in *Lectures on Logic*: note 3's
    translation provenance and cognition distinction.
14. Kant 2000, *First Introduction to the Critique of the Power of
    Judgment*: technical imperative form/content distinction and note 3.
15. Kant 2012, *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals*: imperatives,
    hypothetical ends/means, and note 3.
16. Kelly 2003, *Epistemic Rationality as Instrumental Rationality: A
    Critique*: the two route interests and unwanted-evidence objection.
17. Kolodny 2005, *Why Be Rational?*: the believed-reasons proposal.
18. Kolodny 2008, *Why Be Disposed to Be Coherent?*: that proposal's
    related development.
19. Millgram 1995, *Was Hume a Humean?*: note 6's rival nihilist
    interpretation, not a settled historical verdict.
20. Parfit 2011, *On What Matters*, volume 1: note 8's sufficient
    apparent-reasons account.
21. Raz 1999, *Engaging Reason*: action reasons as facts about goodness,
    shared guidance/constitutive role, and the contested guise-of-good claim.
22. Raz 2011, *From Normativity to Responsibility*: rational powers and
    malfunction, standard truth-related reasons, and rejection of true
    belief as a value.
23. Savage 1972, *The Foundations of Statistics*, second revised edition:
    the precisely bounded surveyed representation role, not a reproduced
    whole theorem or proof.
24. Schroeder 2007, *Slaves of the Passions*: desire grounding and auxiliary
    claims; its separate chapter 7 account of weight is named as a
    dependency rather than invented.
25. Setiya 2007, *Reasons without Rationalism*: intention-as-belief
    complication and objections to the guise-of-good premise.
26. Steglich-Petersen 2018, *Epistemic Instrumentalism, Permissibility,
    and Reasons for Belief*: note 7's contemporary example.
27. Wedgwood 2017, *The Value of Rationality*: note 9's fuller theory,
    whose details are outside this selected presentation.
