# Finite-arity BAO ultrafilter witness lemma

Date: 2026-09-06 Pacific time. Independent derivation under ZF + BPIT.

## Statement and existing dependency

Let B be a Boolean algebra, let n be a nonnegative integer, and let
`f:B^n -> B` preserve binary joins and zero in each coordinate. The zero
condition means that f is zero whenever at least one argument is zero.
For n=0, these coordinate conditions are vacuous; f is a designated element.
Let U be an ultrafilter of B. If `f(a_1,...,a_n) in U`, then there exist
ultrafilters `V_1,...,V_n` such that each `a_i in V_i` and

`for every b_i in V_i (1<=i<=n), f(b_1,...,b_n) in U`.

The needed local dependency is the actual
`thm:p10-ultrafilter-separation` in
`handbook/parts/p10/stone-duality.tex:12–23`, proved in
`handbook/annex/p10/stone-duality.tex:5–48`. In particular, for a filter F
and b absent from F, BPIT supplies an ultrafilter containing F and omitting b.
Its proof also supplies the Boolean dichotomy and finite-join primeness used below.

## Monotonicity

Binary-join preservation implies monotonicity in each coordinate: if x<=y,
then x join y=y, so `f(...,x,...) join f(...,y,...)=f(...,y,...)`.
Applying this in finitely many coordinates gives coordinatewise monotonicity.
If c<=d and d is absent from a filter U, then c is absent too, by the
contrapositive of upward closure. No complete additivity is assumed.

## Inductive construction

For `k=0,...,n`, maintain the following assertion P_k:

There are ultrafilters V_1,...,V_k containing a_1,...,a_k such that for
every tuple `b_i in V_i`, i<=k,

`f(b_1,...,b_k,a_{k+1},...,a_n) in U`.

P_0 is exactly the premise. Suppose P_{k-1} holds, with 1<=k<=n. Define

`I_k = {b in B : there exist c_i in V_i (i<k) such that`
`                    f(c_1,...,c_{k-1},b,a_{k+1},...,a_n) notin U}`.

For k=1, the prior tuple is the unique empty tuple. This definition then
simply tests whether `f(b,a_2,...,a_n)` is absent from U.

### I_k is an ideal

It contains zero: use the prior tuple `(1,...,1)`, whose components belong
to their filters; normality makes the displayed f-value zero, which is
absent from U. For k=1 use the empty prior tuple.

It is downward closed: a witness for b is also a witness for any d<=b,
by monotonicity and downward closure of absence from U.

For join closure, let b,d belong to I_k. Choose respective prior witness
tuples `(c_i)_{i<k}` and `(e_i)_{i<k}`, and put `t_i=c_i meet e_i`.
Every t_i belongs to V_i. Write the fixed trailing tuple as A. Monotonicity gives

`f(t,b,A) <= f(c,b,A) notin U`,
`f(t,d,A) <= f(e,d,A) notin U`.

Thus both left-hand values are absent from U. Additivity in coordinate k gives

`f(t,b join d,A) = f(t,b,A) join f(t,d,A)`.

Ultrafilter finite-join primeness makes this join absent from U. Hence the
single tuple t witnesses `b join d in I_k`. This common-tuple step is essential;
the two original witness tuples cannot simply be retained separately.

The invariant P_{k-1} says that `a_k notin I_k`. Therefore I_k is proper:
if it contained 1, downward closure would put a_k in it.

### Extend the dual filter

Define `F_k={not b:b in I_k}`. It is a proper filter: zero in I_k gives
one in F_k; join closure in I_k gives meet closure in F_k; downward
closure in I_k gives upward closure in F_k; and one absent from I_k
gives zero absent from F_k.

Since `a_k notin I_k`, involution gives `not a_k notin F_k`.
Apply the existing avoiding-element extension lemma to F_k and the element
`not a_k`. It yields an ultrafilter V_k containing F_k and omitting
`not a_k`, hence containing a_k by dichotomy.

Moreover `V_k intersect I_k` is empty: if b belonged to I_k, its complement
would belong to F_k and hence to V_k, so properness forbids b in V_k.
Consequently each b in V_k is absent from I_k. By the definition of I_k,
every prior tuple from the V_i then gives `f(prior,b,A) in U`.
This is P_k. Induction yields P_n, proving the lemma.

Equivalently one can extend the filter generated by
`F_k union {a_k}` directly. It is proper because an equation
`a_k meet not c=0`, with c in I_k, would imply a_k<=c and hence
a_k in I_k. The existing avoiding-element lemma packages this calculation.

## Edge cases and necessity of hypotheses

- For n=0 the requested witness tuple is the unique empty tuple, and the
  conclusion is the premise `f() in U`. No extension is performed. Do not
  impose `f()=0` unless that additional nullary convention is separately declared.
- If B is trivial, it has no ultrafilters. The lemma quantified over U is
  vacuous. In the representation, its ultrafilter space is empty and the
  one-element Boolean algebra of subsets of that space handles every arity.
- For positive n, normality and the premise imply each a_i is nonzero.
  Thus no witness filter is asked to contain zero.
- Normality is necessary for the stated general implication: on the
  two-element algebra the unary constant-one map preserves binary joins but
  sends zero into its ultrafilter, while no ultrafilter can contain zero.
- Binary-join preservation is also substantive: on the four-element Boolean
  algebra let a unary map send 1 to 1 and every other element to 0. It is
  normal and monotone. Taking input 1 gives the premise, but every ultrafilter
  contains an atom whose image is zero, so no witness ultrafilter works.

## Canonical relation and absence of global Choice

For each fixed operator define the relation, without making selections,

`R_f(U,V_1,...,V_n) iff for all b_i in V_i, f(b_1,...,b_n) in U`.

Then the lemma and its immediate converse give

`f(a_1,...,a_n) in U`
`iff there exist V_1,...,V_n with R_f(U,V_1,...,V_n) and all a_i in V_i`.

For n=0, this is the unary relation `R_f(U) iff f() in U`.
The associated nullary complex-algebra value is therefore `hat(f())`.
For positive arities the equivalence proves preservation of f by the Stone
embedding into the full powerset complex algebra.

Each witness proof uses finitely many dependent existential choices, one
per coordinate, and finitely many choices when combining two witness tuples.
Finite induction and finite choice are available in ZF. BPIT supplies the
ultrafilter extensions. No Choice principle for infinite families is used.

An arbitrary *set* of operator symbols, each of finite arity, causes no
additional choice requirement. Each R_f is defined by the displayed formula
as a subset of the appropriate finite power of the ultrafilter set. These
unique definable relations form a family by Replacement. Preservation is
proved separately for each operator, tuple, and U; it asserts existence of
witness tuples without selecting one simultaneously for every such instance.

No manuscript edits, tests, builds, renders, or other reviewers' results were
used for this independent derivation.

## Strengthened filter extension and bounded-morphism back condition

Independently checked at approximately 02:11 UTC on 2026-09-07.

Suppose proper filters F_1,...,F_n satisfy

`f(a_1,...,a_n) in U for every tuple a_i in F_i`.

Then there are ultrafilters V_i containing F_i with the same universal
tuple property. Maintain the invariant with V_i in the first k coordinates
and F_i in the remaining coordinates. At stage k define I_k to consist of
the b for which some tuple from the prior V_i AND the future F_j makes
`f(prior,b,future)` absent from U.

I_k contains zero by normality, using ones in every other coordinate.
It is downward closed by monotonicity. For two members b,d, take their
witness tuples and meet them coordinatewise in EVERY coordinate other
than k, including the future-filter coordinates. Each such meet remains
in its required filter. Monotonicity gives a common witness tuple t for
which both `f(t,b)` and `f(t,d)` are absent from U. Additivity at k and
ultrafilter join-primeness give `f(t,b join d) notin U`, proving ideal
closure. The current invariant gives `I_k intersect F_k = empty`.

The filter generated by F_k and the complements of I_k is exactly

`E_k={d: some a in F_k and i in I_k satisfy a meet not i <= d}`.

Its filter laws follow by combining witnesses with a meet in F_k and
a join in I_k. This includes empty selections because `1 in F_k` and
`0 in I_k`. If E_k contained zero, then a<=i for such witnesses. Since
I_k is downward closed, a would belong to I_k as well as F_k, a
contradiction. Thus E_k is proper. BPIT extends it to V_k. Since V_k
contains every complement of an element of I_k, it is disjoint from
I_k. This is exactly the next invariant. Finite induction proves the
strengthened statement.

Now let h:A->B be a Boolean homomorphism preserving the chosen operator,
let U be an ultrafilter of B, and suppose

`R_f^A(h^(-1)[U],W_1,...,W_n)`.

For positive arity define

`F_i={b in B: some w in W_i satisfies h(w)<=b}`.

Because h preserves 1 and meets, this is the filter generated by h[W_i].
It is proper under the edge hypothesis. Indeed, if h(w)=0 for w in W_i,
put w in coordinate i and 1 in every other source coordinate. The source
edge puts the resulting f_A-value in h^(-1)[U]. Applying h and operator
preservation puts an f_B-value with a zero argument in U. Normality makes
that value zero, a contradiction. Thus no h(w) is zero and 0 is absent
from F_i. Properness here must not be inferred from a Boolean homomorphism
alone; the canonical edge supplies it.

For b_i in F_i choose the finitely many corresponding w_i in W_i. The
source edge and preservation give

`f_B(h(w_1),...,h(w_n)) = h(f_A(w_1,...,w_n)) in U`.

Since h(w_i)<=b_i, monotonicity gives `f_B(b_1,...,b_n) in U`.
Apply the strengthened lemma to obtain V_i containing F_i with
`R_f^B(U,V_1,...,V_n)`. Each h^(-1)[V_i] is an ultrafilter of A by the
existing inverse-image lemma and contains W_i. Maximality of W_i therefore
gives `h^(-1)[V_i]=W_i`, proving the back condition.

For n=0, no filters or tuple witnesses occur. The back condition follows
directly from

`f_A() in h^(-1)[U] iff h(f_A())=f_B() in U`.

The same equivalence gives nullary forth. In positive arity, forth follows
by applying operator preservation to each tuple from the preimage
ultrafilters. Thus the canonical inverse-image map has both bounded-morphism
conditions for every finite arity.

If B is trivial there is no U, so the pointwise back condition is vacuous.
A Boolean homomorphism from a trivial A to a nontrivial B cannot preserve
both constants. The trivial/empty cases therefore require no additional
witnesses. The entire argument uses finitely many BPIT extensions per
instance; no product compactness, countable Choice, or global witness
selection is involved.
