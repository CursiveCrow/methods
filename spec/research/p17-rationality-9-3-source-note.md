## Direct correction provenance and qualifications

- p.554: Hawk-Dove with 0<V<C has two asymmetric pure Nash profiles;
  it has no symmetric pure Nash profile. The active reconstruction states
  all three Nash profiles and the unique symmetric mixed ESS.
- p.555: the second ESS clause compares only alternative best responses
  to the incumbent; it does not make the incumbent best against every
  possible alternative opponent.
- p.559: impartiality means independence of the incumbent index of the
  response function, not of its argument's incumbent payoff. Theorem5's
  one-sixth statement is qualified by the primary's common function,
  Lipschitz sign preservation and positive right derivative at zero.
  Most means outside an open set whose prescribed positive measure can
  be made arbitrarily small by selecting the game, not necessarily almost
  everywhere for one fixed game.
- Figure9.3.6 prints -1 as the column payoff in the Scissors/Paper cell
  while its transpose requires -2. The active matrix uses the primary
  symmetric normal-form feeble-twin construction; the held chapter is
  unchanged.
- p.560: role symmetry does not imply pi(S_i|S_j)=pi(S_j|S_i).
  The role-swapped pair is (A_ij,A_ji); A need not equal its transpose.
- p.561: replace the printed non-strict local superiority inequality
  by strict > for an evolutionarily stable state. The zero matrix is
  a counterexample to the printed claimed equivalence with ESS.
- p.562: tangent vectors may have support contained in, not necessarily
  properly contained in, the equilibrium support. Proper containment
  would omit every nonzero two-strategy tangent vector.
- p.562: the regular-ESS stability theorem is Sandholm2010 Theorem1,
  not the cited survival paper's theorem. The source's genericity remark
  is recorded as an attributed external claim, not a local genericity
  proof or probability assertion.
- pp.562-564: the globally attracting point in Figure9.3.10 is S2,
  not an undefined point A. The final strict-dominance reference is
  Figure9.3.6, not 9.3.7b. Local attraction, Lyapunov stability, basin
  size and typical outcome retain different quantifiers.
