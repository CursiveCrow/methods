## Cross-presentation distinctions that the chapter must preserve

1. ALC concept satisfiability without a TBox is not ALC knowledge-base consistency with an acyclic or general TBox. Schmidt-Schauß--Smolka's original calculus is a concept-description calculus; the later books separately treat TBoxes, ABoxes, blocking, and the complexity increase caused by general TBoxes ([Schmidt-Schauß--Smolka PDF](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf); [2017 complexity chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/an-introduction-to-description-logic/complexity/28FF8265D9C77A0886D75CADBB337A42)).
2. The polynomial result for EL++ with general concept and role inclusions does not transfer to arbitrary additions of negation, disjunction, number restrictions, or non-convex concrete domains ([Baader--Brandt--Lutz PDF](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/05/Papers/0372.pdf)). DL-Lite's first-order query rewriting is a different tractability mechanism and its paper separates TBox size, ABox/data size, and combined complexity ([Calvanese et al. PDF](https://www.inf.unibz.it/~calvanese/papers/calv-etal-JAR-2007.pdf)).
3. EL++, DL-Lite, SHOIQ, and SROIQ are source-family names, while OWL 2 EL, QL, RL, and OWL 2 DL are W3C language/profile specifications with their own grammar and restrictions. The W3C explicitly describes the profiles as expressivity/efficiency trade-offs and says the three profiles are not syntactic subsets of one another ([OWL 2 Profiles, introduction and §2](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-profiles/)). The SROIQ paper presents the description logic underlying OWL 2 DL, while the W3C profile document gives the normative OWL boundaries ([Horrocks--Kutz--Sattler 2006 PDF](https://cdn.aaai.org/KR/2006/KR06-009.pdf); [OWL 2 Profiles](https://www.w3.org/TR/owl2-profiles/)).
4. Kamp-style first-order/temporal expressiveness, Pnueli-style program specification, GPSS expressiveness and proof systems, and Sistla--Clarke decision complexity are four different presentations. No one result should be narrated as if it supplied the others.
5. Finite words, infinite words, finite trees, and infinite trees have different automata, acceptance conditions, and monadic second-order structures. Büchi and Elgot give distinct finite-word correspondences; Trakhtenbrot reaches a finite-word bridge inside a broader theory of arbitrary predicates and finite operators; Büchi 1962 and McNaughton concern infinite words; Thatcher--Wright and TATA concern finite trees; Rabin 1969 concerns infinite trees. Thomas 1997 surveys several of these, but does not erase the separation among their original sources ([Thomas preprint](https://www.labri.fr/perso/anca/Logic/thomas96_handbook.pdf); [TATA PDF](https://inria.hal.science/hal-03367725/file/tata.pdf); [Rabin PDF](https://lara.epfl.ch/w/_media/sav08/rabin69s2s.pdf)).
6. CTL is a state-formula branching-time logic with paired path quantifiers and temporal operators; CTL* distinguishes state and path formulae and permits freer mixing. A CTL bottom-up labeling algorithm is not by itself a CTL* model-checking algorithm ([Baier--Katoen PDF, ch. 6](https://www.labri.fr/perso/anca/Verif/Baier-Katoen.pdf); [Emerson--Halpern publisher record](https://research.ibm.com/publications/sometimes-and-not-never-revisited-on-branching-versus-linear-time-temporal-logic)).
7. Reduced ordered binary decision diagrams, symbolic model checking, bounded model checking, and counterexample-guided abstraction refinement are distinct mechanisms. Canonicity is conditional on reduction and a fixed variable order; symbolic encodings can still blow up; failure to find a bounded counterexample is not an unbounded proof without a completeness bound; and an abstract counterexample can be spurious ([Bryant PDF](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~bryant/pubdir/ieeetc86.pdf); [Burch et al. PDF](https://mcmil.net/pubs/IC92.pdf); [Biere et al. PDF](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~emc/15-820A/reading/biere99symbolic.pdf); [Clarke et al. PDF](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~emc/papers/Papers%20In%20Refereed%20Journals/Counterexample-guided%20abstraction%20refinement.pdf)).
8. Kozen's 1983 restricted-calculus decision/completeness results, Emerson--Jutla's 1991 automata equivalence and determinacy construction, and Bradfield--Stirling's 2007 synthesis of syntax, fixed-point semantics, games, automata, and alternation are separate presentations. In particular, the simple-minded, Emerson--Lei, and Niwiński alternation measures are not interchangeable ([Kozen author record](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~kozen/Papers/papers_by_year.htm); [Emerson--Jutla PDF](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs6860/2021sp/Handouts/EmersonJutla91.pdf); [Bradfield--Stirling accepted manuscript](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/16483207/Modal_Mu_Calculi.pdf)).

### `N-c-010.a` Franz Baader, Sebastian Brandt, and Carsten Lutz (2005)

**Identity, access, and locator.** Franz Baader, Sebastian Brandt, and Carsten Lutz, “Pushing the EL Envelope,” *Proceedings of IJCAI-05*, pp. 364--369, [official IJCAI PDF](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/05/Papers/0372.pdf). **Body acquired.** Operative locations: §2 and Table 1 for EL++ syntax/semantics and CBoxes; §3 and Table 2 for normalization and completion; Lemmas 2--3 and Theorem 4 for the polynomial result; §4 and Theorems 6--12 for intractable extensions and qualifications.

**Source notation, hypotheses, and model class.** EL is based on concept names, top, conjunction, and existential role restriction. EL++ adds bottom, nominals, concrete-domain predicates, general concept inclusions, and role inclusions including certain role compositions. A CBox is the finite set of general concept and role inclusions. Interpretations have a nonempty domain, concepts as subsets, and roles as binary relations; subsumption asks whether one concept's extension is included in another's in every model of the CBox ([source §2 and Table 1](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/05/Papers/0372.pdf)). For concrete domains (D_1,\ldots,D_n), Theorem 4 assumes each is *p-admissible*: the source requires polynomial-time satisfiability/implication and convexity ([source §3](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/05/Papers/0372.pdf)).

**Result and proof boundary.** Normalization reduces inclusions to rule-ready forms. Table 2 saturates mappings (S(C)) and (R(r)); Lemma 2 bounds the number and cost of rule applications polynomially; Lemma 3 characterizes computed subsumption between concept names; Theorem 4 concludes polynomial-time subsumption for EL++ with p-admissible concrete domains ([source §3](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/05/Papers/0372.pdf)). Soundness is explained by invariants saying entries in (S) and (R) denote valid subsumptions; completeness constructs a countermodel when the completion omits the queried relation. The six-page conference paper presents that core argument but explicitly sends some details to a technical report. The handbook may give a complete proof outline through normalization, invariants, polynomial saturation, countermodel, and reduction back to arbitrary concepts; it must not claim the IJCAI text contains every omitted rule case.

**Examples, counter-boundaries, and qualifications.** Section 4 is essential, not optional context. Theorems 6 and 7 make general-TBox reasoning EXPTIME-complete after adding atomic negation/full negation or disjunction; Theorem 8 does so for at-least-two restrictions; Theorem 9 gives EXPTIME-hardness for non-convex concrete domains; Theorem 11 treats at-most-one; and Theorem 12 shows FL0 subsumption with general TBoxes is EXPTIME-complete. For inverse roles the paper gives a PSPACE lower bound and an EXPTIME upper bound and says the exact complexity was open ([source §4](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/05/Papers/0372.pdf)). These are counterexamples to “small syntactic extension preserves tractability.”

The journal article supplies the central chase, homomorphism, rewriting, and complexity arguments, but Definition 5 omits the saturated chase case, Lemma 28 leaves unused fresh symbols unmapped, Lemma 39 contains several type/index slips, Theorem 41 refers rather than prints its inconsistent-KB FO augmentation, Theorems 46--47 are transfers, and Theorem 48 imports its hardness route from reference [19]. ### `N-c-018.c` Julian Bradfield and Colin Stirling (2007)

**Identity, access, and locator.** Julian Bradfield and Colin Stirling, “Modal Mu-Calculi,” in Patrick Blackburn, Johan van Benthem, and Frank Wolter, eds., *Handbook of Modal Logic*, Elsevier, 2007, pp. 721--756, DOI [10.1016/S1570-2464(07)80015-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1570-2464(07)80015-2); [University of Edinburgh publication record](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/modal-mu-calculi) and [accepted-manuscript PDF](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/16483207/Modal_Mu_Calculi.pdf). **Body acquired.** Locators below use the accepted manuscript's internal pages/sections, whose internal p. 1 begins the chapter text rather than published p. 721: §§3.1--3.4, internal pp. 5--8; examples §3.5, pp. 8--10; bisimulation/automata/games §§4.2--4.4, pp. 13--16; alternating parity automata §5.3, pp. 18--20; axioms/completeness §6, pp. 20--21; alternation §7, pp. 21--23; and monadic second-order logic §8.1, pp. 23--24.

**Fixed-point basis, exact syntax, and semantics.** On the complete lattice \(\mathcal P(S)\), a monotone \(f\) has a least fixed point equal to the intersection of its pre-fixed points and a greatest fixed point equal to the union of its post-fixed points; §3.2 also presents ordinal approximants and notes the transfinite bound ([source §§3.1--3.2](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/16483207/Modal_Mu_Calculi.pdf)). In the parsimonious grammar of §3.3, `Var` is an infinite variable set, `Prop` a proposition set, and \(L\) a label set; atomic propositions and variables, conjunction, negation, box modality, and \(\nu Z.\varphi\) are primitive, with every free occurrence of \(Z\) positive. Disjunction, diamond, and \(\mu Z.\varphi\) are then defined by De Morgan/fixed-point duality, and positive normal form restricts negation to atomic propositions while making bound variables distinct ([source §3.3, internal p. 7](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/16483207/Modal_Mu_Calculi.pdf)). Section 3.4 evaluates formulas over a labeled transition system \((S,{\to},V_{Prop})\) relative to a variable environment \(V:\mathrm{Var}\to\mathcal P(S)\); the \(\nu\) clause is the union of post-fixed points and the derived \(\mu\) clause is the intersection of pre-fixed points ([source §3.4, internal p. 8](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/16483207/Modal_Mu_Calculi.pdf)).

**Examples and semantic proof architecture.** Section 3.5 gives concrete readings including \(\nu Z.(P\land[a]Z)\) for invariantly \(P\) along \(a\)-behavior, \(\nu Z.(Q\lor(P\land[a]Z))\) for a “while” form, \(\mu Y.(P\lor(Q\land\langle a\rangle Y))\) for a reach/until form, and an alternating formula expressing that \(P\) occurs only finitely often on every path ([source §3.5](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/16483207/Modal_Mu_Calculi.pdf)). Theorem 3.1 says a well-founded pre-model is a model, while Theorem 3.2 supplies the converse annotated-model direction. These are the chapter's fundamental semantic proof principles, not merely informal unfolding slogans ([source internal pp. 9--11](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/16483207/Modal_Mu_Calculi.pdf)).

**Bisimulation, automata, and games.** Theorem 4.2 gives bisimulation invariance of \(L_\mu\), while Theorem 4.3 records the image-finite modal converse in the surrounding Hennessy--Milner discussion; Proposition 4.4 supplies a tree-model reduction ([source §4.2](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/16483207/Modal_Mu_Calculi.pdf)). Theorem 4.5 states the tree-automata expressive correspondence. In the model-checking game, positions pair a transition-system state with a subformula; Theorem 4.6 states \(s\models\varphi\) iff Player \(\exists\) has a history-free winning strategy. For finite structures the game becomes a finite parity game, and §4.4's literal convention makes the **least** priority recurring infinitely often even ([source §§4.3--4.4](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/16483207/Modal_Mu_Calculi.pdf)). Section 5.3 defines modal alternating parity automata; Theorem 5.2 translates between each modal automaton and a closed \(L_\mu\) formula, Theorem 5.3 gives a restricted automaton form, and Proposition 5.4 gives automaton normal form ([source §5.3](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/16483207/Modal_Mu_Calculi.pdf)).

**The three alternation definitions must remain exact.** Section 7 first defines Niwiński's positive-form classes. \(\Sigma^{N\mu}_0=\Pi^{N\mu}_0\) consists of formulas with no fixed-point operators. To form \(\Sigma^{N\mu}_{n+1}\) (dually \(\Pi^{N\mu}_{n+1}\)), take \(\Sigma^{N\mu}_n\cup\Pi^{N\mu}_n\), close under Boolean and modal formation, permit \(\mu\)-binding for \(\Sigma\) (dually \(\nu\)-binding for \(\Pi\)), and permit substitution \(\varphi(\psi)\) within the same class provided no free variable of \(\psi\) is captured by a fixed-point operator in \(\varphi\). The **simple-minded** classes \(\Sigma^{S\mu}_n\) omit this substitution clause altogether. The Emerson--Lei classes \(\Sigma^{EL\mu}_n\) retain substitution only when \(\psi\) is closed ([source §7, internal pp. 21--22](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/16483207/Modal_Mu_Calculi.pdf)). Symmetric alternation depth is the least \(n\) such that the formula belongs to both \(\Sigma^{\mu}_{n+1}\) and \(\Pi^{\mu}_{n+1}\). These are syntactic statement families/metalinguistic classifications of formulas; none is a semantic property name or a new kind of model.

**Separating examples among the definitions.** For the chapter's “always eventually” formula

\[
\nu Y.\bigl((\mu Z.(P\lor\langle a\rangle Z))\land\langle a\rangle Y\bigr),
\]

the source gives membership in \(\Pi^{S\mu}_2\) but not \(\Sigma^{S\mu}_2\), hence simple-minded depth 2; under Emerson--Lei and Niwiński it has depth 1 because the substituted eventuality subformula is closed. The “infinitely often” formula

\[
\nu Y.\mu Z.\bigl((P\lor\langle a\rangle Z)\land\langle a\rangle Y\bigr)
\]

belongs to \(\Sigma^\mu_2\) but not \(\Pi^\mu_2\) under all three definitions, hence has depth 2. Finally, the formula

\[
\mu X.\nu Y.\bigl([-]Y\land\mu Z.[-](X\lor Z)\bigr)
\]

belongs to \(\Sigma^{S\mu}_3\setminus\Pi^{S\mu}_3\) and \(\Sigma^{EL\mu}_3\setminus\Pi^{EL\mu}_3\), but belongs to \(\Sigma^{N\mu}_2\), because the inner \(\mu Z\)-formula does not depend on the intervening \(Y\) and Niwiński's capture-safe substitution clause exposes that dependency fact ([source §7 examples, internal pp. 21--22](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/16483207/Modal_Mu_Calculi.pdf)). These examples are required: without them, the three definitions look like redundant notational variants.

**Theorem 7.1 and its exact distinction.** Theorem 7.1 states: for every \(n\), there is a formula \(\varphi\in\Sigma^{\mu}_n\) not equivalent to any \(\Pi^{\mu}_n\) formula. The following paragraph specifies that Bradfield established this for the **Niwiński** hierarchy, which implies the result for the other two notions, whereas Lenzi independently established a slightly weaker theorem for the **Emerson--Lei** hierarchy ([source Theorem 7.1 and following paragraph, internal pp. 22--23](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/16483207/Modal_Mu_Calculi.pdf)). The chapter gives a proof outline, not the full hierarchy proof: first show strictness of an arithmetic fixed-point alternation hierarchy by coding and diagonalization; next translate between arithmetic fixed-point formulas and formulas evaluated on recursively presented transition systems while preserving alternation level; finally use the finite-model property to transfer the separation from the constructed infinite system to finite models. It also displays explicit hard formulas and notes separate binary-tree strictness results ([source §7 proof discussion](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/16483207/Modal_Mu_Calculi.pdf)). The handbook must not rewrite Theorem 7.1 as three independently proved identical theorems or call Lenzi's result the proof of Bradfield's stronger Niwiński statement.
