## Countability and nontriviality

The definition permits finite or countably infinite algebras under “countable,” but excludes the one-element algebra in the uniqueness and Cantor correspondence theorems. This is necessary and sufficient: the one-element algebra is vacuously atomless, while every finite nontrivial Boolean algebra has a minimal nonzero element and hence an atom. Thus the algebras enumerated in the uniqueness proof really are countably infinite.

Subalgebras share their ambient constants. Consequently a finite subalgebra of a nontrivial algebra is nontrivial, and its atoms form a nonempty finite partition of 1. No trivial finite subalgebra can invalidate the extension argument.

## Finite decomposition and splitting

The proof that finite Boolean elements are uniquely joins of atoms is correct: distinct atoms have zero meet, and a nonzero remainder below an element would contain an additional atom. A bijection between finite atom partitions therefore extends uniquely to an isomorphism.

The Boolean-cell argument proves that a subalgebra generated by m elements has at most 2^(2^m) elements. Zero cells are discarded and the remaining cells partition 1. The empty-generator case is covered by the empty meet 1 and the subalgebra {0,1}.

An atomless target can partition any nonzero element into every prescribed positive finite number of nonzero pieces: splitting d by 0<e<d gives the disjoint nonzero pieces e and d∧¬e. The refined atoms of F' below each atom p of F form a positive finite partition. Matching them with a partition of h(p) yields a well-defined isomorphism on all joins and preserves the original isomorphism on F. The proof also verifies that G is contained in the enlarged target G'. Only finitely many choices are needed for the finite extension theorem.

## Cylinders, compactness, and the direct limit

The initial-segment cylinders are nonempty, partition K at each finite level, are clopen, and refine arbitrary finite coordinate restrictions. They form a basis and separate different sequences. The all-zero sequence witnesses nonemptiness.

The compactness argument works for an arbitrary open cover in ZF. If a cylinder is not finitely coverable, one of its two child cylinders has the same property. The rule preferring child 0 when possible defines the path by ordinary recursion, without choosing arbitrarily at countably many stages. An open cover member containing the resulting path contains an initial-segment cylinder, contradicting its badness.

For a clopen V, its contained cylinders together with K\V cover K. A finite subcover gives a finite cylinder union for V; refining to a common length puts it into one finite algebra B_n. The empty clopen is handled by the empty union. Conversely every B_n element is clopen.

The size |B_n|=2^(2^n) follows because the 2^n cylinders are disjoint and nonempty. Every cylinder splits into two nonempty child cylinders, so the inclusions are strict. Explicit finite descriptions of levels and subsets enumerate their union without invoking a general countable-union-of-finite-sets choice principle. The same split supplies a strict nonzero element below every nonzero union element, proving atomlessness.

**The direct-limit assertion is fully present and proved.** The body states that the union of the B_n is the direct limit of the inclusion sequence. Annex `annex:p10.atomless.3`, in its final paragraphs, verifies the universal property for every compatible family of Boolean homomorphisms g_n:B_n→D. Their union is well defined; every finite tuple is contained in one level, so the union preserves operations; and every element belongs to a level, forcing uniqueness. Both the concrete finite stages and the universal mapping assertion are retained.

## Cantor point map and the BPIT boundary

For the chosen algebra isomorphism α:B→B_cyl, the map H(x) consists of elements whose represented clopen contains x. Point evaluation gives a Boolean homomorphism to 2, so H(x) is an ultrafilter.

For any ultrafilter U of B, its transport along α contains exactly one cylinder at every level. These uniquely determined cylinders must intersect at consecutive levels and therefore define compatible words and a unique infinite sequence. At a fixed level, a union of cylinders belongs to the ultrafilter exactly when it contains the selected cylinder; equivalently, it contains that sequence. This proves surjectivity of H, while cylinder separation proves injectivity. The displayed preimage identity proves continuity, and the images of cylinders are basic opens, proving openness and the homeomorphism.

This proof neither assumes arbitrary ultrafilter existence nor uses a global choice function: the selected word at each level is unique. Thus the direct Cantor correspondence for the countable algebras is also valid in ZF, as the revised body now states.

For arbitrary B, the atom-to-point direction constructs the principal ultrafilter U_p directly and proves that its basic clopen is a singleton. Conversely an isolated point has a singleton basic neighborhood represented by some b≠0. If b were splittable, BPIT would extend the principal filters of the two nonzero pieces to distinct ultrafilters in that same neighborhood, a contradiction. This is exactly the additional principle stated in the theorem. The finite cardinality and discreteness assertions follow from the finite atom decomposition. The trivial algebra has neither atoms nor ultrafilters, so the atomless/no-isolated-points equivalence also covers the empty Stone space.
