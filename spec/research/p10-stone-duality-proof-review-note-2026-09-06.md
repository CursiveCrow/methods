## 1. Filters, dichotomy, and separation

Allow the trivial Boolean algebra. Define a filter by `1 in F`, binary-meet closure, and upward closure. Define *proper* explicitly by `0 notin F`. The generated filter is

`<S> = {b : meet E <= b for some finite E subset S}`,

with the empty meet equal to 1. It is proper iff every finite meet of elements of S is nonzero. This includes S empty: the filter `{1}` is proper precisely in a nontrivial algebra.

If U is a maximal proper filter and a is absent, adjoining a generates the improper filter. The finite-meet description supplies u in U with `u meet a = 0`; hence `u <= not a` and `not a in U`. Both a and its complement cannot belong to a proper filter. Conversely, dichotomy makes every strict filter extension improper. Deduce `a join b in U` iff a or b belongs to U, using complements and meet closure. These steps use no choice.

BPIT then extends any proper filter to an ultrafilter. More precisely, if a is not in F, the filter generated by `F union {not a}` is proper: otherwise some u in F satisfies `u meet not a = 0`, so `u <= a` and a belongs to F. Extending this filter yields U containing F and omitting a.

For order separation, if `a not<= b`, then `a meet not b != 0`; extend its principal filter. The resulting U contains a and omits b. Thus ultrafilters separate distinct elements, and also detect nonzero elements. No family of ultrafilters is chosen simultaneously.

## 2. Basis, separation, and compactness of S(B)

Let `S(B)=Ult(B)` and `hat a={U:a in U}`. Dichotomy gives

`hat 0=empty`, `hat 1=S(B)`, `hat(a meet b)=hat a intersect hat b`,
`hat(not a)=S(B) minus hat a`, and `hat(a join b)=hat a union hat b`.

Consequently the hats themselves form a basis, not merely a subbasis, and every basis member is clopen. If U and W differ, an element in their symmetric difference and its complement give disjoint basic neighborhoods. Hence S(B) is Hausdorff with a clopen basis.

For an arbitrary set of open sets O covering S(B), use the entire subordinate basic refinement

`J={a in B : hat a subset O for some O in the cover}`.

This covers S(B) by the definition of a basis. Do not select one basic neighborhood and one parent open set separately for every point. Suppose the original cover has no finite subcover. Then no finite family of hats indexed by J can cover: finitely many associated parent open sets can be selected in ZF. In particular, for every finite E subset J, `meet{not a:a in E} != 0`; a zero meet would give `join E=1` and a finite basic cover. BPIT extends the filter generated by all these complements. Its ultrafilter belongs to no hat a with a in J, contradicting the refinement's coverage. This proves compactness for arbitrary open covers using BPIT and only finite choice.

Handle B trivial first, or note separately that S(B) is empty and has the empty finite subcover. The empty family in the finite-intersection property must use the convention that its intersection is the whole ambient space.

## 3. Representation of the Boolean algebra

The map `eta_B:B -> Clop(S(B))`, `a -> hat a`, preserves the Boolean operations by the basis laws. Separation proves injectivity. For a clopen K, its closedness in compact S(B) makes it compact. Its basic cover by all hats contained in K therefore has a finite subcover, so `K=hat(a_1 join ... join a_n)`. For K empty use the empty join 0. This proves surjectivity.

When B is trivial, S(B) is empty and Clop(empty) is the one-element Boolean algebra `{empty}`. The representation map is the unique isomorphism. Do not exclude this case by silently requiring Stone spaces nonempty.

## 4. Recovering a Stone space

Define Stone spaces as compact Hausdorff spaces with a clopen basis, including the empty space. Let `C(X)=Clop(X)` and

`epsilon_X(x)={K in C(X):x in K}`.

This is an ultrafilter by the point-membership laws. Distinct points are separated by a clopen neighborhood, so epsilon is injective. For any ultrafilter U on C(X), its clopens form a closed family with the finite-intersection property: a finite intersection belongs to U and cannot be empty. Compactness gives a point x in their total intersection. Then U is contained in epsilon(x), and maximality gives equality. Clopen separation makes x unique. The unique-point assertion defines the inverse function without choosing one point from each of many non-singleton sets.

For each clopen K,

`epsilon_X^(-1)(hat K)=K` and `epsilon_X[K]=hat K`.

The first identity gives continuity and the second, with the clopen basis, openness. Thus epsilon is a homeomorphism. If X is empty, C(X) is trivial and has no ultrafilters; epsilon is the unique empty-to-empty homeomorphism. A purported ultrafilter cannot create an empty-space exception to the finite-intersection argument.

## 5. Contravariant functors and naturality

For a Boolean homomorphism `h:B -> D`, define

`S(h):S(D) -> S(B)`, `U -> h^(-1)[U]`.

The preimage is a proper filter, and preservation of complements gives dichotomy. It is continuous because

`S(h)^(-1)[hat b] = hat(h(b))`.

For a continuous map `f:X -> Y`, define

`C(f):C(Y) -> C(X)`, `K -> f^(-1)[K]`.

Continuity preserves open preimages and closed preimages, hence clopens, and inverse images preserve all Boolean operations. Both constructions preserve identities and reverse composition. Their natural double-dual isomorphisms satisfy

`C(S(h)) o eta_B = eta_D o h`,

`S(C(f)) o epsilon_X = epsilon_Y o f`.

Each follows by elementwise membership. These formulas, with the proved isomorphisms, establish the claimed contravariant equivalence. They cover maps involving the empty Stone space and the trivial Boolean algebra as well: there is no homomorphism from the trivial Boolean algebra to a nontrivial one preserving both constants, just as there is no map from a nonempty space to the empty space.

If explicit triangle/coherence identities are wanted, the correct orientations are

`S(eta_B) o epsilon_{S(B)} = id_{S(B)}`,

`C(epsilon_X) o eta_{C(X)} = id_{C(X)}`.

For the first, the resulting preimage ultrafilter is `{b:U in hat b}=U`; for the second, the resulting clopen is `epsilon_X^(-1)[hat K]=K`. No further adjunction machinery is needed.

## Failure cases to guard against

- Requiring all Stone spaces nonempty while allowing trivial Boolean algebras breaks the stated duality.
- A nonempty-FIP convention used without handling X empty can conceal an invalid compactness argument.
- Passing from arbitrary covers to a pointwise chosen basic refinement may import more choice than BPIT; the full subordinate refinement above avoids this.
- Defining a “proper filter” only by closure omits its essential exclusion of 0.
- The existing corrected CPL countermodel uses the right principal filter, `upward(not[phi])`. Keep that direction.
- The nearby “unique countable atomless Boolean algebra” sentence should specify *countably infinite* (or nontrivial): under the usual no-atoms condition the trivial Boolean algebra is vacuously atomless too. The present proof plan does not otherwise revisit that separate classification.
