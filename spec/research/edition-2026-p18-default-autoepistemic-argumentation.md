## Direct scope corrections and completed details

The attack equation is given with an explicitly involutive normalized contrary. Double negations are removed in sentence notation, so an argument concluding the contrary of j attacks a support containing j. This prevents silently using double-negation cancellation inside a raw syntactic membership test.

The admissible-support proof retains the finite union of supports and supplies the missing argument that a contradiction attacks an actual member of the admissible set. It also proves that flattening every complete extension is classically closed, which justifies the consistency/membership equivalences in the reverse stable correspondence.

The syntax explicitly permits zero justifications and therefore states the exact consistency condition using C(empty), the closure under justification-free rules. With at least one justification per default this reduces to Th(W), recovering the source statement. The counterexample top:/false shows why the unconditional-closure condition cannot be replaced by background consistency in the enlarged syntax. The text also keeps Dung's background-consistency usage distinct from Reiter's separate terminology for existence of a consistent extension.

Individual consistency of each justification is preserved. The example top:p,not p/r has extension Th(r) even though its justifications are jointly inconsistent; they are licenses, not ordinary premises.

The source's top:not p/p with W={q} is calculated completely: no Reiter/stable extension, but a unique preferred/grounded argument set of empty-support arguments with flattening Th(q). Absence of the stable family is not described as a derivation of not q.

The direct modal rule L alpha AND all not L not beta -> gamma yields exactly the explicitly defined weak extensions. Its general Reiter-to-expansion direction is proved by the generating-default identity. For prerequisite-free defaults, the weak and Reiter operators coincide, giving the full three-way bijection with Dung's stable argument extensions, including inconsistent theories. For p:p/p, two ordinary expansions but only one Reiter extension prove the failure of the unrestricted direct bijection.
