### Status ceiling, variable sharing, gamma, and decision boundary

- `\mathbf E` and `\mathbf R` have the variable-sharing property: if
  `\derives_{\mathbf E}A\to B` or
  `\derives_{\mathbf R}A\to B`, then `A` and `B` share a propositional
  variable.  Status: `cited=AndersonBelnap1975`.
- `\mathbf{RM}` does **not** have that property.  The standard counterexample
  is `\neg(p\to p)\to(q\to q)`.  Its safe replacement is the weak statement:
  if `A\to B` is an RM theorem, then either `A` and `B` share a variable, or
  both `\neg A` and `B` are RM theorems.  Status:
  `cited=DunnRestall2002`.  A-087 must say "no" rather than leaving the cell
  blank.
- Ackermann gamma is exactly the theoremhood metarule: from
  `\derives A` and `\derives \neg A\lor B`, infer `\derives B`.  Meyer--Dunn
  prove it admissible for E and R.  It is not primitive, not ordinary modus
  ponens, and not a rule from arbitrary assumptions.  The corresponding
  universal claim fails for RM theories.  Status: `cited=MeyerDunn1969`.
- Full propositional `\(\mathbf E\)` and `\(\mathbf R\)` are undecidable,
  while their pure implicational fragments `\(\mathbf E_{\to}\)` and
  `\(\mathbf R_{\to}\)` are decidable.  Status:
  `cited=Urquhart1984,DunnRestall2002`.
- RM is decidable, and the standard survey reports a finite-model-property
  result for RM.  Status: `cited=DunnRestall2002,Avron2016`.  This is a
  distinct claim; Urquhart's E/R undecidability theorem neither proves nor
  refutes it.

A-087.  It does not prove any of them inside the handbook.
