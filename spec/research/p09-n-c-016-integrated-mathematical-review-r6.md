## Must-fix 2 — serial \(R\)-generability is not equivalent to only the three path-closure properties

**Location:** `handbook/parts/p09/presentations/n-c-016.tex`, lines 1016--1024, especially lines 1016--1019.

The text defines \(R\)-generability using a **total** relation \(R\subseteq S\times S\), i.e. a serial relation on all of \(S\), and then says that for infinite paths this is equivalent to suffix, fusion, and limit closure. Under the stated types that equivalence is false without state completeness.

A finite counterexample is enough. Let
\[
 S=\{a,b\},\qquad X=\{a^\omega\}.
\]
Then \(X\) is suffix closed, fusion closed, and limit closed. But no total relation on \(S\) has exactly \(X\) as its infinite paths: totality supplies a successor at \(b\), and because \(S\) is finite it supplies an infinite \(R\)-path beginning at \(b\), which is not in \(X\). Equivalently, every path set generated by a serial relation on all of \(S\) is state complete. The definition of state completeness on line 1020 is therefore not optional in the displayed equivalence.

**Minimal repair:** move the line-1020 definition of state completeness before the equivalence and revise lines 1018--1019 to say that, for sets of infinite paths, \(R\)-generability is equivalent to the conjunction of suffix closure, fusion closure, limit closure, **and state completeness**. Because the controlling note reports only the three-closure wording, identify the added conjunct as the reader-facing mathematical qualification forced by the presentation's use of a total relation. If “total” was not intended to mean serial on all of \(S\), the alternative repair would have to retype that definition explicitly; the current definition and current equivalence cannot both remain.

## Must-fix 3 — the host labelling method uses its input formula before typing it

**Location:** `handbook/parts/p09/logic-in-computer-science.tex`, lines 974--1005, especially line 979.

The method introduces fixed-point notation and then instructs the reader to process the subformulas of \(A\), but its own method environment never says that \(A\) is a CTL formula or explicitly gives the finite total transition structure on which the displayed \(S\), \(V\), and predecessor maps depend. **Minimal repair:** begin the host method with a sentence such as: “Given a finite total transition structure \(\mathcal K=(S,R,V)\) and a CTL formula \(A\), use the predecessor maps of Definition~\(\ref{def:p09-ctl}\) and process the subformulas of \(A\) bottom-up.” Retain the existing fixed-point typing and all displayed clauses.
