## Full source argument and corrected formal scope

The body preserves negativeκ, positiveβ and signedτ; threshold belief;
conditional finite-support restrictions; every value of all three Tweety
tables; material implication versus conditional belief; subject-relative
reasons and the open objective-reason issue; finite firmness, contraction and

measurement qualification, AI knowledge/belief ambiguity, default entailment.

Corrected qualifications occur before the strong active claims:

1. **InfiniteW:** binary minimum preservation alone does not ensure the
   source's nonempty full belief intersection. FiniteW or an attained
   world-minimum representation does. The cofinite-filter counterexample
   satisfies binary minima and finite consistency but its full intersection
   is empty. No unqualified false strong-closure claim is left active.
2. **Conditional-consistency converse:** normalization and conditional
   negation at finite-rank conditions do not alone imply binary minima.
   Zero singleton/infinite doubleton ranks on three worlds give a counterexample.
   The proved converse includes subset antitonicity.
3. **Firmness vsincrement:** Tweety's second update subtracts1 on penguin
   worlds and adds1 on other worlds, changingτ(P) from-1 to+1. The target
   firmness is1; it is not an unspecified1-unit signed-rank increment.
   The two-cell formula is uniquely forced by target cell ranks plus preserved
   within-cell conditionals, as the local proof shows.
4. **Support/contraction:** two-sided updates require both cells finite.
   Hard conditioning makes the complement impossible. Equalizing cell ranks
   is contraction of a previously held belief, not vacuous contraction of a
   proposition already disbelieved.
5. **Static possibility:** integer ranks map to a dyadic grid; general
   real possibilities do not all lie on it. The transform matches4.7's
   corrected Pi(A)=2^-κ(A), N(A)=1-Pi(notA). Rank arithmetic corresponds to
   multiplicative conditioning, not every ordinal possibility update.
6. **Probability bridge:** a finite regular epsilon-family proves a limiting
   exponent illustration. It is editorial, not the source's printed formula
   or one unique ordinary probability assigned to a rank.
