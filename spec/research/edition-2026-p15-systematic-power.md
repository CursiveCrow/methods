## Exact mathematics and editorial supplements

The finite ground vocabulary includes its zero-atom case. State
descriptions D_v and minimal clauses M_v are Boolean complements,
up to logical equivalence. The optimal partition assigns to its
deduced component exactly the minimal clauses entailed by T alone.
The proof works for an arbitrary sentence T, including quantified
or inconsistent T. Applying the singular uniform formula to a
quantified T requires the explicitly constructed ground projection.

The original one-name example scores 1/3. The three equivalent
four-name groupings have different naive sentence-count scores
(1/3, 0, 1/4), but the same normalized-content score 175/252 = 25/36.
The annex supplies each countermodel and every state count.

The general range assumptions are finitely additive. The chapter
derives the complement and inclusion-exclusion laws before defining
conditional probability and systematic power. Their denominators
and domains are distinct. All eight identities of 9.12 are proved,
including zero-inner-denominator cases where a product expression
would otherwise be undefined. The formal duality of 9.13 includes
truth constants, expanded implications, numerical operations,
definedness conditions and reversal of entailment hypotheses.

Editorial examples establish the roles of zero-weight states, prior
weights, additional names and recoding. A specified two-point latent
mixture gives 5/8 where uniform weights give 1/2; this is not claimed
to be Carnap's separately defined measure. Fresh unconstrained atoms
preserve the uniform score for singular T and S. Additional constants
can change the score of a quantified theory because new ground
instances add constraints. These are different operations.

Systematic power is not empirical truth, entropy, posterior theory
probability or the Part III explanation criterion. In particular
contradiction and self-systematization can obtain score one. The
finite procedure does not claim to decide general first-order
satisfiability.
