## Separation and representation

In `annex:p10.stone.1`, the expression F[a] is exactly the filter generated by F together with a. Its use proves the complement dichotomy from maximality and the converse without choice. Finite-join primeness follows, including the empty join. If b∉F, the properness of F[¬b] is proved correctly; BPIT then supplies the desired ultrafilter extending F while excluding b. Nonzero elements and unequal elements are separated as stated.

In `annex:p10.stone.2`, the five basic-set identities hold and prove the clopen basis and Hausdorff separation. The arbitrary-cover compactness proof forms all subordinate basic opens as a set rather than selecting a neighborhood independently at every point. A finite join equal to 1 would give a finite subcover using only finite selection of containing cover members. Therefore complements have nonzero finite meets, their displayed generated filter is proper, and a BPIT extension contradicts the cover. There is no hidden infinite choice or circular use of compactness.

The representation map preserves the Boolean operations and is injective by the preceding separation lemma. Compactness is then correctly used to express each clopen as a finite union of basic clopens. The empty clopen is represented by 0. The trivial-algebra case is additionally checked directly, with both Boolean algebras having one element and the dual space empty.

## Space reconstruction

In `annex:p10.stone.3`, clopen neighborhoods of a point form an ultrafilter. Conversely, any ultrafilter on Clop(X) is a family of closed sets with the finite-intersection property. Compactness gives a point in their intersection, and the complement criterion proves that the ultrafilter consists of precisely that point's clopen neighborhoods. Clopen separation proves uniqueness.

The equations ε_X⁻¹[Ĉ]=C and ε_X[C]=Ĉ are valid after bijectivity has been established. They prove both continuity and openness using the respective clopen bases. Thus the inverse is continuous directly; no additional separation theorem or choice principle is needed. When X is empty there is no ultrafilter on its trivial clopen algebra, and the empty map is the asserted homeomorphism. In fact this reconstruction argument itself works in ZF for a space already satisfying the Stone-space hypotheses.

## Typed maps and naturality

Every displayed composition in the body and annex has the correct domain and codomain. For h:B→C, the inverse-image map X(h):X_C→X_B sends ultrafilters to ultrafilters and satisfies X(h)⁻¹[â]=h(a)^. For g:X→Y, Clop(g):Clop(Y)→Clop(X) preserves all Boolean operations, including both constants. Inverse-image composition gives the claimed contravariance.

The first naturality equation

\[
\operatorname{Clop}(X(h))\circ\beta_B=\beta_C\circ h
\]

has type B→Clop(X_C), and the proof computes both sides on each a∈B. The second

\[
X(\operatorname{Clop}(g))\circ\varepsilon_X=\varepsilon_Y\circ g
\]

has type X→X_Clop(Y); the membership equivalences for each clopen D⊆Y prove equality of the two ultrafilters.

The inverse identities are also correctly typed:

\[
X(\beta_B)\circ\varepsilon_{X_B}=1_{X_B},
\qquad
\operatorname{Clop}(\varepsilon_X)\circ
\beta_{\operatorname{Clop}(X)}=1_{\operatorname{Clop}(X)}.
\]

The annex proves them by element membership and inverse-image evaluation, respectively. Together with the isomorphism/homeomorphism results and naturality, these are full data for the stated contravariant equivalence, not only an object-level correspondence.

For a continuous f:X_C→X_B, the recovered arrow

\[
h=\beta_C^{-1}\circ\operatorname{Clop}(f)\circ\beta_B
\]

has type B→C and is a Boolean homomorphism. The annex's membership calculation proves X(h)(U)=f(U) for every U∈X_C. If another arrow gives the same dual map, equality of the represented clopens and injectivity of β_C prove equality at every a∈B. Thus the exact unique-arrow claim at the end of `thm:p10-stone-duality` is proved.

These inverse-image formulas cover empty domains and trivial algebras. In particular, when C is trivial, X_C is empty and the unique Boolean arrow B→C agrees with the unique continuous map from that empty space. A nonexistent Boolean arrow from a trivial algebra to a nontrivial one is not introduced by any construction: the corresponding continuous map would have to map a nonempty Stone space into the empty space.

## Finite example

For finite S, the singleton atoms have finite join 1. The primeness argument forces an ultrafilter on P(S) to contain one singleton, and properness excludes two distinct singletons. The remaining membership characterization follows from the filter laws and complement criterion. The corresponding basic set is a singleton point, so the topology is discrete. The separately stated S=∅ case correctly gives a trivial algebra and empty dual space.
