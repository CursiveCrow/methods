## Mathematical boundary and source correction

The chapter itself promises no mathematical theorem or omitted formal
proof. The authored noisy-OR and four-variable benchmark results are
explicit editorial finite derivations; they require reciprocal annex
proofs and do not establish a cognitive algorithm. The2008 appendix
contains a printing inconsistency: A4 begins with P(d|c), but its expanded
right side uses absent-c factors. The reconstruction uses the correctly
indexed finite sum for c=1 and c=0 separately, agreeing with A1, A2-A3,
the intended graph, and the surrounding argument. This correction is
not silently ascribed to the source's literal A4. Figure1 prints0.93
for the both-causes conditional; the finite calibration uses that printed
value, not the exact0.9375 implied by two independent0.75 generators.
The source Table 2's theoretical values are integer-rounded frequencies
out of 40, not observed means or exact probability identities.
The chapter's p. 445 move from equal-and-opposite contact forces to
equal linguistic appropriateness is not adopted as a mathematical
implication: the body separates force magnitude from initial motion,
attention and conversational goals. No Newtonian equality is taken
as a proof about a person's preferred causal description.
