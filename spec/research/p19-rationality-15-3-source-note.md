## Corrections and evidence qualifications

- The stated screening inputs 1% prevalence, 90% sensitivity and 9% false
  positives imply `10/109`, approximately 9.17%; the source's 10% is retained
  as an approximation rather than labelled a materially false answer. The body
  supplies a 10000-person frequency construction and the annex calculation.
  The 21%/87% doctor result remains attributed to the chapter's review; no
  original medical study completion or clinical advice is implied.
- Validity does not guarantee true conclusions without true premises.
  Source discussion of dialectical change/contradiction is retained as an
  attributed cultural account, not a mathematical denial of consistency.
- The chapter's Wason1960 bibliography points to a conceptual hypothesis
  task, not the exact card-selection source. Its card illustration is treated
  as an illustrative logical task, not as a verified 1960 card experiment.
- The chapter's law-of-large-numbers and regression explanations are not
  promoted into unconditional finite-sample probability laws. The body
  states the sampling/population hypotheses needed for the teaching example.
- Morewedge's game superiority is not uniform: Experiment2 follow-up overall
  comparison is `p=.057`, anchoring shows no detected game advantage, and
  representativeness no detected advantage at follow-up. The game/video
  comparison varies duration, interaction and practice together; feedback's
  isolated effect is not identified. There is no untreated control.
- Morewedge Experiment2 prints 238 lab completers, 192 follow-up completers
  and 45 follow-up omissions; these differ by one when simply subtracted.
  The body retains the published totals and flags the reconciliation gap.
- Mellers' tracking group is selected on previous success, not randomly
  assigned from equivalent forecasters. Year2 includes returnees, new recruits,
  retained assignments and prior scenario-trained people in the nominal
  no-training group. It is not an independent clean replication of Year1.
- Correlated salary/GPA/risk outcomes are not causal training outcomes.
  Forecasting during continuing practice/feedback does not establish
  off-task retention without reinforcement or generalized life benefits.
