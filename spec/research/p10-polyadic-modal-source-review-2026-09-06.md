## Source normality and additivity

The held preprint p. 4 permits a set of modal symbols with arbitrary
finite arities, takes a countably infinite variable set by default, and
defines the induced unary diamond by placing top in every unused
coordinate. Page 6 gives theorem-level monotonicity, normality of those
induced diamonds, and the forward coordinate-additivity axiom, together
with modus ponens and uniform substitution.

The body correctly converts simultaneous monotonicity to its coordinate
form by reflexive implications in unchanged positions, and converts
coordinate monotonicity back by finite iteration. Its stronger-looking
normality axiom in an arbitrary context follows by moving the other
arguments upward to top and using the source's top-context zero law.
The converse is an immediate instance. Reverse additivity follows from
the two tautological inclusions into a disjunction.

For nullary symbols there is no coordinate to which these axioms apply.
Their interpretation as an arbitrary designated Boolean element matches
the BAO convention. No zero law is wrongly imposed.

## Exact unary compatibility

For one diamond, Boolean duality gives box-top and box-meet
preservation. Contraposition converts diamond monotonicity to box
monotonicity. Applying this to the conjunction form of modus ponens
proves the usual distribution axiom. For a theorem \(\chi\),
the equivalence \(\neg\chi\leftrightarrow\bot\), operator congruence,
and diamond normality prove \(\Box\chi\).

Conversely the unary box calculus gives box monotonicity by
necessitation and distribution, and box-meet preservation by its
already stated proof. Duality gives the diamond rules. This establishes
the equivalence of the theorem closures under the displayed dual
definitions. Both local proof conventions use premises, theorems,
and modus ponens, so the theorem-rule comparison does not introduce
premise-dependent necessitation or coordinate monotonicity.
