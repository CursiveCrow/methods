## Direct corrections and proof boundaries

1. **Printed299, first-column partial-Ramsey paragraph:** the source labels
   the resulting not-C cases as A&C once, while its following omitted-term
   expression is correctly P(A & not C). The manuscript consistently uses
   **A & not C** for the opposing cases. The source image confirms this is
   a printed slip, not an extraction-negation guess.
2. **Printed300, second-column model example:** “if A then C; not C;
   therefore not A” is labeled DA. Its form is **MT**. The manuscript
   identifies the four forms correctly and assigns that inverse-negative
   probability to MT. The source image was inspected.
3. **Printed300, small-e implication:** high MP/small e alone does not
   mathematically force high MT without a base-rate qualification.
   The new complete derivation gives MT=1-ae/(1-b) and the strictly positive
   counterexample a=.5,e=.01,b=.994, with masses .495,.005,.499,.001.
   MP=.99 but MT=1/6. The chapter's empirical fitted-model critique stays
   attributed and is not denied by this mathematical correction.
4. The missing-link example needs its joint-probability assumption:
   independence of an irrelevant antecedent and a likely consequent gives
   high conditional probability; high P(C) alone does not. The earlier
   injured-run argument already demonstrates that distinction.
5. The local fixed-event proof is not promoted to every Lewis triviality
   formulation or to the claim that every conditional lacks semantics.
   The Equation's source assessment, a material event, a primitive
   conditional event and a conditional random quantity keep separate types.
6. The source's conclusion that purely Bayesian accounts lack a rational
   conditional-premise update is presented as the authors' criticism of
   the examined models, not a universal impossibility theorem. Their own
   discussion of parameter adjustment/KL/network options and the separate
   Chater-Oaksford4.5 response are preserved.
