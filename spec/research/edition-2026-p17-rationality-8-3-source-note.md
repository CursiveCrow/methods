## Precision corrections governing the reader treatment

- Concave power utility is the chapter's chosen EU implementation, not a defining axiom of every expected-utility model. Its power formula is used only for nonnegative consequences. General vNM preferences need not be risk averse; the existing P17 theorem supplies the exact concavity condition.
- Rank-dependent CPT weights sum to one for a strictly one-sign prospect with total probability one. They need not do so across a mixed gain/loss prospect; 1992 printed 301 states this explicitly. The chapter's unqualified normalization wording is narrowed directly in the exposition.
- Loss multiplier lambda gives a constant equal-magnitude loss/gain value ratio only when the two exponents agree. Otherwise the ratio depends on magnitude. Neither lambda nor empirical loss aversion is inferred from one accept/reject choice.
- Nonlinear weighting and curvature interact. The fourfold pattern is a characteristic pattern, not a theorem from merely stating that small probabilities are overweighted. The local binary comparison will state its exact inequalities.
- The binomial note's n=10, p=.2 arithmetic is checked explicitly. Binomial skewness does not by itself establish that underrepresentation is more likely for every small sample; a stipulated n=4 countercase makes the limitation exact.
- Source applications to insurance, equity premia, endowment, stock disposition and horse betting are explanatory illustrations, not personal financial recommendations or proof that the named mechanism uniquely explains the field data.
