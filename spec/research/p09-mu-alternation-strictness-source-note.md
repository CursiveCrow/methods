# Alternation strictness: exact classes and two primary proof routes

Date: 2026-09-06.

## Outcome and recommended proof boundary

There are two concrete routes, not just theorem statements:

1. **Bradfield's elementary arithmetic route:** the 1999 truth-predicate
   construction and diagonal argument, together with the 1998 paper's
   modal/arithmetic translations and its explicit treatment of
   capture-avoiding substitution. This reaches the Niwinski classes.
   It avoids using Lubarsky's deep hierarchy theorem as a black box,
   although ordinary effective syntax coding and pairing-normal-form
   lemmas still have to be supplied.
2. **Arnold's short tree-game diagonal:** a productive, contracting
   reduction from an alternating automaton to its acceptance-game tree,
   followed by a fixed-point contradiction. The central diagonal proof
   is short enough to reconstruct fully. However, applying it to the
   host's Niwinski syntax classes requires an **index-preserving**
   formula/automaton bridge. General expressive equivalence alone does
   not provide that bridge. The nonempty-transition convention also
   requires separate treatment of the first formula level.

The note gives the working proof interfaces and the elementary core
arguments below. It does not claim that the host already contains all
the required arithmetic coding lemmas or the Niwinski/index bridge.
Lenzi's weaker separation is not substituted for the target result.

## Exact target: Niwinski classes, not simple alternation

Use `n >= 1` for strictness. At level zero, both classes are the
fixed-point-free positive formulas, so separation at `n=0` is false.

The Niwinski classes are generated by

\[
 \Sigma^N_0=\Pi^N_0,
\]

and, at level `n+1`, by starting with `Sigma^N_n union Pi^N_n`,
closing under finite Boolean and modal constructors, allowing mu
binding for `Sigma` and nu binding for `Pi`, and closing under
substitution of same-class formulas for variables **without capture
of any free variable of the inserted formula**. The simple classes
omit the substitution rule; Emerson--Lei restricts its inserted
formula to be closed.

The needed semantic statement is

\[
 \forall n\geq1\ \exists\varphi_n\in\Sigma^N_n\ 
 \forall\psi\in\Pi^N_n\quad \varphi_n\not\equiv\psi.
                                                               \tag{N}
\]

B98 deliberately goes beyond its simple-hierarchy argument in
Section 3: Theorem 8/Corollary 9 handle the larger class of possible
Niwinski opponents. The construction can use a witness already in
the simple `Sigma_n` class while excluding every `Pi^N_n` formula.
That stronger combination also yields the simple and Emerson--Lei
separations. Merely exhibiting a syntactically deep formula, or
excluding only `Pi^S_n`/`Pi^EL_n` opponents, does not prove (N).

B&S states the Niwinski scope explicitly and identifies Lenzi's
independent result as weaker. Its phrase "for every n" must be read
with the positive-level condition above.

## Route I: elementary arithmetic diagonalization

### I.1. Fix the coding universe before writing a universal evaluator

Use a fixed effectively presented arithmetic syntax with total
recursive term evaluation and decidable primitive-atom evaluation,
for example a finite ordinary arithmetic signature. Recursive
function abbreviations used in metamathematical displays may be
expanded into first-order definitions before assigning formula codes.
Their use does not add a fixed-point alternation.

This supplied precision is important: a total recursive evaluator for
arbitrary codes of **all total recursive functions** cannot simply
be postulated. B99's use of recursive functions as convenient
primitives must not be turned into that invalid universal-evaluation
assumption. Likewise, malformed codes must receive an explicit
default result so that syntactic extraction functions are total.

Code formulas, finite individual-variable assignments and updates,
and pair components effectively. The formulas to be evaluated have
no free set variables. Pair-normalization and alpha-renaming put
their bound set variables into a fixed list `X_n,...,X_1`, with
matching bound individual variables `x_n,...,x_1`. Shorter prefixes
can be padded by vacuous binders when needed.

### I.2. The first-order truth operator has a unique fixed point

On pairs `(code(theta),assignment)`, define a positive one-step
operator `E(W)` by the following schematic clauses:

\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
 \theta & (\ulcorner\theta\urcorner,a)\in E(W)\ \text{iff}\\ \hline
 P(\tau) & P(\operatorname{eval}(\tau,a))\\
 \theta_1\land\theta_2
   & (\ulcorner\theta_1\urcorner,a),(\ulcorner\theta_2\urcorner,a)\in W\\
 \theta_1\lor\theta_2
   & (\ulcorner\theta_1\urcorner,a)\in W\ \text{or}\
                       (\ulcorner\theta_2\urcorner,a)\in W\\
 \exists z.\theta_1
   & \exists v\ (\ulcorner\theta_1\urcorner,a[v/z])\in W\\
 \forall z.\theta_1
   & \forall v\ (\ulcorner\theta_1\urcorner,a[v/z])\in W.
\end{array}                                                     \tag{A1}
\]

Negative arithmetic literals are handled by their decidable atomic
truth conditions. Standard syntactic tag predicates make (A1) one
first-order arithmetic formula positive in `W`.

Every recursive dependency strictly decreases the object formula's
syntax size. Induction on that size therefore proves that **every**
fixed point of `E` contains exactly the true well-formed first-order
formula/assignment pairs. Thus its least and greatest fixed points
agree on these pairs. Ill-formed pairs can be fixed false by the
definition. This gives the truth predicate `Sat_0` both as a
`Sigma_1` and as a `Pi_1` mu-arithmetic formula.

This argument does not say that arithmetic truth is recursive.
Universal-quantifier branching may be infinite. It is well-founded
dependency on finite formula syntax, not a terminating decision
algorithm for first-order arithmetic.

### I.3. Build truth for pair-normal level n

A pair-normal `Sigma_n` formula has the shape

\[
 \tau_n\in\mu X_n.
 \tau_{n-1}\in\nu X_{n-1}.\ \cdots\
 \tau_1\in\sigma_1 X_1.\theta,
                                                               \tag{A2}
\]

with first-order `theta`, including positive atoms `tau in X_i`.
Here `sigma_1` is mu when `n` is odd and nu when `n` is even.
Extend (A1) with

\[
 (\ulcorner\tau\in X_i\urcorner,a)\in E(W;X_1,\ldots,X_n)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
            \operatorname{eval}(\tau,a)\in X_i.                 \tag{A3}
\]

The object-level sets are represented by actual metalevel set
variables; they are not encoded as integers. Copy the fixed-point
prefix (A2) into the metalevel truth formula, using recursive syntax
extractors to obtain each `tau_i` and the body. Update the coded
individual assignment on entering each binding input `x_i`.
Use the mu or nu version of the first-order evaluator to match the
innermost sign `sigma_1`.

The resulting formula `Sat_n(code,assignment)` has the same `n`
alternating blocks. The extra evaluator binder has the innermost
sign and so adds no alternation. Induction from the innermost set
operator outward, uniformly in outer set values and individual
assignments, identifies every metalevel monotone operator with the
object-level operator. Their least/greatest fixed points therefore
coincide. This is the construction of B99 Theorem 3.2.

The class-preserving pair-normalization used here is a real lemma,
not just formatting. B98 Lemma 2 gives its constructor-by-constructor
pairing argument, including first-order quantifiers and adjacent
same-sign fixed points. It is one of the proof dependencies that a
self-contained host version must present.

### I.4. Diagonalize at the same level

Define

\[
 D_n(e):=\operatorname{Sat}_n(e,[e/z_0]).                         \tag{A4}
\]

It is `Sigma_n`. Suppose it had an equivalent `Pi_n` arithmetic
formula. By duality and pair-normalization, there would be a
pair-normal `Sigma_n` formula `theta(z_0)` equivalent to `not D_n`.
For `e=code(theta)`, truth coding gives

\[
 \theta(e)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \neg D_n(e)
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\
 \neg\operatorname{Sat}_n(\ulcorner\theta\urcorner,[e/z_0])
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \neg\theta(e),
\]

a contradiction. This is B99 Lemma 3.1. The diagonal needs the
evaluator to cover the very same fixed effective syntax in which
`theta` is written; this is why I.1 is not optional bookkeeping.

### I.5. Transfer arithmetic truth into a recursive modal model

B98 Theorem 5 gives an explicit finite-signature recipe. For a fixed
arithmetic formula, states are finite tuples of integers, with a
register for each individual term. There are deterministic term-
evaluation/copy transitions, arbitrary-value transitions for
individual quantifiers, and recursively interpreted atomic tests.
Translate arithmetic Booleans to modal Booleans, universal and
existential individual quantifiers to boxes and diamonds, and each
arithmetic fixed point to the same modal fixed-point sign.

The induction invariant identifies an arithmetic set variable `X`
with the cylinder of register states whose `x` register belongs to
`X`. Induction on the fixed-point approximants, including limit
unions/intersections, proves correspondence of the arithmetic and
modal operators. The fixed-point structure is preserved.

Applied to (A4), this gives a closed modal witness `Phi_n` in the
simple `Sigma_n` class, a recursively presented system `T_n`, and
a recursive input encoding `j` with

\[
 e\in D_n\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                  T_n,j(e)\models\Phi_n.                       \tag{A5}
\]

Atomic tests may be regarded as proposition letters with recursive
valuations. They are not free fixed-point variables in the host's
two-sorted proposition/variable convention.

B99 Sections 4--5 specialize this generic recipe to small displayed
`MuSat_n` and parity-game witnesses. The parity-game display in
Section 5 assumes odd `n` and uses **greatest** recurring priority;
do not transplant it as an all-n, least-recurring formula without
the parity/index conversion. The general truth-predicate route
above already handles both parities of `n`.

### I.6. The essential Niwinski upper bound

For any modal formula on a recursively presented system, its semantic
clauses translate into arithmetic: atoms use recursive predicates,
modalities use first-order quantifiers over the recursive edge
relation, and fixed points retain their signs. B98 Theorem 4 first
proves the simple-class version.

For a `Pi^N_n` opponent, one must additionally use the dual of
**B98 Theorem 8/Corollary 9**. The translated arithmetic formula is
"nice": its set terms have no external free individual parameters.
Theorem 8 folds same-level capture-avoiding substitutions into one
same-sign fixed point by tagged pairing. Its key form is

\[
 W=\{(0,u):u\in X\}\cup\{(1,u):u\in Y\}.                     \tag{A6}
\]

Membership in each component is replaced by the corresponding tagged
membership in `W`. The substitution side condition ensures that the
inserted `Y` computation has no captured dependency on a bound set
variable of the context. Niceness prevents hidden individual-variable
dependencies when moving that computation. Same-sign product
fixed-point elimination then justifies the fusion. The lower-level
bodies remain in the appropriate class, so the enclosing block adds
no alternation. This establishes the arithmetic simple-class upper
bound for Niwinski modal formulas, not merely simple modal formulas.

If a modal `Psi in Pi^N_n` were equivalent to `Phi_n`, its denotation
on the specific recursive `T_n`, restricted along the recursive map
`j`, would define `D_n` by a `Pi_n` arithmetic formula. This contradicts
I.4. Together I.1--I.6 prove (N), provided the stated coding and
pairing-normal-form interfaces are supplied. No use of Lubarsky's
strictness theorem is needed in this combined route.

## Route II: the short direct tree-game diagonal

### II.1. The automaton classes and their limited identification

Use full binary trees over

\[
 A_n=\{c_1,d_1,\ldots,c_n,d_n\}.
\]

At a `c_i` node the universal player chooses a child; at a `d_i`
node the existential player chooses. Let `W_n` be the language where
the existential player wins with least-recurring-even index.
An alternating automaton has ranks in `{1,...,n}` and positive
Boolean transitions over `(next control,direction)` pairs.

A99 uses nonempty disjunctions of nonempty conjunctions, so all plays
are infinite. For `n >= 2`, accepting and rejecting constants can be
represented within the range using even-rank-2 and odd-rank-1 sinks.
At `n=1` this does not work: with only rank 1 and no terminals every
infinite play loses under the even convention. Therefore that
nonempty-transition rank-one class is not automatically the host's
`Sigma^N_1` class, which contains `top` and other fixed-point-free
formulas. Use a separate first-level argument or a separately
verified terminal-aware convention.

### II.2. Construct the reduction, not just its name

For an automaton `A`, control `q`, and input tree `t=a(t_1,t_2)`,
form `G_(A,q)(t)` by reading the finite binary syntax tree of
`delta(q,a)`:

- replace every conjunction node by `c_(rank(q))`;
- replace every disjunction node by `d_(rank(q))`;
- replace each atom `(q',i)` by `G_(A,q')(t_i)`.

First replace each transition `delta(q,a)` by
`delta(q,a) or delta(q,a)`. This does not change acceptance, and
makes the output root `d_(rank(q))` independent of the input root
letter. Every recursive call is beneath that new root.

Local Boolean game correspondence then gives

\[
 t\in L(A,q)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                    G_{A,q}(t)\in W_n.                         \tag{T1}
\]

An infinite branch consists of finite transition-syntax blocks;
each block repeats the current control's rank. It therefore has the
same least infinitely recurring rank as its automaton-control play.
This proves the parity part of (T1), in addition to its ownership
and child-direction clauses. It is A99 Proposition 2.1.

### II.3. A self-contained fixed-point argument

Equip full binary trees with the prefix metric: the distance between
unequal trees is `2^(-k)`, where `k` is their first differing depth.
Padding above makes

\[
 d(G_{A,q}(t),G_{A,q}(u))\leq\tfrac12 d(t,u).                  \tag{T2}
\]

Indeed, agreement of input prefixes of depth `k` implies agreement
of all output prefixes of depth `k+1`, by induction through the
finite transition trees and their recursively attached subtrees.
Uniformity over the finitely many controls is used in that induction.
Without the input-independent added root, nonexpansiveness would
not alone give (T2).

When the input alphabet is `A_n`, start with any tree `t_0` and put
`t_(k+1)=G_(A,q)(t_k)`. Equation (T2) makes each finite prefix
eventually constant. These stable labels define a full binary tree
`t_*`. The same prefix estimate shows `G_(A,q)(t_*)=t_*`.
Uniqueness follows because two fixed trees would agree at every
finite depth. This supplies the fixed point used by A99 without
requiring a separate analytic Banach theorem.

### II.4. Diagonalize against a same-index complement automaton

Suppose an automaton `A` of this same rank type recognized the
complement of `W_n`. Its fixed tree would satisfy

\[
 t_*\notin W_n
 \Longleftrightarrow t_*\in L(A,q)
 \Longleftrightarrow G_{A,q}(t_*)\in W_n
 \Longleftrightarrow t_*\in W_n,
\]

a contradiction. Together with automaton duality, this is A99
Theorem 3.3. The proof core is complete once the rank-class and
constant conventions of II.1 are fixed.

To conclude (N), one still needs both:

1. a witness formula for `W_n` in the required positive Niwinski
   class; and
2. a translation of **every candidate opposite Niwinski-class
   formula** into the same-index automaton class.

The existing general formula/automaton equivalence, if it uses
priorities based only on raw binder depth or permits a larger
index after epsilon elimination, does not establish item 2. A
proof by the class-generation rules must treat capture-avoiding
substitution and index-preserving guarding. A99 cites this
correspondence in its introduction rather than proving it.
This note does not silently identify its rank classes with the
host's `Sigma^N_n,Pi^N_n`.

## Transfer to finite models: exact quantifier order

Once a closed witness `Phi_n` is inequivalent over all models to
every `Psi in Pi^N_n`, fix any one such `Psi`. Their symmetric
difference

\[
 (\Phi_n\land\neg\Psi)\lor(\neg\Phi_n\land\Psi)
\]

is satisfiable. The full finite-model property supplies a finite
pointed model where they differ. Therefore

\[
 \forall\Psi\in\Pi^N_n\ \exists\text{ finite }(M_\Psi,s_\Psi)
 \quad M_\Psi,s_\Psi\models\Phi_n\mathbin{\triangle}\Psi.
\]

This is the finite-model transfer used in B98 Theorem 7 and B&S's
Section 7 discussion. It requires neither a finite original
arithmetic coding system nor a finite direct diagonal tree. It
does not assert one fixed finite model or one uniform finite size
bound separating the witness from all opponents simultaneously.

## Handoff and proof-cost comparison

The arithmetic route has an explicitly documented Niwinski step:
its extra cost is the fixed effective coding package, pair-normal
form, and the same-sign tagged fusion. The short tree route makes
the diagonal step much smaller, but moves that cost into the
index-preserving syntax bridge and first-level treatment. Neither
route may replace the Niwinski target by a simple or Emerson--Lei
target unnoticed.
