## Necessary correction to the live Part 10 semantics

The current `handbook/parts/p10/algebraic-and-categorical-logic.tex`, lines 39–50 (`def:p10-equational-logic`), defines consequence using an algebra **and valuation satisfying the premises**, but then allows unrestricted substitution. These describe different consequence relations.

Use the source's identity consequence:

\[
A\models s\approx t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{for every valuation }v:X\to A,
\ s^A[v]=t^A[v],
\]
\[
A\models\Gamma\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(\forall\gamma\in\Gamma)\ A\models\gamma,
\qquad
\Gamma\models_{\mathrm{eq}}s\approx t
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(\forall A)(A\models\Gamma\Rightarrow A\models s\approx t).
\]

The current alternative instead puts the implication *inside* the valuation quantifier. Its incompatibility with unrestricted substitution already appears in the empty operation signature: from the premise `x≈y`, substitution derives `z≈y`. In a two-element algebra, a valuation with x=y=0 and z=1 satisfies the former and falsifies the latter. Under identity semantics, `x≈y` forces a one-element algebra, so the derived identity is valid.

Substitution soundness under the corrected semantics follows by composing the valuation with the term substitution: evaluate the substituted identity at v by evaluating the original identity at the assignment sending x to the value at v of the term substituted for x. This argument requires that the original identity be true under every assignment in the fixed algebra. For local premises, substituting a consequence requires also substituting the premise set; one cannot retain arbitrary local premises unchanged.

The finite deduction system in the current presentation can remain, with contextual replacement expressed equivalently by operation compatibility. The conclusion and every premise are equations in the same fixed signature and variable supply. Use a countably infinite supply of variables to avoid an empty term set and to supply all finite tuples of variables needed for finitary identities.

## Canonical completeness proof and finite support

Let θ be the least fully invariant congruence on T(X) containing the premise pairs. Full invariance means preservation by every term-algebra endomorphism, equivalently by simultaneous substitution. A quotient by an arbitrary congruence containing the premise pairs would only make the premises true at the canonical assignment; full invariance is what makes them identities under all assignments.

The source's II Theorem 14.12 and Lemma 14.7 establish that T(X)/θ satisfies Γ and that it satisfies an identity s≈t exactly when (s,t) belongs to θ. If the pair is absent, the canonical valuation already separates its two quotient terms. Reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, compatibility/replacement, and substitution generate exactly θ; II Theorem 14.19 converts this into the desired finite formal proof. Consequently every semantic consequence has a finite subset of Γ supporting it, because its finite deduction uses only finitely many premises. Arbitrary premise sets cause no additional finite-support hypothesis.

## Free algebras and the empty-generator boundary

For a nonempty variety V and **X union the set of nullary symbols nonempty**, the source constructs F_V(X) as a quotient of the nonempty term algebra. It belongs to V and has a canonical map η:X→F_V(X) with the property that every map f:X→A, A∈V, has a unique homomorphic extension h satisfying hη=f. Stating this with η avoids treating distinct formal generators as distinct elements in a trivial variety. The image η[X] generates the free algebra.

The live free-algebra paragraph (lines 63–66) should not assert this term-generated construction without the stated existence proviso. If X is empty and there are no nullary symbols, the term set is empty and is not an algebra under the manuscript's nonempty-carrier convention. A general existence assertion is false: in the empty signature, the category of nonempty sets has no initial object, because every nonempty candidate has at least two distinct constant maps to a two-element set.

Do not strengthen this example to a claim that every constant-free variety lacks an initial object. Particular varieties can have one; the term-generated construction and a bare categorical initial-object assertion must be distinguished. If nullary symbols exist, the empty-generator construction applies and gives the initial algebra, as the source's remark after Definition 10.9 states.

## HSP incorporation convention

The source uses nonempty products and separately assumes the class called a variety is nonempty. The manuscript instead explicitly includes the empty product, whose algebra has one element. These conventions give the same HSP characterization once stated consistently: closure under the manuscript's empty product forces the class to contain the trivial algebra. Isomorphic copies are covered by H, since an isomorphism is a surjective homomorphism. Keep the nonempty-carrier convention throughout.

For variety generation from an arbitrary class K, either use the manuscript's products including the empty product or state K nonempty when citing the source's nonempty-product HSP expression. If K is empty, the equational class determined by all its identities is the trivial variety, whereas HSP(K) formed using only nonempty products is empty. II Theorem 11.9 is the direct primary-source anchor for the manuscript's identity/HSP equivalence.
