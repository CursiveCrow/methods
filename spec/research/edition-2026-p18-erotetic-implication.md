## Source-sensitive proof boundaries

The two clauses of implication are soundness transmission and a nonempty
proper source-answer-set reduction for every target answer. General implication
is not transitive; the second leg's regular implication licenses composition.
The proof packet retains the actual source definition rather than turning
questions into declarative material implications or asserting a PMCE extension.

The 2018 conjunction reduction displays nested answer prefixes, but its printed
CQ1-conjunction hypothesis licenses adding a conjunct only to a question which
is not already added-conjunct. Repeating that hypothesis on intermediate
added-conjunct questions is not justified by its literal statement. The reader
must separately state the existence of the exact nested transformed questions,
or use the stronger uniform conjunction closure supplied by its explicit finite
question language. A one-bundled-conjunct variant can use the weaker hypothesis
on the original non-added-conjunct questions. No unproved general syntax closure
will be imported merely from the semantic associativity of conjunction.

The syntax-boundary proof makes the conjunction qualification concrete. Its
