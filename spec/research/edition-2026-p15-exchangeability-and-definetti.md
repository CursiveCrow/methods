## Source fidelity, corrections and extensions

The canonical sequence law is specified before the invariant fields.
Finite-vector exchangeability is proved to preserve the full product
measure. The original-space statements are pulled back through the
coordinate map; no regular conditional probability on every arbitrary
original sample space is inferred.

The source's bounded symmetrization deletion uses an absolute norm
bound. Deleting a fixed initial block gives a tail-measurable version
of the limit. This is not asserted as literal membership of a chosen
conditional-expectation version in every raw tail field.
The independent-projection lemma includes the L2 truncation needed
for unbounded pullout.

The ballot source does not assume a finite first moment. The proof
therefore conditions on each positive-probability final integer sum
m < N before applying the bounded reverse stopping theorem. All
coordinates are then bounded by m. It retains the source's threshold
crossing argument, the integer and nonnegative conditions and the
strict-lead election interpretation. The same proof supplies the
explicit finite-exchangeable extension.

The conditional-product theorem retains its scalar meaning on arbitrary
measurable coordinate spaces. The probability-valued mixture is proved
for standard Borel targets through the existing real coding and kernel
construction. All product-kernel measurability is supplied before use.
The evaluation sigma-field is proved to have countable determining
generators. Equality of invariant, tail and directing information is
stated modulo ambient measurable null events, not as a raw equality.

The empirical strong law under only conditional integrability uses the
actual joint kernel measure and a measurable convergence event. It does
not intersect an uncountable family of parameter-dependent null sets.
The full-law mixture and its uniqueness are supplied constructions
beyond the source's brief regular-law remark.

The finite-urn proof gives event-supremum total variation at most
k(k-1)/(2N), including endpoint populations and only k <= N.
This completes the argument described in Durrett's paragraph.
It is not presented as the full or sharp quantitative theorem of the
unacquired Diaconis–Freedman original.

The source Lp exercise is given its finite-p range and an explicit
bounded counterexample for infinity. The second-moment exercise uses
an L2 clipping limit; the unbounded pairwise statistic uses the
integrable conditional-symmetrization identity. All five exercises
are supplied at their precise finite/infinite sequence scopes.
