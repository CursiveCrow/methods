## Frozen two-source corpus

The corpus was fixed before drafting:

1. Roger N. Shepard and Jacqueline Metzler, Mental Rotation of
   Three-Dimensional Objects, Science 171(3972), 19 February 1971,
   701–703; DOI 10.1126/science.171.3972.701.
2. Yangqing Xu and Steven L. Franconeri, Capacity for Visual Features
   in Mental Rotation, Psychological Science 26(8), 1241–1251;
   DOI 10.1177/0956797615585002.

The second paper supplies a task/strategy contrast: feature-part
binding during cued object rotation, needle rotation and scaling,
followed by eye tracking. It is not a direct replication of the
original shape-identity response-time task. All of its Experiments
1A, 1B, 1C and 2 and both brief footnote controls are included.
This component does not establish E5 completion or a systematic
review of the imagery debate.

## Evidence treatment and reporting limits

The 1971 record preserves the sample, full stimulus/trial design,
instructions, timing, error exclusion, plane/depth comparison,
all reported polynomial-test outcomes, figure error-bar meaning,
different-pair response time and tentative pure/mixed decomposition.
No recruitment facts, raw plot coordinates or new confidence
intervals are invented.

The 2015 record preserves all four numbered experiments, every
reported principal mean/SE/CI/test, trial and participant counts,
exclusions, task controls and the retrospective top-part comparisons.
The two footnote controls retain their unreported sample sizes.
The printed eta-squared label is not relabelled partial eta-squared.
The original figures were read alongside the text:

- Figure 1A is an illustrative organic-chemistry comparison, not
  an additional experiment. Figure 1B gives the trial sequences;
  1C gives capacity summaries; 1D separates task-condition hit rates.
- Figure 2 supplies the two-part task, quadrant selections,
  normalized eye trajectories and the correlation plot.

Exact reporting boundaries are retained in the manuscript:

- The primary cites a K estimator but does not print its algebra or
  participant response records. Published K values are reported,
  not reconstructed from rounded hit rates.
- Experiment 1C Methods specifies a 2400-ms interval; Fig1B has
  a shared three-interval label. The exact 144-trial replication
  schedule is not supplied.
- Experiment 2 supplies no separate trial/block count. Rounded
  eye-analysis percentages do not supply exact observation counts.
- Figure 1D separates task conditions, whereas the text supplies
  one top/non-top mean pair per experiment; these are not labelled
  as rotation-only cells.
- Figure 2D labels performance d-prime, but does not specify its
  calculation or extreme-rate convention.
- Cross-experiment controls and retrospective analyses are not
  described as preregistered within-participant causal comparisons.

Exact reproduction of K and d-prime would require the cited
estimator specification, original response records and analysis
conventions. These are not supplied by this two-source record.
The ideal swap-counting theorem is expressly an editorial model,
not a replacement attributed to the source. The discussion's
depth-rotation, expertise and educational suggestions are not
promoted to results of the four experiments.
