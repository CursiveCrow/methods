### Verbatim passage: colligation, II.IV.1, printed pp.59-60

```text
This part of the formation of our
knowledge I have called the _Colligation of Facts_: and we may apply
this term to every case in which, by an act of the intellect, we
establish a precise connexion among the phenomena which are
presented to our senses. The knowledge of such connexions,
accumulated and systematized, is Science.
```

The first quoted words follow the earlier paragraph's sentence about
facts being bound together by suitable conceptions. They are not a new
standalone source definition invented by this note.

### Verbatim passage: induction adds a mental element, II.V, Aphorism XV, printed p.71

```text
_An Induction is not the mere_ sum _of the Facts which are colligated.
The Facts are not only brought together, but seen in a new point of
view. A new mental Element is_ superinduced; _and a peculiar
constitution and discipline of mind are requisite in order to make
this Induction._
```

### Verbatim passage: invention and verification, II.V.3, printed p.75

```text
The Invention of the Conception was the great step in
the _discovery_; the Verification of the Proposition was the great

```

### Verbatim passage: definition and proposition, II.V.5, printed pp.77-78

```text
5. In collecting scientific truths by Induction, we often find (as
has already been observed) a Definition {78} and a Proposition
established at the same time,--introduced together, and mutually
dependent on each other. The combination of the two constitutes the
Inductive act; and we may consider the Definition as representing
the superinduced Conception, and the Proposition as exhibiting the
Colligation of Facts.
```

### Verbatim passage: predictive success with a false representation, II.V.10, printed p.86

```text
The truth and accuracy of
these predictions were a proof that the hypothesis was valuable,
and, at least to a great extent, true; although, as was afterwards
found, it involved a false representation of the structure of the
heavens.
```

### Verbatim passage: consilience's limiting condition and historical qualification, II.V.11, printed p.90

```text
No example can be pointed out, in the whole history of science, so
far as I am aware, in which this Consilience of Inductions has given
testimony in favour of an hypothesis afterwards discovered to be
false. If we take one class of facts only, knowing the law which
they follow, we may construct an hypothesis, or perhaps several,
which may represent them: and as new circumstances are discovered,
we may often adjust the hypothesis so as to correspond to these
also. But when the hypothesis, of itself and without adjustment for
the purpose, gives us the rule and reason of a class of facts not
contemplated in its construction, we have a criterion of its
reality, which has never yet been produced in favour of falsehood.
```

### Locator and dependency guide for the author

- II.III.1-3, pp.50-54: apparent pure observation cannot exclude mind-supplied
  Ideas; juggler/ventriloquist, inscription, wind-blown oak, windmill, diagrams,
  lunar apparent size and counting illustrate perception/inference dependence.
  The source's proposed response is distinct, rigorously applied Ideas, not
  elimination of all conceptual contribution. II.III.4-11, pp.54-58, supply
  observation rules, decomposition, terms/classification and the weather/crystal
  cases showing that precise elementary observations are not sufficient.
- II.IV.1-5, pp.59-63: facts are bound by conceptions; periods/distances through
  squares/cubes/proportionality and then central force are the opening examples.
  Common observation, controlled observation/experiment, decomposition and
  clear appropriate Ideas remain connected. Number/weight/measure is not his
  exhaustive restriction: polarization and elementary composition are examples.
- II.IV.6-9, pp.63-66: inventive sagacity, guessing and tentative hypotheses;
  Archimedes/pressure, Pascal/weight of air, lens/refraction, Kepler's nineteen
  Mars hypotheses, Galileo, Mariotte, Snell and Dalton are Whewell's examples.
  New conceptions need not be slight changes in an old hypothesis. No mechanical
  discovery rule is supplied by this historical account.
- II.IV.10-12, pp.66-69: a hypothesis can contain dispensable apparatus:
  polarized particles, caloric fluid, gravitational ether and atoms. Success
  requires comparing consequences with observed relations, not elegance alone.
  His epicyclical lunar places and musical-string/temperament examples belong
  to this testing discussion.
- II.V.1-5, pp.71-78: Ideas/Facts, colligation, invention and its subsequent
  disappearance from awareness; Kepler's third law, circles/epicycles, Newton's
  central force, inertial motion and momentum exemplify the added conception.
  The Aristotle/planet-ellipse syllogism distinguishes discovering the predicate
  from the evidence for extension to all planets; it is not a licensed modern
  deductive rule from three planets alone. §4 explains why a once-introduced
  conception subsequently appears merely factual. This edition's selected
  chapters do not name Mill or provide an explicit reply to Mill's text.
- II.V.6-8, pp.78-83: conjecture-generation and candid comparison/rejection;
  Kepler's willingness to discard costly guesses and Newton's moon-fall account
  illustrate this. The latter's sixteen/thirteen-feet narrative is Whewell's
  historical illustration, not a separately verified modern reconstruction.
- II.V.9, pp.83-85: useful but incomplete/false hypotheses: epicycles and
  nature's horror of a vacuum. This is an explicit limit on equating explanatory
  utility with complete literal truth.
- II.V.10, pp.85-87: prediction of unobserved same-kind cases. Epicycles, lenses,
  Newton's fits, undulations, phlogiston, oxygen and electromagnetic motions
  are all discussed; retain the false-structural-picture qualification above.
- II.V.11, pp.87-90: different-kind prediction/explanation and consilience;
  Kepler's laws, perturbations/precession, fringes/thin-plate colours,
  Huygens/Fresnel double refraction/planes of polarization, Brewster's angle,
  polarized Newton rings, dipolarized light, and wavelength measurements are
  the complete principal example families. The last contrast is one-class
  adjustable fitting versus a new class not contemplated in construction,
  without an adjustment made for that purpose. Retain his "so far as I am
  aware" historical qualifier; do not promote it into an infallibility theorem.
- II.V.12-13, pp.90-96: theory development towards simplification/coherence;
  epicycles, Cartesian vortices, universal gravitation, phlogiston/weight,
  wave versus emission optics, and Laplace's gas/latent-heat theory are the
  complete principal comparison families. Progressive simplicity means
  less need for disconnected new apparatus when scope expands, not merely
  the initially shortest formula. §13 joins this to consilience and successive
  generalization; it is a historical-philosophical claim, not an established
  modern universal theorem or an automatic confirmation calculus.
