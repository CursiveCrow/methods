## Exact empirical safeguards

Study 1 has 18 people, 39 generated paraphrases, 18 paraphrase categories
and 64 later selections. These are different units. Its mathematical
coding includes certainty and logicality; that source convention is
reported without assuming that these words establish coherent numbers.
The 7/39 and 56/64 summaries concern responses, not proportions of
participants. The full figures' category counts are supplied. The six
exclusive teller readings are out of 17 respondents from the same
Study 1 sample, with one missing answer.

Study 2 has 90 people: silent probability 21/24 reversals, probability
with thinking aloud 18/22, silent typicality-first 13/22, typicality-first
with thinking aloud 6/22. Protocol subgroups are nested within these
cells. Class-inclusion reasons and an asserted empty intersection are
distinguished; the latter imports a separate substantive premise.
The unexplained difference between combined and silent treatments is
retained rather than converted into a proven interaction mechanism.

Study 3 has 20 people and 55 paraphrases, 54 coded numerosity-related.
Its zero reversals is an observed sample outcome, not a zero population
parameter. The hypothetical 200 women are not study participants and
have no recorded outcomes establishing estimation accuracy.

Study 4 recruited 98 and has 97 complete Linda responses. Its exact
counts are 3/23, 5/25, 4/25, 16/24. One incomplete response belongs to
the first condition. The two manipulated cues do not exhaust every
possible inclusion cue. The design is not a complete factorial study
of all wording, content and response-format factors. Comparisons with
the Study 2 ranking group are explicitly across studies.

are 425/212 and medians 300/50; common-group means 282/457 and medians
150/250; 8/31 common-group respondents reverse inclusion. The source

## Direct mathematical clarifications and author corrections

The active eight-atom example defines E1,E2 as a partition with equal
probability. Both conditional four-cell rows sum to one. The context
therefore changes marginal values and the ordering of the nonnested
constituents while leaving all nested comparisons coherent. The source's
relevance-degree reply on p. 297 is retained. This demonstrates a logical
possibility, not a recovered participant interpretation.

The source's .041/.082/.016 alternative dice calculation is made exact
as 10/243, 20/243 and 4/243. Those are binomial counts in different fixed
five/six-roll blocks. They are not the probabilities of contiguous
strings occurring in twenty rolls; the latter complete calculation is
already supplied in P19.7. The active account follows the printed
betting instruction, which omits probability, rather than importing the
contrary phrase in the p. 294 discussion into the described instruction.

The social-choice example uses the precise contraction-consistency
condition for a nonempty choice correspondence on finite menus.
Its x,y,z alternatives are defined before the example. The changed
social consequence and its relevance to the model's alternatives are
explained without attributing a new general choice theorem to the
psychological study.
