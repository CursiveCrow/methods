## Argument and formal boundaries to preserve

The source’s two conceptions differ in evaluand and standard: participant
choices/efficiency versus group attitudes/consistency. The proposed convergence
uses the capacity to think and act as a team. Team identification is not derived
from equilibrium; treating identification as just another strategic action
recurs to the coordination question. Subpersonal framing alone also does not
settle reasoned role conflicts. The self-awareness conclusion is a philosophical
proposal with reasons, not an existence theorem or psychological law.

The majority cycle proves failure of one rule on one profile, not Arrow’s
universal-domain theorem. Source p595 labels unanimity preservation “Pareto
efficiency”; manuscript distinguishes the aggregation axiom from efficiency
of outcomes. Its “unique and complete ranking” description of unrestricted
domain mixes an input-domain and output condition; the bridge separates them.
The p596 general judgment-impossibility summary is explicitly rough and does
not supply a precise new theorem statement. Its formal support in the
manuscript is the existing separately typed List–Pettit result; it is not a
claim that all procedures or deliberation are impossible.

The source’s political-party narrative does not specify a numerical budget
model. The manuscript states an explicit unit-budget model in which tax
increase finances both increases and reduction in one spending category offsets
increase in the other. Under that premise the three individual rows are
feasible but the majority row is not. No factual fiscal claim is made.
Money-pump vulnerability requires willingness to pay, stable sequential
comparisons and liquidity. An ordinal cycle alone supplies none of those.
Consensus, coherent judgments and identification neither entail truth nor
ethical legitimacy. A joint judgment supplies, on the source’s proposal,
a pro tanto reason; assurance or contrary reasons can still matter.
