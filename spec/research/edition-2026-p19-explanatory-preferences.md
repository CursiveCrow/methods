## Corrections and exact interpretive boundaries

1. Source note 4 treats explanations inclusively. Hs=D1 and Hc=D2 intersect
   D3 overlap. For E=Hs union Hc, actual posterior denominators are p1+j-t.
   Normalizing by p1+j instead gives a two-label score, not generally the
   actual posterior probability of Hs. A P15 model-posterior interpretation
   needs a separately declared disjoint-label experiment and response rule.
2. Inclusive equal-prevalence probabilities p and p squared do not reverse
   at .5. Source note 2's threshold belongs instead to exclusive
   configurations under mutual independence. Both exact claims are proved.
3. A 50/50 split in the twenty-person more/less question, with no equal
   option, does not establish individual independence beliefs.
   Three complete finite laws show how identical marginals yield different
   explanatory comparisons.
4. Table 1 ratio labels are nominal. Exact arithmetic on displayed counts
   gives R=37500/(n2*n3), including 15/22 for the nominal 2:3 row.
   Experiment 3 does not supply all integer positive counts for its 75-case
   presentations; fractional counts obtained by dividing every earlier
   number by ten are not presented as observations.
5. The positive simple-choice orientation q=logistic(a+b log R) requires
   a=+1.306 for the source's .79 interpretation, while its constant is
   printed -1.306. The original signed coefficient is preserved and the
   reader's formula has an explicit orientation. Coding/design matrix and
   exact fitted ratio inputs are not printed. The same issue applies to
   the -1.164 sensitivity and -.77 Experiment 3 constants.
   No raw-data refit or unreported coding convention is invented.
6. The source prints chi-square(3)=7.26 with p<.01. The conventional upper
   tail for those exact inputs is approximately 0.064056576067.
   A full gamma/Gaussian-integral derivation and a strict analytic lower
   bound greater than .01 are supplied. The result does not identify
   whether the observed statistic, degrees of freedom, p field or actual
   analysis was wrong. No corrected participant table or statistic is invented.
7. Figures 1–2 have error bars without a specified meaning in their
   captions/text. They are not called SEM or confidence intervals.
   Unprinted coordinates are not converted into exact participant counts.
   All explicitly printed percentages and tests, including unfavorable or
   non-significant comparisons, are retained.
8. Explanation choice is not randomized in Experiment 3's prevalence
   subgroups. A finite countermodel produces the association from
   pre-choice estimates while choice has no effect on the later report.
9. A group logistic curve does not uniquely identify individual mental
   priors or probability matching: a proved threshold-population model
   generates the same curve.
