## Consequential precision findings

- Bias, error, heuristic, computational cost and ecological success are
  different properties. An error signature is not unique process evidence.
- Exact six-character strings have equal probability under independent
  fair Bernoulli sampling, not merely invariance of successive marginals.
  Equal probabilities do not imply equal observed family counts. The
  task's reported first count of 72 also conditions information about a
  finite sample and its unknown size.
- A stereotype likelihood comparison requires both likelihoods and base
  rates. A real-world assertion about farmer/librarian prevalences is not
  inferred from the hypothetical task or treated as a current population fact.
- A lens cue's ecological validity and its utilization weight are different.
  Combining weak cues need not monotonically improve performance without
  assumptions about dependence, orientation, weights and training.
- Recognition-based ranking, the existence of a less-is-more case and
  actual human recognition use require separate evidence.
- A percentage of majority-choice predictions is not a percentage of
  expected-value-maximizing choices, and maximizing expected monetary
  value is not a universal normative preference requirement.
- Missing unique population parameters or a feasible global optimization
  does not establish that there is no normative constraint at all. A
  source's proposal to emphasize adaptation must preserve coherence and
  decision-cost assumptions rather than erase them.
- Post hoc labels System 1/2 or adaptive-toolbox choices do not identify
  an independent mechanism. Identifiable predictions about search,
  stopping, choice and retrieval are required; no impossibility theorem
  follows merely from alternative architectures.
