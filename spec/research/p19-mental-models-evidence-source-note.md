### Source facts and interpretation boundaries

Methods 783/Table 1 at 784: eligibility required frequencies of the four
canonical selections; 228 experiments comprise 104 abstract, 44 everyday,
80 deontic. This is a literature database, not a single recruited sample.
Dependence analysis 785/Table 3 at 786 uses only 99 experiments reporting
all 16 selections. Each receives 10,000 independent-card simulations with
its sample size and observed marginal choice probabilities. Real/simulated
mean entropy (bits): abstract 2.33/2.60, everyday 2.43/2.75, deontic
1.57/1.80; all three Wilcoxon comparisons report p < .001.
Table 2 at 784 identifies this subset as 43 abstract, 30 everyday and
26 deontic experiments. The actual 786 table page was rendered and read;
its Table 3 and Table 4 figures agree with these recorded results.

Table 4 at 786 summarizes canonical selections: counterexample selections
19%/28%/64% for abstract/everyday/deontic. Content and abstract-instruction
rank tests report chi-square 54.6 and 62.3 respectively, df 2, p < .001.
These are not all-selection population prevalence or a common randomized
training effect. Some content/instruction studies had null effects (785-786).

Fitting 789/Table 6 at 791 compares three model parameters with a rival
pruned from ten to four, using canonical selections. The authors acknowledge
pruning objections and deny that superior single-task fit establishes general
theory superiority (789). They retain unexplained insight parameters (792).
Do not extend dependence from 99 to all 228, assert a uniquely identified
psychological mechanism, invent demographics, or replace missing uncertainty
with the source's argumentative claims.

and three groups: unaided in both blocks; instructed after the first block;
instructed before the first block. Untaught accuracy is approximately
two-thirds, with 24-second mean evaluations; instructed accuracy is 95%,
with 15-second means. The page does not report a participant count,
recruitment, random assignment, item count, tests, confidence intervals or
delayed transfer.
