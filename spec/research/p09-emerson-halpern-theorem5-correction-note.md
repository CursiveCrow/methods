# Emerson-Halpern Theorem 5: exact witness and model families

Date: 2026-09-06.

## Finding

Theorem 5's witness is

\[
W=A[F(P\land XP)],
\]

with ordinary eventuality `F`. The primary theorem, its invariant (1),
and its concluding contradiction all print this ordinary operator.
The infinitely-often operator in the comparison language remains
`widetilde F` in the handbook's notation. It must not be inserted into
the witness.

The descriptions of the two Figure 2 base chains are correct. With
ordinary `F`, the consecutive initial `P` states in `M_1` give the
required witness at time zero. With infinitely-often `F`, the formula
would fail on both model families, since every path eventually stays
at a state where `P` is false.

## Figure 2: complete base models

Use all infinite paths generated by the displayed edges.

For `M_1`, let

\[
S(M_1)=\{a_1,b_1,d_1\},\qquad
R(M_1)=\{(a_1,b_1),(b_1,d_1),(d_1,d_1)\},
\]

with

\[
L(a_1)=L(b_1)=\{P\},\qquad L(d_1)=\varnothing.
\]

For `N_1`, let

\[
S(N_1)=\{a_1,d_1\},\qquad
R(N_1)=\{(a_1,d_1),(d_1,d_1)\},
\]

with

\[
L(a_1)=\{P\},\qquad L(d_1)=\varnothing.
\]

State names here are local to their respective structures. The primary
prose on p. 161 explicitly says that `d_1` satisfies `not P` in both
models; that prose settles the valuation independently of the dot style
in the scan. A valuation over a larger proposition alphabet can assign
every other atom false uniformly throughout the family.

The root path words are respectively

\[
M_1:\quad P\,P\,(\neg P)^\omega,
\qquad
N_1:\quad P\,(\neg P)^\omega.
\]

At index zero on the `M_1` path, both `P` and `XP` hold. Since ordinary
`F` permits index zero, its root satisfies `W`. On the `N_1` path the
only `P` state is followed by `not P`, and all later states fail `P`.
No suffix satisfies `P and XP`, so its root fails `W`.

No self-loop at `a_1` or `b_1`, and no extra edge bypassing `b_1`, is
drawn in `M_1`. The sole self-loop in either base model is at `d_1`.

## Figure 3: exact successor construction

All component copies are disjoint, retain their internal edges and
valuations, and have no new outgoing edges to other components. Write
`m_i^0,m_i^1` for the roots of two copies `M_i^0,M_i^1` of `M_i`, and
`n_i^0,n_i^1` for the corresponding roots of copies of `N_i`.
The source denotes the direct copies by primes. The superscripts here
only distinguish component occurrences unambiguously.

In `M_{i+1}`, take new states `a,b`, components `M_i^0,M_i^1,N_i^1`,
and exactly the new edges

\[
a\longrightarrow b,\qquad
a\longrightarrow m_i^1,\qquad
a\longrightarrow n_i^1,\qquad
b\longrightarrow m_i^0.
\tag{M}
\]

In `N_{i+1}`, take new states `a,b`, components `N_i^0,M_i^1,N_i^1`,
and exactly the new edges

\[
a\longrightarrow b,\qquad
a\longrightarrow m_i^1,\qquad
a\longrightarrow n_i^1,\qquad
b\longrightarrow n_i^0.
\tag{N}
\]

In both new structures, `a` is the designated root `a_{i+1}` and
satisfies `P`; `b=b_{i+1}` satisfies `not P`. All component roots
`m_i^0,m_i^1,n_i^0,n_i^1` satisfy `P` by the family construction.
Thus both new roots have three successors; the difference is the
unprimed component reached after the intervening `not P` state.

Every path in either new graph enters a lower-level component in one
or two steps. Induction from Figure 2 therefore shows that every path
eventually remains in a `not P` terminal self-loop. The drawing permits
distinct copies of these sinks. The later argument needs their identical
labelled self-loop behavior, not their identity as one shared graph state.

## Proof of the witness invariant

We prove for every `i >= 1` that

\[
M_i,a_i\models A[F(P\land XP)],\qquad
N_i,a_i\not\models A[F(P\land XP)].
\tag{1}
\]

The base case was calculated above. Suppose (1) holds at level `i`.
Consider an arbitrary path from the root of `M_{i+1}`.

- If its first edge goes directly into either primed component, the new
  root and that component root both satisfy `P`. Thus `P and XP`
  already holds at position zero on this path.
- Otherwise its first two edges are `a -> b -> m_i^0`. The remaining
  suffix is a path from the root of `M_i^0`. By the induction hypothesis
  it eventually has two consecutive `P` states, so the entire path
  satisfies `F(P and XP)`.

These exhaust the outgoing choices. Hence the new `M` root satisfies
the universal witness.

For `N_{i+1}`, take `a -> b -> n_i^0` and then a path witnessing the
failure of `A[F(P and XP)]` in `N_i^0`. The new initial pairs have
labels `P,not P` and `not P,P`, respectively, so neither adds two
consecutive `P` states. The selected suffix contains no such pair by
the induction hypothesis. This is a complete bad path, so the new
`N` root fails the universal witness.

One recursively chosen bad root path in `N_i` has the word

\[
(P\,\neg P)^{i-1}\,P\,(\neg P)^\omega.
\]

In contrast, since every path in both families eventually stays at
`not P`, every such path has only finitely many occurrences of
`P and XP`. Consequently

\[
M_i,a_i\not\models A[\widetilde F(P\land XP)],\qquad
N_i,a_i\not\models A[\widetilde F(P\land XP)]
\]

for every level. The infinity-decorated witness cannot be repaired by
merely rewording the already correct base-chain description.

> The formula `A[F(P and XP)]` is not equivalent to any
> `B(F,X,U,widetilde F,and,not)` formula.

directly to copies of `M_i` and `N_i`, while their additional `not P`
successor points to `M_i` in the first structure and `N_i` in the second.
