## Important scope choices carried into the reader

The first-order decision lookup distinguishes unrestricted validity,
finite satisfiability, finite validity and evaluation in a supplied model.
The finite-model classification retains the fixed finite relational
signature containing a binary relation. The expressibility lookup retains
the all-finite-ranks requirement for the EF inexpressibility criterion.

The proof-transformation lookup explicitly identifies empty-discharge
NJ_v and its distinct nonvacuous presentation. It does not reintroduce the
corrected unconditional normalization claim. The STLC strong-normalization
pointer remains arrow-only. Standard clausification, resolution completeness
and search termination retain different scopes.

The causal lookup states the ID positive-law and supplied-graph conditions;
it does not classify arbitrary ID failures as proof of data-specific
nonidentification or silently extend the run theorem to zero support.
Identification from a law is distinguished from estimating that law.

The statistical lookups separate posterior conditioning, prediction,
frequentist coverage, finite-class generalization and posterior predictive
criticism. No sample size, confidence level, predictive accuracy or causal
effect is invented. The empirical lookup is an interpretation procedure,
not a new experiment or consensus review.

Abduction requires consistency and explanatory entailment with permitted
positive hypothesis atoms. Diagnosis uses minimal withdrawal and the exact
consistency-oracle premise. Neither automatically ranks hypotheses by
probability or proves a causal explanation.
