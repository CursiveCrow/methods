## Consequential reconstruction boundaries

- A coherence interval is not a precise predicted answer or evidence of
  a particular representation; the null-antecedent case needs its own
  conditional-probability convention.
- The Equation is an interpreted conditional-probability identification,
  not a new event automatically available in a classical Boolean algebra.
  A triviality result and an experiment on a premise of its application
  are different claims.
- Jeffrey updating retains within-partition conditional laws; assigning a
  new antecedent probability alone does not determine dynamic revision.
- Default prediction, pragmatic message selection and fixed material
  implication are not interchangeable readings of the hot-tap example.
- Existential quantifiers need support conditions. A probability-one
  inclusion can be almost-sure rather than universal when atoms have zero
  mass; the dependency graph is not automatically a causal/Markov graph.
- Printed 288's `I(s)=1/Pr(s)` is an inverse-probability score, not Shannon
  surprisal `-log_2 Pr(s)`. Their monotone ordering agrees for positive
  probabilities, but their numerical averages do not generally agree.
- Description-length priors yield a MAP tradeoff with likelihood, not
  an unconditional theorem that the shortest explanation has the largest
  posterior. The actual code/language and candidate set matter.
- Expected information gain, verification, and expected decision value
  are different search objectives. A confirming observation can be
  diagnostic without completely verifying a universal rule.
- Slippery-slope probability and utilities require evidence; the source's
  contested policy illustrations are not current legal/medical forecasts.
- Abductively comparing likelihoods differs from circularly counting the
  desired conclusion as new independent evidence.
