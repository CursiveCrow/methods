## Source limitations to retain

- Nichols Study 1 recruits 24 adults and retains 18, but describes exclusions
  with ambiguous 'below 75th percentile of completion rate' wording. The
  count and criterion are retained separately, without silently repairing
  an empirical method. Its footnote says significance conclusions persist
  when all participants are included; no independent reanalysis is claimed.
- Study 1 did not distinguish one from three narrow-scope samples. The
  authors explicitly say it does not establish the size principle rather
  than a subset alternative. Study 2a is a separate sample and design.
- Nichols's child-directed corpus covers four named children at 33--36
  months; compatibility of instruction with a narrow rule is not direct
  measurement of children's posterior beliefs or of a morally correct rule.
- Kahane's criterion of intuitiveness is an independent pilot majority,
  not each scanner participant's immediate intuition. Its main behavioral
  comparisons have varying degrees of freedom; the main methods do not
  fully reconcile their analysis-inclusion denominators. N=16 recruitment
  is not automatically N=16 for every reported comparison.
- Prediction, causal/process identification, rational statistical learning
  and moral authority need separate premises. Sacrificial acceptance is
  not a universal measure of impartial concern or moral competence.
