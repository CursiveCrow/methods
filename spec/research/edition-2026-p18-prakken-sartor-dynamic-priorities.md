## Corrections and scope decisions

- Extracted priorities are read from actual conclusions. An arbitrary
  conflict-free support set need not contain a merged transitivity
  argument. No transitive closure was silently appended to the extraction.
  All stability proofs require and establish asymmetry rather than
  borrowing the external strict-partial-order premise without proof.
- The four exact strict priority schemata are retained; priority assertions
  outside these are defeasible. Other strict rules do not mention the
  priority predicate. Ground instance names remain distinct.
- The inherited global-minimality restriction is removed, linked to the
  preceding four-rule counterexample. This correction does not alone
  prove the dynamic completeness theorem.
- The original source game responds with one argument. The finite support
  selected by a semantic defense is represented as a set only in the
  separately labelled additional certificate construction. No merger is
  presumed legal or rank-preserving.
- The 1996 applicability attack condition on p. 32 prints the argument
  positions in the opposite direction to its prose. The body places the
  inapplicability conclusion in the attacker and the excluded rule in
  the attacked argument.
- Priority-free direct exclusion retains the source defeat formula's
  reverse-undercut block. It consequently differs from inserting an
  inapplicability weak premise; the two-rule countermodel proves this.
- With priority-sensitive direct exclusion, X = {[s],[k]} for
  r: => p, s: => not app(r), k: => s < r is conflict-free, but G(X)
  accepts both [r] and [s] while [s] still exclusion-attacks [r].
  The original compatible-support proof therefore cannot be inherited
  for that variant. The premise-encoding alternative is explicitly a
  different theory, and compares the fresh applicability default name.
- The building example distinguishes T(RP,RM) from T(T(RP,RM),K(RP,RM)).
  Its final superiority is an instance-level conclusion, not circular
  self-justification.
- The pasta illustration includes an explicit priority default realizing
  the priority assumed in the source prose but absent from its displayed
  rule list. Only the relevant ground instances are admitted to the
  worked system; no current legal conclusion is asserted.
- The source's residual defensible label uses attack, whereas the
  reversed game's exact characterization uses defeat. The empty
  incoherence case supplies a complete finite counterexample to merging
  these conditions.
- Companion Definition 5.10 adds a maximality word to a fixed-point
  condition while the following text calls the least fixed point complete.
  The reader treatment states the valid fixed-point consequences directly:
  every conflict-free fixed point contains J, and none is a proper subset
  of J. It does not call the least fixed point maximal. Proposition 5.11
  and the proper-subset reading of Corollary 5.12 follow by the supplied
  leastness proof. The unqualified subset reading would exclude J itself.
