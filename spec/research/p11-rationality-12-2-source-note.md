## Consequential interpretation boundaries

- Antirationalism denies the chapter's conjunction of a-priori basic
  moral knowability and requirement-to-reason entailment. Denial does not
  necessarily deny both conjuncts separately.
- Humeanism is not egoism: intrinsic desires may have altruistic or moral
  contents. The issue is their rational criticizability and contingent
  grounding, not a universal claim that actual people are selfish.
- Some objective reason, most subjective reason, actual motivation and
  ideal counterfactual motivation are different targets. Expected-utility
  mathematics alone does not settle a philosophical account of desire.
- Appropriate blame and observed blame are different. The responsibility
  argument requires normative premises about fitting blame, exemptions,
  excuses and reason possession.
- Primitivism concerns a primitive relation to judgment-sensitive attitudes;
  action reasons are derived in this source's version. Reasons judged
  sufficient and reasons actually sufficient must remain distinct.
- Constitutivism's transition from reliable deliberation conditions to
  intrinsic impartial desires, universal reasons and Rationalism is the
  substantive bridge to expose, not an established theorem obtained by
  naming a constitutive norm. The chapter's final welfare/freedom stance
  belongs to that particular proposal, not to all theories of morality.
