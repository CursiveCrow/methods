Six saved formal constructions:

1. Classical package: ((H and A and I) implies E), not E entails not(H and A and I); adding A,I isolates not H. Countervaluations isolate each alternative failure and show positive E does not entail H.
2. Thread diagnosis: law L>S implies breaking; calibration rL=L,rS=S; detector R iff breaking; reports rL=2,rS=1,R=false. One-element models: L2/S1/breakfalse (law false); L2/S3/breakfalse (calibration false); L2/S1/breaktrue (detector false). All share the observed record. The annex proves the prediction and evaluates all three models explicitly.
3. Finite named raven observations: an unnamed object gives identical records and opposed universal truth; a witness refutes the universal, while a finite negative P-record does not refute unbounded existence. The exhaustive-domain premise restores finite equivalence. The proof covers n=0 and nonempty domains.
4. In a fixed classical interpretation class K with individually consistent admitted tests B, F(T) consists of tests incompatible with T. T1 entails T2 relative K gives F(T2) subset F(T1); the stated witness gives strictness. Explicit countermodels show equal observed falsifiers despite proper strengthening and incomparable classes, without inventing a universal numerical severity metric.
5. Measurable nested hypothesis-world events V1 subset V2 and positive-probability evidence D give a conditional-probability difference equal to probability((V2 minus V1) intersect D)/probability(D). The proof uses disjoint additivity and includes equality despite proper inclusion.
6. Uniform binary length-n law: all-heads singleton has positive mass2^-n, hence is consistent with the law even when a rejection convention acts on it. At n10 its mass1/1024 is below0.05. This is not proof that actual sampling is uniform.
