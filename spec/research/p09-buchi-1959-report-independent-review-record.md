## Mathematical and source determinations

Printed Definition 2 is the pointwise identity
\(U[Y^m]\equiv U[H(Y^m,\bot^n)]\). It does not imply
\(U(\bot^m)=\top\). The accepted presentation keeps the actual run, finite weak
witnesses, output predicate, eventual-truth behavior, and terminal-marker
string behavior in distinct standings.

The report's printed Theorem 2 formula is false. With one state bit,
\(E=\top\), \(H(y,x)=y\), and \(U(y)=y\), the actual output is always true, but
every finite weak transit witness is eventually false and the printed tail
clause then requires the false assertion \(U(\bot)=\top\). The accepted reader
labels that defect rather than silently strengthening Definition 2.

The handbook-owned repair quantifies a finite state tuple \(r\) and finite
downward-closed cutoff \(j\), traces the actual run only through the last true
point \(h\), forces false input from \(h\), and tests \(U(r(h))\). In the forward
direction, pointwise special-output invariance propagates truth along the
omitted all-false tail. In the reverse direction, an accepted run is truncated
after both input extinction and eventual output truth. The reviewers checked
both directions, tuple inputs and states, \(h=0\), the absence of any
\(U(\bot^m)=\top\) assumption, and the promised existential local-successor
form.

The accepted presentation adopts Theorem 3's displayed last-true-point
boundary \(s(x)\land\neg s(x+1)\) and records its conflict with the nearby
printed statement that \(x+1\) is the length. Corollary 1 first requires
\(B(\bot,\bot)\), then searches paths with the source's inclusive \(x\leq k\)
bound and deletes loops. The zero-length path is the all-false assignment.

The report's displayed bit formula rejects the genuine occurrence \((1,1)\)
and falsely accepts \((2,8)\) with \((u,v)=(2,4)\). The one-character proposal
also falsely accepts \((2,8)\) with \((u,v)=(0,6)\). The later 1994 source records
McNaughton's bit-occurrence replacement, the equivalence with \(V_2\), and
Semenov's proof that \(V_2\) is not definable in
\((\mathbb N,+,P_2)\). Thus Theorems 4 and 9 are false in their unary-powers
reverse directions. Corollary 5's decidability conclusion survives through the
sound first-order-to-\(W.2.A.\) translation; Theorem 9 retains only the
\(P_2\)-definable-to-2-regular direction.

The terminal-marker encoding, empty word, and Theorems 6--8 remain sound. The
analysis half of Theorem 7 uses the repaired Theorem 2 construction; the
synthesis half does not. Their finite-word regularity correspondence is
independent of the false arithmetic converse. The report's binary-to-ternary
converter sentence remains tentative at its printed “seems to mean” strength.
