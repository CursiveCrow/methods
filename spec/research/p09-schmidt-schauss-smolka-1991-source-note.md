## Exact concept language and interpretation clauses

The source begins with two disjoint alphabets: concept symbols and role
symbols. It reserves \(\top\) as a concept symbol, \(A,B\) for concept symbols,
and \(R\) for role symbols. The full language is the source’s
\(\mathcal{ALC}\), with abstract grammar

\[
 C,D ::= A
 \mid \forall R:C
 \mid \exists R:C
 \mid C\sqcap D
 \mid C\sqcup D
 \mid \neg C.
\]

Thus the source’s printed grammar contains both existential restriction and
union even though it later proves them eliminable in the presence of
complement. Concept, role, and concept description are object-language
categories; \(\mathcal{ALC}\) names the resulting description language
([§2, printed p. 5](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=5)).

An interpretation is printed as
\(\mathcal I=(D^{\mathcal I},{}^{\mathcal I})\). The text says that
\(D^{\mathcal I}\) is a set and that the interpretation function maps every
concept description to a subset of that set and every role to a subset of its
square. It gives these clauses:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \top^{\mathcal I} &= D^{\mathcal I},\\
 (\forall R:C)^{\mathcal I}
   &=\{a\in D^{\mathcal I}\mid
       \forall(a,b)\in R^{\mathcal I}: b\in C^{\mathcal I}\},\\
 (\exists R:C)^{\mathcal I}
   &=\{a\in D^{\mathcal I}\mid
       \exists(a,b)\in R^{\mathcal I}: b\in C^{\mathcal I}\},\\
 (C\sqcap D)^{\mathcal I}&=C^{\mathcal I}\cap D^{\mathcal I},\\
 (C\sqcup D)^{\mathcal I}&=C^{\mathcal I}\cup D^{\mathcal I},\\
 (\neg C)^{\mathcal I}&=D^{\mathcal I}-C^{\mathcal I}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The article does **not** explicitly add \(D^{\mathcal I}\ne\varnothing\) to
this definition. Coherence itself requires a nonempty concept extension, so
every coherence witness has a nonempty domain. A later presentation that
adopts the now-standard nonempty-domain convention must state that convention
and repair the empty-individual edge case in Proposition 4.2 rather than
silently attributing nonemptiness to this definition
([§2, printed p. 5](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=5)).

For concept descriptions \(C,D\):

- \(C\) is **coherent** iff some interpretation makes \(C^{\mathcal I}\)
  nonempty;
- \(C\) is **subsumed by** \(D\) iff
  \(C^{\mathcal I}\subseteq D^{\mathcal I}\) in every interpretation;
- \(C\sim D\) iff their extensions agree in every interpretation.

The Introduction also uses the active phrase “\(C\) subsumes \(D\)” for the
reverse containment \(D^{\mathcal I}\subseteq C^{\mathcal I}\). Those two
English constructions reverse the argument order; their displayed reductions
are consistent when read literally
([printed pp. 3 and 5](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=3)).

## Derived constructors, normalization, and exact sublanguages

The source calls the full syntax redundant and displays these eliminations:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \top &\sim A\sqcup\neg A,\\
 \exists R:C &\sim \neg(\forall R:\neg C),\\
 C\sqcup D &\sim \neg(\neg C\sqcap\neg D),\\
 C-D &:= C\sqcap\neg D.
\end{aligned}
\]

It also uses concept implication \(C\to D\) as an abbreviation for
\(\neg C\sqcup D\), hence as a concept equivalent to the inclusion condition
\(C\sqsubseteq D\). These are derived descriptions or metalinguistic
abbreviations, not new semantic categories
([printed pp. 2--4 and 6](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=2)).

A **simple complement** is \(\neg A\) with \(A\) a concept symbol. A concept
description is **simple** when all its complements are simple. Complex
complements are pushed to atoms using

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \neg(\forall R:C)&\sim\exists R:\neg C,\\
 \neg(\exists R:C)&\sim\forall R:\neg C,\\
 \neg(C\sqcap D)&\sim\neg C\sqcup\neg D,\\
 \neg(C\sqcup D)&\sim\neg C\sqcap\neg D,\\
 \neg\neg C&\sim C.
\end{aligned}
\]

Proposition 2.2 says that an equivalent simple description is computable in
linear time. The article gives the rewrite equations but no separate induction
or explicit size proof
([§2, printed p. 6](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=6)).

The source defines exactly three proper restrictions of \(\mathcal{ALC}\):

| Source language | Exact syntactic restriction |
|---|---|
| \(\mathcal{ALE}\) | simple descriptions with no unions |
| \(\mathcal{ALU}\) | simple descriptions; existential restrictions only as \(\exists R:\top\) |
| \(\mathcal{AL}\) | simple descriptions with no unions and existentials only as \(\exists R:\top\) |

Consequently \(\mathcal{AL}=\mathcal{ALE}\cap\mathcal{ALU}\). The letters are
explained historically: U adds unions, E adds general existential
restrictions, and C adds general complements. The paper regards
\(\mathcal{AL}\) as the minimal sensible language because simple complements
permit disjoint descriptions
([§2, printed p. 6](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=6)).

## Coherence/subsumption reductions and problem scope

Proposition 2.1 gives the formal all-interpretations reduction

\[
 C\text{ is subsumed by }D
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 C\sqcap\neg D\text{ is incoherent}.
\]

The converse service reduction uses

\[
 C\text{ is incoherent}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 C\text{ is subsumed by }\neg\top.
\]

Both transformations are linear in the input description. In the
Introduction’s active orientation, the equivalent first display is
“\(C\) subsumes \(D\) iff \(\neg C\sqcap D\) is incoherent”
([printed pp. 3 and 5](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=3)).

The Introduction discusses terminological axioms as background and gives
equations/inclusions for man, parent, father, and grandfather, the definition
woman = person - man, and a conditional inclusion from animals that are
featherless bipeds to humans. It says that under restrictions cited to earlier
work, reasoning relative to such a terminology can be reduced to reasoning
over all interpretations. The article does not define or analyze a general
TBox calculus. Its announced complexity results explicitly say “everything
with respect to all interpretations”
([printed pp. 2--4](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=2)).

Proposition 2.3 reduces propositional CNF satisfiability to
\(\mathcal{ALU}\)-coherence by reading propositional variables as concepts,
conjunction as intersection, disjunction as union, and literal negation as
complement. The reverse direction extracts one literal made true at a chosen
domain element from each clause. The forward direction gives a one-element
interpretation, making precisely the selected positive concept symbols true.
It concludes that \(\mathcal{ALU}\)-coherence is NP-hard and
\(\mathcal{ALU}\)-subsumption is co-NP-hard even without roles
([Proposition 2.3, printed pp. 6--7](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=6)).

## Constraint language and exact satisfaction clauses

Section 3 introduces disjoint alphabets of individual variables \(x,y,z\) and
concept variables \(X,Y,Z\). For an interpretation \(\mathcal I\), an
\(\mathcal I\)-assignment \(\alpha\) maps individual variables to
\(D^{\mathcal I}\) and concept variables to subsets of \(D^{\mathcal I}\).
The source writes the set of all such assignments as
\(\operatorname{Ass}^{\mathcal I}\)
([§3, printed pp. 8--9](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=8)).

There are exactly six constraint forms:

\[
 X\sqsubseteq C,\qquad
 X(\forall R)Y,\qquad
 X(\exists R)Y,\qquad
 X\sqsubseteq Y\sqcup Z,\qquad
 x:X,\qquad
 xRy,
\]

where \(C\) in the first form must be a simple concept description. Their
interpretations as sets of assignments are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 (X\sqsubseteq C)^{\mathcal I}
 &=\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\mathcal I}
       \mid\alpha(X)\subseteq C^{\mathcal I}\},\\
 (X(\forall R)Y)^{\mathcal I}
 &=\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\mathcal I}
       \mid\forall a\in\alpha(X)\,
       \forall(a,b)\in R^{\mathcal I}:b\in\alpha(Y)\},\\
 (X(\exists R)Y)^{\mathcal I}
 &=\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\mathcal I}
       \mid\forall a\in\alpha(X)\,
       \exists(a,b)\in R^{\mathcal I}:b\in\alpha(Y)\},\\
 (X\sqsubseteq Y\sqcup Z)^{\mathcal I}
 &=\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\mathcal I}
       \mid\alpha(X)\subseteq\alpha(Y)\cup\alpha(Z)\},\\
 (x:X)^{\mathcal I}
 &=\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\mathcal I}
       \mid\alpha(x)\in\alpha(X)\},\\
 (xRy)^{\mathcal I}
 &=\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\mathcal I}
       \mid(\alpha(x),\alpha(y))\in R^{\mathcal I}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

A constraint system is a finite nonempty set \(S\) of constraints,
\(S^{\mathcal I}=\bigcap_{\phi\in S}\phi^{\mathcal I}\), and \(S\) is
satisfiable iff \(S^{\mathcal I}\ne\varnothing\) for some interpretation.
Proposition 3.1 states that for fresh variables \(x,X\), a simple description
\(C\) is coherent iff \(\{x:X,X\sqsubseteq C\}\) is satisfiable
([§3 and Proposition 3.1, printed p. 9](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=9)).

## Every unfolding rule

A constraint system is **simple** when every constraint \(X\sqsubseteq C\)
has \(C=A\ne\top\) or \(C=\neg A\). The source gives these five deterministic
unfolding rewrites:

\[
\begin{array}{rcll}
 X\sqsubseteq\forall R:C
 &\rightsquigarrow&X(\forall R)Y,\ Y\sqsubseteq C,
 &Y\text{ new},\\
 X\sqsubseteq\exists R:C
 &\rightsquigarrow&X(\exists R)Y,\ Y\sqsubseteq C,
 &Y\text{ new},\\
 X\sqsubseteq C\sqcap D
 &\rightsquigarrow&X\sqsubseteq C,\ X\sqsubseteq D,\\
 X\sqsubseteq C\sqcup D
 &\rightsquigarrow&X\sqsubseteq Y\sqcup Z,\
                   Y\sqsubseteq C,\ Z\sqsubseteq D,
 &Y,Z\text{ new},\\
 X\sqsubseteq\top&\rightsquigarrow&\text{nothing}.&&
\end{array}
\]

Fresh-variable choice is immaterial up to renaming; none of these rules is a
semantic choice point. Proposition 3.2 says each application preserves and
reflects satisfiability. Proposition 3.3 says a satisfiability-equivalent simple
system is computable in linear time. Neither proposition has a printed proof
([printed p. 10](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=10)).

The **skeleton** of a simple system has concept variables as nodes,
\(X(\exists R)Y\) and \(X(\forall R)Y\) as existential and universal directed
edges, \(X\sqsubseteq Y\sqcup Z\) as an or-connected edge pair, and
\(X\sqsubseteq A\) or \(X\sqsubseteq\neg A\) as node labels. Individual
constraints do not enter the skeleton. A constraint tree or forest is a simple
system whose skeleton is a tree or forest. A tree is **fresh** iff it can be
obtained by unfolding a simple \(\mathcal{ALC}\) description; it has exactly
one individual constraint \(x:X\), at the skeleton root. Theorem 3.4 composes
simple-form conversion, the Proposition 3.1 translation, and unfolding to give
in linear time a fresh tree satisfiable exactly when the original description
is coherent
([printed pp. 10--11](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=10)).

## Clash, completeness, and Proposition 4.2 model extraction

A **clash** is either pattern

\[
 \{x:X,\ X\sqsubseteq\neg\top\}
\]

or

\[
 \{x:X,\ X\sqsubseteq A,\ x:Y,\ Y\sqsubseteq\neg A\}.
\]

Proposition 4.1 says every system containing either pattern is unsatisfiable
and that clash detection is linear-time. No proof is printed
([§4, printed p. 11](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=11)).

A simple system \(S\) is **complete** exactly when all three conditions hold:

1. from \(x:X\) and \(X(\exists R)Y\), some \(y\) has both \(xRy\) and \(y:Y\);
2. from \(x:X\), \(X(\forall R)Y\), and \(xRy\), it contains \(y:Y\);
3. from \(x:X\) and \(X\sqsubseteq Y\sqcup Z\), it contains \(x:Y\) or \(x:Z\).

Proposition 4.2 states the exact equivalence

\[
 S\text{ complete}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \bigl(S\text{ satisfiable}\Longleftrightarrow S\text{ clash-free}\bigr).
\]

The reverse implication is established by a source-defined interpretation and
assignment. The domain is the set of individual variables occurring in \(S\);
for each concept symbol \(A\) and role symbol \(R\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 A^{\mathcal I}
 &=\{x\mid\exists X\,(x:X\in S\land X\sqsubseteq A\in S)\},\\
 R^{\mathcal I}
 &=\{(x,y)\mid xRy\in S\},\\
 \alpha(x)&=x,\\
 \alpha(X)&=\{x\mid x:X\in S\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The printed proof then says this assignment is a solution. It does not print
the constraint-by-constraint verification. It also does not totalize
\(\alpha\) on individual variables absent from \(S\). Under the printed
definition of an assignment as a map on the individual-variable alphabet,
\(\alpha(x)=x\) is typed only when \(x\) occurs in \(S\). If at least one
individual variable occurs, fix an occurring \(x_0\), retain
\(\alpha(x)=x\) for occurring variables, and map every nonoccurring variable
to \(x_0\). No constraint can distinguish that extension.

If no individual variable occurs, the printed domain is empty and cannot
support a total assignment from the nonempty variable alphabet, independently
of whether empty interpretation domains are otherwise permitted. A typed
repair takes a singleton domain, maps all individual variables to its element,
interprets ordinary concept symbols and roles as empty, and assigns every
concept variable the empty set. Every constraint is then vacuously satisfied.
Every fresh tree has a root individual, so only the harmless
\(x_0\)-totalization is needed in the coherence applications.

The remaining verification, if supplied in the handbook, is a reconstruction
with these exact dependencies:

| Constraint | Why the constructed assignment satisfies it |
|---|---|
| \(x:X\) | definition of \(\alpha(X)\) |
| \(xRy\) | definition of \(R^{\mathcal I}\) |
| \(X\sqsubseteq A\) | definition of \(A^{\mathcal I}\) |
| \(X\sqsubseteq\neg A\) | membership in \(A^{\mathcal I}\) would form the second clash pattern |
| \(X(\exists R)Y\) | completeness condition 1 supplies a witness |
| \(X(\forall R)Y\) | completeness condition 2 propagates \(Y\) along every represented \(R\)-edge |
| \(X\sqsubseteq Y\sqcup Z\) | completeness condition 3 selects one membership |

This is the typed model-extraction/truth verification implicit in Proposition
4.2; the article does not name a separate truth lemma or acknowledge the
total-assignment defect. The singleton repair also supplies the nonempty domain
required by later standard semantics
([Proposition 4.2, printed p. 11](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=11)).

## Proposition 4.3 and every basic completion rule

Proposition 4.3 gives the semantic rule facts before control is imposed:

1. If \(x:X,X(\exists R)Y\in S\) and \(y\) is fresh, then
   \(S\) is satisfiable iff
   \(S\cup\{xRy,y:Y\}\) is satisfiable.
2. If \(x:X,xRy,X(\forall R)Y\in S\), then \(S\) is satisfiable iff
   \(S\cup\{y:Y\}\) is satisfiable.
3. If \(x:X,X\sqsubseteq Y\sqcup Z\in S\), then \(S\) is satisfiable iff
   at least one of \(S\cup\{x:Y\}\) and \(S\cup\{x:Z\}\) is satisfiable.

No proof is printed. The exact unprinted dependencies are: extend a satisfying
assignment to fresh \(y\) with the existential witness for item 1; use the
universal and represented-role conditions for item 2; use membership in
\(\alpha(Y)\cup\alpha(Z)\) for item 3. Reverse preservation in every item is
set inclusion of constraints. Item 3 is a disjunction between two systems,
not a claim that both branches preserve satisfiability
([Proposition 4.3, printed p. 12](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=12)).

Figure 1 imposes the following exact applicability conditions:

\[
 S\to_{\exists}\{y:Y,xRy\}\cup S
\]

when \(x:X,X(\exists R)Y\in S\), no \(z\) satisfies both
\(xRz,z:Y\in S\), and \(y\) is fresh. This rule is deterministic up to the
name of its fresh witness.

\[
 S\to_{\forall}\{y:Y\}\cup S
\]

when \(x:X,xRy,X(\forall R)Y\in S\) and \(y:Y\notin S\). This rule is
deterministic.

\[
 S\to_{\sqcup}\{x:Z\}\cup S
\]

when \(x:X,X\sqsubseteq Y_1\sqcup Y_2\in S\), neither \(x:Y_1\) nor
\(x:Y_2\) is already in \(S\), and \(Z\) is chosen as either \(Y_1\) or
\(Y_2\). This is the sole branching basic rule: the two possible choices are
the two alternatives of Proposition 4.3(3)
([Fig. 1, printed p. 12](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=12)).

Proposition 4.4 states: no infinite basic-rule chain starts at a fresh tree; a
simple system is complete exactly when no basic rule applies; and one basic
step from tree \(T\) to \(T'\) preserves tree shape and supports the backward
implication “\(T'\) satisfiable \(\Rightarrow T\) satisfiable.” The last item
is only the direction needed for soundness of an arbitrarily chosen branch.
Proposition 4.3 supplies the stronger choice-sensitive preservation needed for
completeness. Proposition 4.4 has no printed proof
([printed p. 12](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=12)).

A **completion** of \(T\) is a complete constraint tree reachable by finitely
many basic-rule applications. Theorem 4.5 states exactly:

- every fresh constraint tree has a completion; and
- a fresh constraint tree is satisfiable iff it has a clash-free completion.

Its entire printed proof is the sentence “Follows from the preceding
propositions.” The dependency chain is nevertheless determinate. Termination
plus normal-form completeness gives existence. For soundness, Proposition 4.2
makes a clash-free completion satisfiable and Proposition 4.4 propagates that
fact backward. For completeness, Proposition 4.3 chooses a satisfiable union
branch whenever needed; termination yields a satisfiable completion, which is
clash-free by Proposition 4.1. Any handbook expansion of this chain must be
labelled as reconstruction, not as a detailed proof printed by the article
([Theorem 4.5, printed p. 13](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=13)).

The article's exact Theorem 4.6 states

\[
\text{Every \(\mathcal{FL}\) concept description is coherent.}
\]

The symbol \(\mathcal{FL}\) is retained here only inside the quoted source
theorem as the article's identifier for an earlier formal language. The article
refers that language's full grammar to prior sources rather than defining it in
full, so a separate source presentation of those authorities remains owed; the
present reconstruction does not adopt \(\mathcal{FL}\) as a usable handbook
language. The theorem's short proof translates the earlier language's
universal-restriction form into an \(\mathcal{ALC}\) union constraint and
always chooses the right-hand union alternative. Only its local printed
constraint defect is recorded below
([Theorem 4.6, printed p. 13](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=13)).

## Source examples in §§1--4

The source contains these concrete examples or constructions:

1. The Introduction’s defining axioms use person, male, sex, child, man,
   parent, father, and grandfather; complement then defines woman as
   person minus man, and concept implication expresses the featherless-biped
   condition. These illustrate constructors and terminology, not the no-TBox
   decision procedure
   ([printed pp. 2--3](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=2)).
2. Proposition 2.3’s CNF reduction constructs an explicit one-element model
   from one noncomplementary literal chosen in every clause
   ([printed p. 7](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=7)).
3. Figure 2 displays a recursively growing skeleton family in which every
   completion has exponentially many individual variables relative to the
   skeleton. It uses a single role symbol, single-line existential edges, and

## Section 5 trace vocabulary and exact control relations

A **partial completion** of a fresh tree \(T\) is any tree reachable from
\(T\) by finitely many basic completion steps. If \(xRy\) occurs, \(y\) is a
successor of \(x\) and \(x\) a predecessor of \(y\). An **individual root** is
an individual variable \(x\) with \(x:X\), where \(X\) is the skeleton root.
Proposition 5.1 says every partial completion has one individual root, that
root has no predecessor, and every other individual variable has exactly one
predecessor. No proof is printed
([§5 and Proposition 5.1, printed p. 14](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=14)).

A partial completion \(U\) is a **trace** of \(T\) exactly when:

1. no individual variable in \(U\) has more than one successor;
2. neither the universal nor the union basic rule applies to \(U\); and
3. every applicable basic existential step would give some individual two
   successors.

The trace-specific existential rule is

\[
 S\to_{T\exists}\{y:Y,xRy\}\cup S
\]

when \(x:X,X(\exists R)Y\in S\), there is **no** role \(R'\) and individual
\(y'\) with \(xR'y'\in S\), and \(y\) is fresh. The primed \(R'\) is
essential: this rule creates at most one successor total for the current
individual, not one successor per role. The source defines

\[
 \to_T := \to_{T\exists}\ \cup\ \to_{\forall}\ \cup\ \to_{\sqcup}.
\]

Traces are exactly the \(\to_T\)-normal forms reachable from \(T\). Union
steps retain their two-way branching; \(\to_{T\exists}\) and
\(\to_{\forall}\) are deterministic up to fresh-name choice
([printed p. 14](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=14)).

Proposition 5.2 supplies the structural facts used by the algorithm:

1. a \(\to_T^*\)-derivative never associates two distinct individual
   variables with the same concept variable;
2. the length of every such derivation is linear in the size of the initial
   tree;
3. every trace is contained in a completion; and
4. every completion is the union of finitely many traces.

None of the four items receives a printed proof
([Proposition 5.2, printed p. 15](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=15)).

## Figure 3 algorithm and the exact linear-space claim

Figure 3 defines a Boolean recursive function \(\operatorname{eval}\) on a
natural-number individual variable and a constraint tree. Its control is:

1. return false when the current individual participates in either clash
   pattern;
2. if an unresolved union \(X\sqsubseteq Y\sqcup Z\) is active at that
   individual, return the disjunction of the two recursive branch results;
3. otherwise set the successor name to \(y=x+1\), and for every active
   existential edge \(X(\exists R)Y\), recursively evaluate the tree extended
   by
   \[
   \{y:Y\}\cup
   \{y:Z'\mid\exists Z\,(x:Z\in S\land Z(\forall R)Z'\in S)\},
   \]
   using the same role \(R\) as the selected existential edge.

In the third clause the recursive results are universally combined: every
existential trace successor must admit the required clash-free continuation.
The displayed recursive extension does not contain \(xRy\); the call moves to
current individual \(y\) and retains only the successor memberships needed for
that trace. It is therefore not literally the basic existential completion
step, which does add \(xRy\). The figure assumes individual variables are
natural numbers. If \(x\) is the individual root of fresh \(T\), its caption
asserts

\[
 \operatorname{eval}(x,T)=\mathsf{true}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 T\text{ has a clash-free completion}.
\]

This is a direct control presentation of the completion characterization, not
a modern tableau with node caching or blocking
([Fig. 3, printed p. 15](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=15)).

The source’s space measure is syntactic size of the input tree/description. It
states that recursion depth is at most the height of the input tree and that,
apart from control data, execution retains at most one trace. Proposition
5.2(2) bounds a trace linearly in the initial tree; Theorem 3.4 makes that tree
linear in the original description. Theorem 5.3 therefore states that
coherence and subsumption of \(\mathcal{ALC}\) descriptions can be checked
with linear space. The Introduction additionally describes the resulting
procedure as exponential-time. The article does not print a machine encoding,
explicit constant, or bit-by-bit stack accounting; “total correctness” is said
to follow from the preceding propositions
([printed pp. 4 and 15](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=4)).

## The second restricted existential relation and sublanguage bounds

For \(\mathcal{ALU}\) and \(\mathcal{AL}\), §5 uses the broader rule

\[
 S\to_{\top\exists}\{y:Y,xRy\}\cup S
\]

when \(x:X,X(\exists R)Y\in S\), no \(y'\) has \(xRy'\in S\), and \(y\) is
fresh. Unlike the trace rule, this side condition fixes the same role \(R\),
so different role labels may create different successors. The source records

\[
 \to_{T\exists}\subseteq\to_{\top\exists},
 \qquad
 \to_{\top}:=\to_{\top\exists}\cup\to_{\forall}\cup\to_{\sqcup},
 \qquad
 \to_T\subseteq\to_{\top}.
\]

A **\(\top\)-completion** is a \(\to_\top\)-normal form. The distinction
between subscripts \(T\) and \(\top\) is visible in the printed formulas and
must not be flattened by text extraction
([printed p. 16](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=16)).

The exact auxiliary and final bounds are:

- Proposition 5.4: without union constraints, all completions agree up to
  consistent individual-variable renaming, and satisfiability, existence of a
  clash-free completion, clash-freedom of every completion, and absence of a
  clash from every trace are equivalent
  ([printed pp. 15--16](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=15)).
- Theorem 5.5: \(\mathcal{ALE}\)-incoherence is decidable in nondeterministic
  linear time, hence \(\mathcal{ALE}\)-coherence lies in co-NP. The proof
  guesses a linearly long trace and looks for a clash
  ([printed p. 16](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=16)).
- Proposition 5.6: \(\to_\top\)-derivation length from a fresh tree is linear.
  Its proof gives the invariant that at most one individual variable is
  associated with each concept variable and then calls the conclusion obvious
  ([printed p. 16](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=16)).
- Proposition 5.7: for a \(\top\)-completion obtained from an
  \(\mathcal{ALU}\) description, adding the missing target membership \(y:Y\)
  for a represented existential edge preserves satisfiability in both
  directions. The one-sentence proof cites the fact that every existentially
  reachable concept variable is an unlabeled leaf
  ([printed pp. 16--17](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=16)).
- Proposition 5.8: a fresh tree unfolded from \(\mathcal{ALU}\) is satisfiable
  iff it has a clash-free \(\top\)-completion. The proof extends such a normal
  form to a basic completion via Proposition 5.7 and then invokes Theorem 4.5
  ([printed p. 17](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=17)).
- Theorem 5.9: \(\mathcal{ALU}\)-coherence is NP-complete. Proposition 2.3

- Theorem 5.10: \(\mathcal{AL}\)-coherence is decidable in linear time because

  ([printed p. 17](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=17)).

## Section 6 QBF objects and source semantics

A literal is a nonzero integer. A clause is a nonempty sequence
\(l_1\ldots l_k\) ordered by
\(|l_1|\le\cdots\le|l_k|\). For positive integers \(m\le n\), a prefix from
\(m\) to \(n\) is

\[
 (Q_m m)(Q_{m+1}(m+1))\cdots(Q_n n),
\]

where each \(Q_i\) is \(\forall\) or \(\exists\). A quantified Boolean formula
is a pair \(P.M\), with \(P\) a prefix from 1 to \(n\) and \(M\) a finite
nonempty set of clauses using only literals from \(-n\) through \(n\)
([§6.1, printed p. 18](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=18)).

A \(P\)-assignment for a prefix from \(m\) to \(n\) maps
\(\{m,\ldots,n\}\) to \(\{0,1\}\). It satisfies positive \(l\) when
\(\alpha(l)=1\), negative \(l\) when \(\alpha(-l)=0\), and a clause when at
least one literal is satisfied. A nonempty set \(\mathscr A\) of
\(P\)-assignments is **canonical for \(P\)** by this recursion:

- if \(P=(\exists m)P'\), all assignments agree on \(m\), and their
  restrictions to \(m+1,\ldots,n\) are canonical for \(P'\) when \(P'\) is
  nonempty;
- if \(P=(\forall m)P'\), the set contains assignments with value 1 and value
  0 at \(m\), and each corresponding restricted subset is canonical for
  \(P'\) when \(P'\) is nonempty.

The formula \(P.M\) is **valid** iff some canonical set for \(P\) consists
entirely of assignments satisfying every clause in \(M\). This is a
set-of-assignments encoding of the quantifier game, not a new truth definition
for arbitrary propositional syntax. The source’s concrete example is

\[
 \forall x\exists y\;((\neg x\lor y)\land(x\lor\neg y)),
\]

which it labels valid. Theorem 6.1, cited to Garey--Johnson [12], states that
this QBF validity problem is PSPACE-complete; no proof is printed
([printed p. 18](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=18)).

## Complete formula translation

Fix one role symbol \(R\) and one concept symbol \(A\). For
\(M=\{C_1,\ldots,C_k\}\), the source defines

\[
 [P.M]=[P]\sqcap[C_1]^0\sqcap\cdots\sqcap[C_k]^0.
\]

The prefix translation is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 [(\exists m)P]
   &=((\exists R:A)\sqcup(\exists R:\neg A))
     \sqcap(\forall R:[P]),\\
 [(\forall m)P]
   &=(\exists R:A)\sqcap(\exists R:\neg A)
     \sqcap(\forall R:[P]),\\
 [(\exists m)]&=(\exists R:A)\sqcup(\exists R:\neg A),\\
 [(\forall m)]&=(\exists R:A)\sqcap(\exists R:\neg A).
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus an existential quantifier chooses an \(A\)- or \(\neg A\)-successor by a
union, while a universal quantifier requires both successors. The universal
restriction sends the remaining prefix down every \(R\)-successor
([§6.2, printed p. 19](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=19)).

For an ordered clause, writing \(lC\) for first literal \(l\) followed by the
remaining sequence \(C\), the level-indexed translation is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 [lC]^m&=\forall R:[lC]^{m+1}&&(|l|>m),\\
 [mC]^m&=A\sqcup[C]^m,\\
 [-mC]^m&=\neg A\sqcup[C]^m,\\
 [l]^m&=\forall R:[l]^{m+1}&&(|l|>m),\\
 [m]^m&=A,\\
 [-m]^m&=\neg A.
\end{aligned}
\]

The numeric parameter pads a literal down to its variable level. It also
ensures that every introduced clause union is exactly \(A\sqcup C\) or
\(\neg A\sqcup C\); the source says this restricted shape is essential. The
translation is later bounded as quadratic-time, not linear-time
([printed pp. 19 and 21](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=19)).

Figure 4 draws the fresh constraint tree for the valid example. At level 1 the
universal \(x\) creates both \(A\) and \(\neg A\) branches; at level 2 each
existential \(y\) chooses one successor. The two clause trees are aligned to
those levels. The figure is the article’s only worked QBF translation
([Fig. 4, printed p. 19](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=19)).

## Level assignment and Lemma 6.2

The source assigns levels in a constraint tree by five clauses:

1. the skeleton root concept variable has level 0;
2. an existential or universal edge raises the target concept-variable level
   by one;
3. all three concept variables in a union constraint have the same level;
4. \(x:X\) gives \(x\) and \(X\) the same level;
5. \(xRy\) raises \(y\)’s level one above \(x\)’s.

These clauses give unique levels to every tree reachable from a fresh tree by
completion
([printed p. 20](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=20)).

Lemma 6.2 states the exact reduction invariant:

\[
 P.M\text{ is valid}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 [P.M]\text{ is a coherent }\mathcal{ALC}\text{ description}.
\]

Let the fresh tree for the translation be decomposed as prefix tree
\(\overline P\) and clause trees \(\overline C_i\). Every trace of
\(\overline P\) contains exactly one individual \(x_i\) at each level
\(0,\ldots,m\) and the chain
\(x_0Rx_1,\ldots,x_{m-1}Rx_m\). Level \(i\) is true in a trace when its
individual is constrained through some concept variable to \(A\), and false
when constrained to \(\neg A\). Exactly one holds, defining a \(P\)-assignment
([Lemma 6.2 proof, printed p. 20](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=20)).

Every prefix completion is clash-free. The assignments represented by its
traces form a canonical set for \(P\), and conversely every canonical set for
\(P\) is represented by a prefix completion. The article calls this
correspondence crucial but does not print the quantifier-by-quantifier
induction. Its dependencies are exactly the prefix equations: existential
union retains one value branch, while universal intersection creates both;
the trailing \(\forall R:[P]\) recursively applies the remainder to every
successor. A detailed induction would be handbook-supplied reconstruction
([printed p. 20](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=20)).

Each clause tree has no existential edge. Its leaves correspond one-for-one
to clause literals: a positive literal \(l\) gives an \(A\)-labelled leaf at
level \(l\), and a negative literal \(l\) gives a \(\neg A\)-labelled leaf at
level \(-l\). Completion of its union chain selects exactly one leaf along a
trace
([printed p. 20](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=20)).

### Coherence implies validity

A clash-free completion of the full translated tree contains a prefix
completion. Choose any prefix trace and its assignment. For each clause, use a
trace of the corresponding prefix-plus-clause part contained in the full
completion. That clause trace contains one active leaf. Clash-freedom forces
the leaf’s \(A\) or \(\neg A\) label to agree with the prefix value at its
level, so the assignment satisfies that literal. Hence every assignment
represented by the prefix completion satisfies every clause, and the
represented canonical set witnesses QBF validity
([printed pp. 20--21](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=20)).

### Validity implies coherence

From a canonical satisfying set \(\mathscr A\), choose a prefix completion
that represents it. For a fixed clause, the proof controls union choice with
the restriction \(\to_Q\): if

\[
 X\sqsubseteq Y_1\sqcup Y_2,\quad
 x:X,\quad x:Z,\quad Z\sqsubseteq C,\quad Y_1\sqsubseteq D
\]

are in the current system, while \(x:Y_1\notin S\) and \(x:Y_2\notin S\), add
\(x:Y_1\) when \(C=D\) and add \(x:Y_2\) when \(C\ne D\). The two absence
conditions are inherited from the basic union rule. This is a deterministic
restriction of that branching rule relative to the already fixed
\(A\)/\(\neg A\) label at that level: it selects the literal branch exactly
when the label agrees and otherwise continues to the remaining clause
([printed p. 21](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=21)).

Applying \(\to_{\forall}\) and \(\to_Q\) completes each clause tree without
adding existential edges. If a clash appeared at greatest clause level \(k\),
choose a prefix trace containing the same chain through \(x_k\). The way
\(\to_Q\) passed every earlier union would make the resulting canonical
assignment satisfy no literal of that clause, contradicting the hypothesis on
\(\mathscr A\). The source gives this contradiction but calls the final
literal-by-literal verification easy; it does not expand that induction
([printed p. 21](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=21)).

## Exact Theorem 6.3 strength and dependency chain

Theorem 6.3 states:

\[
 \boxed{\text{Coherence and subsumption of }\mathcal{ALC}
 \text{ concept descriptions are PSPACE-complete.}}
\]

Its scope is concept descriptions interpreted over all interpretations. It
does not mention TBoxes, ABoxes, cyclic definitions, role hierarchies, number
restrictions, or blocking
([Theorem 6.3, printed p. 21](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=21)).

The printed proof depends on:

1. Proposition 2.1 and the empty-concept reduction between subsumption and
   incoherence;
2. Theorem 5.3’s linear-space procedure for coherence and subsumption;
3. cited Theorem 6.1, QBF validity is PSPACE-complete;
4. Lemma 6.2, QBF validity iff translated-concept coherence; and
5. the explicit claim that the translation is computable in quadratic time.

The coherence lower bound is direct. For subsumption, the printed proof does
not spell out the complement-closure step: QBF invalidity is also
PSPACE-complete because PSPACE is closed under complement, and incoherence
then maps linearly to subsumption by \(C\sqsubseteq\neg\top\). Equivalently, a
deterministic polynomial-space coherence decider can have its Boolean result
reversed. This is a standard completion of the printed argument, but it should
not be presented as an explicit sentence in the article
([Theorem 6.3 proof, printed p. 21](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=21)).

## Section 7 boundary from the complete body

Section 7 compares the article’s general binary roles with functional roles,
called **features**. It recasts a binary relation as a union-preserving set
function, defines a path as a finite role sequence, and defines an agreement
\(p\downarrow q\) by equality of the two path images at an element. A role is
a feature in an interpretation when its relation is functional; a feature
interpretation makes every role functional
([§7, printed pp. 22--23](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=22)).

The undecidability and NP/co-NP comparisons in §7 are cited from
Schmidt-Schauß, Smolka, and Hollunder rather than proved in this article. The
source stresses that distributivities such as

\[
 \forall R:(C\sqcup D)
 \sim (\forall R:C)\sqcup(\forall R:D)
\]

and

\[
 \exists R:(C\sqcap D)
 \sim (\exists R:C)\sqcap(\exists R:D)
\]

hold for functional features but fail for general roles. It ends by observing
that its \(\mathcal{ALC}\) procedure is not incremental, unlike the cited
feature-description algorithms. These comparisons do not alter Theorem 6.3’s
language or supply a later ABox/TBox calculus
([printed pp. 23--24](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=23)).

## Printed defects, notation traps, and source limitations

1. **Nonempty-domain condition not printed.** The interpretation definition
   says only that \(D^{\mathcal I}\) is a set. If a later standard nonempty-
   domain convention is imposed, Proposition 4.2’s domain construction needs
   a dummy element when a complete clash-free system has no individual
   variable. Fresh trees always have an individual root, so the main coherence
   argument is unaffected
   ([printed pp. 5 and 11](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=5)).
2. **The canonical individual assignment is not total as printed.** The source
   defines assignments on the individual-variable alphabet, takes the model
   domain to be only the individual variables occurring in \(S\), and then
   writes \(\alpha(x)=x\). A nonoccurring \(x\) is not in that domain; if no
   individual occurs, no total assignment into the displayed empty domain
   exists. The \(x_0\)-extension and singleton repair above close the typing
   gap without changing any constraint or fresh-tree theorem
   ([printed pp. 9 and 11](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=9)).
3. **Malformed constraint in the out-of-scope earlier-language Theorem 4.6’s last line.** The scan prints the
   pair as \(\{x:X,X:\neg A\}\). The constraint grammar and clash definition
   require \(\{x:X,X\sqsubseteq\neg A\}\). This is a local
   colon-for-inclusion typographical error, not a new constraint form
   ([printed p. 13](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=13)).
4. **Subsumption English reverses with grammatical voice.** “\(C\) subsumes
   \(D\)” on p. 3 and “\(C\) is subsumed by \(D\)” on p. 5 have opposite
   containment orientations. The formulas are consistent; a reconstruction
   should standardize one phrasing and keep argument order explicit
   ([printed pp. 3 and 5](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=3)).
5. **The subsumption hardness proof compresses a closure step.** Theorem 6.3
   jumps from QBF-to-coherence and coherence/incoherence reductions to both
   PSPACE-hard problems without explicitly stating PSPACE closure under
   complement. The theorem is not weakened, but a complete proof should make
   that dependency explicit
   ([printed p. 21](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=21)).
6. **Rule subscripts are visually distinct.** \(\to_{T\exists}\) on p. 14 has
   the trace letter \(T\); \(\to_{\top\exists}\) on p. 16 has the top symbol.
   Noisy OCR flattens them. Their side conditions differ: no successor by any
   role for the former, no successor by the same role for the latter
   ([printed pp. 14 and 16](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Schmidt-SchaussSmolka_1991_Attributive-concept.pdf#page=14)).
7. **Proof completeness is routinely overstated in summaries.** Proposition
   4.3 is unproved, Theorem 4.5 is one sentence, Proposition 5.2 is unproved,
   and Lemma 6.2 leaves its crucial canonical correspondence at assertion
