   large-fiber selection, local witness grafts, frozen levels and
   countable intersection; a minimal positive deciding trunk is shortened

5. **Strong dense cones:** independent successive cone-witness grafts,
   canonical witness cones and level homogeneity give a uniform level
   and all later cone conditions in the dense open set.
6. **Generic branch:** length/unboundedness dense requirements, a
   canonical name, eventual individual measure containment, exact

7. **Preservation:** bounded-name direct tree fusion; check-name witness

## Standing and native notation

The presentation retains source `P`, `<t,T>`, `Suc_T`, `Lev_n`, cones
`(T)_r`, and native full/direct **greater-is-stronger** orders. Source
finite-stem length `|t|` is typed: for finite sequences it is also their
function cardinality. Signed sentences use the source superscript-zero/
superscript-one convention. Host subscripts do not redefine native glyphs.

Trees are rooted above trunks: intermediate initial segments of nodes
above that root are retained. The source cone keeps only extensions of
its new trunk; ancestors below it are not compulsory. This is stated
before level grafting and branch arguments, not replaced by a demand
that every cone contain the global empty node.

Section 1.2 removes normality from the earlier nonprincipal measure setup.
Nonprincipality is explicit and necessary for successors to meet every
ground tail. Families/intersections are ground computations, not assertions
over new ambient families. Generic existence is supplied; countability
is merely sufficient to construct it externally. No ground or measurable
cardinal is obtained from Con(ZFC).

The worked empty-intersection family has all complements of singletons,
indexed by kappa, in U, yet empty intersection. It shows why mere
kappa-completeness cannot flatten all tree successors by a global
intersection. The normal-free proof uses fewer-than-kappa intersections
at each node. Diagonal normality appears only in the final conditional
comparison.
