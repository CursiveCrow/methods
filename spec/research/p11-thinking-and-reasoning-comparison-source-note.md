## Version and source qualifications

The actual 2012 body explicitly gives seven parts and includes Thinking in
Practice (chapters 35--40). The Oxford abstract instead says six parts and
orders some categories differently. The manuscript follows the actual
published body, not the abstract's six-part account. Publisher pp. 1--8 is
retained as the locator while the text/notes/references end at 7.
The guide's phrase "seven chapters" in Part I also conflicts with its own
actual chapter range 2--7 and the published contents. The active treatment
uses that explicit range (six chapters), without promoting the mistaken
count. Chapter 1 is the Reader's Guide before the printed Part I divider.

Figure 1.1 is a reported summary of college students' judgments about mental
activity words, adapted by the source from Rips and Conrad (1989). Its kinds
and parts relations have opposite arrow directions and different meanings.
It is not an experimentally verified hierarchy of brain operations. The
source does not give sample size, recruitment details, response instrument,
analysis estimates or uncertainty for that underlying study. No such details
or general-population result are invented; the original 1989 article is not
represented as separately acquired/read.

The historical capsule is treated as the editors' selection of connections,
not an exhaustive priority history. The body does not adopt unverified
exclusive founder claims or the etymological aside in note 3 about dead
reckoning. The substantive Hobbes computation metaphor, Craik model analogy,
Newell--Simon simulations and Marr three-level distinction are retained.
Marr's cash-register example requires the actual purchase-total goal; order
independence alone does not characterize addition. The reader treatment
explains that limitation rather than promoting one property into a unique
function theorem.
