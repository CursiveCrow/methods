# Prime filters, Priestley duality, and the Esakia back argument

Independent mathematical plan, 2026-09-06 Pacific time. No manuscript edits.

## Logical dependency and conventions

The actual `thm:p02-arbitrary-prop-compactness-bpi` at
`handbook/parts/p02/propositional-metatheory-supplement.tex:29–35` gives
arbitrary-variable propositional compactness under ZF + BPIT. For a
countable alphabet, the held Part II theorem `thm:prop-compactness` uses
the explicit countable Lindenbaum construction `lem:prop-lindenbaum`
and requires no Choice. The distinction is explicitly explained at
supplement lines 37–42.

All lattices below are bounded distributive lattices, possibly trivial.
Filters contain 1 and are meet-closed and upward closed. Ideals contain 0
and are join-closed and downward closed. Prime filters are proper and
satisfy binary-join primeness. All lattices, signatures, and index
families are sets. Prime spectra are ordered by inclusion.

## 1. Prime-filter extension

Given a filter F and ideal I with `F intersect I=empty`, a prime filter P
containing F and avoiding I exists under BPIT. The countable-lattice
version is provable in ZF.

**Finite maximal-extension step.** In a finite lattice choose a filter P
maximal among those containing F and avoiding I. For an element a,

`P[a]={x: some p in P satisfies p meet a <= x}`.

If `a join b in P` but neither a nor b belongs to P, maximality gives
`p meet a <= i` and `q meet b <= j` for some p,q in P and i,j in I.
Then `r=p meet q meet (a join b)` belongs to P. Distributivity gives

`r=(p meet q meet a) join (p meet q meet b) <= i join j`.

Since I is downward closed, r belongs to I, a contradiction. Thus P is
prime. Finite maximality requires no Choice.

**Finite local models for propositional compactness.** A bounded
distributive sublattice generated by m elements is finite: every term
is a join of meets of subsets of those m generators. Include the empty
meet 1 and empty join 0. There are at most `2^(2^m)` such forms.
Introduce one propositional variable p_a for every lattice element and
the clauses

`p_1`, `not p_0`,
`p_(a meet b) iff (p_a and p_b)`,
`p_(a join b) iff (p_a or p_b)`,
`p_a` for a in F, and `not p_b` for b in I.

A finite set of clauses mentions finitely many lattice elements. Put
them in the finite bounded distributive sublattice L_0 they generate.
The intersections `F intersect L_0` and `I intersect L_0` are disjoint
filter and ideal. The finite result gives a homomorphism L_0 -> 2
satisfying the finite clauses. Set every unmentioned variable outside
L_0 to false. Compactness supplies one total valuation; its true set
is the required prime filter. No family of finite-model witnesses is
chosen simultaneously.

**Countable ZF alternative.** Fix an enumeration a_0,a_1,... of L and
start with F_0=F. Adjoin a_n at stage n iff `F_n[a_n]` is disjoint from
the *entire* ideal I; otherwise retain F_n. This is a uniquely specified
recursion using excluded middle, not a claim of an effective decision
procedure. Its union avoids I. If a is absent from the union, its
rejected stage shows that adjoining a to any later extension meets I.
The union is therefore maximal among filters avoiding I and is prime
by the displayed distributive argument. No countable Choice is needed.

For order separation use `F=upward(a)`, `I=downward(b)` when a is not
below b. They are disjoint exactly under that hypothesis. A trivial
lattice has no disjoint filter/ideal pair and no prime filters.

## 2. Priestley spectrum and arbitrary-cover compactness

Put `X_L=PrimeFilt(L)`, ordered by inclusion, and
`eta(a)={P:a in P}`. Use the basis

`B_(a,b)=eta(a) minus eta(b)`.

Finite intersections remain of this form by replacing the positive
elements by their meet and the negative elements by their join.
Bounds handle absent positive or negative conditions. Each eta(a) is
a clopen upset. Distinct prime filters are topologically separated by
one eta(a) and its complement. If P is not contained in Q, an element
of P minus Q gives a clopen upset containing P and excluding Q, which
is the Priestley separation property.

For an arbitrary open cover take ALL basic B_(a,b) subordinate to one
cover member. If it has no finite subcover, no finite subordinate
basic family covers, because finitely many parent cover members can
be selected in ZF. Adjoin to the full lattice-homomorphism clause theory
above the clauses `p_a -> p_b` for all subordinate pairs. Every finite
set is satisfiable by a prime filter outside the corresponding finite
basic union. Propositional compactness supplies a prime filter outside
the full cover, contradiction. This proves compactness using BPIT;
when L is countable, the propositional variables are countable and
the Part II ZF compactness result suffices. The arbitrary open cover
itself need not be countable.

## 3. Surjectivity onto clopen upsets

The map eta preserves 0,1, meets, and joins; prime separation reflects
order, hence makes it injective. Let C be a clopen upset of X_L.
For P in C and Q outside C, P cannot be contained in Q. Hence some
a in P is absent from Q. For fixed P, all complements of eta(a),
a in P, cover `X_L minus C`. Compactness gives finitely many such a,
whose meet d lies in P and satisfies `eta(d) subset C`.

Now all eta(d) contained in C cover C. A second compactness application
gives finitely many; their union is eta of their join. This proves
`L ~= ClopUp(X_L)`. It uses full families, not a globally chosen d_P.
If C is empty, use eta(0); if C is the whole space, empty intermediate
meets give 1. For trivial L, X_L is empty and its clopen-upset lattice
is the one-element lattice.

## 4. Recovering a Priestley space

For a compact Priestley space X let D=ClopUp(X) and

`epsilon(x)={A in D:x in A}`.

This is a prime filter. Inclusion of the point filters is equivalent
to the original order, by upward closure and Priestley separation.
For a prime filter P of D, consider the closed family

`P union {X minus A:A in D minus P}`.

It has the finite-intersection property. Otherwise a finite meet of
members of P would be contained in a finite join of elements outside
P, contradicting upward closure and finite-join primeness. Include
empty meets and joins. Compactness supplies a point realizing exactly
P, and separation makes it unique. Positive members alone would not
establish this exact reconstruction.

To check the topology, differences of clopen upsets form a basis on
any Priestley space. For x in an open O and y outside O, either
x is not below y or y is not below x. Priestley separation supplies
respectively a clopen upset or the complement of a clopen upset that
contains x and excludes y. Use all such neighborhoods; compactness
of the closed complement of O yields a finite intersection inside O.
That intersection is one difference of clopen upsets. The inverse
image of the spectral basic `eta(A) minus eta(B)` is A minus B.
Bijectivity also gives the corresponding image equality, proving
the homeomorphism directly. Empty X and trivial D fit exactly.

Bounded lattice homomorphisms act by prime-filter inverse images;
continuous order-preserving maps act by inverse images of clopen
upsets. Basic preimage identities prove continuity and naturality.
The same membership calculations as Stone duality prove inverse
identities, now with ClopUp in place of Clop.

## 5. Esakia implication and the back condition

For a Heyting algebra H with prime spectrum X_H,

`eta(a -> b)=X_H minus downward(eta(a) minus eta(b))`.

If a->b lies in P, every Q extending P that contains a contains b.
Conversely, if a->b is absent from P, the filter P[a] avoids the ideal
downward(b): an intersection would give p meet a<=b for p in P,
hence p<=a->b, contradiction. Prime extension gives Q extending P
with a in Q and b absent. This proves the formula.

Every clopen is a finite union of basic differences, and downclosure
commutes with unions. The formula therefore proves clopenness of
downclosures of all clopens. Conversely, on an Esakia space put

`A => B = X minus downward(A minus B)`

for clopen upsets A,B. It is a clopen upset. For an upset C,
`C subset (A=>B)` iff `C intersect A subset B`; upward closure of C
proves the nontrivial direction. This supplies the Heyting adjunction.

For the morphism back step let h:H->K preserve Heyting implication,
let F be a prime filter of K, and let G be a prime filter of H with
`h^(-1)[F] subset G`. Seek F' extending F with `h^(-1)[F']=G`.
The filter and ideal to separate are

`E={k: exists f in F, g in G, f meet h(g)<=k}`,
`J={k: exists b notin G, k<=h(b)}`.

Here H minus G is an ideal, so the displayed J is the ideal generated
by its image. If E meets J, then for such f,g,b,

`f meet h(g)<=h(b)`, hence
`f<=h(g)->h(b)=h(g->b)`.

It follows that `g->b in h^(-1)[F] subset G`. Since g belongs to G,
the Heyting inequality `g meet (g->b)<=b` forces b in G, contradiction.
Thus prime-filter extension gives F' containing E and avoiding J.
Its inverse image contains G and excludes every element outside G,
so equals G exactly. This proves the order back condition for h^*.
Implication preservation is essential to the displayed argument;
an arbitrary bounded distributive-lattice homomorphism need not have it.

For a continuous order-preserving p-morphism g:X->Y, order back gives
`g^(-1)[downward S]=downward(g^(-1)[S])` for every subset S of Y.
Use S=A minus B to prove preservation of Heyting implication by
inverse images. Empty/trivial cases are vacuous or unique-map cases;
there are no prime points at which to demand an extension when the
relevant spectrum is empty. Each prime extension is used for one
given point/filter configuration, not chosen globally.
