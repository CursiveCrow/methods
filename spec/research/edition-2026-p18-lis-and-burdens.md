## Corrections applied in the reader construction

- The 2001 Definition 2.1 display forbids repeated arguments by either
  same player, while its following prose and pp. 9–10 distinguish
  prohibited P repetitions from permitted O repetitions.
  The corrected base game permits O repeats. A complete five-node
  countermodel proves that forbidding both players' repetitions destroys
  grounded correspondence.
- Definition 4.2's unrestricted deductively closed burden sets cannot
  also be disjoint if deductive closure means full classical closure:
  both contain every tautology. The reader first proves this exact
  obstruction, then states the added issue-vocabulary premise Q and
  Q-relative closure. It gives nonempty literal-profile witnesses.
  This is a declared correction, not proof of the unrepaired condition.
- Definition 4.4's parent pointer is to the current argument Ai, not
  the printed Arg1. The reader uses subtree occurrences, so a proper
  occurrence has one parent even when derivation text is duplicated.
- The source explicitly excludes multiple incomparable attack targets.
  The reader retains that domain restriction and proves why omitting it
  can make the role assignment ambiguous.
- Historical roles are attached to moves and their occurrences when
  played, rather than silently recalculated under later burdens.
- A single won play is not equated with a winning strategy.
  Infinite plays are not silently declared finite losses; the full
  reachability characterization handles unbounded histories.
- Overruling every argument for p is not equated with proving not-p;
  an applicability undercutter need not support a contrary proposition.
  The no-argument case is explicitly recorded as unsupported.
- The 2001 changing-role counterexample is retained. No grounded
  equivalence is claimed for general LIS.
- The original 1996/1997 priority generation is not silently replaced
  by the supplied fixed-defeat interface.
