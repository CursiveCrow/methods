## Scope separation and proof dependencies

The original 1987 trip switches both tensor and par and requires one orbit
over both gates of every occurrence. The modern graph switch severs one par
incidence only. These are separate defined criteria; no unproved direct
criterion-equivalence theorem is claimed.

The graph proof deliberately permits general-formula dual axiom links,
preserves formula occurrence identity, counts parallel-edge cycles and
distinguishes an acyclic disconnected Mix structure from a connected
Mix-free structure. Cut normalization is for CLOSED, unit-free binary
structures; it does not falsely reduce a cut facing an open hypothesis.
Component-count preservation is proved by the outside-forest calculation
for both choices of the removed par. Termination counts link vertices;
this is identified as a different measure from Girard's occurrence-plus-CUT
measure. Confluence explicitly handles disjoint redexes, two cuts sharing
an axiom, and both axiom choices at one cut.

The modern splitting dependency is the internal-par method of the 2023
primary, not a relabeled tensor splitting lemma. Its minimal-bridge bypass,
strict first-bridge dependency and maximal-par argument are supplied in the
annex before sequentialization uses them.
