# P15.33 — Normal domains of attraction

## Completed mathematical target

The new body and annex supply the complete nondegenerate normal-domain
criterion stated in Durrett Theorem 3.4.14: necessity, sufficiency,
existence of the mean, exact centering, construction of positive
normalizers, and characterization of every normalization giving the
prescribed standard normal limit. There are 12 reciprocal proof pairs.

The independent proof gives the full equivalence between negligible
tails and slow variation of the truncated second moment. It handles
finite and infinite variance, strict and closed cutoffs, atoms, and
arbitrary initial normalizer sequences. Necessity uses independent-copy
symmetrization and a nonnegative trigonometric kernel; it does not
assume a general infinitely divisible-law theorem.

The exact 1954 Gnedenko–Kolmogorov source was acquired during the block.
Its Section 35, Theorem 1, printed pp. 172–175, is retained as a separate
cutoff presentation, including its weighted Cauchy–Schwarz argument,
cutoff sandwich, tail-quantile construction, and diagonal choice.
The actual infinite-variance i.i.d. cutoff interface that this proof
uses is stated and proved in both directions.

Scope distinction: the source's broader general-array theorems and
their own canonical-measure proofs in Sections 19–26 have not been
reproduced at their full original scope. The needed i.i.d. interface
has a complete independent proof. Thus there is no missing premise
in the mathematical proof supplied here, but there remains a distinct
historical-presentation obligation for those larger original proof
routes. This note does not claim full reproduction of that broader
source chain.

## Mathematical and source distinctions

Nondegeneracy is explicit. The constant law X=1 has the printed tail
ratio tending to zero but cannot yield a nondegenerate normal limit
under deterministic centering/scaling. The constant zero law has an
undefined zero denominator. Neither case is silently admitted.

The denominator used to construct a standard-normal scale is the
truncated second moment of X minus its actual mean. For a finite
second moment, using the uncentered second moment instead would
produce the wrong limiting variance when the mean is nonzero.
For infinite second moment, the fixed-shift comparison is proved
through bounded-cutoff integrals and the integrable pointwise
difference of the squared functions; no infinity-minus-infinity
expression is used.

The first normalizer construction solves the continuous equation
E[min(Y squared/b squared,1)]=1/n. Continuity, strict decrease on an
eventually valid interval, existence, uniqueness and growth are proved.
No exact solution is asserted for a discontinuous truncated moment.
The source's distinct normalizer construction uses a tail-quantile
cutoff C_n(delta), followed by a proved diagonal delta_n tending
to zero and by B_n squared = n times the truncated variance.
These two constructions are retained separately.

The source writes open truncations in its displayed Stieltjes integrals.
The manuscript uses closed retained cutoffs and strict removed tails,
so their union is exhaustive even at atoms. It proves the endpoint
equivalence for the final ratio criterion. It does not silently discard
mass exactly at a truncation threshold.

The source's scaled H on p. 174 is written as mathcal H = U/x squared,
distinct from the chapter's unscaled H_X. The cutoff comparison
explicitly proves eventual positivity of its denominator. A first
crossing index supplies the source interpolation without assuming the
cutoff sequence is monotone.

The finite-variance branch uses the needed sufficient case of Section 21
Theorem 3, with the full Lindeberg theorem already supplied in P15.31.
The infinite-variance branch uses the exact needed i.i.d. specialization
of Section 26 Theorem 4; that specialization is fully proved here.
Its necessity proof is explicitly derived from the independent kernel
argument, not misattributed to the source's canonical-measure derivation.
Full original generality and original proof routes for Sections 19–26
remain a separate source-presentation obligation.
