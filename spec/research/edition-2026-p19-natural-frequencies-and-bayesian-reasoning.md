## Scope frozen before drafting

The source contains **two studies**, both included. Study 1 crosses probability
and natural-frequency formats with standard and short menus, over fifteen
problems. Study 2 crosses probability and relative-frequency formats with
standard, hybrid and short menus, over twenty-four problems. Study 2 was
conducted first (note 4), despite its presentation order. It contains no
natural-frequency input condition. The three elementary shortcut families,
their combination and the beam construction are retained as distinct
algorithmic presentations rather than folded into an unexplained slogan.

Scope does not silently expand to the other experiments cited in the article's
historical discussion, a current clinical application, training-system efficacy,
multiple dependent cues, continuous variables, or a proof of evolutionary
mechanism. Their discussion is attributed and bounded. The exact same joint
law is an explicit hypothesis of the normative representation bridge.

## Evidence retained: Study 1

Sixty paid Salzburg students; 21 men, 39 women, ten disciplines (mainly
psychology), median age 21; no familiarity with Bayes' theorem. Individual
or small-group sessions (2-3; two instances of 5); scheduled about one hour
with extra time allowed. Actual first/second mean minutes 73/53 and ranges
25-180/30-120. No population probability sample, payment amount, recruitment
dates or inter-coder coefficient is invented.

Randomized assignment to two groups receiving paired conditions: standard
probability with short frequency; standard frequency with short probability.
Fifteen contents each twice across sessions one week apart; formats/menus
balanced by session; same-content versions separated across sessions; two
random orders per group. Thirty repeated tasks per person. Probability
questions request percentages for a single case; frequency questions request
both numerator and denominator for a new representative evidence-positive
sample. Output wording changes with input format.

Both process and outcome criteria retained, including floor/ceiling to a whole
percentage, correct unevaluated fractions, ignored final division errors,
and exclusion of insufficiently accurate shortcuts. Concurrent write-aloud
instructions, guessing/calculation declarations and limited clarification
interviews are described. A coded Bayesian method is not the same variable
as a numerically exact final answer or a person's unobserved process.

reported benchmark percentages are preserved. Table 3 is reproduced as a full

## Evidence retained: Study 2 and additional analyses

Fifteen paid Konstanz students, seven listed disciplines; 8 men, 7 women,
median age 22, no familiarity with Bayes; one group; 25-DM best-performance
prize. Twenty-four problems (twelve shared with Study 1, some numbers changed)
in three menus, giving 72 tasks per person and 1,080 responses. Probability
versus relative-frequency wording assigned to twelve problems each; both use
percentages and ask single-event probability. Six one-hour sessions in three
weeks; versions never together; menus balanced within session; source's
randomization of tasks, session order and numerical-information order retained.

No instructed write-aloud protocol: spontaneous notes and retrospective

## Corrections and qualifications applied directly

- Mammography: .01/.8/.096 implies 25/322, while 8/103 is the exact answer
  for the source's smaller count table and short menus. Their rounded equality
  near 7.8% is not exact equality of inputs. Exact matching N=12,500 counts
  are (100,25,1188,11187).
- Five Table 2 H-complement denominators are rounded: measles, HIV, needle,
  suicide, Rolex. The annex preserves both leaf and literal-rate calculations,
  rather than declaring all printed ratios one coherent frequency tree.
  Study 1 measles is 1/6 by leaves or 2000/11979 by literal rates.
- Study 2 measles: 4000/4199 from standard inputs; 20/21 from rounded
  hybrid/short inputs. Full count realizations of both are supplied. The
  Study 1 measles numbers are not reused for Study 2.
- Source HIV shortcut illustration (.002,.99,.02) is distinct from its
  Study 1 HIV row; exact 99/1097 versus shortcut .10.
- Appendix M.'s 10/210 has correct complement 95.2381%, while the recorded
  answer is 95.7% and the source accepts the process under its division-error
  rule. Appendix S.'s reported .04% is retained, but the stated factors
  correctly multiply to .00042%, with the units shown.
- Natural frequencies can have equal subgroup sizes; their defining feature
  here is a common nested scale, not mandatory inequality. Hypothesis-null
  or evidence-null cases are not assigned fictitious conditional rates.
- Generic frequencies per 1,000 do not establish that 1,000 observations
  were collected. Population prediction and parameter uncertainty need their
  additional sampling/model premises; source theoretical Result 4 is not a
  newly demonstrated empirical result of these studies.
