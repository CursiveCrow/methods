## Invariants

- I01: The four-page 1961 Doklady item is the registered announcement.  The
  twenty-nine-page 1962 Sibirskii Matematicheskii Zhurnal item is the complete
  expansion.  Pagination, theorem numbering, and claim strength are never
  silently transferred between them.
- I02: The two bodies have the same Russian title.  The official Math-Net
  English titles differ: “Single-Place Predicates” for 1961 and “One-Place
  Predicates” for 1962.  Neither is silently normalized.
- I03: The formal system printed as Cyrillic И is typographically rendered
  \(\mathcal I\).  Its iterated-successor terms, atomic predicate applications,
  Boolean operations, and individual and monadic-predicate quantifiers are
  given as an eliminable primitive grammar.  It is an interpreted formal
  language, not an automaton, set, theorem, or metalinguistic slogan.
- I04: Individuals range over \(1,2,\ldots\), monadic variables over arbitrary
  subsets, and successor is primitive.  Equality, order, bounded individual
  quantifiers, constants, and \(t+k\) are eliminable conveniences.
- I05: Fictitious occurrence outside an interval is semantic independence.
  Controlled-from-above/below and lower/upper \(t\)-formulae are the source's
  syntactically restricted classes, defined by exact atomic-argument and
  individual-quantifier range forms.  Their bounded-prefix and tail dependence
  are separately stated semantic consequences, not replacement definitions.
- I06: Announcement Theorem 1 and expanded Section 1 normal form are reported
  with their effective transformation and proof architecture, not merely a
  conclusion.
- I07: Anticipation, finite memory, finite operator, and the automaton-realizable
  zero-anticipation class are distinct.  Finite memory permits transition
  functions of the whole future suffix; a finite operator adds bounded
  anticipation.  The 1961 announcement names the zero-anticipation finite
  class with Cyrillic Л and the 1962 expansion with Latin \(A\); any normalized
  use is declared rather than transferred silently.
- I08: Weight is the least cardinality of a finite memory-state set.  A
  one-state finite-memory operator can still have unbounded, noneffective
  future dependence.
- I09: Portion, homogeneous degree, basis, and internal transition are defined
  as properties/data of stream sets.  They are not invented automaton kinds.
  The 1961 announcement's all-prefix convention permits the empty portion; the
  1962 expansion names only portions following nonempty prefix intersections.
  Many-coordinate portions are typed with equal-length coordinate prefixes
  and coordinatewise concatenation.  For a zero-anticipation graph,
  \(\operatorname{tail}_r\), the residual map \(T_u\), and the exact
  consistent-versus-inconsistent paired-prefix graph-portion equation are
  defined before use; \(T_u\) has stream-operator standing and is expressly
  distinguished from a broad memory state.
  Under the source's whole-future finite-memory equations, broad weight bounds
  neither the nonempty nor the all-prefix graph-portion count.  The reader
  withdraws both the printed \(k\) claim and the former \(k+1\) repair, proves
  this with an exact weight-two zero-anticipation counterexample having three
  nonempty and four all-prefix portions, and retains only the exact statement
  in terms of distinct operators induced on suffixes by consistent prefix
  pairs.
- I10: Every \(\mathcal I\)-definable set is homogeneous.  The converse
  requires closedness in the Cantor product topology.  Homogeneous
  nondefinable sets remain visible.
- I11: The recurrent existential-predicate form has a free input predicate, a
  literal fixed initial basis state, bound state and choice predicates, and a
  quantifier-free current/next matrix.  Its two-way relation with closed
  homogeneous sets is preserved.  The source's total covering construction is
  used only for a nonempty set; the empty set is defined separately by an
  inconsistent initial-state conjunction in the same recurrent shape.  The
  nonempty restriction and empty-set case split are explicitly handbook-owned.
- I12: The full definable class is generated from closed homogeneous sets by
  complement, finite union, and predicate projection.  The announcement's
  open Borel/projective question is not silently answered.
- I13: Internal portion transitions are effectively obtained.  External choice
  may require nonemptiness/truth decisions, hence the source's conditional
  \(\mathcal I\)-algorithm boundary.  Putnam removes the condition only for
  the stated recurrent fragment.
- I14: A continuous total operator with \(\mathcal I\)-definable graph is
  finite.  Compact uniform continuity supplies bounded anticipation and the
  portion basis supplies memory.
- I15: Expanded Section 4, Theorem 2, removes continuity and concludes only
  finite memory.  It does not imply bounded anticipation or finite-operator
  standing.
- I16: A formula defining exactly an operator graph and a relation merely
  solved by an operator are different claims.  Theorem \(4'\) is an existence
  theorem for closed homogeneous relations.
- I17: General \(A\)-solution is defined by both directions: every
  \(A\)-solution arises through an \(A\)-operator parameter substitution, and
  every such substitution yields a solution.  General zero-anticipation
  solution replaces the operator class throughout.
- I18: Announcement Theorem 5 and expanded Section 4, Theorem 3, give the
  no-solution/general-solution alternative for the closed relation defined by
  an \(\mathcal I\)-formula, with finite homogeneous degree, the weight bound,
  stable deletion argument, parameter choices, and conditional emptiness
  boundary.
- I19: Expanded Theorem \(3'\) is separately quantified for each fixed
  anticipation bound \(p\).  It is not one general solution across unbounded
  lookahead classes.
- I20: Explicit definition, finite-memory conclusion, anticipation
  characterization, shifted-formula synthesis, and the two-formula worked
  example retain their exact Section 5 scope.
- I21: Zero anticipation induces extension-independent, prefix-compatible maps
  on words of each positive length.  Automaton-enumerability is defined by
  their lengthwise ranges; automaton-decidability is defined by the terminal
  recognizer output.  The printed converse omits the nonemptiness needed by its
  own construction; the reader gives the corrected nonempty, prefix-closed,
  properly extendible statement and identifies nonemptiness as handbook-owned.
- I22: The weak system \(\mathcal I'\) restricts predicate variables to
  eventually-zero predicates.  A special beginning ends only after the
  predicate is zero forever; it is not an arbitrary prefix.  Expanded Section
  5, Theorem 3, separates existence for arbitrary \(\mathcal I\)-formulae from
  effective construction for \(\mathcal I'\)-formulae and preserves the
  source's explicit Büchi attribution.
- I23: The modern one-sentence regular/MSO equivalence is not quoted as the
  literal theorem of the four-page announcement.  Trakhtenbrot's package is
  broader and remains distinct from Büchi, Elgot, and Thomas.
- I24: Source-time conditionality and the author's Church, Büchi, Elgot,
  Putnam, and priority remarks are historical reports, not independent
  priority adjudications.
- I25: The reconstruction is a bounded scholarly translation of used
  definitions, results, proof moves, examples, and qualifications.  It does
  not claim a complete line-by-line translation or a Russian-language
  specialist's certification.  Every correction not printed by the source,
  including the residual-operator exactification and nonempty/empty split, is
  identified as handbook-owned at its public point of use.
- I26: Every new technical name satisfies the repository AGENTS rule at first

- I27: Public archival access is not an open-content licence.  The Math-Net
