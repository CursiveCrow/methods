## Proof-relevant construction and source corrections

The pointed-map comparison keeps its second coordinate: transport of
the pointing proof is the inverse evaluation path followed by the old
pointing proof. The induction computation and funext beta then cancel
to the target pointing proof. In the II-to-III implication, the
contractibility of pointed self-maps of the path family is obtained from
native J; it is not mistakenly inferred from an assumption about only
maps with domain R.

The two-sided-to-local induction proof uses a global motive indexed by
paths from its variable first endpoint to the chosen point. It supplies
the diagonal datum by right-based identity elimination, then evaluates
the resulting section at reflexivity. It does not assume identity
elimination in a fixed loop type.

For flattening, a horizontal Sigma lift is followed by the vertical
fiber correction. The dependent total-space lift is constructed by
inverse transport of two explicit paths with the same transported
endpoint. The nondependent recursor instead uses the calibrated
component b·ap(a_g)(theta)^(-1); its edge action appends
ap(a_g)(theta), and association/inverse/unit laws prove beta.
Both inverse homotopies then supply equality-family edge lifts;
neither is inferred from agreement at points.

The integer-indexed cover contraction has zero, positive, first-negative
and subsequent-negative cases. Its comparison on canonical integer
representatives uses integer setness to compare the presentation
homotopy to reflexivity, not judgmental normalization at a free integer.

The direct circle identity-system construction follows the native
transported datum along K_n. Its omitted winding-witness comparison is
now supplied: pair composition has second coordinate gamma followed by
transport action on h_n and monodromy; the signed power comparison
changes the first coordinate. Integer setness compares the two parallel
fiber proofs after that change. This supplies the exact second-coordinate
transport required by the Sigma-path comparison.
