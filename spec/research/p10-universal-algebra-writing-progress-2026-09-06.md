The equational proof uses universal identity semantics, finite
deductions, the least fully invariant congruence, and quotient
satisfaction under every valuation. Finite representative selection
is enough for each equation; no global quotient section is assumed.
The proof also establishes the equivalence of operation compatibility
with the source's one-occurrence replacement rule. The finite-premise
conclusion explicitly lets the selected finite subset depend on the
consequence.

Free algebras are given with their generator maps. The generator map
need not be injective in a trivial variety. The nonempty-term
hypothesis, empty generators with constants, failure of a uniform
initial object for nonempty sets in the empty signature, and the
free-monoid example are all treated explicitly.

Subdirect representation fills the missing toolkit item in mandatory
register N-d-001. The proof establishes congruence correspondence,
the monolith criterion, the finite-arity chain-union argument,
maximal congruences omitting a specified pair, and the embedding into
the selected quotients. Choice is explicit in the general case.
The finite case uses finitely many selections and needs no Choice.
Trivial algebras and empty products are covered under stated conventions.

Primary locators: Millennium Edition II §§10–11 and 14 for terms,
identities, free algebras, and deduction; II §8 and Theorem 6.20 for
subdirect representation and quotient correspondence. The book's
nonempty-product variety convention and the handbook's empty-product
convention remain explicit and compatible.

## Direct corrections

The old local-valuation definition of equational consequence has
been replaced in the active development by universal identity
consequence, which validates its substitution rule.

The CPL countermodel paragraph now extends the proper principal
filter generated by the complement of the unprovable formula's class.
It proves the characteristic homomorphism falsifies the formula and
states BPIT explicitly. The Lindenbaum definition requires provable
equivalence to be a congruence. The presheaf-to-topos clause requires
a small category relative to the chosen universe.

In N13, the Safra label-count argument now accounts for absent states
with (n+1)^n label encodings and derives the stated asymptotic bound.
The Büchi emptiness criterion now requires a reachable accepting
state with a positive-length return, with both proof directions.
Historical erroneous readings remain in the respective source notes,
not as instructions for the reader to repair the active mathematics.
