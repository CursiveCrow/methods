## Verdict and one definedness clarification

The mathematical construction validates the claimed priority intervals
for formulas generated by the displayed Niwinski classes. In particular,
binding an arbitrary body graph at the least interval priority is sound;
it does not require the graph to retain a syntax-tree shape or a unique
outer binder. The substitution construction and the parity-preserving
embeddings are sound. The earlier two strategy constructions establish
epsilon elimination for these graphs with the same priority interval.

There is one small missing detail in the statement of epsilon elimination
for *open* graphs. The phrase “For every current letter” near original
line 7739 reuses an earlier construction whose letters record proposition
truth values only. A local game for an open graph must also know the truth
values of its free-variable exits. Without those values the local winning
function is not defined: the graph consisting of the exit `Z` must have
different constant summaries at states inside and outside `eta(Z)`, even
when their proposition letters agree.

Add the following clarification before defining the local winning function:

> For an open graph, its finite local letter records both the proposition
> truth values and, for each free variable occurring in the graph, whether
> the current state belongs to its environment value. Free-variable exits
> terminate with these truth values. In the closed case the additional
> coordinate is empty.

An equally sufficient narrower statement is to apply epsilon elimination
only after the formula graph has been closed. The final binary-tree
automaton claim concerns closed formulas and is unaffected by this issue.

## Class-generation induction

The least classes in the host definition have finitary generating rules,
so every member has a finite generation derivation. Induction on the
level, followed by induction on that derivation, covers every displayed
rule. Fixed-point-free formula graphs are acyclic, so giving their
controls any single priority in the target interval does not affect
their finite-play semantics.

For positive levels, the inclusions are exactly as stated:

- The previous same-side interval is a subset of the next one.
- Adding 2 to all priorities of a `Pi_(n-1)` graph maps `[0,n-2]` into
  `[2,n]`, preserving the order and parity of every recurring minimum.
- The interval `[1,n-1]` of a `Sigma_(n-1)` graph already lies in
  `[0,n-1]`.

The special treatment of level zero is necessary and supplied. Boolean
and modal entries occur only in a finite prefix before entry into an
operand graph. Thus their priority can be the interval maximum even when
that maximum has the opposite parity from a recurring operand priority.

## Substitution and the status of exits

Let `D` be the denotation of `B` under the current environment. By the
inductive invariant the host graph, with its free `Z` exits interpreted
by `D`, has exactly the denotation of `A` under `eta[Z := D]`.
Capture-avoiding substitution identifies this set with the denotation of
`A[B/Z]` under `eta`.

Redirecting designated host exits into a disjoint graph for `B` implements
that interpretation. When a winning host strategy reaches such an exit,
its current state is a winning state for the same player in the `B`
graph. Switch to a winning strategy there. The switch occurs at most
once in every play, because the inserted graph has no edge back to the
host. A play that never switches keeps the host outcome. The same
argument applies to each player, including finite terminal outcomes.

A single shared copy of `B` is sufficient even if the host has several
designated exits. The strategy may use the finite history, and the
semantics of `B` depends only on its entry state and environment. Separate
copies per exit would also work but are unnecessary. Free exits belonging
to `B`, including its own free `Z` exits, must remain open. Binding a
variable around the *whole* resulting formula in a later induction step
may then redirect these exits as well; this implements that later
explicit binding and creates no illicit capture by an existing host
binder.

## Binding an arbitrary body graph

Fix the other free-variable values. Let `F(U)` be the existential winning
set at the entry of the open body graph when its `Z` exits terminate
according to `U`. The semantic invariant identifies this with the body
formula's denotation; positivity makes `F` monotone. No stronger structural
invariant on the graph is needed.

For the odd bottom priority 1, redirect every body `Z` exit through a new
regeneration control of priority 1 and back to the body entry.

If `s` belongs to the least fixed point, let `rho(s)` be its first entry
stage in the increasing approximants `U_0 = empty`,
`U_(alpha+1) = F(U_alpha)`, with unions at limits. A first entry stage is a
successor: membership in a limit union already occurs at an earlier
stage. At a state of rank `alpha+1`, use a winning body strategy with
`Z := U_alpha`. Every reached `Z` exit lies in `U_alpha`, so its rank is
at most `alpha` and strictly smaller than the current rank. Reset at
regeneration using the new rank. Infinite regeneration would give an
infinite strictly descending sequence of ordinals, which is impossible.
Every final finite outcome or infinite body tail wins by its body
strategy. Finite rank-based prefixes do not affect parity.

If `s` is outside the least fixed point `U`, the equality `F(U) = U`
gives a universal winning body strategy with `Z := U`. Every `Z` exit
reached under it is outside `U`. Restart the same prescription at its
state. Finitely many regenerations leave a universal winning terminal
outcome or body tail. Infinitely many regenerations force recurring
minimum 1, because all body priorities are at least 1; this also wins
for the universal player.

The greatest-fixed-point argument at bottom priority 0 is the exact dual.
Inside `U = nu F`, use existential winning body strategies with `Z := U`.
Returns preserve membership; infinitely many returns have recurring
minimum 0. Outside `U`, use the first exclusion stage in the descending
approximants, starting with the entire state set and taking intersections
at limits. A first exclusion stage is a successor. A universal winning
body strategy under the preceding approximation reaches only `Z` exits
already excluded there, giving a strictly smaller exclusion rank. Thus
only finitely many regenerations occur on each compatible play, followed
by a universal winning body outcome.

These four constructions remain valid when the body already contains
priority 1 or 0, several cycles, shared components, or graphs assembled
by substitution. Equality with the least body priority is sufficient;
strict inequality is unnecessary. The only needed priority condition is
that the regeneration priority is no larger than any body priority.

## Epsilon elimination and terminal padding

With the open-letter clarification above, the earlier finite local games
apply verbatim to the finite graph controls. Retain the identities of
modal controls, even when two controls express the same modality. A
colored exit comprises that control and the minimum of its finite local
episode, including its starting control and final modal control.

Resetting the register to `M = max I` before reading the initial control
produces a value in `I`; updating by minimum keeps it in `I`. Infinite
local episodes use their actual recurring control priorities. Using the
permanently accumulated register instead would be unsound, but the
manuscript expressly avoids that error.

The exact disjunction of winning supports is monotone and handles both
empty disjunctions and empty conjunctions. A global positional winning
graph strategy supplies realizable colored supports. After the opponent
selects an exit, any finite realization compatible with that strategy
can be appended to the current history. Positionality ensures that
successive realizations fit the same strategy. In the reverse direction,
the selected support supplies a winning local strategy, and the virtual
acceptance history supplies the modal choices and subsequent supports.
Local divergence and finite outcomes are covered explicitly in the
earlier proof. The use of positional determinacy here is for a possibly
infinite model-state/control arena with finitely many priorities, as
provided by the manuscript's earlier positional theorem.

On infinitely many completed episodes, finite nonempty blocks of actual
priorities have exactly the same least recurring priority as their
minima. The output control sequence is the finite initial color `M`
followed by those minima. Every color belongs to `I`. Expression
administration may also receive priority `M`: every infinite play visits
control states infinitely often, so recurring administration cannot
introduce a smaller recurring priority. This establishes preservation of
the interval without assuming bounded local episode lengths.

For a closed `Sigma_n` graph on a full binary tree, the two modalities
for a direction agree because the successor in that direction is unique.
For `n >= 2`, the interval `[1,n]` contains both an odd priority 1 and an
even priority 2. Replacing transition constants by atoms leading to
absorbing states of those priorities preserves their outcomes. Following
direction 1 forever is always possible on a full binary tree. No original
priority can recur after entry into an absorbing state. The final
transitions therefore satisfy the stated nonempty positive Boolean
grammar while retaining the claimed interval.
