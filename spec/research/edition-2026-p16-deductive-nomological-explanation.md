## Content and necessary qualifications

The chapter gives the four explanation conditions, the prediction
comparison, the relational model language, and definitions 7.1-7.4.
The intended interpretation and the qualitative character of primitives
are stated separately from syntactic form. The original work does not
provide an automatic classifier for qualitative predicates.

The proof of 7.7 supplies the complete common-content decomposition.
The final potential-explanation definition is 7.8: a basic-literal set
Gamma verifies C, does not entail E, and is compatible with T. The
compatibility model and the countermodel to E are necessarily different.
T is an individual sentence, so the satisfiability premise is for
{T} union Gamma. An editorial finite-certificate proof restricts Gamma
to atoms occurring in C; it does not turn first-order satisfiability
into a decision procedure. Four examples include partial self-explanation
and a possible verifying observation that is false in the actual model.

Relative emergence follows the source's Part II distinctions between
whole, parts, part attributes, structure and available theory. The
finite bridge model separates missing structural information from a
missing theoretical implication. The two-time-scale thermal model is
an explicitly stipulated ODE reconstruction, with a full solution,
turning point and sign calculation; its coefficients are not recovered
experimental measurements. The treatment of functions, motives and
historical understanding preserves the source's arguments without
presenting them as newly validated psychological findings.

The final causal-direction example is an editorial comparison with the
existing finite structural causal models. It proves why an observational
deduction alone does not determine intervention behavior. It is not
attributed to the 1948 paper.

Logical prerequisites use the active first-order soundness theorem and
countable-language completeness corollary. The arbitrary-signature
choice assumption is not imported into the countable model language.
