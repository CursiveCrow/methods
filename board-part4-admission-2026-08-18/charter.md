# Board charter: Part IV source/content review

- Artifact: the complete live reader graph rooted at `handbook/parts/p04.tex`
  and `handbook/annex/p04.tex`, including exact source wrappers and editorial
  supplements.
- Purpose: determine whether *Computability and Complexity* is source-faithful,
  mathematically correct, complete for every Part IV register commitment, and
  free of reader-facing construction debt before the one final book build.
- Mode: audit.
- Invariants:
  - exact inherited artifacts and their order remain unchanged;
  - editorial material must be correct, sourced, nonduplicative, and visibly
    distinct from inherited source;
  - definitions precede use and every reduction, hierarchy, complexity bound,
    theorem hypothesis, proof step, and proof status is exact;
  - no reader-visible OPEN, unadmitted, owed, stub, pointer-only substitute,
    internal workflow ID, or unsupported technical name survives;
  - source/content review remains separate from final build, visual, human,
    administrative, and user acceptance.
- Criteria: source correspondence; computability/complexity correctness;
  registered topic and apparatus completeness; labels/citations/index/annex
  reciprocity; reader-facing provenance and status.
- Severity: blocker, must-fix, should-fix, consider, discard, unresolved-user.
- Source rule: exact file/line evidence and primary or authoritative locators;
  an unverified claim cannot block.
- Consensus: rough consensus; blind opening review followed by focused repair
  rounds and a final source/content poll.
- Roster: source-fidelity, computability/complexity topic specialist, and
  adversarial-publication seats.
- Board type: live; exact deployment identifiers unavailable to the chair.

1. Machines/effective procedures, mu-recursion, equivalence theorems, Church--

3. Turing degrees, jumps, Post's problem, finite injury, simple/creative sets,

4. Hyperarithmetical/analytical hierarchies, Kolmogorov complexity, MRDP, word
   problems, busy beaver, computable structures, complexity theory, all
   tables/figures/indexes/citations, and every reader-visible status claim.
