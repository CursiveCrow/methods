### F4 [must-fix] [open] Arbitrary many-sorted signatures cannot flatten into the fixed countable variable supply
- author: topic-specialist r1
- criterion: I3, C2, and C3 — the translation theorem must be defined for every signature quantified over in its statement.
- location: `handbook/restoration/inherited-03038-03104.tex:4-7`; `handbook/parts/p02/variants.tex:22-49,95-110,152-167`; `handbook/parts/p02/second-and-higher-order-logic-supplement.tex:15-35`.
- claim: The governing one-sorted first-order syntax fixes the countable variable set (\{x_n:n\in\mathbb N\}). The strict many-sorted definition allows an arbitrary nonempty set of sorts and assigns each sort a disjoint stock of variables. Its flattening then says to regard all those stocks as distinct untyped variable names, and the theorem quantifies over every strict many-sorted signature. - counterexample: Take an uncountable set of sorts and no nonlogical symbols. A disjoint nonempty (indeed displayed countably infinite) stock for each sort has uncountably many variable tokens. There is no injection of those tokens into the fixed countable (\Var), so (\operatorname{tr}_{\mathrm{MS}}(\varphi)) need not be a member of the earlier (\Fm(\Lang_{\mathrm{MS}}^\flat)), and the right side of Theorem `thm:p02-many-sorted-translation` is undefined.
- correction: Either restrict (\mathsf{Sort}) to a countable set and fix an explicit injection of the disjoint sorted stocks into the governing (\Var), or parameterize the one-sorted syntax, semantics, substitution, and completeness theorem by a variable supply of adequate cardinality and use that enlarged supply in the flattening. The all-variable guard must be rebuilt over the same declared supply.
