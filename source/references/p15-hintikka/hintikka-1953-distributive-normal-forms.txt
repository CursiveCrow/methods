                    INTRODUCTION

 In the analysis of various logical calculi, an important role
is often played by the normal forms of their expressions. As
characteristic examples we can mention:
  (a) the normal forms of the sentential calculus;
  (b) the normal forms used in the calculus of monadic predicates;
  (c) the normal form in the calculus of predicates which is
      referred to in Herman literature as pränex.
  These examples illustrate different methods of generating
normal forms. The pränex form is obtained by driving the
quantifiers out of the bulk of the formula in question, so that
one obtains an expression which results from a quantifier-free
expression by prefixing to it suitable quantifiers. The other
standard forms mentioned above are formed by means of an
entirely different method. In these normal forms, the quantifiers
and the negation-signs are driven or »distributed» as deep into
the expressions as possible. Normal forms obtained with the
aid of methods illustrated by the examples (a) —(b) may be
called distributive. In this paper, we shall examine some classes
of such distributive normal forms.
 In the sentential logic, distributive methods were used already
by Boole ((1), pp. 72 -76). He obtained one of the normal
forms (a) by his process of »development» or »expansion». He
also employed the term »constituent» in agreement with our
usage. In the calculus of predicates, distributive methods go
back at least to Behmann (1). The normal forms (b) are due
to J. Herbrand ((1) pp. 52 —54; it is easy to construct a
standard form which is dual to Herbrand’s normal form).
With respect to the normal forms (b), a distinction has been

made by Quine and by von Wright between the validity
in ali domains and the validity in ali non-empty domains.
 Hilbert and Bernays ((1), pp. 146 —148) make use of Beh-
mann’s methods, but State that »eine einfach charakterisierbare
 Normalform erhalten wir auf diese Weise im allgemeinen nicht».
  The purpose of this investigation is to generalize the normal
forms (b) to the whole calculus of predicates (of the first

forms. In § 3 we shall give concise definitions of certain normal
forms which generalize (b), and prove that every formula of
the calculus of predicates is transformable into these forms.
 This shows that the above statement by Hilbert and Bernays

suspected, the generalized normal forms are not uniquely
determined in the sense that equivalent formulae should have
the same normal form. Our normal forms will usually contain
redundant parts, the elimination of which is tantamount to
the giving of a method of decision for the calculus of predicates,
 and thus effectively unsolvable.
  In spite of this fact, the distributive normal forms are believed
to constitute an organic part of the thcory of the calculus
 of predicates. They have a clear-cut structure which is the
 source of the many interesting properties of theirs. They admit
 certain generalizations and exhihit an ahundance of Special
cases. Moreover, it seems that the technique of the distributive
 normal forms is not limited to the calculus of first-order pred-

or operators behaving like quantifiers. Ali the calculi of
predicates of higher order are cases in point, and so is modal
logic (cf. von Wright (3)). Professor G. H. von Wright has

the pränex form in modal logic, in contradistinction to the
 distributive forms. In this respect, the latter forms are more
general than the pränex form. In this paper we shall, how-

 28. Aims of a theory of kernels. In this Chapter we shall investi-
                                            a
gate some properties of the constituents C a+ n (n > 2) which
 have no analogues in the sentential calculus or in the calculus
 of monadic predicates. The most important property of this
kind is that constituents O a a+n with n > 2 may be refutable.
As pointed out in section 26, the discovery of ali the refutable
 constituents is tantamount to a solution of the Entscheidungs-
problem for the calculus of predicates. According to a well-
known result of Alonzo B. Church, the problem is effectively
 unsolvable. But even if we cannot find, for the constituents,
an effective criterion of satisfiability which is necessary as
well as sufficient, it is of interest to try to find some general
 sufficient conditions of refutahility. For certain Special classes
 of constituents these conditions may also be necessary, and
thus give a method of decision for them. An inquiry into such
 criteria may also the expected to give some insight into the
reasons for the refutahility of constituents in general.
  The purpose of this Chapter is to formulate and to prove
some such sufficient criteria of refutahility. These criteria may
 be regarded as the simplest non-trivial criteria of this kind.
 They are closely related to the results of von Wright in (2)
 and (4).

      OUTLINES FOR FURTHER WORK


  In the course of our discussion of the distributive normal
forms, we quoted a few results for which we have not given
complete proofs. Some of the unproved statements may be
verified by means of arguments which are similar to those
used above. Hence they do not require any new principles
or methods. As an example, we may refer to the results on
      mentioned in section 40. Their proofs involve no essen-
tially new methods over and above those used in § 5.
 There remain, however, a few results the most natural
derivations of which involve different methods. Apart from
the questions related to identity, we note the following results
of this kind:
 i) the fact the results of § 5 really amount to a method of
     decision for formulae of degree two;
 ii) the fact that the results of § 5 can be extended so as to
     give a method of decision for the formulae mentioned
     on page 61;
iii) the results concerning isomorphic constituents formulated
    on p. 50.

 In order to be able to prove these results, we need soine
sufficient conditions of satisfiability in constituents. Yet the
above discussion gives only sufficient conditions of refu-
tability, i. e., necessary conditions of satisfiability. The best
known result which may be used to find sufficient conditions
of satisfiability in the calculus of predicates is perhaps Her-
brand’s theorem (Herbrand (1), pp. 112—117). As a matter
of fact, it is not difficult to establish certain metatheorems
on constituents which may be said to be related to Her-

                               69
                      Jaakko Hintikka


brand’s theorem and which may be nsed to prove the results
i—hi above. The reason why we have here omitted these
metatheorems is that they seem to us to be in no way peculiar
to the distributive normal forms. We hope to be able to
 investigate the questions connected with the concept of
 satisfiability elsewhere. We shall try to formulate and to
prove certain general criteria of satisfiability which may be
 applied to ali the formulae of the calculus of predicates.
When they are applied to the constituents, they give exactly
those metatheorems to which we Just referred; when they
are applied to pränex formulae, they yield certain usual
formulations of Hekbrand’s theorem. By formulating these
 criteria, we hope to bring out more fully the ideasunderlying
the truly remarkable Theorem.
